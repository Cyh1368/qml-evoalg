# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Dual RY–RZ rotation blocks around structured entanglement yield +0.02 combined score  
  • Seed program (depth 51, 315 gates, 180 params → combined 0.62) and Gen 49 “Graph-Regularized Two-Layer Ansatz” (depth 53, 315 gates, 180 params → combined 0.62) both wrap four CZ matchings in an initial and a shallow output RY–RZ layer, outperforming single-layer designs (e.g. Gen 41’s combined 0.60).  
- Expanded CZ matchings for fuller coverage improves expressivity  
  • The seed program’s four disjoint CZ layers + final CZ edge (versus Gen 41’s two matchings + hub) correlates with a +0.02 combined boost (0.62 vs. 0.60) while maintaining a low generalization gap (0.02).  
- Graph-aware parameter tying maintains generalization with deeper circuits  
  • Both the seed and Gen 52 “Graph-Structured Variational Ansatz” (depth 51, combined 0.62, gap 0.02) share RY parameters across automorphic vertices (e.g. “ry2_23,” “ry2_14”) to regularize 180 parameters without overfitting.  
- Consistent peak performance across implementations  
  • Three independent programs—seed, Gen 49, Gen 52—all achieve combined 0.62 using the same two-layer RY–RZ + multi-matching CZ pattern, demonstrating the robustness of this architecture.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Single post-entanglement rotation layers under-express correlations  
  • Gen 41 “Graph-Regularized Quantum Ansatz” (depth 39, 96 params → combined 0.60, gap 0.05) uses only one RY–RZ output and cannot match the 0.62 peak.  
- Parameterized two-qubit probes add depth without score gain  
  • Gen 53 “Peripheral CRX Probe” (depth 47, 144 params → combined 0.57, gap 0.08) shows that swapping CZ matchings for CRX/CRY/CRZ edges inflates resource use but lowers test accuracy.  
- Redundant rotation stacking wastes circuit budget  
  • Gen 50 “Duplicate RZ Controls” (depth 47, 138 params → combined 0.54, gap 0.11) reuses back-to-back RZ layers with no accuracy improvement, confirming that rotation depth alone is not beneficial.  
- Over-tying across broad symmetry groups reduces expressivity  
  • Gen 36-style heavy tying yields combined 0.56 and gap 0.08, indicating that beyond graph automorphisms, excessive parameter sharing harms performance.

## Implementation Insights  
- Layered ANSATZ_SPEC structure clarifies design phases  
  • Organize ANSATZ_SPEC as: 1) input RY with graph-tied param names (“ry_14,” “ry_23”), 2) per-qubit RZ pre-rotations (“rz_pre_i”), 3) explicit CZ matchings, 4) final CZ, 5) output RY/RZ with “ry2_…”/“rz2_…” naming.  
- Explicit matching lists ensure non-overlapping entanglement  
  • Use Python dicts for each CZ matching:  
    {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,2]}, {"gate":"CZ","wires":[1,3]}, …  
  to cover the graph in successive commuting layers.  
- Param naming encodes layer and graph class  
  • Tie output rotations by reusing names across equivalent wires (e.g. “ry2_56” on qubits 5 and 6), preserving symmetry regularization in the second block.  
- Leverage unchanged ALLOWED_* sets for gate legality  
  • Keeping ALLOWED_SINGLE_QUBIT_GATES and ALLOWED_TWO_QUBIT_GATES definitions stable allows ANSATZ_SPEC experimentation without backend conflicts.

## Performance Analysis  
- Depth vs. combined score trade-off  
  • Depth 39–41 designs (Gen 34, Gen 41/50) reach combined 0.59–0.60; depth 51–53 circuits (seed, Gen 49, Gen 52) consistently hit 0.62, marking a +0.02 gain for +12 depth.  
- Parameter count vs. expressivity  
  • Jumping from ~96 params to 180 params moves combined from 0.60 to 0.62, though parameter efficiency drops to zero, highlighting a capacity-driven improvement in test accuracy (0.62).  
- Gate count correlation with top scores  
  • All 0.62 programs use ~315 gates, versus 207–261 gates in lower-scoring ansätze, suggesting entanglement density supports higher accuracy.  
- Generalization remains stable despite scale-up  
  • Even with doubled depth and params, the generalization gap stays at 0.02 for the 0.62 designs, on par with shallower, more efficient circuits.

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a mid-circuit RX mixing layer between the second and third CZ matching blocks.  For example, right after  
   • `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[1,8]}` and `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[2,4]}`, add  
     – `{"gate":"RX","wire":0, "param":"rx_mid_0"}`  
     – `{"gate":"RX","wire":1, "param":"rx_mid_14"}`  
     – `{"gate":"RX","wire":2, "param":"rx_mid_23"}`, etc., tying by the same automorphism classes as the input RY.  
   Rationale: a mid-circuit rotation block breaks up the commuting entanglement layers, mirroring the benefit of dual RY–RZ blocks and boosting expressivity with minimal depth overhead.

2. Replace the two hub CZs on [0,7] and [0,8] with a single three-qubit CCRZ.  Delete  
   • `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,7]}` and `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,8]}`  
   and insert  
   • `{"gate":"CCRZ","wires":[0,7,8],"param":"ccrz_0_7_8"}`.  
   Rationale: consolidating hub entanglement into one controlled-controlled-RZ captures higher-order interactions around the central node in one gate, preserving depth while enriching multi-qubit correlations.

3. Add a targeted ZZZ interaction on a strongly-connected triangle.  After the final CZ (or replacing a less-critical CZ), insert  
   • `{"gate":"ZZZ","wires":[2,3,5]}`  
   with no extra param tying.  
   Rationale: three-body phase gates leverage ALLOWED_THREE_QUBIT_GATES to encode triangle subgraph correlations in a single operation, extending expressivity beyond pairwise matchings without inflating depth.

4. Convert the peripheral CZ on [5,7] into a trainable CRZ.  Replace  
   • `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[5,7]}`

   • `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[5,7],"param":"crz_5_7"}`.

5. Partially relax the output RY parameter tying on qubits 2 and 3.  Instead of sharing `"ry2_23"` on both wires, use two distinct entries:

   Rationale: slightly lifting the symmetry constraint where tying may be too restrictive lets the ansatz fine-tune local rotations on mid-chain nodes, combating any under-expressivity from over-regularization.
