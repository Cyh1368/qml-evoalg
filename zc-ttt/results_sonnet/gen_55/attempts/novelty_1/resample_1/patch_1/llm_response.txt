hybrid_threequbit_lowparam_ansatz

<DESCRIPTION>
Instead of stacking many independently-parameterized two-qubit CRZ gates (which drove the seed programs to 160+ trainable parameters and a persistent train/validation gap), this design separates "connectivity" from "expressivity". A full fixed CNOT backbone (one CNOT on every allowed physical pair, at zero parameter cost) first entangles the entire 9-qubit register into a single connected graph. On top of that fixed backbone, a *heavily shared* parametrized CRZ layer (only 4 unique angles, one per qubit-degree/hub group) reintroduces just enough tunable two-body correlation. Then, to capture genuinely higher-order structure that plain two-qubit rotations cannot represent, three explicit three-qubit interactions are added: two CCRZ gates (each shared across two triples) and one shared ZZZ gate spanning disjoint qubit triples, giving the ansatz access to 3-body correlations that are impossible to obtain from any stack of 2-qubit gates without much greater depth. Finally, independent single-qubit RY (encoding-refresh) and RZ (readout-phase) layers bookend the circuit for full local expressivity. The net result is a much leaner parameter budget (~25 versus ~160-190), which should shrink the generalization gap and improve parameter efficiency, while the fixed high-connectivity backbone plus explicit 3-qubit terms preserve — and arguably deepen (via 3-body terms) — the achievable expressive power relative to pure 2-qubit hub graphs.
