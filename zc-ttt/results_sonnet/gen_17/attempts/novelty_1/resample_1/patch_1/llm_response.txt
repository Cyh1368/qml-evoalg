<DESCRIPTION>
The current version turned three previously-fixed CZ gates (0,8), (1,8), (2,6) into fully independent CRZ gates, raising the parameter count from 150 to 180. This increased train accuracy (0.75) but widened the generalization gap (0.14) and lowered validation accuracy (0.65) compared to the 150-parameter seed (gap 0.09, val 0.66). This suggests the extra free parameters are overfitting rather than helping.

Instead of reverting these edges to non-parametrized CZ gates (losing expressivity entirely), we share their rotation parameter with an existing CRZ that shares the same hub qubit: (0,8) shares "crz_2" with (0,7), (1,8) shares "crz_3" with (1,3), and (2,6) shares "crz_4" with (2,4). This keeps the entangling gates parametrized (preserving some flexibility from the encoded data) while cutting the parameter count back to 150, matching the well-generalizing seed's budget. Sharing within a hub's outgoing edges is a natural symmetry prior: it encodes that a qubit's coupling strength to its neighbors is governed by one learned "hub activity" parameter, which should reduce overfitting and shrink the train/validation gap while retaining entangling coverage that the previous experiment lacked.
</DESCRIPTION>
