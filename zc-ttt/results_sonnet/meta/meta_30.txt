# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- RY→CRZ→RZ “sandwich” with pure CRZ entanglers  
  • Current Best (Seed ANSATZ_SPEC): RY on all 9 qubits → 11 CRZs on 12 high-centrality edges (with shared parameters) → RZ on all qubits. Combined score 0.63, depth 41, gates 207, gap 0.09.  
  • Gen 17 (9-Q RY-CRZ-RZ Ansatz): Same structure, shared parameters on symmetric edges, combined 0.63.  
- Targeted two-qubit CRZ on high-centrality edges  
  • Both the Seed and Gen 17 select 12 informative edges (e.g. 0–2, 0–3, 2–4, …, 6–7), avoiding full connectivity. Yields top test accuracies (seed test 0.65).  
- Strategic parameter sharing  
  • Sharing crz_2 over (0–7, 0–8) and crz_3 over (1–3, 1–8) reduces parameters from 180→162 without harming expressivity. Boosts combined from ~0.60 → 0.63.  
- Sweet-spot depth and gate count  
  • Depth ≈ 41 and gate count ≈ 207 consistently deliver ≥ 0.60 combined. Seed exactly hits 41/207 for best performance.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mixed two-qubit gates (CRZ + CRY)  
  • 9-Qubit RY-CRZ/CRY Layered Ansatz (Gen 23): combined 0.59, gap 0.11. Extra CRY gates improved expressivity marginally but inflated gate count and overfit.  
- Excess gate budget or deeper circuits  
  • Hub-Centric Variational Ansatz (Gen 24): 225 gates, depth 41 → combined 0.58, gap 0.13.  
  • 9-Qubit RY–CRZ–RZ Param. Circuit (Gen 27): 237 gates, depth 45 → combined 0.58, gap 0.13.  
- Three-qubit interactions  
  • Prior CCRZ/ZZZ hybrids (Gen 25/26) scored ≤ 0.58 with depth ≥ 51. Overhead outweighed expressivity gains.  
- Overparameterization without pruning  
  • Any design > 180 parameters but without targeted sharing (e.g. Gen 27’s 192 params) yields zero parameter efficiency and plateaus at ≤ 0.58 combined.

## Implementation Insights  
- Strict gate ordering stabilizes training  
  • Placing all RY rotations before any CRZ, then all RZ rotations (as in Seed and Gen 17) yields zero train/val/test std 0.00.  
- Unique, explicit parameter naming  
  • ‘ry_0…ry_8’, ‘crz_0…crz_11’, ‘rz_post_0…rz_post_8’ avoids optimizer conflicts and ensures clear gradient assignments.  
- Correct ‘wires’ key usage  
  • Seed’s use of `"wires"` for multi-qubit gates (vs invalid `"wire"` in Gen 29) prevents parsing failures.  
- Minimalist annotation blocks  
  • Defining only the ansatz inside EVOLVE-BLOCK keeps the interface clean; reuses fixed backend and measurement code for reproducibility.

## Performance Analysis  
- Best vs. runner-up  
  • Seed (0.63 combined, gap 0.09) outperforms the next best, 9-Qubit CRZ Rotation Ansatz (Gen 28) with 0.61 combined, same gap but depth 39. Two extra gates/depth in seed yield +0.02.  
- Depth/gate count correlation  
  • Depth < 39 (Gen 28) dips to 0.61; > 45 (Gen 27) drops to 0.58. Optimal window is 41 ± 2 and ~207 gates.  
- Parameter count vs. generalization  
  • Reducing from 180→162 params cut the generalization gap from ~0.12 to 0.09 without loss in train accuracy.  
- Parameter efficiency remains constant  
  • Top performers (Seed, Gen 17) all report 0.00 parameter efficiency—indicating model size was not penalized but accuracy gains derive from structure, not raw param count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend symmetric CRZ sharing on leaf–leaf edges  
   Merge the CRZ on [4, 8] (`crz_9`) into the one on [3, 5] (`crz_5`), and merge [6, 7] (`crz_11`) into [5, 7] (`crz_10`). This reduces n_params from 162→160 while holding depth at 41 and gate_count at 207. Given that sharing on ([0,7],[0,8]) and ([1,3],[1,8]) preserved the 0.63 combined score, testing further symmetry-driven pruning on leaf edges may maintain expressivity and tighten the generalization gap.  

2. Insert a lightweight mid‐layer of RY rotations  
   Between the CRZ block and the final RZ layer, add four single‐qubit RY gates on qubits [1, 3, 5, 7] with new parameters `ry_mid_0…ry_mid_3`. This “RY→CRZ→RY→RZ” sandwich adds only 4 gates (gate_count≈211) and ≈2 depth units (depth≈43), probing whether a targeted amplitude refresh on high‐centrality qubits stabilizes convergence without triggering overfitting.  

3. Parallelize CRZ entanglers into two commuting sublayers  
   Reorder the twelve CRZ operations into Sublayer A: [0,2],[1,3],[2,4],[3,5],[5,7] and Sublayer B: [0,3],[0,7],[1,8],[2,6],[4,8],[6,7], executing all gates in A before B. This scheduling exploits maximum commutativity to shave ~2 depth units (target depth≈39) while keeping gate_count=207 and n_params=162. Testing a slightly shallower circuit may reveal whether the original performance can be preserved or improved at lower depth.  

4. Add a second CRZ entanglement layer with shared parameters  
   Immediately after the existing CRZ block and before the final RZ layer, repeat the same twelve CRZ gates on the identical wire pairs, reusing the original `crz_0…crz_11` parameters. This “double‐entanglement” increases gate_count to ~219 and depth to ~43 but introduces zero new parameters. It probes whether deeper two‐qubit correlations, enforced without increasing the parameter budget, boost expressivity beyond the single‐layer ansatz.  

5. Test extreme hub‐edge CRZ parameter collapse  
   Collapse all four CRZ gates incident on qubit 0—edges [0,2],[0,3],[0,7],[0,8]—into one shared parameter `crz_hub0`. Replace the four separate entries with a single repeated-param gate across those wire pairs, reducing n_params from 162→159 while preserving depth 41 and gate_count 207. This stronger symmetry assumption examines whether further hub‐centric pruning enhances generalization without sacrificing accuracy.
