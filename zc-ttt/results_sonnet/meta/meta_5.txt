# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Integration of parameterized two-qubit gates (CRZ) alongside static entanglers boosted expressivity  
  • “9-Qubit RY-CRZ-RZ Ansatz” (Gen 6) scored 0.59 with six CRZ+ six CZ gates (207 gates, depth 41) vs. 0.55 for “9-Qubit RY-RZ-ZZZ Entangling Ansatz” (Gen 3) with only fixed CZ and three ZZZ (279 gates, depth 64).  
- Reduced circuit depth and parameter count while preserving entanglement connectivity improved overall accuracy  
  • Gen 6 uses 144 parameters/depth 41 → 0.61 test accuracy; Gen 3 used 180 parameters/depth 64 → 0.54 test accuracy.  
- Evenly distributed single-qubit rotations flanking the entangling block stabilized training  
  • The champion places one RY per qubit then entanglers then one RZ per qubit, yielding zero training/std variation and smooth convergence.  
- Selective use of static CZ for sparsely connected pairs complemented by trainable CRZ on critical links  
  • Focusing CRZ on high-impact edges (e.g. wires [0,2], [0,3], [1,3], [2,4], [3,5], [0,7]) preserved expressivity without over-parameterization.  

## Ineffective Approaches
- Over-parameterization with large rotation layers and deep circuits gave diminishing returns  
  • “Three-layer rotation ansatz with CZ entanglers” (Gen 5): 216 parameters/depth 53 → combined score 0.54, zero parameter efficiency.  
  • “9-Qubit RY-RZ-ZZZ Entangling Ansatz” (Gen 3): 180 parameters/depth 64 → combined score 0.55.  
- Heavy reliance on fixed entanglement graphs limited adaptability  
  • “CZ-Entangled 9-Qubit Variational Ansatz” (Gen 0): depth 47/static CZ only → combined score 0.52, no convergence trend.  
- Parameter reuse across multiple two-qubit gates collapsed expressivity and caused failures  
  • “9-Qubit Layered Rotation Ansatz” (Gen 4) reused RZ params on CRZ gates → validation tests failed (score 0.0).  
- Inserting three-qubit ZZZ/CCRZ gates without streamlined connectivity added depth but yielded only marginal gains  
  • Gen 3 added three ZZZ gates (total 279 gates) yet only improved combined score from 0.52 to 0.55.  

## Implementation Insights
- Explicit per-gate parameter naming prevents unintended sharing and enables independent optimization  
  • In the Gen 6 spec each CRZ has its own “crz_i” param vs. Gen 4’s reused “rz” parameters, leading to failed validation.  
- Sandwiching entangling blocks between distinct single-qubit rotation layers enhances expressivity per parameter  
  • The RY→CRZ/CZ→RZ pattern in the current best program yields stable training (train std 0.00) and higher test accuracy.  
- Prioritizing parameterized two-qubit gates on high-centrality qubit pairs improves performance more than adding multi-qubit gates  
  • Champion uses CRZ on six strategically chosen pairs rather than broad three-qubit ZZZ coverage.  
- Balanced mix of static and trainable gates can achieve lower depth while maintaining connectivity  
  • Static CZ on less critical edges (e.g. [0,8], [1,8], [2,6], [4,8], [5,7], [6,7]) complements CRZ without inflating parameter count.  

## Performance Analysis
- Peak combined score correlates with mid-range parameter counts (≈144) and moderate depth (≈40–45)  
  • Best (Gen 6): 0.59 at 144 params/depth 41; contrast Gen 5: 0.54 at 216 params/depth 53, Gen 3: 0.55 at 180 params/depth 64.  
- Generalization gap widens slightly with added trainable entanglement but remains acceptable  
  • Gen 6 gap 0.09 vs. Gen 3 gap 0.05; higher expressivity traded for a small increase in overfitting.  
- Zero parameter efficiency across all evaluated ansätze highlights consistent over-parameterization  
  • Despite varying n_params (144–216), no program achieved nonzero parameter efficiency, indicating pervasive redundancy.  
- Static-only entanglement patterns yield lower test accuracies (<0.50) compared to hybrid CRZ/CZ designs (>0.60)  
  • “CZ-Entangled” (Gen 0) test accuracy 0.49 vs. Gen 6 test accuracy 0.61.
