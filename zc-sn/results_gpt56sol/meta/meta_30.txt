# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

• Dual-phase “sandwich” around a linear CZ chain  
  – Parity-Sharing CZ-RZ Sandwich Ansatz (Gen 24) and the Current Best both wrap a nearest-neighbor CZ chain (7 CZs) with pre- and post-RZ layers.  
  – Gen 24 hits a combined 0.87 with only a 0.02 generalization gap and converges by ∼150 steps; the Current Best also scores 0.87 (gap 0.05).  
  – Sandwiching RZ before & after entanglement maximizes expressivity while controlling overfitting.  

• Per-qubit RY front end  
  – All top scorers (Current Best, Gen 24) begin with eight independent RY rotations.  
  – Ensures rich input encoding: train accuracy ≥ 0.94 for the Current Best and Gen 24.  

• Parity-shared pre-entanglement RZ  
  – Both the Current Best and Gen 24 collapse eight pre-CZ RZ parameters into two (“rz_pre_even”/“rz_pre_odd”).  
  – Maintains high validation/test (0.89/0.89 for the seed) and cuts parameter count.  

• Nearest-neighbor linear CZ connectivity  
  – The 7-CZ chain in the Current Best and Gen 24 outperforms ring (Gen 27, 0.78) and long-range chorded schemes (Gen 28, 0.77).  
  – Simpler topology maps more efficiently to hardware and avoids redundant entangling gates.  

## Ineffective Approaches

• Ring or non-linear entanglers  
  – CZ-Ring Eight-Qubit Ansatz (Gen 27) drops to a combined 0.78 despite 98 parameters and lower depth (81).  
  – Long-range chords in Gen 28 further reduce score to 0.77 (gap 0.09).  

• Over-deep, gate-heavy circuits without structural phase sharing  
  – Gen 28 (depth 117, 294 gates) and Gen 24 (depth 153, 510 gates) both have low parameter efficiency (0.01).  
  – Extra gates beyond the 270-gate sweet spot do not improve accuracy and slow convergence.  

• Under-parameterization of post-entanglement phases  
  – 8-Qubit Layered RY-RZ CZ Ansatz (Gen 31) uses only 74 params but achieves 0.81 combined—missing expressivity.  

• Faulty/shared-param blocks  
  – Layered Shared-Param RY-RZ-CZ Ansatz (Gen 32) fails entirely due to syntax errors and over-sharing of parameters, illustrating that broken implementation supersedes any theoretical benefit.  

## Implementation Insights

• Block-structured ANSATZ_SPEC  
  – Defining clear “RY front → RZ pre → CZ chain → RZ post” sections (as in the Current Best) aids readability, debugging, and potential compiler optimizations.  

• Consistent, descriptive parameter naming  
  – Prefixes like `ry_0…ry_7`, `rz_pre_even/odd`, `rz_post_0…rz_post_7` simplify parameter binding and reduce mapping errors (seen in the seed and Gen 24).  

• Exclusive use of allowed gates  
  – The Current Best employs only RY, RZ, and CZ (no CNOT or CRX/CRY/CRZ), ensuring hardware-compatible depth and gate fidelity.  

• Decoupled experiment runner  
  – Encapsulating the ansatz in `ANSATZ_SPEC` and invoking `_run(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` isolates circuit design from training code, accelerating iteration.  

## Performance Analysis

• Plateau at combined 0.87 with sandwich-style circuits  
  – Both Gen 24 and the Current Best reach 0.87; no evaluated ansatz exceeds this.  

• Fast vs. stable convergence  
  – Gen 24 converges by ~150 steps with 122 parameters, whereas the Current Best converges more slowly (~450 steps) but with fewer gates (270 vs. 510) and more compact depth (99 vs. 153).  

• Generalization gap trends  
  – Dual-phase sandwich designs hold gap ≈ 0.02–0.05 (Gen 24: 0.02; seed: 0.05), whereas single-phase or overly dense layouts exhibit larger gaps (Gen 27: 0.06; Gen 28: 0.09).  

• Gate-count sweet spot around 270  
  – Circuits with ~270 gates (Current Best, Gen 31) hit combined scores ≥ 0.81; adding gates beyond this yields diminishing returns (Gen 24’s 510 gates vs. same 0.87 score).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the eight per-qubit post-RZ gates with two parity-shared parameters.  In your ANSATZ_SPEC, change `rz_post_0…rz_post_7` to `rz_post_even` on qubits 0,2,4,6 and `rz_post_odd` on qubits 1,3,5,7.  This mirrors the successful pre-RZ sharing, cuts your total parameters from 110 to 94, and should tighten the generalization gap without hurting expressivity.  

2. Stack a second RZ→CZ→RZ sandwich after the existing post-RZ layer.  Immediately after your new parity-shared post-RZ, insert `RZ(rz2_pre_even/odd) → CZ` on the same linear chain (0–1,…,6–7) → `RZ(rz2_post_even/odd)`.  Dual-phase circuits like Gen 24 converge in ∼150 steps and hit the 0.87 score more rapidly than single-sandwich designs.  

3. Inject a lightweight mid-circuit mixing layer using parity-shared RY’s.  Right after the first CZ chain and before the post-RZ block, apply `RY` on all qubits with two parameters: `ry_mix_even` on 0,2,4,6 and `ry_mix_odd` on 1,3,5,7.  This adds only ~8 gates and 2 parameters, boosts non-linearity, and leverages the proven strength of per-qubit RY encoding.  

4. Augment each CZ bond with a trainable CRZ gate, sharing parameters across even and odd edges.  For edges (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7) use `crz_even`; for (1–2,3–4,5–6,7–Ø) use `crz_odd`.  Since CRZ commutes with CZ, you can interleave it without increasing depth, fine-tuning entanglement phases with only +2 parameters.  

5. Add a sparse long-range “chord” entanglement block before your post-RZ layer.  Insert CZ gates on pairs (0–4,1–5,2–6,3–7), each wrapped by parity-shared `rz_chord_pre_even/odd` and `rz_chord_post_even/odd`.  This 4-CZ, 4-RZ module captures global correlations at minimal depth cost, borrowing the benefits of chorded designs while avoiding full-ring gate bloat.
