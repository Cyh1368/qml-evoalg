# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Sandwich-style phase layering around a linear CZ chain  
  • Current Best Program (combined 0.87; 0.94/0.89/0.89; gap 0.05; depth 99; 270 gates; conv ~450 steps) and Gen 14 both use:  
    1) Per-qubit RY front-end  
    2) Parity-shared pre-RZ (2 params)  
    3) Nearest-neighbor CZ chain (7 CZs)  
    4) Per-qubit post-RZ  
  • Yields top combined score (0.87), stable convergence (≈450 steps), small generalization gap (0.05).  

- Dedicated per-qubit RY front-end  
  • Present in all top scorers (Current Best, Gen 14, Gen 19), train ≥ 0.93.  
  • Provides strong expressivity and accelerates early optimization.  

- Parity-shared pre-entanglement RZ  
  • Reduces pre-RZ from 8→2 parameters (“rz_pre_even/odd” in Current Best, Gen 19)  
  • Maintains validation/test accuracy (0.89) while cutting parameter count.  

- Linear nearest-neighbor CZ connectivity  
  • Chain of 7 CZs (no ring closure) in Current Best and Gen 19 matches best accuracy.  
  • Simpler graph → better hardware mapping and fewer gates than rings or cross-links.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Excessive circuit depth and gate density  
  • Gen 19 (depth 166, 516 gates) ties top score (0.87) but halves parameter efficiency (0.01) vs. Current Best.  
  • Gen 23 (depth 132, 140 params) achieves only 0.84, showing deeper ≠ better.  

- Parameter-shared CRY correlation layers  
  • Gen 23’s CRY-based mixing (140 params, open-chain CZ) yields 0.84 and gap 0.09—worse than CZ chain + RY/RZ sandwich.  

- Ring or wraparound entanglers  
  • Gen 25’s brickwall ring + wraparound CZ (372 gates) scores 0.86 vs. 0.87 for linear chain; more gates without net gain.  

- Reduced post-entanglement phase sharing alone  
  • Gen 21 (shared pre-RZ + symmetric post-RZ, 86 params) scores 0.81, gap 0.05—lacks dual-phase expressivity.  

## Implementation Insights

- Block-structured ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • Define four clear sections—{RY front, parity-shared RZ pre, CZ chain, per-qubit RZ post}—for readability and compiler optimizations.  

- Parity-indexed parameter naming  
  • Map wires {0,2,4,6}→“rz_pre_even” & {1,3,5,7}→“rz_pre_odd” to cut 75% of pre-RZ params with no loss in accuracy.  

- Consistent parameter prefixes  
  • ry_i, rz_pre_*, rz_post_j → simplifies automated binding and reduces mapping errors.  

- Decoupled ansatz definition vs. execution  
  • `run_experiment(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` isolates circuit design, enabling rapid gate-pattern iteration without touching training code.  

## Performance Analysis

- Plateau at combined 0.87 with sandwich-style circuits  
  • Current Best and Gen 14 both hit 0.87 at depth 99–110 and 270–300 gates, converging ~450 steps.  

- Gate-count vs. accuracy trade-off  
  • Increasing gates from 270→372 (Gen 25) only recovers 0.86; 270 gates appears sweet-spot.  

- Parameter count marginally impacts efficiency  
  • Range 86–140 params yields efficiency ~0.01; score improvements driven by gate pattern and phase sharing, not raw param count.  

- Generalization gap correlates with phase sandwiching  
  • Dual-phase (pre+post-RZ) sandwich circuits hold gap ~0.05; single-phase or over-dense designs show gap ≥ 0.06.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the eight per-qubit post-RZ gates with two parity-shared parameters (“rz_post_even” on qubits 0,2,4,6 and “rz_post_odd” on qubits 1,3,5,7).  This mirrors the parity-sharing in the pre-RZ block, cuts your post-phase parameters from 8→2, and should preserve expressivity while tightening the generalization gap.  

2. Add a second RZ→CZ→RZ “sandwich” after the existing post-RZ layer: insert parity-shared “rz2_pre_even”/“rz2_pre_odd,” repeat the same nearest-neighbor CZ chain (0–1,1–2,…,6–7), then add parity-shared “rz2_post_even”/“rz2_post_odd.”  Top variants like Gen 14 saw richer expressivity and similar gate counts when stacking two sandwiches, driving faster convergence without sacrificing the 0.87 score.  

3. Insert a lightweight mid-circuit mixing layer immediately after the first CZ chain and before post-RZ: apply RY on each qubit using two parameters (“ry_mix_even” on 0,2,4,6 and “ry_mix_odd” on 1,3,5,7).  All top performers use per-qubit RY front-ends; a second parity-shared RY layer injects extra nonlinearity with just 2 more parameters and minimal depth cost (~8 gates).  

4. Augment each CZ bond with a trainable controlled-Z rotation (CRZ), sharing parameters across even and odd bonds (“crz_even” on edges 0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7; “crz_odd” on 1–2,3–4,5–6,7–Ø).  CRZ commutes with CZ so it fits seamlessly into the entangler block, adds only two parameters, and can fine-tune entanglement phases to improve expressivity without blowing up depth.  

5. Append a sparse long-range entanglement block before the post-RZ layer: add CZ gates on qubit pairs (0–4,1–5,2–6,3–7) wrapped by parity-shared pre/post RZ phases (“rz_chord_pre_even/odd” and “rz_chord_post_even/odd”).  This chord-style augmentation captures global correlations at minimal depth overhead (~4 CZs + 4 RZs), borrowing insights from chord-augmented designs but avoiding the full ring’s gate bloat.
