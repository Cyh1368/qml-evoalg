# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parity-shared RY input layer + alternating CZ/CRZ entanglers (Gen 68 “8-Qubit Layered Quantum Circuit”): achieved the top combined score (0.90) and converged in only 120 steps, demonstrating that an initial RY_even/odd layer tightly integrated with ring + next-neighbor entanglement is highly expressive.  
- Even/odd parameter sharing across all RY and CRZ layers (62 params in Gen 68 vs. 122–146 params in Gen 67/70/72) preserves accuracy (0.90) and keeps the generalization gap low (0.04) while halving the total parameter count.  
- Two-block entangler structure (one alternating CZ+CRZ ring, one pure-CZ next-neighbor matching) at depth 202/gate 510 outperforms deeper three-block designs (depth ≥ 214, gates 606) in both final score and convergence speed.  
- Single parity-shared RY mixer after each entangler block yields sufficient non-commuting rotations, replacing more complex multi-axis mixers without sacrificing performance.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Deep three-block phase-sandwich architectures (Gen 70 “Ring-Structured Variational Ansatz” and Gen 72 “Parity-Shared Phase Sandwich Ansatz”) stalled at combined scores ≲ 0.79 despite high parameter counts (146) and large gate counts (606), indicating diminishing returns from additional blocks.  
- Pure-CZ chain entanglers without CRZ modulation (Gen 71 “Layered 8-Qubit CZ-Rotation Ansatz”) peaked at 0.86 combined score and exhibited a larger generalization gap (0.09), showing that homogenous two-qubit gates lack the tunable phase control provided by interleaved CRZs.  
- Overly deep circuits (depth > 214) with mixed CZ/CRZ in every link (Gen 67) improved on pure-CZ only marginally (0.88 vs. 0.86) at the cost of slower convergence (330 vs. 120 steps) and higher resource use.  
- Excessive parameter reuse across non-parity axes (global sharing) was not directly evaluated here but previously shown (Gen 60) to collapse expressivity—parity-level sharing is the effective granularity.

## Implementation Insights  
- Flat EVOLVE-BLOCK “gate” dict lists with explicit parity-naming (e.g. “ry_in_even” vs. “ry_in_odd”) let the backend fuse identical operations and minimize memory footprint, as seen in Gen 68’s sub-1,000-line spec.  
- Reusing a single RZ_pre_even/odd parameter for all pre-entangling rotations across two blocks streamlines the ansatz (62 params total) and yields compiler-friendly symmetry, without observable loss in expressivity.  
- Alternating CZ and parameterized CRZ on the same wires (e.g. [0,1] CZ→CRZ_even→[1,2] CZ→CRZ_odd…) injects tunable phases while keeping to hardware-native gates, boosting accuracy over pure-CZ chains.  
- Parity-aware single-qubit mixers (RY_even/odd) placed immediately after each entanglement layer serve as efficient stand-ins for more complex dual-axis mixers, simplifying the circuit and halving gate depth compared to three-block dual-axis designs.

## Performance Analysis  
- Halving parameters from 122 (Gen 67) to 62 (Gen 68) and reducing gates from 606 to 510 raised the combined score from 0.88 to 0.90, showing a strong correlation between leaner ansätze and higher accuracy when expressivity is well-targeted.  
- Convergence steps dropped from 330 (Gen 67) to 120 (Gen 68) with the two-block design, indicating that reducing entangler layers can accelerate optimization without affecting final performance.  
- All high-performance, parity-shared architectures maintained a generalization gap of ~0.04–0.05, suggesting that parity sharing robustly controls overfitting across depths of 200–214.  
- Circuits clustered around depth 200/gate 500 mark the sweet spot: deeper (> 214) networks underperform or plateau, while shallower (< 165) ones (e.g. Gen 70/72) achieve only ~0.79 combined.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parallelize both entangler blocks into even-/odd-bond sublayers  
   – For each CZ+CRZ sequence, split into Sub-layer A on even bonds [(0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7)] and Sub-layer B on odd bonds [(1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,0)], keeping the same alternating CZ→CRZ pattern.  
   – This reordering preserves full connectivity while allowing four two-qubit gates to execute in parallel, reducing effective circuit depth and leveraging hardware-native fusion just as the ring+next-neighbor design did in Gen 68.

2. Decouple the second block’s pre-entangling RZ rotations
