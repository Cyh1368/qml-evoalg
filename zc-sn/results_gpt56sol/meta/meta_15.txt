# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Sandwich-style phase layering around a linear CZ chain  
   • Current best (Gen 14 “Layered RY-RZ-CZ Variational Circuit”, score 0.87) and its predecessor (Gen 6, score 0.87) both use parity-shared pre-RZ → CZ chain → per-qubit post-RZ. This “sandwich” yields top accuracy (train/val/test ≈ 0.94/0.89/0.89), small gap (0.05), and fast convergence (~450 steps).  
- Dedicated per-qubit RY front-end  
   • All high scorers (Gen 10,11,12,14) begin with individual RY wrappings, boosting training accuracy (≥ 0.94) and stabilizing optimization.  
- Parity-based parameter sharing for Z-rotations  
   • Reducing pre-entanglement RZ from 8→2 shared parameters (even/odd) in Gen 14 preserves expressivity while cutting circuit width, enabling a lean depth (99) and gate count (270).  
- Linear nearest-neighbor CZ connectivity  
   • Linear chains (Gen 14, Gen 10–12) hit or exceed ring/topology‐mixed scores (0.86–0.87) with fewer gates and simpler hardware mapping.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Overly complex two-qubit mixers  
   • Gen 11’s CRY-based pairwise layers (score 0.86, conv ~1,320 steps) under-performed the simpler CZ chain, showing little accuracy gain for added depth (108) and gates (294).  
- Dense entangling graphs  
   • Gen 12’s dual CZ layers (ring + next-nearest, depth 154, gates 420) matched only 0.86 and required similar convergence (~450 steps) at 50% greater depth/gate count.  
- Single-gate families with no RY component  
   • Gen 18’s RZ-only + CZ open-chain ansatz failed entirely (score 0.0), evidencing that eliminating the RY initializer and dual‐phase layering collapses expressivity.  
- All-unique RZ parameterization  
   • Circuits with 8-unique pre- or post-RZ (no sharing) saw no score improvements over shared schemes but incurred deeper circuits (e.g., Gen 10 with 122 params, score 0.86).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Contiguous pipeline structuring in ANSATZ_SPEC  
   • “[all RY] → [all rz_pre] → [all CZ] → [all rz_post]” (as in Gen 14 code) streamlines both human understanding and potential compiler optimizations.  
- Parity-indexed parameter naming  
   • Mapping wires 0,2,4,6→“rz_pre_even” and 1,3,5,7→“rz_pre_odd” slashes parameter count while retaining phase control.  
- Clear, consistent param prefixes  
   • Using “ry_i”, “rz_pre_*”, “rz_post_j” aides automated tooling and reduces mapping errors (crucial for passing validation tests).  
- Decoupled ansatz definition and execution  
   • Keeping `_run(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` fixed isolates circuit design from training logic, accelerating iteration on gate patterns.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score uplift via parameter-shared sandwich vs. ring or CRY mixers  
   • Switching from Gen 10/11/12’s more parameters (98–122) and mixed topologies to Gen 14’s 110-param sandwich boosted combined score from 0.86→0.87.  
- Depth vs. convergence speed  
   • Gen 14’s depth 99 converges in ~450 steps, compared to ~1,320 steps for Gen 10/11 at depths 108–122.  
- Generalization gap tightening under sandwich designs  
   • Gen 14 gap 0.05 vs. gaps 0.07–0.08 in unshared or single-phase circuits, underscoring the regularizing effect of dual-phase layering.  
- Gate-count efficiency  
   • At 270 gates, Gen 14 matches or outperforms heavier circuits (up to 420 gates) in both accuracy and convergence, proving that thoughtful sharing and linear entanglement trump brute entangler density.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the eight unique post-entanglement RZ gates with two parity-shared parameters “rz_post_even” (wires 0,2,4,6) and “rz_post_odd” (wires 1,3,5,7). This cuts the post-phase parameter count from 8 to 2—mirroring the successful pre-RZ sharing in Gen 14—and should tighten generalization gaps without reducing expressivity.  

2. Append a second “RZ→CZ chain→RZ” sandwich after the existing post-RZ block: add a layer of parity-shared pre-phase RZ (“rz2_pre_even”/“rz2_pre_odd”), repeat the same linear CZ connections (0–1,1–2,…,6–7), then another parity-shared post-phase RZ (“rz2_post_even”/“rz2_post_odd”). Dual sandwich layering has consistently driven richer expressivity and faster convergence in top‐scoring circuits.  

3. Introduce a light mid-circuit RY mixing layer immediately after the CZ chain and before the post-RZ gates: apply per-qubit RY rotations using either a single “ry_mix” parameter or two parity-shared “ry_mix_even”/“ry_mix_odd” parameters. This small nonlinear injection builds on the powerful initialization boost from per-qubit RY front-ends in Gens 10–14 and can help the optimizer navigate complex loss landscapes.  

4. Decompose the linear CZ chain into two interleaved sublayers—first on even bonds (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7), then on odd bonds (1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0)—and wrap each sublayer with its own parity-shared pre- and post-RZ pair (“rz_pre1_even/odd” & “rz_post1_even/odd”, then “rz_pre2_even/odd” & “rz_post2_even/odd”). This brick-wall entanglement pattern enriches connectivity like ring/chord mixers while preserving the sandwich structure and parameter efficiency.  

5. Insert four long-range CZ gates on pairs (0,4), (1,5), (2,6), (3,7) between the primary linear CZ block and the first post-RZ layer. These targeted global entanglers capture distant correlations at minimal depth overhead—leveraging insights from chord-enhanced designs—without resorting to fully dense graphs that previously inflated depth and convergence time.
