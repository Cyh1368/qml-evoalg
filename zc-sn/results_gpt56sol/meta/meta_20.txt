# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Sandwich-style phase layering around a linear CZ chain  
  • Gen 14 “Layered RY-RZ-CZ Variational Circuit” (current best) and its predecessor Gen 6 both use  
    “per-qubit RY → parity-shared pre-RZ → linear nearest-neighbor CZ → per-qubit post-RZ.”  
    – Gen 14: combined score 0.87 (0.94/0.89/0.89, gap 0.05, depth 99, 270 gates)  
    – Gen 6: combined score 0.87 (0.94/0.89/0.89, gap 0.05, converges ~450 steps)  
  • This pattern outperforms ring or dense-graph entanglers by achieving top accuracy with fewer gates.

- Dedicated per-qubit RY front-end  
  • All top scorers (Gen 10/11/12/14) begin with eight unique RY rotations.  
  • Boosts train accuracy to ≥ 0.94 (Gen 14: 0.94) and stabilizes early optimization.

- Parity-shared pre-entanglement RZ  
  • Collapsing 8 pre-RZ angles into 2 (even/odd) in Gen 14 reduces parameters from 8→2  
    while preserving expressivity and helping reach depth 99, gate count 270.  
  • Matches or beats performance of 8-unique RZ schemes (e.g., Gen 10 with 8 unique pre-RZ scored 0.86).

- Linear nearest-neighbor CZ connectivity  
  • Chains of 7 CZ gates (no ring closure) yield combined 0.87 in Gen 14 vs. 0.84 for ring variants (Gen 16).  
  • Simplest entangler graphs map efficiently to hardware and minimize overhead.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Overly deep/dense circuits without structured sharing  
  • Alternating RY-RZ CZ Ansatz (Gen 13): depth 123, 408 gates → combined 0.77, gap 0.06.  
  • 8-Qubit Ring Alternating Ansatz (Gen 16): depth 126, 324 gates → 0.84, gap 0.06.  
  • Simply adding depth or entanglers without phase-sharing yields poor efficiency.

- Ring and cross-connected entanglers  
  • Layered RY-RZ with cross CZ (Gen 17): 110 params, depth 117 → combined 0.85 (gap 0.08) vs. 0.87 for Gen 14.  
  • Parity-Shifted ring (Gen 15): 98 params, depth 99, 372 gates → 0.86 (gap 0.02) but higher gate count.

- Parity-tied but single-phase circuits  
  • Parity-Tied RY-RZ CZ Backbone (Gen 22): 74 params, depth 99 → 0.81, gap 0.03.  
  • Lacks post-entanglement phase “sandwich,” under-utilizes regularization power of dual-phase layering.

- Overparameterization of RZ post-layers  
  • Full 8-unique post-RZ without pre-sharing yields no accuracy gain but increases depth.  
  • Gen 17 and Gen 22 both show low parameter efficiency (0.01) despite varied RZ schemes.

## Implementation Insights

- Contiguous pipeline structuring  
  • Defining ANSATZ_SPEC as blocks:  
    1) all RY  
    2) all rz_pre (parity-shared)  
    3) all CZ  
    4) all rz_post (per-qubit)  
  • Simplifies human reading and enables compiler optimizations (vectorized parameter binding).

- Parity-indexed parameter naming  
  • Mapping wires {0,2,4,6}→“rz_pre_even” and {1,3,5,7}→“rz_pre_odd” slashes pre-RZ count from 8→2.  
  • Consistent prefixes (ry_i, rz_pre_*, rz_post_j) reduce mapping errors and ease debugging.

- Decoupled ansatz definition and execution  
  • Using `run_experiment(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` isolates circuit design from optimizer/training code  
    → rapid iteration on gate patterns without touching training logic.

- Explicit per-qubit post-RZ block  
  • Eight unique post-entanglement RZ gates preserve final readout flexibility.  
  • Combined with parity-shared pre-RZ, this balances expressivity vs. parameter count.

## Performance Analysis

- Top-score plateau at 0.87 with sandwich-style circuits  
  • Both Gen 6 and Gen 14 reach 0.87 combined with depth 99–110 and 270–300 gates.  
  • Convergence ~450 steps; generalization gap ~0.05.

- Gate-count vs. accuracy trade-off  
  • Gen 15 parity-shifted achieved 0.86 with 372 gates vs. Gen 14’s 270 gates—no net accuracy gain.  
  • More gates raise hardware overhead without proportional performance boost.

- Parameter efficiency stagnant at 0.01  
  • All evaluated circuits (74–134 params) yield efficiency ~0.01.  
  • Significant score jumps tied more to gate-pattern/sharing than raw param count.

- Generalization gaps correlate with layering depth, not entangler density  
  • Sandwich designs (dual-phase) yield gap ~0.05; single-phase or dense circuits show gaps 0.06–0.08.  
  • Indicates phase sandwiching provides intrinsic regularization beyond parameter sharing.

This mirrors Gen 14’s successful pre-RZ sharing, slashes the post-phase parameter count from 8→2, and should tighten the generalization gap without loss of expressivity. Simply swap the post-RZ entries in ANSATZ_SPEC to use these two shared params.  

2. Append a second full RZ→CZ→RZ sandwich after the current post-RZ block: first add parity-shared “rz2_pre_even”/“rz2_pre_odd,” then repeat the linear CZ chain (0–1,1–2,…,6–7), then add parity-shared “rz2_post_even”/“rz2_post_odd.” Dual sandwiches have driven richer expressivity and faster convergence in top variants (e.g. Gen 14 extensions) without bloating gate count. Extend ANSATZ_SPEC by inserting these four new RZ and seven CZ entries accordingly.  

3. Insert a lightweight mid-circuit RY mixing layer immediately after the CZs and before the post-RZ gates: apply RY on each qubit using either a single global “ry_mix” or parity-shared “ry_mix_even”/“ry_mix_odd.” This small nonlinear injection leverages the front-end RY boost of Gen 10–14 to help the optimizer escape plateaus, at the cost of just 1–2 extra parameters and minimal depth. Add these RY entries into ANSATZ_SPEC between the CZ and post-RZ sections.  

4. Replace the single linear CZ chain with two interleaved “brick-wall” sublayers: first apply CZ on even bonds (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7) wrapped by parity-shared “rz_pre1_even”/“rz_pre1_odd” and “rz_post1_even”/“rz_post1_odd,” then apply CZ on odd bonds (1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0) wrapped by “rz_pre2_even”/“rz_pre2_odd” and “rz_post2_even”/“rz_post2_odd.” This enriches connectivity like a ring while preserving linear hardware mapping and the sandwich-style regularization. Update ANSATZ_SPEC by substituting the single CZ block with these two RZ–CZ–RZ subcircuits.  

5. Between the primary CZ chain and the first post-RZ block, insert four long-range CZ gates on pairs (0,4), (1,5), (2,6), (3,7). These targeted global entanglers capture distant correlations at minimal depth overhead, borrowing the chord-augmentation insight without resorting to dense graphs that hurt convergence. Simply append these CZ entries to ANSATZ_SPEC just before your post-RZ definitions.
