# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity-shared pre-entangling Z rotations combined with individual post-entangling Z rotations around a CZ chain  
  –  “8-Qubit RY-RZ Ansatz with Linear CZ Connectivity” (Gen 6) achieved the top score (0.87) with this “sandwich” (parity-shared pre, CZ chain, per-qubit post) and converged in ~450 steps with a small gap (0.05).  
- Dedicated per-qubit RY initialization layer  
  – Present in all top performers (Gen 6: 0.87, Gen 9: 0.86, Gen 8: 0.85), consistently boosting train/test accuracies (≥0.90/0.86).  
- Two-phase RZ layering (pre- and post-entanglement)  
  – Enables richer expressivity and faster convergence. Gen 6 (0.87) and Gen 9 (0.86) both rely on symmetric RZ blocks around entanglers to tighten generalization.  
- Linear CZ connectivity with structured layering  
  – While ring topologies excel (Gen 1 at 0.86), a linear chain with proper phase layers (Gen 6 at 0.87) matches or exceeds ring performance, offering hardware simplicity without loss in accuracy.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Deep, hierarchical two-qubit mixers (butterfly CRY layers)  
  – “Butterfly-structured 8-qubit ansatz” (Gen 3) scored only 0.80, had the lowest parameter efficiency (0.01) and showed an 0.08 generalization gap—overly complex entanglers with little accuracy gain.  
- Fully individual RZ parametrization (no parameter sharing)  
  – “8-Qubit Chain Entangling Ansatz” (Gen 8) used per-qubit pre- and post-RZ rotations (24 params) but reached only 0.85; “Layered RY-RZ CZ” (Gen 9) at 110 params hit 0.86. Excessive unique parameters inflated depth/gates without commensurate score boosts.  
- Absence of dual RZ layering  
  – Ansatz variants lacking symmetric pre- and post-phase blocks (e.g., simple CZ chain with only single-phase rotation) generally plateaued around 0.81–0.82, underperforming sandwich designs.  

## Implementation Insights

- Use small, parity-indexed parameter dictionaries in ANSATZ_SPEC  
  – E.g., mapping wires 0, 2, 4, 6 → “rz_pre_even” and 1, 3, 5, 7 → “rz_pre_odd” cuts RZ params from 8→2 while preserving phase expressivity (Gen 6).  
- Contiguous block structuring of layers  
  – List all RY gates → all pre-RZ gates → entanglers → all post-RZ gates. This clear pipeline (as in the current best program) simplifies both pruning and layer insertion.  
- Explicit naming conventions for clarity  
  – Prefixing params with “rz_pre_*” and “rz_post_*” aides both human readability and backend reuse detection, streamlining optimizer performance.  
- Decouple ansatz spec from execution  
  – Consistently invoking `_run(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` keeps circuit design modular, enabling rapid swapping of gate patterns without touching training logic.

## Performance Analysis

- Peak combined score of 0.87 (Gen 6) exceeds the best ring-CZ implementation (0.86, Gen 9) by leveraging parity-shared pre-phases on a simpler linear topology.  
- Convergence speed trades off with topology: pure ring designs (Gen 1, ~270 steps) converge faster but only match—never surpass—the 0.87 accuracy of the parity-sandwich linear chain (~450 steps).  
- Parameter efficiency remains low (~0.01) across all high-scoring circuits; however, controlled sharing in Gen 6 recovers expressivity lost by aggressive compression, hitting the sweet spot of depth ~99 and ~110 parameters.  
- Generalization gaps shrink under sandwich architectures (0.04–0.06) versus more entangled or unshared schemes (0.07–0.08), underscoring the regularizing effect of structured phase layering.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the eight individual post-entanglement RZ gates with two parity-shared parameters (“rz_post_even” on wires 0,2,4,6 and “rz_post_odd” on wires 1,3,5,7). This cuts the post‐phase parameter count from 8 to 2, preserving the sandwich architecture that shrank generalization gaps in Gen 6 while improving parameter efficiency.

2. Append a second “pre-phase → CZ chain → post-phase” sandwich after the existing RZ–CZ block. Concretely, after the current post-RZ gates add a new RZ layer with keys “rz2_pre_even”/“rz2_pre_odd”, the same linear CZ chain, and a final RZ layer “rz2_post_even”/“rz2_post_odd”. This two-layer phase–entanglement structure has driven faster convergence and richer expressivity in top performers.

3. Decompose the CZ chain into two interleaved sublayers—first on even bonds (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7), then on odd bonds (1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0)—and wrap each sublayer with its own parity-shared pre/post RZ pair. Alternating entanglement patterns in this brick-wall fashion increases connectivity diversity (mimicking ring-chord benefits) while respecting the proven sandwich paradigm.

4. Introduce a light RY mixing layer immediately after the CZs and before the post-RZ gates: apply per‐qubit RY rotations all using a single “ry_mix” parameter (or two parity shares “ry_mix_even”/“ry_mix_odd”). This mid-circuit mixing augments the two-phase RZ expressivity with additional nonlinearity, analogous to the strong boost from dedicated initialization RYs.

5. Add long-range CZs between qubits i and i+4 (i.e. CZ on [0,4],[1,5],[2,6],[3,7]) in the center of the circuit—between the pre-RZ and post-RZ layers—to capture global correlations. These four extra static entanglers come at modest depth cost and complement the linear chain’s local coupling, tightening multi-qubit correlations as seen in chord-enhanced designs.
