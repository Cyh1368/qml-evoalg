# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Layered RY → pre-RZ → linear CZ chain → post-RZ block  
  • Seed program (Gen 5) with this exact structure: depth 99, 270 gates, 146 params → combined 0.85 (train 0.94/val 0.89/test 0.87, gap 0.07).  
  • “Eight-Qubit Rotational Sandwich Ansatz” (Gen 19) reuses the same sandwich plus CRZ entanglers: depth 105, 212 params → combined 0.83.  
  Insight: the sandwich structure yields the top combined scores when balanced in depth (~100) and moderate param count.  
- Nearest-neighbor CZ chain  
  • Seed (Gen 5) vs. ring‐entangled “Ring-Entangled Two-Layer RY-RZ Ansatz” (Gen 18, depth 120, 276 gates): 0.85 vs. 0.78 combined.  
  Insight: linear entanglement minimizes depth overhead and noise accumulation.  
- Distinct naming of pre/post RZ parameters  
  • Seed and Gen 19 use “rz_pre_i” and “rz_post_i” → stable gradient flow, consistent convergence (~750 steps for seed, ~330 steps for Gen 19).  
  Insight: explicit parameter keys aid optimizer clarity and reproducibility.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive depth via multi-layer or ring entanglement  
  • Gen 18 (ring CZ, two RY–RZ layers): depth 120 → combined 0.78.  
  • Gen 20 (adds CRZ to the basic sandwich, 164 params): depth 99 but gate count still 270 → combined 0.76.  
  Conclusion: added entangling layers or complex topologies inflate circuit cost without boosting generalization.  
- Over-parameterization with CRZ/CRX two-qubit gates  
  • Gen 19 adds CRZ gates to the sandwich: 212 params, depth 105 → combined 0.83 (below seed).  
  Insight: two-qubit parametrized gates increase depth and optimization burden more than they expand useful expressivity.  
- Parameter count reduction without structure retention  
  • Gen 17 & Gen 21 (“8-Qubit Symmetric RY-RZ-CZ Ansatz” and “Layered RY–RZ CZ Variational Ansatz”): 98 params, depth 99 → combined 0.79.  
  Conclusion: pruning parameters alone (removing rotation layers) harms expressivity; the sandwich block must be repeated sufficiently.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit ANSATZ_SPEC entries with “wire” vs. “wires”  
  • All top programs (seed, Gen 19) adhere to schema: prevents run-time errors and ensures consistent gate placement.  
- Modular EVOLVE-BLOCK containing only gate spec  
  • Isolates ansatz evolution from encoding/training details (fixed backend `_run`), enabling fair comparison across generations.  
- Repetition of a single sandwich layer vs. multi-layer stacking  
  • Seed’s repeated sandwich yields moderate depth (99) and high score (0.85). Gen 19 adds CRZ interleaves but only marginally affects expressivity relative to cost.  
- Clear parameter naming (“ry_i”, “rz_pre_i”, “rz_post_i”)  
  • Improves traceability and gradient mapping; correlated with stable convergence in seed and Gen 19.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Best combined score (0.85) at depth 99 and 146 params (seed) vs. all evaluated variants:  
  • Gen 19: 0.83 (depth 105, 212 params)  
  • Gen 17/21: 0.79 (depth 99, 98 params)  
  • Gen 18: 0.78 (depth 120, 146 params)  
  • Gen 20: 0.76 (depth 99, 164 params)  
- Depth correlation: performance drops once depth > 105 (Gen 18 at 120 → 0.78). Even slight depth increases (+6 layers in Gen 19) yield modest gains at high param cost.  
- Parameter count alone is not predictive: seed’s 146 params outperform Gen 19’s 212 params and Gen 20’s 164 params; the quality of gate layering matters more than raw param count.  
- Generalization gaps remain low (~0.07–0.10) across all sandwich‐style ansätze, but only the seed program hits the optimal expressivity/efficiency balance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate initial rotation axes without adding depth  
   In your first RY layer, replace RY on even‐indexed qubits (0,2,4,6) with RX gates (`{"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"rx_i"}`) while keeping RY on odd qubits. This simple mutation diversifies the initial parameter manifold—an evidence‐backed trick that often accelerates convergence—without changing circuit depth or entanglement structure.  

2. Tie pre‐ and post-RZ parameters across symmetric qubit pairs  
   Enforce `rz_pre_i = rz_pre_{7−i}` and `rz_post_i = rz_post_{7−i}` by defining only `rz_pre_0…rz_pre_3` on wires (0,7),(1,6),(2,5),(3,4) and similarly for `rz_post`. This cuts your RZ count in half (16→8) while preserving the linear sandwich architecture, leveraging successful shared‐parameter patterns to reduce training variance and potentially speed up optimization.  

3. Insert a lightweight mid‐circuit RY layer  
   After the CZ chain but before the existing post-RZ block, append eight gates:  
     `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"ry_mid_i"}` for i=0…7.  
   This +8-depth, +8-parameter “RY mid‐layer” enriches non-commuting rotations between your entanglers and post-RZ, building on the proven expressivity of RY–RZ–CZ sandwiches while keeping total depth ≲107.  

4. Mix in CNOT entanglers for noise‐aware tuning  
   In your linear chain, replace every second CZ (e.g., on wires [1,2], [3,4], [5,6]) with CNOT gates:  
     `{"gate":"CNOT","wires":[i,j]}`.  
   This zero-param swap tests whether hardware‐specific CNOT fidelities can yield lower noise accumulation than CZ, all while retaining nearest‐neighbor entanglement and overall depth.  

5. Explore a parameter-shared double sandwich  
   Duplicate the entire RY→RZ_pre→CZ chain→RZ_post block and append it to the EVOLVE-BLOCK, reusing the same parameter names (`ry_i`, `rz_pre_i`, `rz_post_i`) in both repetitions. This mutation doubles depth (~198) but keeps the parameter footprint at 146, enabling you to probe whether deeper, parameter-shared circuits can match the seed’s generalization while offering richer expressivity.
