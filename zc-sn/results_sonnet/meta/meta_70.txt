# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Multi-distance CRZ topology (ring + skip-2 + skip-3 + diameter) repeatedly yielded score lifts.  
  • Gen 64/61 (0.88→0.89) introduced skip-3 to fill the distance-3 gap; current best (0.89) retains that full set.  
- “Shared-depth” parameter reuse across two blocks accelerated convergence and tightened generalization.  
  • Gen 71 (Shared-Depth Alternating Skip-Entanglement Ansatz) reached 0.87 with zero run-to-run variance in ∼180 steps; current best matches that convergence speed and further boosts validation/test to 0.92/0.90 (combined 0.89).  
- Strict layer ordering RY→ring-CRZ (even/odd)→RX→skip-2→skip-3→diameter correlates with top performance.  
  • All top designs (Gen 61, Gen 71, current best) use this exact sequence.  
- Aggressive sharing of long-range entanglers (single-knob skip-2, skip-3, diameter) preserves global connectivity at minimal parameter cost and yields parameter efficiency ≈0.01.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Omitting skip-3 entanglers capped scores at 0.86 and widened gaps to ~0.10.  
  • Gen 60/63/65 (no skip-3) peaked at 0.86 with slower convergence and higher generalization gap.  
- Mixing CRY into skip-2 layers (Gen 68) underperformed (0.85 combined) despite similar depth, indicating that uniform CRZ chemistry is preferable for stable training.  
- Partial de-sharing or block-specific ring parameters without deeper sharing increased parameter count (140 params) but only matched 0.86 (Gen 69/70/72) and raised complexity without accuracy gain.  
- Over-parameterization beyond ~128 independent knobs did not improve expressivity—140 params yielded ≤0.87, while shared‐depth 128 param designs hit 0.89.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Modular layer constructors (_ry_layer, _ring_crz, _skip_crz, _diameter_crz) make it easy to toggle connectivity patterns and parameter sharing.  
- Alternating ring entanglement with two shared parameters (“crz_ring_even/odd”) achieves full nearest-neighbor mixing with only 2 knobs.  
- Single-parameter skip-3 (_skip_crz step=3) is critical: it completes mid-range links left out by skip-2, directly correlating with the jump to 0.89.  
- The shared-depth trick—concatenating the same _BLOCK spec twice—doubles entangling power without raising n_params, delivering both expressivity and strong regularization (zero std).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined scores progressed: 0.85 (Gen 68) → 0.86 (Gen 69/70/72) → 0.87 (Gen 71) → 0.89 (current best).  
- Generalization gap shrank from ~0.09–0.10 (Gen 68) → 0.07 (Gen 69/70/72) → 0.06 (current best), tracking the addition of skip-3 and parameter sharing.  
- Convergence steps fell from 450–1 080 (earlier) to ∼180 in all shared-depth multi-scale designs (Gen 61/71/current), highlighting the efficiency of balanced multi-scale entanglement with shared parameters.  
- Parameter efficiency for top designs stabilized at ~0.01 once multi-distance CRZ and shared depth were both in play, indicating a sweet spot in the expressivity–regularization trade-off.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Restore an alternating two-parameter diameter entanglement  
   • In `_diameter_crz`, add distinct `even_param="crz_diam_even"` and `odd_param="crz_diam_odd"` arguments, then assign them to antipodal pairs `[0,4]`/`[2,6]` vs. `[1,5]`/`[3,7]`.  
   • Evidence: earlier generations (Gen35/38) showed that a two-knob diameter enhances expressivity; combining this with shared-depth across blocks should retain low variance while pushing past the 0.89 score plateau.  

2. Insert a global “star” CRZ layer after diameter  
   • Define `_star_crz(n, center=0, param="crz_star")` to apply `CRZ` between qubit 0 and all others, then append its gates to each block.  
   • Evidence: multi-distance entanglers (ring+skip+diameter) drove the best performance; adding a single-parameter hub-and-spoke layer further strengthens global mixing at just +1 knob and minimal depth cost.  

3. Append a second ring-CRZ entanglement at the end of each block  
   • After the diameter (and optional star), call `_ring_crz(n, even_param="crz_ring_even", odd_param="crz_ring_odd")` again.  
   • Evidence: reinforcing nearest-neighbor mixing post long‐range entanglement can close local connectivity loops and was correlated with tighter generalization gaps in ablations of deep-ring designs.  

4. Add a symmetric mid-block RZ phase layer  
   • Implement `_mirror_rz_layer(qubits, prefix="rz_mid")` that applies `RZ` with shared params `rz_mid_0…rz_mid_3` on qubit pairs (0,7), (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), and insert it between the RX and skip-2 layers.  
   • Evidence: mirror-paired phase controls sharpen local expressivity and were shown in ablation studies to accelerate convergence to the ~180-step plateau without increasing parameter count beyond +4.  

5. Partially de-share skip-3 across blocks  
   • In the first block invocation, use `_skip_crz(n, step=3, param="crz_skip3_b0")`, and in the second block keep `param="crz_skip3"`.  
   • Evidence: block-specific long-range entanglement knobs introduce gentle flexibility—akin to partial ring de-sharing—that may lift accuracy by allowing each depth segment to adapt its mid-range mixing without blowing up parameter count.
