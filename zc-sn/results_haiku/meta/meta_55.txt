# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Pairwise-shared single-qubit rotations on qubit pairs  
  • In the current best program (Pairwise-Shared RY-CZ-CRZ Variational Ansatz, Gen 50, combined score 0.91), RY and RZ rotations are shared across fixed qubit pairs. This pattern mirrors the seed ansatz but extends sharing to mid-circuit RZ, boosting validation accuracy to 0.94 with only 50 parameters.  
- Three-stage RZ layering (pre-, mid-, post-entanglement)  
  • Gen 50 reuses the same two RZ parameters before, during, and after the CZ chain. This adds expressivity without increasing n_params (50) and reduces the generalization gap to 0.02—an improvement over the seed’s 0.03 gap.  
- Linear CZ forward chain + two-parameter alternating CRZ reverse chain  
  • Both the seed program (combined 0.91, convergence ~330 steps) and Gen 50 (0.91, convergence 180 steps) employ a 7-link CZ chain followed by alternating crz_rev_a/crz_rev_b on the reverse links. This simple entanglement topology yields top accuracy and accelerates convergence when paired with mid-circuit RZ reuse.  
- Parameter reuse for expressivity  
  • Sharing “rz_pre” across four mid-circuit RZ gates in Gen 50 added phase flexibility and raised validation accuracy (0.94 vs. seed’s 0.93) without adding parameters or appreciably increasing depth (160 vs. 154).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive parameter sharing & linear-only entanglement  
  • Generations 55 and 56 (both combined score 0.0) over-shared parameters and used strictly linear CZ/CRZ chains. The lack of expressivity and, in Gen 55, spec errors (“wire” vs. “wires”) caused complete validation failure.  
- Under-parameterization below ~50 parameters  
  • The Alternating Parameter-Sharing Hybrid Entanglement Ansatz (Gen 53, n_params 44) dropped to a combined score of 0.88 and saw its generalization gap rise to 0.04. Reducing parameters too far harms both accuracy (validation 0.90) and robustness.  
- Introducing diverse two-qubit gates without parameter budget increase  
  • Gen 53’s hybrid reverse chain that mixed CRZ and central CRX did not surpass the 0.91 ceiling and added implementation complexity, suggesting that entanglement diversity must be balanced with parameter sharing.

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit ANSATZ_SPEC structure  
  • Defining the circuit as a list of dicts with clear “gate”, “wire(s)”, and “param” keys (seed and Gen 50) simplifies tweaks to sharing, layering, and entanglement blocks independently.  
- Mid-circuit RZ reuse via identical “param” keys  
  • In Gen 50, four mid-circuit RZ gates reuse “rz_pre_a”/“rz_pre_b,” demonstrating how additional rotation layers can be inserted without growing the parameter set.  
- Alternating CRZ parameters via loop or manual pattern  
  • Both seed and Gen 50 implement reverse-chain CRZ alternation (crz_rev_a/crz_rev_b) either through explicit ANSATZ_SPEC entries or a Python loop with `idx % 2` logic—concise, error-free, and highly expressive.  
- Pairwise parameter naming consistency  
  • Using suffixes “_a” and “_b” to toggle shared parameters across even/odd qubits (e.g., ry_a on wires 0,2,4,6; ry_b on 1,3,5,7) enables vectorized sharing that hardware compilers can readily optimize.

## Performance Analysis  
- Sweet spot at 50 parameters for combined score 0.91  
  • Seed and Gen 50 both use 50 parameters, hitting the 0.91 top score. Gen 50 edges out with validation 0.94 vs. seed’s 0.93 by adding mid-circuit RZ reuse.  
- Depth/gate count trade-off  
  • Gen 50’s deeper circuit (depth 160, 360 gates) still converges faster (180 steps) than the shallower seed (depth 154, 312 gates, 330 steps), indicating that well-placed mid-circuit rotations can speed up training despite added gates.  
- Parameter reduction hurts gap and accuracy  
  • Gen 49 (38 parameters) attained combined 0.90 with a wider generalization gap (0.03), while Gen 53 (44 parameters) slipped to 0.88 and gap 0.04. Keeping n_params ≈50 is crucial for both accuracy and generalization.  
- Failure modes confirm expressivity threshold  
  • Absolute parameter reuse (Gen 55/56) leads to total validation failure (score 0.0), reinforcing that a minimum set of distinct parameters and moderate entanglement diversity are required for any learning to occur.
