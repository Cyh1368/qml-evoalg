# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Parameter Sharing on Single-Qubit Rotations  
  • Shared RY/RZ parameters across qubit pairs (Shared-parameter CZ-entangled ansatz, combined score 0.87) doubled parameter efficiency (0.02 vs. 0.01) compared to fully independent rotations (0.01 in RY-RZ-CZ and RY-RZ-CRZ ansätze).  
- Forward-and-Reverse CZ Chains for Entanglement  
  • Adding a reversed CZ chain (Shared-parameter CZ-entangled: 14 CZ gates total, depth 154) improved generalization (gap 0.01) over a single-direction chain (8-qubit RY-RZ-CZ: gap 0.07).  
- Low Parameter Count with High Expressivity  
  • Reducing n_params to 38 while preserving near-uniform accuracies (0.86 validation/test) showed that compact, shared-parameter designs can match or exceed larger circuits (140–146 parameters) in combined score.  
- Fast Convergence via Simplified Ansatz  
  • Shared-parameter CZ-entangled converged in ~180 steps vs. 750 steps for the fully independent RY-RZ-CZ ansatz, indicating the benefits of parameter tying on training speed.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Full Parameterization of Two-Qubit Gates  
  • Ansätze with CRZ or CRY entanglement (RY-RZ-CRZ: combined 0.84, gap 0.12; Layered RY/RZ+CRY: combined 0.86, gap 0.07) overfit more and offered only marginal accuracy gains at the cost of deeper circuits or high overfitting.  
- Large Independent Parameter Sets  
  • Designs with 140–146 parameters (Layered RY-RZ+CRZ, Layered RY/RZ+CRY) yielded low parameter efficiency (0.01) and no improvement in combined score over more compact variants.  
- Dense Entangling Followed by Heavy Rotation Layers  
  • The explicit Python-list implementation in RY-RZ-CRZ Linear Ansatz achieved 0.96 training accuracy but test dropped to 0.84 (gap 0.12), showing that dense controlled-rotation layering without parameter tying amplifies overfitting.

## Implementation Insights
- JSON-Style Ansatz Specification Enables Easy Parameter Sharing  
  • The current best program’s ANSATZ_SPEC groups qubits by shared labels (“ry_a” vs. “ry_b”, “rz_pre_a”/“_b”, “rz_post_a”/“_b”), illustrating a concise way to enforce parameter tying in code.  
- Reusing Native CZ Gates for Efficient Entanglement  
  • Using unparameterized CZ gates in a bidirectional chain avoided the extra parameter overhead of CRX/CRY/CRZ while still achieving strong entanglement and generalization.  
- Pre- and Post-Entanglement Rotation Layers  
  • Splitting RZ rotations into “pre” and “post” entanglement blocks helped retain expressivity without blowing up parameter count, as seen in both the current best and initial RY-RZ-CZ programs.  
- Fixed Backend Abstraction Simplifies Comparison  
  • All evaluated programs rely on the same run_experiment backend, so performance differences directly reflect ansatz design choices rather than measurement or optimization variations.

## Performance Analysis
- Combined Score Ranking  
  • Best: Shared-parameter CZ-entangled ansatz (0.87) ≈ Layered RY/RZ+CRY (0.86) > RY-RZ-CZ (0.85) > RY-RZ-CRZ and Layered RY-RZ+CRZ (0.84).  
- Generalization Gap vs. Parameter Efficiency  
  • Only the shared-parameter design achieved both a minimal gap (0.01) and doubled efficiency (0.02); all low-efficiency (>0.01) designs had gaps ≥0.07.  
- Convergence Speed Correlates with Parameter Tying  
  • Shared-parameter circuit reached stable accuracy in ~180 steps; the fully independent RY-RZ-CZ ansatz took 750 steps, highlighting faster optimization with fewer free parameters.  
- Depth and Gate Count Trade-Off  
  • Although the best ansatz is the deepest (154) and highest gate count (312), its low parameter count and simple CZ gates offset hardware concerns by ensuring robustness and stable training.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a second shared-parameter single-qubit rotation layer after the post-entanglement RZ block. For example, append another set of RY rotations using the existing “ry_a”/“ry_b” parameters. This adds no new parameters but may boost expressivity by mirroring the successful pre-entanglement RY–RZ–CZ–RZ–RY structure.  

2. Refine the qubit grouping for parameter sharing from two groups (“a”/“b”) to four groups (“a”–“d”). E.g., assign qubits [0,1]→ry_a, [2,3]→ry_b, [4,5]→ry_c, [6,7]→ry_d (and similarly for pre/post RZ). This modestly increases n_params (from 38 to 76) while preserving tied rotations, allowing finer control over local rotations without fully independent parameters.  

3. Partially parameterize the reverse entanglement chain by replacing its CZ gates with a shared-parameter CRZ gate. Use one new parameter (e.g. “crz_rev”) across all reverse-direction edges. This tests whether a lightweight, global controlled-phase enhances expressivity beyond unparameterized CZs while retaining the low overfitting profile of shared parameters.  

4. Enhance entanglement connectivity with sparse “skip” CZ links in addition to the bidirectional chain. For instance, add CZ gates between qubit pairs (0,2), (2,4), (4,6) and (1,3), (3,5), (5,7) without extra parameters. This broader yet still simple CZ-only topology can improve multi-qubit correlations and generalization at minimal depth cost.  

5. Mix CZ and CNOT gates in the forward/reverse chains to diversify entangling phases. Keep the forward chain as CZ(0–1–…–7) and use CNOT(7–6–…–0) in reverse, or vice versa. Since both are native, this swap adds no parameters but may produce richer entanglement patterns that further close the generalization gap.
