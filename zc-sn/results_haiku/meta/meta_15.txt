# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Pairwise parameter sharing in single-qubit rotations  
  • Programs “8-Qubit Shared-Rotation CZ-CRZ Ansatz” (Gen 11) and the seed current best both share RY/RZ parameters on alternating qubit pairs (“ry_a”/“ry_b”, “rz_pre_a”/“rz_pre_b”, “rz_post_a”/“rz_post_b”), yielding a combined score of 0.91 and a minimal generalization gap of 0.01 with only 44 parameters (parameter efficiency 0.02).  
  • The same pattern in the “Parameter-Sharing Linear Entanglement Ansatz” (Gen 15) achieved score 0.89, gap 0.01, and convergence in ~180 steps with 50 parameters.

- Bidirectional entanglement chains with zero-cost CZ + shared CRZ reverse pass  
  • The current best program’s forward CZ chain (7 gates) plus a single shared “crz_rev” parameter on the reverse CRZ chain delivered high expressivity and strong generalization (0.91 combined score, 0.01 gap).  
  • This outperformed pure CZ chains (e.g., “Shared-parameter Sparse Entanglement Ansatz”, score 0.87, gap 0.04).

- Two-stage RZ rotations (pre- and post-entanglement)  
  • Splitting RZ into shared “rz_pre_*” before entanglement and “rz_post_*” after allowed rich expressivity with only 16 RZ parameters, matching high-param designs’ accuracy while keeping parameter count low.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Dense CRY-heavy entanglement patterns  
  • The “Shared-angle 8-qubit variational ansatz” (Gen 8) used a dense CRY pattern (218 parameters, 342 gates) but scored only 0.76 with a large generalization gap (0.12), indicating overfitting and poor parameter efficiency (0.00).

- Excessive circuit depth without parameter tying  
  • The “Shared-parameter layered 8-qubit ansatz” (Gen 12) had depth 111, 420 gates, and 86 parameters, yet only reached score 0.87 with gap 0.06, showing diminishing returns from brute-force depth increases when parameters remain largely independent.

- Untied parametrized two-qubit gates (CRX/CRY/CRZ) scattered through the circuit  
  • Designs that freely interleaved distinct CRX/CRY gates (e.g., RY–RZ–CRZ ansatz) inflated depth and overfitting (gap 0.12) without score gains, compared to tying them into a single shared CRZ in the reverse chain.

## Implementation Insights

- Use of a JSON-style ANSATZ_SPEC with consistent parameter labels  
  • Labeling patterns like “ry_a”/“ry_b” and “rz_pre_a”/“rz_pre_b” across multiple wires enforces pairwise tying succinctly and boosts parameter efficiency (0.02).

- Leverage native CZ for full connectivity, supplement with a shared-parameter CRZ reverse pass  
  • Native CZ gates build robust entanglement at zero parameter cost; a subsequent reverse CRZ chain (all with “crz_rev”) injects tunable correlations without blowing up the parameter count.

- Clear separation of pre- and post-entanglement rotation layers  
  • Defining distinct “pre” and “post” RZ layers around the entangling blocks stabilizes optimization and maximizes expressivity per parameter, as demonstrated by the current best program’s rapid convergence (180 steps).

## Performance Analysis

- Top combined score 0.91 tied to high parameter efficiency and minimal overfitting  
  • The current best (Gen 11) achieves 0.91 combined score, 0.01 gap, 44 parameters, efficiency 0.02, depth 154, gate count 312, converging in 180 steps—outpacing all other designs.

- Efficiency ≥0.02 correlates with score ≥0.89 and gap ≤0.01  
  • Both Gen 11 (0.02 eff., 0.91 score) and Gen 15 (0.02 eff., 0.89 score) hit these thresholds, whereas all designs with efficiency ≤0.01 cap at scores ≤0.87 and exhibit larger gaps (0.04–0.12).

- Convergence speed inversely related to free-parameter count  
  • Tied-parameter designs (44–50 params) converge in ~150–180 steps; designs with >70 parameters or untied two-qubit gates require >500 steps (e.g., Gen 12 converged in 540 steps).

- Moderate increases in depth/gate count justified by superior net performance  
  • Though the best program has the highest depth (154) and gate count (312), its algorithmic gains (score, gap, efficiency) validate trading moderate hardware cost for optimal accuracy and generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a mid-circuit RZ layer between the forward CZ chain and the reverse CRZ pass, using two new shared parameters “rz_mid_a” on qubits [0,2,4,6] and “rz_mid_b” on [1,3,5,7]. This adds just 2 parameters but gives a three-stage RZ structure (pre-, mid-, post-entanglement) that may capture additional phase flexibility per the success of pre/post tying.  

2. Hybridize the forward entanglement block by replacing every other CZ with a shared-parameter CRZ gate “crz_fwd” (e.g. CRZ on wires [1,2], [3,4], [5,6] share “crz_fwd”). This lightly parameterizes the forward chain, testing whether a minimal tunable two-qubit rotation up front can boost expressivity without inflating the parameter count.  

3. Augment the zero-cost entanglement topology with skip-link CZs between qubit pairs (0–2, 2–4, 4–6, 6–0) in the forward block. These extra CZs draw on the star/fan-out motif to broaden connectivity at no parameter overhead and may tighten long-range correlations.  

4. Append a two-gate global mixing layer after the final RZ: apply RX on all qubits with a single parameter “rx_global”, then RY with “ry_global”. Inspired by the efficiency of shared RY/RZ rotations, this 2-parameter layer maximizes Hilbert-space coverage before measurement.  

5. Split the reverse CRZ chain into two shared parameters—“crz_rev_a” on the odd-indexed links ([7→6], [5→4], [3→2], [1→0]) and “crz_rev_b” on the even-indexed links ([6→5], [4→3], [2→1]). This doubles the entangling-angle variety with only one extra parameter, leveraging the success of parameter sharing to probe richer correlations.
