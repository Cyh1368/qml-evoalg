<DESCRIPTION>
This edit replaces the fully collective 8-qubit layers with a parity-split brickwork ansatz.

Why this should help:
- It keeps the same parameter budget per block as the current program (4 trainable angles per block, 24 total circuit parameters), so efficiency is not sacrificed.
- It breaks the overly rigid global symmetry of the seed by giving even and odd qubits separate mixers, which is often better for binary-feature data with heterogeneous feature-pair couplings.
- It reduces gate count substantially by removing the redundant RZ layer and compressing the entangling pattern to two staggered CZ matchings.
- The sequence RY -> CZ -> RX -> CZ provides a strong noncommuting mixer-entangler-mixer structure that should convert the fixed phase encodings into measurable Z-population differences more effectively, while remaining shallow enough to train well.

Overall, this is a targeted shift toward a lighter, more expressive hardware-efficient ansatz that should improve validation/test accuracy and convergence speed while also lowering depth and gate count.
</DESCRIPTION>
