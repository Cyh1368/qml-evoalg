<DESCRIPTION>
The current design achieves excellent performance (0.90 test accuracy, 0.01 generalization gap) with minimal parameters through aggressive parameter sharing. However, there is room to improve while maintaining efficiency.

Key observation: The output layer currently uses collective parameter sharing (all qubits share `collective_ry_out` and `collective_rx_out`). This enforces perfect symmetry at readout, which is overly restrictive given that individual qubits may encode distinct information.

Proposed change: Break parameter sharing on the RY output layer by giving each qubit an individual parameter, while keeping the RX layer collective. This modest increase (from 4 to 12 unique parameters, ~36 total params) should:

1. Allow individual qubits to rotate optimally before measurement
2. Preserve the efficient collective structure for phases and input mixing
3. Improve test accuracy by removing artificial symmetry constraints at readout
4. Maintain good generalization since we're only unfreezing a late-stage layer
5. Converge quickly due to the small parameter increase

This follows the principle of using shared parameters where symmetry is justified (input mixing, global phases) but individual parameters where qubits play different roles (just before measurement).
</DESCRIPTION>
