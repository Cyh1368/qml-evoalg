An exceptionally effective and fundamentally different approach is to reconceptualize this classification problem as a parameterized Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA). 

### The Insight
In the fixed pipeline, the dataset's features are encoded using an all-to-all `IsingZZ` coupling (acting on all 28 possible pairs of the 8 qubits). This means the `feature_map` is mathematically equivalent to applying the time-evolution of a fully-connected Ising problem Hamiltonian ($e^{-i \gamma \sum J_{ab} Z_a Z_b}$). 

Prior architectures treated this like a standard Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) task, adding arbitrary fixed two-qubit gates (like rings or 3D cubes of `CZ` gates) inside the ansatz. However, because the feature map *already* provides a dense, complete $K_8$ entanglement graph, injecting additional artificial two-qubit gates causes extreme over-entanglement, leading to barren plateaus, scrambling, and unnecessary depth. 

### The Solution: Data-Driven QAOA
We can completely eliminate all two-qubit gates from the ansatz block and rely **100% on the data for entanglement**.
1. **Local Z-Fields ($h_q$)**: The ansatz begins with independent `RZ` rotations on every qubit. Because $Z$ commutes with the preceding `IsingZZ` feature map, these act as learnable local magnetic fields, allowing the model to independently weight the importance of each feature/qubit. (Prior collective models failed to do this because their parameters were tied).
2. **Transverse Mixers**: We follow the $Z$ fields with collective `RY` and `RX` rotations. These act as the QAOA global transverse mixing Hamiltonians ($e^{-i \beta \sum X_i}$), rotating the state out of the computational basis to create interference for the next feature upload.

This approach massively reduces the circuit depth and gate count, entirely circumvents over-entanglement barren plateaus, and strikes an optimal balance: it gives the model exactly enough expressivity to weight individual features (using only 10 parameters per block) while regularizing the mixing operations.
