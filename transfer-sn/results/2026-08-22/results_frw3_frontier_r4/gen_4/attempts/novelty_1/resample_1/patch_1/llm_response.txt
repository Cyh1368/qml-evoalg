An excellent way to improve expressiveness without causing overfitting is to introduce a slight symmetry-breaking that respects the underlying geometry of the entangler.

The current best program achieves high parameter efficiency and avoids barren plateaus by sharing a single parameter per layer across all 8 qubits. The entanglement structure is a degree-3 hypercube graph.
A hypercube is naturally bipartite: its vertices can be partitioned into two sets based on the parity of their Hamming weight (i.e., whether the binary representation of their index has an even or odd number of `1`s). Crucially, **every edge** in a hypercube connects a parity-0 node to a parity-1 node.

We can double the expressiveness of the ansatz—from 4 parameters per block to 8—by grouping the parameters according to this exact bipartite splitting. Since the parameter assignment matches the connectivity rules of the CZ gates, it maintains a highly regular and symmetric training manifold while allowing the optimizer to learn different feature weightings.
