<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz utilizes a highly symmetric "collective" parameter sharing scheme (all 8 qubits share the same parameters for each layer), which strongly regularizes the model (generalization gap is only 0.01) but limits its expressivity, plateauing the accuracy around 0.90. The entanglement graph used is a degree-3 hypercube (`Q_3`).

To safely increase expressiveness without risking the severe overfitting seen in unshared architectures, we can introduce a "bipartite parameter sharing" scheme. The hypercube is a perfectly bipartite graph, meaning we can partition its vertices into an "even parity" group (wires 0, 3, 5, 6) and an "odd parity" group (wires 1, 2, 4, 7). Every `CZ` entangling edge perfectly connects one even vertex to one odd vertex.

By assigning one parameter to the "even" qubits and a separate parameter to the "odd" qubits for each rotation layer, we perfectly match the symmetry of the entanglement graph while doubling the parameter count per block from 4 to 8. This slightly breaks the full permutation symmetry—giving the ansatz more freedom to align with the asymmetrical feature pairs—while maintaining strict regularization across the bipartite sets.
</DESCRIPTION>
