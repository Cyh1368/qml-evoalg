# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats  
  • Observed in the current best program and in the “8-qubit multi-upload CRZ ansatz classifier” (Gen 58), both reaching a combined score of 0.95 (96% train/val/test, zero gap) with depth 153 and 408 CZ/CRZ gates.  
  • Balances expressivity versus resource cost: compared to 159-depth designs at 0.93 (e.g. Gen 57/60/61), the 153-depth circuits hit 0.96 accuracy with no overfitting.  

- Full-graph CZ entanglement plus two centrally shared CRZ bridges  
  • Both top scorers (current best & Gen 58) use full pairwise CZ across 8 qubits, then inject exactly two trainable CRZ gates on complementary central edges (wires [3,4] and [2,5]) with one shared “crz_center” parameter.  
  • This minimal adaptive two-qubit phase layer yields a 1% absolute accuracy gain over fixed-CZ-only variants (e.g. Gen 57 “Symmetric CZ-Bridge,” 0.93 combined, 94% test accuracy).  

- Global parameter sharing of pre- and post-rotation layers  
  • A single “ry_pre” across all 8 qubits and one “rz_phase” per block concentrate parameter budget, delivering zero accuracy standard deviation and a 0.00 generalization gap.  
  • Shared “ry_post” before measurement further regularizes; designs without symmetric sharing (more independent RY/RZ keys) incurred up to 0.03 gap or lower stability (prior Gen 45 CRY-swap drop to 0.93).  

- Trainable gain & bias on mean-Z readout  
  • All top 0.95 scorers initialize gain=1, bias=0 and learn them via backpropagation.  
  • Contributes ~0.01–0.02 uplift in final test accuracy and accelerates convergence to ~450 Adam steps versus ~360 for 0.93 circuits.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Replacing CRZ with CRY or CRX in the bridge  
  • Gen 45 “shared_complete_graph_cry” (CRY-bridge) fell from 0.94→0.93 combined and saw the generalization gap widen to 0.03, indicating phase-aligned CRZ better complements IsingZZ uploads.  

- Adding extra adaptive two-qubit gates beyond a single CRZ per block  
  • Gen 48 “symmetric_crz_bridge” increased parameters to 32 and gate count to 450 but remained at a 0.93 combined score, showing diminishing returns from multiple controlled rotations.  

- Omitting adaptive two-qubit phases entirely  
  • Gen 49 “symmetric_complete_graph_bridge” (no CRZ) kept depth/gate_count constant yet dropped to a 0.92 combined score, underlining the necessity of at least one trainable entangling phase.  

- Invalid or mis-indexed ansatz specifications  
  • Gen 62 failed validation (“untie_phase_readout”), scored 0.0 combined—errors traceable to parameter-key indexing issues in ANSATZ_SPEC, stressing the importance of `validate_ansatz_spec` and consistent key-to-position mapping.  

## Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC with explicit gate dictionaries  
  • Defines exactly four parameter keys per block (`ry_pre`, `rz_phase`, `crz_center`, `ry_post`), ensuring N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK = 4 and total N_PARAMS = 4×3×2+2 = 26.  
  • `validate_ansatz_spec` and the key-to-position lookup in `apply_ansatz_block` decouple specification order from runtime parameter indexing, preventing off-by-one errors.  

- Use of qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue and tape.graph.get_depth()  
  • Instantly extracts depth (153) and gate_count (408) metadata for tight resource–performance tracking (versus manual counting).  

- Pennylane autograd/backprop with early stopping  
  • Adam optimizer converges to 96% test accuracy in ∼450 steps when using L2 loss on mean-Z expectation, gain/bias calibration, and patience-based early stopping.  
  • Restoring best weights on validation loss is critical to maintain zero generalization gap.  

- Fixed IsingZZ feature map scaling and mapping  
  • FEATURE_SCALE=π/2 applied uniformly across 28 two-qubit couplings ensures consistent embedding; coupling to the same qubits used in the CZ entangling graph fosters symmetry and expressivity.  

## Performance Analysis

- Top-scoring designs achieve combined 0.95 with 96% test accuracy and 0.00 generalization gap  
  • Current best and Gen 58 share identical architectural motifs (3 uploads×2 repeats, 26 params, depth 153, 408 gates, central CRZ bridges).  
  • Their parameter efficiency (~0.04 accuracy/param) matches prior best but maximizes absolute accuracy.  

- Moderate depth increase yields measurable accuracy gains  
  • Shallow variants at depth 141–147 (with fewer CZ edges or missing CRZ) peaked at 0.94 combined, while depth 153 circuits rose to 0.96.  

- Convergence dynamics correlate with score  
  • 0.93-scoring programs (Gen 57/60/61) converged in ∼360 steps; 0.95-scoring runs needed ∼450 steps, reflecting deeper entangling structure and extra adaptive phases.  

- Zero generalization gap tied to strong parameter sharing and single-CRZ strategy  
  • Designs with per-block independent rotations or multiple adaptive gates incurred nonzero gaps (0.02–0.03), whereas the symmetric schemes hold train/val/test accuracies identically.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Promote the fixed FEATURE_SCALE into three independent trainable parameters (`feature_scale_0`, `feature_scale_1`, `feature_scale_2`), each applied to the IsingZZ calls in uploads 0, 1, and 2 respectively. Initialize them to π/2 and include them in `circuit_params` so they’re optimized alongside the ansatz. Prior runs (Gen 25/27) showed that tuning embedding strength per upload accelerates convergence by ~200 Adam steps and adds ~0.01–0.02 test accuracy without increasing depth or gate count.

2. Replace the single global gain and bias with per-qubit affine readout corrections (`gain_0…gain_7`, `bias_0…bias_7`) applied individually to each ⟨Z⟩ before computing the mean. This extends the existing predict_batch calibration to capture qubit-specific offsets and scales. Given the ~0.01–0.02 uplift from global calibration, per-qubit adjustments should further tighten generalization and reduce any residual bias diversity.

3. Enforce parameter sharing across the two repeats within each upload by mapping both repeats’ blocks to the same four keys (`ry_pre`, `rz_phase`, `crz_center`, `ry_post`). In practice, let `block_index = upload_index` in `apply_ansatz_block` when computing the parameter offset, so each upload has only one unique set of four parameters instead of two. This doubles the strength of the proven global‐sharing regularization (zero accuracy std) and frees up ~12 circuit parameters to reinvest in additional expressivity.

4. Augment the minimal CRZ bridge with a third shared CRZ per block on wires [1, 6] using the existing `crz_center` key. Add `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[1,6],"param":"crz_center"}` immediately after the other two CRZ entries in the ANSATZ_SPEC. Since the two‐bridge design already delivers a +1% absolute gain, a third central phase may yield further accuracy improvements at zero extra parameter cost.

5. Introduce low-cost entangler diversity by cycling CZ patterns across uploads: keep the full complete graph on uploads 0 and 2 but swap in a sparse “skip-chain” CZ pattern (e.g. edges [(0,2),(1,3),(2,4),(3,5),(4,6),(5,7),(6,0),(7,1)]) on upload 1. This structured variation enriches state mixing without increasing gate count or depth. Pattern diversity has proven effective in classical ensembles and may similarly improve quantum generalization.
