# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Symmetric, paired single-qubit rotations with shared parameters  
  • As in Gen 9 (“8-Qubit Reuploading Variational Classifier”, raw 0.86, test 0.88, gap 0.06) and Gen 4 (“butterfly_tied_ansatz”, raw 0.81), tying RY/RY, RZ/RZ, RX/RX on mirrored wires yields zero std in accuracies and reliable convergence.  
- Modest circuit depth (~99) with three feature re-uploads and two ansatz repeats  
  • Depth 99/gate 348 (Gen 9, Gen 4, seed) balances expressivity and generalization—test ≥ 0.87 with gap ≤ 0.06.  
- Gain/bias readout calibration on mean-pooled Z expectations  
  • Both the current best (Gen 9) and Gen 6 incorporate trainable gain/bias after averaging qubit outputs, stabilizing predictions and reducing validation/std fluctuations.  
- Parameter reuse via shared keys across blocks  
  • Mirrored parameter keys (e.g. “ry_a” on wires 0&4) cut N_params_per_block by ~30% versus fully independent layers (Gen 10), preserving test accuracy while improving training speed and slightly narrowing the gap.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Deep, overparameterized multi-scale ansätze  
  • Gen 7 (“multi_scale_entanglement_network”) achieved 0.96 train but only 0.84 test (gap 0.12, raw 0.75) at depth 93/gates 300; added entanglement hierarchy overfits.  
- Circular CNOT ladders with skip-connection entanglers  
  • Gen 6 (“circular_hea_ansatz”) ballooned to depth 120 with 98 params, yet test 0.72 (gap 0.10, raw 0.69), indicating poor expressivity-to-depth ratio.  
- Dense CZ connectivity without parameter tying  
  • Gen 10 (“phase_ladder_symmetry”) used ring + cross CZs twice per upload (146 params), but only raw 0.78 and gap 0.08, underscoring diminishing returns from connectivity alone.  
- Excessive gate count (>300) without strategic parameter sharing  
  • All models with gate_count ≥ 300 but >140 free parameters failed to exceed test 0.88 or reduce gap below ~0.06, highlighting cost of unguided depth increases.

## Implementation Insights

- ANSATZ_SPEC as a list of gate-dicts with centralized param-key ordering  
  • Enables compute of PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK automatically via `validate_ansatz_spec`, and straightforward shared-parameter indexing in `apply_ansatz_block`.  
- Shared parameter keys drive parameter reuse  
  • Current best (seed/Gen 9) ties each “ry_*”, “rz_*”, “rx_*” across two wires, halving single-qubit rotation parameters per block while retaining ansatz expressivity.  
- Autograd QNode on “default.qubit” with backprop and pnp arrays  
  • Facilitates batch evaluation of IsingZZ feature maps and mean-pooling of Z-expectations, delivering consistent gradient flow and convergence (~150 steps).  
- Trainable gain/bias applied to mean-Z output  
  • Calibrates raw outputs post-circuit (as in Gen 9 and Gen 6), improving small-data generalization and stabilizing loss landscapes.

## Performance Analysis

- Best raw score plateau at ~0.86 for depth ≈ 99, params ≈ 120  
  • Seed/Gen 9 achieves raw 0.86 (test 0.88), matching Gen 5’s 0.85 but with 74 params vs. 220+, underscoring efficiency of shared-parameter symmetry.  
- Depth vs. generalization gap  
  • Depth 75–105 (Gen 2 seed, Gen 4, Gen 9) yields gap 4–6%; increasing depth to 120+ (Gen 6, Gen 10) drives gap to 8–10%.  
- Parameter count vs. test accuracy  
  • Shrinking blocks from 146→122 params (Gen 10→Gen 9) boosts raw 0.78→0.86 and narrows gap by 2 points, illustrating overparameterization drag.  
- Gate-count sweet spot ~300–350  
  • Gate_count ~300–350 (Gen 4, Gen 9) sustains test ≥ 0.87; neither raising gates beyond 350 nor adding entanglers materially improves accuracy, consistent with diminishing returns.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend parameter tying across all ansatz blocks  
   Instead of using block‐specific offsets in `apply_ansatz_block`, collapse the offset logic so that every upload/repeat reads from the same `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`. Concretely, remove the `block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` offset and adjust `N_PARAMS` to `N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK + N_READOUT_PARAMS`. This global reuse builds on the proven efficiency of mirrored keys (Gen 9) and should further halve the total trainable weights, speeding up convergence and—if expressivity is preserved—narrowing the generalization gap.  

2. Introduce trainable CRZ cross-couplings in place of fixed CZs  
   Swap the four cross-link CZ gates (`[0,4]`,`[1,5]`,`[2,6]`,`[3,7]`) in `ANSATZ_SPEC` for parameterized CRZs, e.g.  
     {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[0,4],"param":"crz_a"}  
     …  
   and tie each `crz_*` across mirrored wires. This injects just four additional angles of entanglement (versus zero in the current best), harnessing the low-param two-qubit rotations that in prior gens (e.g. Gen 5) lifted validation accuracy without exceeding the ~350-gate sweet spot.  

3. Rebalance feature uploads and ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3)  
   Keep the total of six ansatz blocks but change to two data re-uploads and three repeats per upload. Update the constants at the top and let the existing loop structure distribute blocks accordingly. This preserves the optimal depth (~99) and gate count (~348) while shifting more variational depth between each upload, testing whether denser parameterized mixing per feature pass can push test accuracy above 0.88.  

4. Add per-half readout gain and bias for localized calibration  
   Replace the single global gain/bias in `predict_batch` with two sets—one for qubits 0–3 and one for qubits 4–7. Compute `raw1 = mean(z[0:4])`, `raw2 = mean(z[4:8])`, then apply `gain1*(raw1‐bias1)` and `gain2*(raw2‐bias2)` before averaging. This introduces only two extra parameters (bringing N_READOUT_PARAMS to 4) and leverages the stability of mean-pooled calibration (as in Gen 9) with finer local correction to reduce prediction variance.  

5. Diversify the post-entanglement single-qubit layer with axis mixing  
   In the final rotation layer of each block (currently eight RX gates), alternate between RX and RY on the mirrored pairs: use RX on wires (0,4) and (2,6) but switch to RY on (1,5) and (3,7), all with shared parameters across each pair. Modify the corresponding entries in `ANSATZ_SPEC` (e.g. change `"gate":"RX"` to `"gate":"RY"` for the selected wires) while keeping the same param keys. This axis heterogeneity boosts expressivity at zero extra param cost, echoing the balanced rotations that converged reliably in past gens.
