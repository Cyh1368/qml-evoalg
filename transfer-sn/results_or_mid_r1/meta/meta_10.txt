# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mirror-symmetric CZ-RY ansatz blocks with both cross-pair and local CZ entanglement, as in the current best program (combined_score_raw = 0.88, depth = 87, n_params = 98) and Gen 2, consistently achieve top performance. Sharing RY/RZ parameters across symmetric qubit pairs cuts the number of trainable angles in half while preserving expressivity.  
- Three feature re-uploads × two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2) form a robust depth/expressivity sweet spot used by all the highest scorers (Gen 2: 0.88, Gen 8: 0.87, current best: 0.88).  
- Incorporating trainable readout gain and bias in every high-performer (current best converged at step 570; Gen 8 converged ~270 steps) stabilizes the calibrated mean-Z measurement and accelerates convergence.  
- Balanced layering of single-qubit rotations and two-qubit CZ gates—specifically 8 RY → 8 CZ → 8 RZ → 8 RY per block—yields high expressivity with moderate depth, outperforming heavier entanglement meshes.  

Ineffective Approaches  
- Replacing CZ entanglers with parametric two-qubit rotations degrades generalization and accuracy:  
  • Gen 6 (RY-CRZ-RX ansatz) achieved only combined_score_raw = 0.78 with a 0.12 gap despite 146 parameters.  
  • Gen 3 (CRY-only entanglers) scored 0.81 over depth = 81.  
- Dense CZ meshes without parameter sharing (Gen 5’s full 8-qubit CZ mesh, 146 params, 300 gates) reached only 0.84 combined, showing that more gates/params alone fail to translate into accuracy gains.  
- Ring-only entanglement patterns (Gen 4’s CRX ring, depth = 114) yielded combined 0.86, underperforming cross+ring CZ layouts at lower depth.  
- Circuits with gate counts >250 but lacking symmetrical parameter reduction never surpass a combined score of 0.86, indicating diminishing returns on brute-force entanglement.  

Implementation Insights  
- Using dict.fromkeys on PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK with an offset index in apply_ansatz_block cleanly maps each gate’s parameter to its place in the flattened parameter vector for any N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS.  
- The current best program defines N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK = 16 by grouping qubits into mirror pairs (e.g. ry_in_0 on qubits 0 and 7), reducing block-level parameters from 32 to 16 and lowering overall circuit params to 98.  
- circuit_metadata via pennylane.queuing.AnnotatedQueue enables precise on-the-fly computation of circuit depth and gate count, crucial for correlating resource usage with empirical performance.  
- Separating quantum circuit parameters and classical readout calibration (gain, bias) in predict_batch isolates their gradient paths, leading to more stable optimization.  

Performance Analysis  
- The best pipelines cluster around ~98 trainable parameters and depth ≈ 87: current best and Gen 2 both hit combined_score_raw = 0.88 under those specs, while shallower (depth < 80) or deeper (>100) circuits fall short.  
- Gen 8 (“8-Qubit Ring-Entangled Binary Classifier”) at 74 params/depth 114 scores a competitive combined 0.87 but trades depth for fewer params; its slightly lower combined highlights the benefit of balanced depth/param sharing in the best program.  
- All top-performers exhibit a generalization gap of ~0.06–0.07, suggesting the 3×2 layout plus early stopping effectively controls overfitting without sacrificing expressivity.  
- Parameter efficiency remains low (~0.01) even at optimal settings, indicating further parameter pruning might be possible, but no existing program outperforms the 98-param configuration.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace one fixed CZ per entangling mesh with a sparse, trainable CRZ. For example, in each 8-CZ block swap the central CZ on wires [0,1] (and its mirror [6,7]) to a CRZ(angle) with its own parameter. This leverages the success of mid-layer RZ rotations to give fine-grained control over the strongest correlations without inflating depth, directly building on the best program’s CZ-RY balance.  

2. Prepend each feature-upload cycle with a shared RZ rotation on every qubit. In `circuit_z_expectations`, immediately before `feature_map(records)` insert a layer of RZ gates whose angles are trainable and shared across mirror pairs (e.g. “rz_pre_0” on qubits 0&7, etc.). This mirrors the proven benefit of mid-ansatz RZ calibration, offering an early corrective phase before data encoding and smoothing the loss landscape.  

3. Test an extra data re-upload (N_UPLOADS=4, N_REPEATS=2) while preserving the 16-parameter block via the same mirror-pair sharing. This expands the 3×2 depth/expressivity sweet spot to a 4×2 layout—totaling eight ansatz blocks—so you can probe whether more frequent data injections further boost validation accuracy, as suggested by the optimal balance around depth ≈ 87.  

4. Alternate CZ connectivity between ansatz blocks to diversify entanglement pathways. In even-indexed blocks keep the cross-pair + local CZ mesh, and in odd blocks switch to the complementary pairing [0,5],[1,4],[2,7],[3,6] plus its adjacent local CZs. By cycling two mirror-symmetric entanglement patterns you retain gate count/depth but broaden the circuit’s ability to capture varied qubit correlations, capitalizing on CZ efficiency.  

5. Introduce a single controlled symmetry break in the shared RY_out layer. Choose one mirror key—e.g. split “ry_out_0” into “ry_out_0a” on qubit 0 and “ry_out_0b” on qubit 7—adding exactly one extra parameter. This minimal untie tests whether a tiny boost in parameter uniqueness can lift accuracy while largely preserving the compact, high-efficiency structure that underpins the current best program.
