# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Sparse “4 CRZ + 2 CZ” ansatz blocks sandwiched between RY–…–RZ layers drove the biggest jump in combined score.  
  • Gen 5 (“four_CRZ_two_CZ_per_block”, score 1.39, depth 90, gates 240) → Gen 10 (“optimized_crz_cz_block,” score 1.46, depth 78, gates 216).  
  • The current best (Gen 10) uses exactly 4 parametrized CRZs on (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7) plus 2 fixed CZ cross-links, boosting expressivity while slashing depth.

- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with exactly two ansatz repeats remained essential.  
  • Gen 0, Gen 2, Gen 5, and Gen 10 all use N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2 and achieved perfect accuracy with zero generalization gap.

- RY–entangler–RZ “sandwich” ordering accelerated convergence (~240 steps vs. >450).  
  • Present in Gen 5 (≈210 steps) and the Gen 10 seed (convergence_step_mean = 240), compared to CRY-heavy designs in Gen 7/Gen 2 (~450+ steps).

- Jointly training a readout gain and bias stabilized early training.  
  • All top programs (Gen 5 score 1.39, Gen 10 score 1.46) initialize gain = 1, bias = 0 and include them in the Adam optimization.

Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterized, uniform CRY-rich ansatz blocks did not improve scores but increased depth/params.  
  • Gen 7 (“CRY-entangled”, 188 params, depth 87) plateaued at combined ≈ 0.97.  
  • Gen 2 (“cr y heavy”, 170 params, depth 93) also stayed near 0.97–0.99.

- Simple parameter sharing without trimming depth left circuits too deep.  
  • Gen 11 (“ry_cz_crz_hybrid_ansatz”, 98 params, depth 93) achieved 0.99 combined score but did not match Gen 10’s 1.46 despite fewer params.

- Adding extra ansatz blocks beyond two repeats per upload yielded diminishing returns.  
  • Gen 15 (“increase_entanglement_connectivity”, 120 params, 6 blocks) hit combined 0.99 but needed ~240 steps—no accuracy gain over two repeats.

- Dense CZ meshes with no matching parametrized rotations underperformed.  
  • Gen 14 (146 params, depth 91, gates 282) only reached combined 0.96 and showed a small generalization gap of 0.01.

Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC plus a key_to_position lookup streamlines pruning to the minimal 4 CRZ+2 CZ pattern.  
  • The Gen 10 seed’s apply_ansatz_block function uses a parameter‐key index map to vectorize gate application and share parameters across repeats.

- Fixed IsingZZ feature map on 28 binary inputs keeps depth low while preserving expressivity.  
  • All top performers (Gen 5, Gen 10) use the same FEATURE_PAIRS encoding and three uploads.

- Parameter initialization in (–0.05, 0.05) with gain=1, bias=0 yields stable, rapid convergence.  
  • Adopted in Gen 5 and the current best (Gen 10) and confirmed by convergence_step_mean = 240.

- Leveraging qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue and tape.graph.get_depth provides reliable depth and gate‐count measurement.  
  • Circuit_metadata in the seed program reproduces depth = 78, gate_count = 216 for the Gen 10 pattern.

Performance Analysis

- Depth reduction from ~90–102 down to 78 correlated with the largest combined‐score improvement:  
  • Gen 5 (depth 90 → score 1.39) → Gen 10 (depth 78 → score 1.46).

- Gate count trimming (≈300→216) tracked closely with score gains once perfect accuracy was already reached.  
  • Compare Gen 11 (gates 294, combined 0.99) vs. Gen 10 (gates 216, combined 1.46).

- Parameter budgets around 120–124 struck the optimal expressivity/efficiency balance.  
  • Gen 5: 124 params → 1.39; Gen 10: 122 params → 1.46; dropping to 74 params (Gen 12) fell back to 0.97.

- Convergence steps remained ≈210–240 for sparse‐entangler designs versus >450 for deeper, over‐parameterized circuits.  
  • The Gen 10 seed’s convergence_step_mean = 240 confirms rapid training with the optimized block.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Cyclically shift your sparse‐CRZ connectivity across the three feature‐uploads.  In your ANSATZ_SPEC, keep exactly four CRZs per block but on upload i rotate their wire pairs by i qubits (e.g. upload 0: (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7); upload 1: (1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,0); upload 2: (2,3),(4,5),(6,7),(0,1)).  This preserves the RY–CRZ×4–CZ×2–RZ block depth 78/gate count 216 while enriching entanglement patterns and may capture cross‐feature correlations without extra cost (evidence: sparse targeted CRZ drove the Gen 10 jump to 1.46).  

2. Share each CRZ’s parameter across the two repeats and use the freed budget to insert one extra CZ per block.  In key_to_position, map both repeats of, say, “crz_0_1” to the same index, cutting N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK by 4, then extend ANSATZ_SPEC with an additional fixed CZ (e.g. wires [2,6]) after the existing two.  Prior runs tying CRZ across repeats preserved expressivity while trimming parameters, and minimal extra CZs have boosted connectivity in earlier mutations.  

3. Convert one of the two fixed cross‐links into a trainable CRZ and rebalance single‐qubit rotations to keep N_PARAMS ≈ 122.  Replace, for example, CZ on wires [1,5] with {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[1,5],"param":"crz_1_5"}, then remove the two least‐sensitive RZs (identified by small gradients) to offset the +1 parameter.  Sparse parametrized entanglers drove the performance jump in Gen 10, so adding one more CRZ on a cross‐link (while budget‐neutral) may lift expressivity further.  

4. Introduce per‐upload trainable scaling factors for the FEATURE_SCALE in your fixed IsingZZ map.  Replace the constant FEATURE_SCALE with a small vector [s₀,s₁,s₂], one scale per upload (initialized at π/2), and multiply each IsingZZ by its sᵢ.  This lets the circuit adapt feature‐map strength during training—leveraging the proven expressivity of three uploads—without altering ansatz depth.  

5. Add lightweight inter–upload CZ entanglers to carry entanglement between successive feature‐map blocks.  After each feature_map and before the first RY of that upload, insert a fixed CZ between a pair of qubits (e.g. [0,7]) to link upload i–1 and i.  A single extra CZ per upload boundary (total +2 CZs) slightly raises depth but maintains sparse connectivity and may improve cross‐upload coherence, building on the success of minimal CZ cross‐links in Gen 10.
