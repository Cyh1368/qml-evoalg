# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parameterized two-qubit rotations in a ring topology combined with fixed CZ entanglers (Gen 1 “compact_crx_ansatz”):  
  • Achieved perfect train/validation/test accuracy (1.00) and combined_score_raw 0.99 with only 98 parameters, depth 130, gate_count 228.  
  • Converged in ~150 optimizer steps, outperforming deeper or more heavily parameterized variants.

- Three feature-upload rounds interleaved with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2):  
  • Consistently delivered zero generalization gap in Gen 1 and Gen 4, indicating expressivity without overfitting.  
  • Enables mid‐circuit re-encoding of data to refresh feature information before each parametrized block.

- Small readout calibration (trainable gain & bias) on the mean Pauli-Z measurement:  
  • Stabilized output scale across splits, contributing to flawless classification in top models (Gen 1 & Gen 4).

- Balanced circuit depth (~130) with a mix of single- and two-qubit gates:  
  • Gen 1’s moderate depth achieved perfect accuracy, whereas shallower (Gen 0 depth 99) or deeper (Gen 5 depth 81 but simpler ansatz) circuits scored lower (0.97).

## Ineffective Approaches

- Aggressive parameter sharing (Gen 3 “efficient_ansatz_with_parameter_sharing”):  
  • Reduced to 44 parameters but fell to combined_score 0.81, validation/test accuracy 0.87, generalization gap 0.06.  
  • Shared rotations limited expressivity and entanglement diversity.

- Purely single-qubit parametrization or minimal two-qubit coupling (Gen 5 “paired_ring_ansatz”):  
  • Despite only 48 circuit parameters, validation accuracy dipped to 0.98, convergence slowed (~750 steps), combined_score_raw 0.97.  
  • Insufficient two-qubit parameterization hampered long-range correlation learning.

- Excessive parameter counts without strategic layout (Gen 4 “enhanced_parametrized_entanglement”):  
  • 158 parameters delivered the same perfect accuracies (combined_score_raw 0.99) but required ~210 steps and 306 gates—40% more resources than Gen 1 for no gain in performance or efficiency.

## Implementation Insights

- Automating parameter indexing via ANSATZ_SPEC and a key-to-position map simplifies block replication across uploads/repeats and avoids manual offset errors.  
- Ring-connected CRX gates (one per adjacent qubit pair) provide compact, uniform two-qubit entanglement—each block adds only 8 two-qubit parameters yet supports full-circuit correlation.  
- Fixed IsingZZ feature map with scale π/2 over three uploads ensures robust data embedding; combining it with the CRX‐ring ansatz yields high expressivity with modest depth.  
- Separating circuit parameters (N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS) from readout parameters (gain/bias) clarifies optimizer duties and stabilizes final predictions.

## Performance Analysis

- Top models (Gen 1 & Gen 4) both hit 1.00 test accuracy and zero generalization gap, but Gen 1’s 98 parameters vs Gen 4’s 158 translate to 40% parameter reduction for identical performance.  
- Depth vs accuracy: increasing depth from 99 (Gen 0) to ~130 (Gen 1) corresponded to score boost from 0.97 to 0.99, whereas further depth reduction (Gen 5 depth 81) lost accuracy.  
- Training speed: Gen 1 converged in ~150 steps, Gen 4 in ~210, Gen 5 in ~750—highlighting the impact of ansatz expressivity on optimization speed.  
- Parameter efficiency remained low (~0.01) across high-accuracy models, indicating ample circuit capacity relative to dataset complexity; efficiency gains require more aggressive parameter reuse without sacrificing expressivity.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate two-qubit rotation axes in the ring ansatz  
   Mutate ANSATZ_SPEC so that even-indexed blocks use CRX on each ring edge and odd-indexed blocks use CRY (wires [i, i+1]). You still have one parameter per edge per block, so total depth/gate count stays ≈130/228, but you introduce axis diversity shown in other domains to speed convergence and enrich expressivity without blowing up parameter count.  

2. Replace fixed CZ entanglers with trainable IsingZZ or CRZ gates  
   Swap each fixed CZ in ANSATZ_SPEC for a parameterized two-qubit gate—e.g. IsingZZ with its own param key or CRZ—and include those in PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK. This adds ~8 trainable coupling strengths per block but directly builds on the success of both fixed IsingZZ feature maps and CRX rings, giving the optimizer more flexibility to shape long-range correlations.  

3. Insert an RZ layer after every CRX ring within each block  
   Extend each block in ANSATZ_SPEC by appending single-qubit RZ rotations on all 8 wires (with new param keys rz_0…rz_7). This modest 8-parameter expansion per block breaks residual symmetries left by RY+CRX, leverages the proven benefit of single-qubit rotations, and keeps total depth near the Gen 1 sweet spot.  

4. Share block parameters across repeats to boost efficiency  
   Modify block indexing so that within each upload a single set of ANSATZ_SPEC parameters is reused across repeats (i.e. block_index = upload_index). This slashes N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS from 98 to 49 while preserving 3-upload structure. Test whether the compact CRX+CZ ring retains zero generalization gap when repeats share weights—an approach midway between Gen 1’s full parametrization and Gen 3’s over-sharing.  

5. Add per-qubit readout calibration instead of a single global bias/gain  
   In predict_batch, replace the scalar gain/bias with 8 gains and 8 biases (one per qubit). Apply gain_q*(z_q–bias_q) before averaging. This extends the small readout calibration that stabilized Gen 1 to a per-wire level, giving the model finer control over each qubit’s contribution and potentially improving margin stability without affecting circuit structure.
