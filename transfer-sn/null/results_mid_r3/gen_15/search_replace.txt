<DESCRIPTION>
The current program achieves perfect test accuracy (1.00) and maximal combined score (1.49) with a cyclic CRY entangler on all 8 qubits, using 8 independent parameters (cry_0 through cry_7). However, the ring structure exhibits rotational symmetry: opposite edge pairs (e.g., [0,1] and [4,5], or [1,2] and [5,6]) play structurally analogous roles.

By collapsing symmetric CRY edges into shared parameters—specifically grouping opposite-edge pairs—we reduce the per-block parameter count from 16 to 12 (8 RY + 4 CRY instead of 8 RY + 8 CRY). This cuts the total parameter budget from 98 to 74 while preserving the cyclic ring topology and the expressivity benefits of parameter tying across symmetric edges.

This edit tests the hypothesis that parameter sharing at symmetric positions maintains perfect accuracy at lower computational cost, potentially improving convergence speed (the current program takes 450 steps vs. 180 in a denser architecture) and parameter efficiency.
