<DESCRIPTION>
The original ansatz used a layer of RY gates, followed by a layer of RZ gates, a linear chain of CZ gates, and another layer of RZ gates. Because CZ gates are diagonal in the computational basis, they commute with RZ gates. This means the two independent RZ layers separated by CZ gates were mathematically equivalent to a single RZ layer, introducing redundant parameters that flattened the loss landscape and worsened parameter efficiency.

To solve this and improve both parameter efficiency and trainability:
1. We remove the redundant RZ layer, effectively reducing the parameter count per block from 24 to 16.
2. We replace the linear chain of CZ gates with two parallelized, alternating layers of CNOT gates (a "brick-layer" or "ring" topology). CNOTs don't commute with RZ gates, producing much richer interference and faster entanglement spread.
3. The alternating CNOT layers drastically reduce circuit depth, improving trainability and keeping the optimization trajectory shorter and more stable.

This improves overall test accuracy and parameter efficiency by structurally preventing over-parameterization while boosting the circuit's expressivity.
</DESCRIPTION>
