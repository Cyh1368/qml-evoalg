Looking at the performance data, the current design (16 params/block, 98 total) achieves val_acc=1.00 but is parameter-heavy, while the 56-param design achieves 0.99. The key insight is that RZ commutes with CZ, so a *shared* RZ between CZ layers provides no interleaving benefit. However, *two* shared RZ groups (even/odd qubits) break this commutation, creating differential phases within every entangled pair while using only 2 RZ params instead of 8.

rz_even_odd_split_interleave
</NAME>

<DESCRIPTION>
Replace the 8 individual RZ parameters with 2 shared groups: even-indexed qubits (0,2,4,6) share one RZ parameter and odd-indexed qubits (1,3,5,7) share another. This gives 10 unique params per block × 6 blocks = 60 + 2 = 62 total parameters.

Key physics insight: After CZ(even) on pairs (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7), applying different RZ angles to even vs odd qubits creates a differential phase within each entangled pair. This is the minimal differentiation needed to break Z-basis commutation while preserving the interleaving structure that achieved val_acc=1.00. Within every CZ pair (both even and odd layers), the two qubits receive different RZ angles, maximizing expressivity per parameter.

This should maintain val_acc=1.00 (unlike the fully-shared 1-param RZ in the 56-param design that only got 0.99) while cutting total params by 37% (from 98 to 62), improving parameter efficiency and likely convergence speed.
</DESCRIPTION>
