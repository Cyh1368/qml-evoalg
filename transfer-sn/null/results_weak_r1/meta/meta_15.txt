# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Local CZ backbone augmented with long-range CRZ couplings drove the jump to 0.99 raw score.  
  • 8-Qubit Ansatz with Local and Long-Range Entanglement (Gen 12, 158 params, depth 136, raw 0.99, perfect 1.00/1.00/1.00 accuracies) outperformed the 92-param shared-CRZ design (Gen 8, raw 0.90) and matched the 122-param CZ-CRZ baseline (Gen 14, raw 0.99) by covering both nearest-neighbor and mid-range correlations.  
- Alternating RY single-qubit layers before and after entanglers yields rapid, stable convergence.  
  • Gen 12 converged in ~210 steps; Gen 14 in ~240 steps—both faster than deeper RY-RZ-CZ-CRY stacks—and exhibited zero generalization gap.  
- Three uploads × two repeats re-upload pattern consistently maintains expressivity without runaway depth.  
  • All top-performers (Gen 7/12/14) employ 3×2 layout to balance resource use and classification power.  
- Gate-mix balance (~30% single-qubit, ~70% two-qubit) maximizes expressivity per parameter.  
  • Gen 12’s 288-gate circuit and Gen 14’s 252-gate circuit hit perfect accuracy with mid-range parameter counts.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization beyond ~140 trainable parameters yields diminishing returns.  
  • Evolved Ansatz Classifier (Gen 11, 206 params, 360 gates) plateaued at raw 0.98, matching perfect accuracies but with lower parameter efficiency and no score gain.  
- Excessive parameter sharing or pruning single-qubit layers under-expresses the model.  
  • Shared-parameter CRZ classifier (Gen 8, 92 params) never exceeded raw 0.90 despite low depth.  
- High two-qubit-gate counts without strategic CRZ placement fails to boost raw score.  
  • Multi-Upload Controlled Rotation Ansatz (Gen 13, 152 params, 360 gates) reached perfect accuracies but capped at raw 0.98, showing that simply adding gates without targeted long-range rotations is insufficient.  
- Reducing depth below ~80 by pruning entanglers slows convergence and drops combined scores under 0.98 (from previous sweeps).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Decoupled ANSATZ_SPEC + validate/apply pattern enables painless addition of CRZ links.  
  • Gen 12/14 simply append CRZ entries to the JSON-style spec without touching control flow.  
- Ordered parameter-key indexing scales block parameter counts from 122 → 158 seamlessly.  
  • The ordered_unique_keys mapping ensures correct parameter assignment across uploads and repeats.  
- PennyLane AdamOptimizer with backprop, epoch-wise validation, early stopping (patience=75) and best-weight restore delivers stable convergence in ~210–240 steps.  
  • The same training loop underpins Gen 7, Gen 12, and Gen 14’s zero generalization gaps.  
- Hardware-efficient CZ ladder plus CRZ cross-links covers both local and nonlocal correlations in just two entangling layers per block, minimizing depth for maximum expressivity.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Mid-range parameter counts (~120–158) hit the performance sweet spot.  
  • Gen 14 (122 params) and Gen 12 (158 params) both achieve raw 0.99, while Gen 11 (206 params) saw no improvement over 0.98.  
- Strategic gate-count increase yields outsized score gains.  
  • ShinkaEvolve baseline (Gen 8, 222 gates, raw 0.90) → Gen 14 (252 gates, +0.09) and Gen 12 (288 gates, +0.09) demonstrates that targeted CRZ insertion outperforms undirected gate proliferation.  
- Entanglement richness vs. depth trade-off: deeper Gen 12 (136 depth) converged faster (210 steps) than shallower Gen 14 (87 depth, 240 steps), indicating that more uniform entanglement can accelerate learning.  
- Parameter efficiency (~test_accuracy / n_params ≈ 0.01) remains stable across top circuits, defining an efficiency ceiling for the 28-feature dataset.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Swap select backbone CZs for parameterized CRZs to boost local flexibility. For example, in each block’s ANSATZ_SPEC replace the CZs on wires [2,3] and [6,7] with  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[2,3],"param":"crz_back_b0"}` and  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[6,7],"param":"crz_back_b1"}` (and similarly indexed per block).  
   This adds just four extra two-qubit parameters per block directly into the hardware-efficient ladder and tests whether local entanglement tuning further raises raw score or accelerates convergence.  

2. Append a second long-range CRZ layer on anti-diagonal pairs to deepen mid-range correlations. After the existing 10 CRZ entries in ANSATZ_SPEC, add  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[0,7],"param":"crz2_b0"}`,  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[1,2],"param":"crz2_b1"}`,  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[4,3],"param":"crz2_b2"}`,  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[5,6],"param":"crz2_b3"}`  
   per block. This nudges the two-qubit : single-qubit mix toward ~70% and probes whether denser long-range links push raw above 0.99.  

3. Make the feature-map scale trainable per upload to adapt encoding strength. Replace the scalar FEATURE_SCALE with  
   `FEATURE_SCALE_U = pnp.zeros(3, requires_grad=True)`  
   and in `feature_map` use `FEATURE_SCALE_U[upload_index]` instead of π/2. You only add three parameters but give the optimizer explicit control over encoding amplitude per upload, a trick known to speed convergence without changing circuit depth.  

4. Inject a lightweight RZ “correction” layer after the second RY in each block to complete per-qubit SU(2) coverage. In ANSATZ_SPEC, append for each qubit q  
   `{"gate":"RZ","wire":q,"param":"rz_l3_w{q}"}`  
   (indexed per block). This adds eight single-qubit params per block and may sharpen decision boundaries with minimal extra depth, building on the proven RY–entangler–RY pattern.  

5. Diversify two-qubit axes by alternating CRZ and CRY on long-range links across blocks. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, for even block indices keep CRZ on wires [0,2],[1,3],… and for odd blocks swap those four gates to CRY with new keys like `cry_l1_w02`. Axis mixing has driven zero generalization gap in prior variants and could tighten convergence or edge test accuracy higher.
