# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity-based parameter sharing on single-qubit rotations  
  • Current best (Gen 14 “parameter_efficient_ring_ansatz”): even/odd sharing on RY, RZ, RX layers reduces N_PARAMS to 44 while achieving combined_score_raw = 0.89 (train/test = 0.95/0.91), parameter_efficiency = 0.02.  
  • Earlier Gen 3 (“parity_tied_ring” with 50 params) hit combined_score_raw = 0.89 and gap ≈ 0.02, demonstrating that reducing independent rotation keys preserves accuracy.  

- Ring-CZ backbone plus a single global CRZ parameter  
  • Gen 14 and Gen 10 (“shared_parity_crossentangler,” 50 params) both use a CZ ring for local entanglement plus one shared CRZ across four long-range links—yielding high expressivity (depth 126–132) without inflating parameter count.  
  • The shared long-range CRZ boosts combined_score_raw by ~0.01 over designs lacking it (e.g., simple ring-only blocks).  

- Three feature-map re-uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  • All top performers (Gens 3, 7, 10, 14) retain N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2. Gen 14 achieves zero std across seeds and stable gap (0.04), underscoring robustness of this layout.  

- Trainable gain/bias readout plus early stopping  
  • Present in all high-scoring circuits (Gens 3, 7, 10, 14, seed program), contributing ~0.02 uplift on validation/test accuracy and tighter generalization gaps (< 0.05).  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterized two-qubit controls without sharing  
  • Gen 6 (“hybrid_selective_share_ccontrol,” 122 params) combined_score_raw = 0.85, gap = 0.05; Gen 8 (“symmetric_brick_longrange,” 104 params) combined_score_raw = 0.70, gap = 0.11. Excess CRY/CRZ and unshared rotations increase parameters but hurt generalization.  

- Excessive independent single-qubit rotations  
  • Gen 13 (“mirrored_cry_hybrid_block,” 86 params) achieves only combined_score_raw = 0.85, gap = 0.06. Mirrored but unshared RY/RX keys offer no accuracy gain over parity-tied alternatives.  

- Deep circuits minus targeted sharing  
  • Depth ≥ 126 alone (e.g., Gen 5 analogues) does not guarantee performance; depth/gate_count are weak predictors absent structured parameter tying.  

- Modifying upload count or repeat structure  
  • Attempts to increase uploads or repeats beyond 3×2 did not yield measurable benefits and often slowed convergence.  

## Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC with validate_ansatz_spec + key_to_position  
  • Enables concise parity-sharing definitions (even/odd keys) and automatic mapping to circuit_params, as in the seed program.  

- List-of-dict block specification  
  • Rapidly swap between per-wire, pairwise, half-block, or parity-tied schemes without custom apply_ansatz_block code. All top circuits leverage this abstraction.  

- circuit_metadata via pennylane.queuing.AnnotatedQueue  
  • Automates depth and gate_count extraction, guiding trade-offs between expressivity and resource cost.  

- Vectorized QNode with batched IsingZZ feature_map  
  • Current best program fuses batch feature encoding and expectation stacking for efficient gradient evaluation under backprop.  

## Performance Analysis

- Parameter count vs. combined_score_raw  
  • 44 params (Gen 14) → 0.89; 50 params (Gen 10) → 0.88; 56 params (Gen 12) → 0.87; 86 params (Gen 13) → 0.85; 104+ params (Gens 6, 8) → ≤ 0.85. Lower N_PARAMS with structured sharing consistently yields top scores.  

- Generalization gap  
  • Parity-shared/ring-CZ programs gap ≈ 0.02–0.04 (Gens 3, 7, 10, 14); unshared or over-parameterized designs gap ≥ 0.05 (Gens 6, 8, 13).  

- Depth and gate_count  
  • Effective designs cluster at depth 120–132, gate_count ≈ 250–336. Depth alone does not predict accuracy; the best program (Gen 14) uses depth 126, gate_count 300.  

- Convergence cost  
  • Gen 15 (even_odd_ring_cross, 26 params) converges in ~150 steps but plateaus at combined_score_raw = 0.89 with gap = 0.01. Gen 14 trades longer convergence (~750 steps) for minimal parameters (44) and matching combined score of 0.89. Convergence speed and parameter efficiency are inversely related in these topologies.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the global CRZ into two parity-shared keys.  
   Replace the single “crz_global” parameter with “crz_e” on long-range links from even controls (wires 0→4, 2→6) and “crz_o” on odd controls (1→5, 3→7). This adds only one extra parameter per block but leverages the proven parity-sharing boost to expressivity without blowing up N_PARAMS.  

2. Mirror long-range CRZ with a parity-shared CRX layer.  
   Immediately after your CRZ gates, insert CRX gates on the same wire pairs using a shared key “crx_global.” CRX+CRZ pairings have raised expressivity in other architectures, and parity-shared two-qubit rotations preserve parameter efficiency.  

3. Introduce one trainable bias per feature upload.  
   In the feature_map function, before each qml.IsingZZ call for upload u, add a scalar bias param “feat_bias_u” that shifts records[..., k] → records[..., k] + feat_bias_u. Analogous to your readout gain/bias trick, per-upload biases let the circuit adapt input scaling and have yielded ~0.01–0.02 accuracy improvements.  

4. Collapse the three single-qubit rotation layers into U3 parity-tied gates.  
   Instead of separate RY→RZ pre-layers and RX post-layers, apply U3(θ,ϕ,0) on each qubit, using two keys “u3_θ_e”/“u3_ϕ_e” on even wires and “u3_θ_o”/“u3_ϕ_o” on odds. This reduces depth and frees up two parameters per block that you can re-invest in extra entanglers.  

5. Alternate entanglement topology across repeats.  
   For each ansatz block index, keep your ring+global pattern on even blocks, but on odd blocks switch to a “star” motif: connect all qubits to qubit 0 via parity-shared CRZ (keys “star_e”/“star_o”). This diversity in connectivity has raised expressivity per gate in prior gens while maintaining structured parameter sharing.
