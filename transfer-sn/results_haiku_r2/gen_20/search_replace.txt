<DESCRIPTION>
The current program achieves 0.85 combined score with 68 parameters and shows a significant generalization gap (0.05) and high test loss (0.50). The prior programs achieved 0.89–0.90 with fewer parameters (44–50) and better generalization.

The recommendation to add variational readout rotations is well-motivated:

1. **Residual phase correction**: The gain–bias calibration (currently just 2 parameters) stabilizes the mean and scale of Z expectations, but cannot correct for residual phase offsets or asymmetries in the qubit measurement basis. A final layer of RY and RZ rotations on every qubit before measurement can capture these corrections.

2. **Minimal overhead**: Adding only 2 new shared parameters (`ry_read` and `rz_read`) keeps the total parameter count low while providing a compact, expressive final transformation.

3. **Improved test accuracy**: By allowing the circuit to rotate the measurement basis adaptively, we can lift test accuracy beyond 0.90 and reduce the generalization gap.

4. **Efficiency**: This layer acts immediately before measurement, so it has direct influence on the readout without requiring deep circuit modifications.

The implementation adds a final layer of single-qubit rotations in the `circuit_z_expectations` function, applied after all ansatz blocks but before measuring PauliZ. These rotations are parameterized by two shared keys that are added to the ANSATZ_SPEC, making them part of the trainable circuit parameters.
