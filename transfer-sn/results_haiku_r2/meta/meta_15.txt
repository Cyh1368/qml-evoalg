# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three IsingZZ feature‐map re‐uploads with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2) plus a layered block structure (RY→RZ_pre→CZ chain→CRY/CRZ→RY_mid→CRX→RZ_post) yielded the top combined score of 0.90 in the “8-Qubit Re-uploading CZ-Rich Ansatz” (Gen 11), with train/val/test ≈0.92/0.92/0.90 and a small gap (0.02).  
- Aggressive parameter tying across wires (shared ry_0, rz_pre, cry_a, crz_b, ry_mid, crx_c, rz_post) cut the circuit to 44 trainable parameters while preserving expressivity—matching or exceeding higher-param designs.  
- Dense, yet structured CZ entanglement (nearest‐neighbor plus cross-block edges) balanced depth (144) and generalization, enabling convergence in ~180 steps.  
- Gain–bias readout calibration combined with best‐weight restoration and early stopping ensured zero run‐to‐run variance in accuracy (std=0.00).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized CRX/CRY/CRZ-rich blocks without sufficient symmetric tying (Gen 15, 206 params) led to a drop to 0.84 combined score and a larger generalization gap (0.07), despite high train accuracy (0.96).  
- Eliding mid‐block parametrized two-qubit rotations (as in Gen 9’s hybrid_prepost_rz_shared_params) capped the combined score at 0.88, indicating that simple RY+CZ chains alone lack sufficient expressivity.  
- Excessive depth reduction (depth < 105) undermines performance—models with shallower circuits consistently scored ≤0.89 despite good parameter efficiency.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC with a minimal, ordered set of shared parameter keys (7 keys) directly yields N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK=7, so that N_PARAMS=7×3×2+2=44. This compact key map drives both expressivity and efficiency.  
- validate_ansatz_spec guardrails ensure that gate dictionaries are correct before QNode construction, preventing silent mis-specs.  
- Using Pennylane’s backprop through qml.IsingZZ feature maps maintains gradient flow from data‐encoding layers into variational parameters, supporting stable Adam optimization.  
- circuit_metadata via qml.tape.QuantumScript extraction provides accurate depth and gate counts for performance logging.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The jump from combined score 0.88 (Gen 9) and 0.89 (Gen 10/13) to 0.90 (Gen 11) correlates with the introduction of mid-block CRY/CRZ and CRX layers in a shared-parameter layout.  
- Optimal parameter count clusters around 40–50 total parameters—below ~70–110—while deeper designs (>140 depth, >380 gates) achieve the highest scores and smallest generalization gaps.  
- Models with generalization gap ≤0.02 (Gen 11) consistently hit ≥0.90 combined, whereas gaps ≥0.04 (Gen 15) pull scores below 0.85.  
- Convergence speed trades off with expressivity: the CZ-rich Gen 11 model converges in ~180 steps versus ~540 steps for CZ-chain–only designs, indicating richer entanglement can accelerate training without hurting generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce parity–based RY sharing  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the single `ry_0` key with two keys—`ry_even` applied to qubits [0,2,4,6] and `ry_odd` applied to [1,3,5,7]. Generation 4 showed that halving RY parameters via even/odd sharing preserves test accuracy, and this split can boost expressivity over the current uniform‐RY while keeping N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK near 7.  

2. Hierarchical tying of two-qubit rotations  
   Instead of one `cry_a` and one `crz_b` for all four CRY/CRZ pairs, define `cry_local`/`crz_local` for nearest-neighbor edges and `cry_cross`/`crz_cross` for the cross-block edges. This coarse-grained parameter sharing tests whether distinguishing local vs. non-local entanglement pathways preserves the small generalization gap (≤0.02) seen in the best program while slightly increasing expressivity.  

3. Mid-block parametric IsingZZ layer  
   After the CZ chain and before the CRY/CRZ gates, insert a layer of `qml.IsingZZ(θ, wires=[a,b])` on the same FEATURE_PAIRS qubit pairs, all sharing a single new key `zz_mid`. Leveraging the stable gradient flow of IsingZZ (as in your feature map) enriches nonlinear interactions without a large parameter overhead, and may further accelerate convergence toward or beyond ~180 steps.  

4. Alternating CZ entanglement cycles  
   In even-indexed ansatz blocks, cyclically shift your nearest-neighbor+cross-block CZ pattern by one (e.g. include CZ(7,0), CZ(0,1)→CZ(1,2), …) while retaining the original pattern in odd blocks. This dynamic re-wiring explores broader connectivity without extra gates, building on the current CZ­-rich schedule that converged quickly and generalizes well.  

5. Variational readout rotations  
   Immediately before measuring PauliZ, append a final layer of single-qubit `RY(φ_read)` and `RZ(ψ_read)` on every wire, using two new shared keys `ry_read` and `rz_read`. Since gain–bias calibration already stabilized readout variance, this compact variational correction may capture residual phase offsets and lift test accuracy beyond 0.90 with minimal impact on depth.
