# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Four-key shared-parameter blocks (ry_init, rz_main, cry_nnn, ry_final) paired with a CZ ring and diagonal CZ backbone delivered top performance (test ≈ 0.93, gap = 0.01) in Gen 42, Gen 45, and Gen 46.  
- A single shared CRY parameter for all nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor two-qubit gates enriched connectivity “for free,” boosting expressivity per parameter (Gen 46 achieved 0.93 test accuracy with only 26 parameters).  
- The 3 re-uploads × 2 repeats scheduling consistently underpins all high-score classifiers (≥0.90 combined score), confirming its necessity for mapping 28-bit inputs (observed across Gen 41, Gen 42, Gen 43, Gen 45, Gen 46).  
- Structured entanglement—nearest-neighbor ring + cross-ring CZ—stabilizes gradient flow when combined with shared rotations, as seen in Gen 42 (depth ≈ 150, 348 gates, test 0.93) and Gen 45 (depth 150, 348 gates, test 0.93).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization (>38 trainable parameters) inflated depth/gate count (e.g., Gen 41: 44 params, 462 gates) but capped combined score at 0.89 and halved parameter efficiency (0.02 vs. 0.04).  
- Complex or tiered sharing schemes without strict per-block key limits bloated circuits without accuracy gains (Gen 41’s mid-block IsingZZ couplings plus extra rotations led to higher depth but no test-accuracy improvement).  
- Omitting or reducing CRY-based entanglement in favor of only IsingZZ or CRZ mid-blocks weakened expressivity, limiting combined scores to ≤0.89 (Gen 43 scored 0.89 with only CZ and shared rotations plus CRY dropped to nearest-neighbor only).  
- Circuit depths beyond ~160 without optimized sharing did not surpass the 0.93 test-accuracy ceiling (Gen 41’s deeper design underperformed relative to more compact 150-depth models).  

## Implementation Insights  
- The validate_ansatz_spec → PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK pattern reliably enforces a flat four-key design, preventing misalignment and off-by-one errors in apply_ansatz_block (used in all top designs).  
- Computing offsets as block_index × N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK with a key_to_position map yields consistent parameter slicing across uploads/repeats, ensuring correct gate-parameter binding.  
- Role-based parameter naming (ry_init, rz_main, cry_nnn, ry_final) caps N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK at four, maximizing readability and simplifying hyperparameter searches.  
- Sharing a single cry_nnn parameter across all CRY gates effectively densifies entanglement without increasing N_PARAMS, directly tying connectivity to performance (exemplified by Gen 46’s balanced depth/gate count trade-off).  

## Performance Analysis  
- The 26-parameter models (Gen 42, Gen 45, Gen 46) uniformly reached test_accuracy ≈ 0.93, generalization_gap = 0.01, and parameter_efficiency ≈ 0.04, marking the current performance ceiling.  
- Models with circuit depth ∼140–150 and gate counts ∼324–348 matched the test-accuracy of deeper (196-depth/396-gate) variants, indicating an optimal depth/gate “sweet spot” around 150/350.  
- Increasing parameters beyond 26 delivered no accuracy gains but worsened efficiency and widened the generalization gap (Gen 41’s 44-param ansatz vs. 26-param top performers).  
- Convergence correlated with final accuracy: top models converged in ~450 global steps (Gen 46) rather than the faster ~180 steps of lower-score circuits, mirroring earlier observations that moderate training durations enable richer loss minimization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Prune the next‐nearest‐neighbor CRY layer to hit the 150/350 “sweet spot.” In your ANSATZ_SPEC, remove the entire Layer 5b (the 8 CRY gates on wires [0,2], [2,4],…, [7,1]) so each block has only the 8 nearest‐neighbor CRY(cry_nnn) calls. This slims gate count from ~396 to ~348 while preserving the proven ring+diagonal CZ backbone and four‐parameter sharing that delivered 0.93 accuracy.  
2. Turn the fixed diagonal CZs into a shared‐parameter CRZ layer. In Layer 3, replace each CZ([i,j]) with CRZ(crz_diag) on the same qubit pairs and add “crz_diag” to PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK. This injects an extra mid‐block two‐qubit tunable gate—mirroring the success of cry_nnn—while only increasing N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK by one, boosting expressivity with minimal overhead.  
3. Make the feature‐map strength trainable via a global scale factor. Introduce one new scalar parameter “phi_scale” (outside of the ANSATZ_SPEC) and apply qml.IsingZZ(phi_scale * records[..., k], wires=[a,b]) in feature_map. By learning the optimal upload amplitude, you decouple data‐encoding from fixed π/2 and leverage the observed benefit of adjustable injection strength.  
4. Rebalance the upload/repeat schedule to 2 uploads × 3 repeats. Change N_UPLOADS=2 and N_REPEATS=3 so the circuit still has six variational blocks (same N_PARAMS and similar total depth) but tests whether deeper intra‐upload entanglement yields faster convergence or higher final accuracy. This directly probes the 3×2 vs. 2×3 trade-off around the established success of three uploads.  
5. Reorder and interleave your CZ entanglers for better parallelism. In ANSATZ_SPEC, split the nearest‐neighbor ring CZs into two commuting sublayers—e.g. first apply CZs on [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7], then on [1,2],[3,4],[5,6],[7,0]—and co‐schedule the diagonal CZs into the second sublayer. This reordering preserves full connectivity but can reduce circuit depth by ~20% through maximal parallel gate execution, edging you closer to the optimal depth/gate “sweet spot.”
