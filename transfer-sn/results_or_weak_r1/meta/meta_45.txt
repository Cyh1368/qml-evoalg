# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Coarse-grained two-bank rotation sharing  
  • Generation 44 (“8-Qubit Coarse-Grained CRZ Ansatz Classifier”) and Generation 43 (“Parameter-Efficient CRZ Ansatz Classifier”) both partitioned qubits 0–3 vs 4–7 for RY and pre/post RZ layers, yielding 50 params, depth 105, gate_count 300, and test_accuracy 0.95 (combined_raw 0.93, gen_gap 0.01).  
  • Generation 35 (“Shared-Parameter RY/RZ CZ 8-Qubit Classifier”) used a similar 2-bank sharing with 26 params and reached raw 0.91 with fast convergence (~180 steps).

- Learnable CRZ ring + chord entanglement  
  • Generation 44 replaced fixed CZ with shared-parameter CRZ “chi_ring” and “chi_chord,” cutting convergence to ~180 steps (vs ~270 without chords in Gen 42) and lifting combined_raw from 0.90 (Gen 42) to 0.93.  
  • Generation 38 (“Coarse-Grained 8-Qubit Evolve Ansatz Classifier”) first introduced trainable CRZ strengths to hit raw 0.93, demonstrating that parameterized entanglers outperform fixed CZ rings.

- Three uploads × two ansatz repeats in a depth-105 corridor  
  • All top scorers (Gen 35, Gen 38, Gen 43–44) used N_UPLOADS=3 and N_REPEATS=2, landing depth≈105, gate_count≈300, and combined_raw≥0.90 consistently.  

- Moderate parameter count (26–50) balances expressivity vs. overfitting  
  • 26 params → raw 0.91 (Gen 35, param_eff 0.04)  
  • 50 params → raw 0.93 (Gen 44, param_eff 0.02)  
  • 92 params → raw 0.88 (Gen 36, param_eff 0.01)

## Ineffective Approaches

- Overparameterized SU(2) blocks and fine-grained sharing  
  • Generation 36’s 92-parameter, fine-grained CRZ/CRX design reached only raw 0.88 (gen_gap 0.05) despite depth 111, indicating diminishing returns beyond ~50 params.  
  • Generation 32’s SU(2)-heavy 50-param ansatz also underperformed (raw 0.88) relative to coarser sharing designs.

- Substituting CRZ with CRX/CRY entanglers  
  • Generations 30 and 33 that experimented with CRX/CRY entanglement stalled at raw 0.84–0.89 and exhibited larger generalization gaps, showing CRZ is the more effective two-qubit rotation for this task.

- Omitting long-range chord gates  
  • Generation 31’s ring-only CZ pattern capped raw at 0.89; adding opposite-qubit chords was necessary to reliably push raw above 0.90 and accelerate convergence.

## Implementation Insights

- ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec for modular block definition  
  • All top programs (Gen 35, 38, 43–44) use a declarative ANSATZ_SPEC and a shared `validate_ansatz_spec` helper to extract `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`, enabling swaps between CZ/CRZ or addition/removal of chords without touching training code.

- Dictionary-based param-to-position mapping  
  • The seed program (Gen 44) builds `key_to_position = {key: idx}` from ordered unique keys, ensuring consistent indexing across N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS blocks and avoiding duplication bugs.

- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue + tape.graph.get_depth for metadata  
  • Automatic measurement of depth and gate count on the final candidate circuit confirmed placement in the empirically optimal “105±5” depth corridor.

- Separate readout calibration parameters  
  • All top performers appended 2 trainable readout params (gain, bias) to the circuit params, stabilizing output scaling without inflating the core ansatz.

## Performance Analysis

- Raw score uplift from 0.90→0.93 via learnable CRZ chords  
  • Gen 42 (shared RY/RZ + fixed CZ rings) scored combined_raw 0.90; swapping to parameterized “chi_ring”+“chi_chord” (Gen 44) raised raw to 0.93.

- Convergence step reduction: ~270→180  
  • Programs without chords (Gen 42) converged in ~270 optimizer steps; adding shared-parameter chords (Gen 44) consistently cut convergence to ~180 steps.

- Generalization gap minimized at ~0.01 with coarse sharing  
  • Gen 44 and Gen 43 both achieved gen_gap 0.01, whereas over-parameterized designs saw gaps up to 0.05.

- Parameter efficiency vs. accuracy trade-off  
  • 26 params (Gen 35) yielded param_eff 0.04 at raw 0.91.  
  • 50 params (Gen 44) yielded raw 0.93 but param_eff 0.02—demonstrating that slight efficiency loss can buy notable accuracy gains.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the shared‐ring CRZ into two per‐block parameters, `chi_ring_even` and `chi_ring_odd`, that control the two interleaved 4-edge rings independently. By doubling the ring degrees of freedom from 1→2 per block while keeping coarse‐grained sharing, you can test whether asymmetric ring tuning pushes test accuracy above 0.93 without blowing up N_PARAMS (~56 total).  

2. Inject a second “diagonal” chord CRZ pattern on wires [(0,5),(1,6),(2,7),(3,4)] with a new per‐block key `chi_diag`. This adds only one extra parameter per block (total ~56) yet enriches the long‐range connectivity beyond opposite‐qubit chords, probing whether this sparse, distance‐2 coupling further accelerates convergence or lifts raw score.  

3. Collapse all per‐block entangler strengths into global shared keys by removing `chi_ring` and `chi_chord` from `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` and defining them once at the top. This slashes 2×(N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS)=12 parameters from the ansatz (down to ~38 circuit params) to evaluate if tighter entangler sharing preserves the 0.93 test accuracy while improving parameter efficiency and reducing gen‐gap.  

4. Augment each block’s RY banks with small per‐block offset keys, e.g. add `Δry_h` and `Δry_h2` alongside the existing `ry_h`/`ry_h2` and apply RY as `RY(ry_h + Δry_h)` on wires 0–3 and `RY(ry_h2 + Δry_h2)` on wires 4–7. This hierarchical sharing retains the two‐bank structure (keeping coarse granularity) but lets each block locally bias its rotations—an intermediate relaxation that could capture subtle feature shifts without more than 2 extra params per block.  

5. In the ANSATZ_SPEC, reorder each block to apply “RZ_pre → CRZ rings/chords → RY → RZ_post” instead of “RY → RZ_pre → CRZ → RZ_post.” By moving the shared RY layer to after entanglement, you alter intra‐block information flow without changing depth or gate count, potentially improving feature mixing and reducing the 180‐step convergence further.
