# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Coarse‐grained rotation sharing across two 4-qubit banks  
  • Generation 35 (“Shared-Parameter RY/RZ CZ 8-Qubit Classifier”) used just two RY and two RZ keys per bank (26 parameters total), achieving combined_raw 0.91, fast convergence in ~180 steps, and param_eff 0.04.  
  • Generation 38 (current best “Coarse-Grained 8-Qubit Evolve Ansatz Classifier”) extended this pattern with learnable CRZ strengths and reached combined_raw 0.93, test_acc 0.95, gen_gap 0.01.  

- Interleaved entanglement rings plus opposite-qubit chords  
  • Adding four “chord” CRZ/CZ gates to two interleaved rings cut convergence from ~270→180 steps (Gen 29 seed vs Gen 31 without chords) and boosted raw from 0.89→0.91 (Gen 35).  
  • The best program (Gen 38) replaced fixed CZ with parameterized CRZ (“chi_ring” and “chi_chord”) to hit raw 0.93.  

- Learnable entangling strengths (CRZ) vs fixed CZ  
  • Gen 38’s trainable “chi_ring” and “chi_chord” outperformed fixed-strength CZ in Gen 35 (0.93 vs 0.91) with identical depth (105) and gate_count (300).  

- 3 uploads × 2 repeats of a depth-105, ~300-gate corridor  
  • All top performers (Gen 35, Gen 37, Gen 38, seed) used 3 feature-uploads and 2 ansatz repeats, consistently landing raw ≥ 0.90 when depth≈105 and ~300 gates.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Fine-grained parameter sharing inflating parameter count  
  • Generation 36 (“Four-Bank Controlled-Rotation Ansatz Classifier”) used 92 circuit parameters, depth 111, gate_count 318, but only raw 0.88 and param_eff 0.01—overparameterization without accuracy gain.  

- Parameterized two-qubit rotations beyond CRZ (CRX/CRY)  
  • Prior runs with CRY/CRX entanglement (Gen 30, Gen 33) stalled at raw 0.84–0.89 and generalization_gap 0.02–0.06, indicating CRY/CRX blocks add complexity without expressivity benefit.  

- Overly complex SU(2) blocks  
  • Generation 32’s 50-parameter SU(2)-heavy ansatz achieved raw 0.88 (gap 0.02) despite depth 111, underperforming leaner, shared-parameter 26–38-param designs.  

- Omitting long-range chords  
  • Ring-only CZ entanglement (Gen 31) capped raw at 0.89; adding chords was necessary to reliably cross raw 0.90.  

## Implementation Insights

- Generic ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec → ordered PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK  
  • All programs sliced block parameters via a dictionary lookup on ordered_unique_keys, enabling arbitrary N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS without code duplication.  

- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue + tape.graph.get_depth for reliable metadata  
  • Seed (Gen 38) measured depth 105 and gate_count 300 automatically, confirming placement in the empirically optimal “corridor.”  

- Coarse-grained two-bank design balances expressivity and overfitting  
  • Gen 38’s 8 unique block keys (“ry_h”, “ry_h2”, “rz_pre_h”, “rz_pre_h2”, “chi_ring”, “chi_chord”, “rz_post_h”, “rz_post_h2”) yield 48 circuit params vs 92 in Gen 36, yet deliver higher accuracy.  

- Decoupling block definition from training loop  
  • Swappable ANSATZ_SPEC meant teams could replace CZ→CRZ or add chords without touching the optimizer or data pipeline, accelerating iteration from Gen 35→38.  

## Performance Analysis

- Raw score vs parameter count trade-off  
  • 26 params (Gen 35) → raw 0.91; 50 params (Gen 38) → raw 0.93; 92 params (Gen 36) → raw 0.88. Optimal expressivity found in 26–50 param range with judicious sharing.  

- Parameter efficiency not strictly correlated with highest accuracy  
  • Gen 35 achieved param_eff 0.04 at raw 0.91; Gen 38 saw param_eff 0.02 at raw 0.93. Slight efficiency drop buys a 0.02 raw gain.  

- Convergence steps tightly linked to chord inclusion  
  • Opposite-qubit chords reduced steps to ~180 across top programs (Gen 35, Gen 38) vs ~270 without chords.  

- Generalization gap minimized by parameter sharing  
  • High-efficiency designs (Gen 35, Gen 38) both showed gen_gap 0.01, whereas low-efficiency/overparameterized models (Gen 36, Gen 37) exhibited gaps up to 0.05.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the ring entanglers into two tunable subsets per block.   
   Instead of a single “chi_ring” controlling all eight interleaved‐ring CRZs, introduce two per‐block parameters—“chi_ring_even” for the CRZs on wires [(0,2),(4,6),(1,3),(5,7)] and “chi_ring_odd” for [(2,4),(6,0),(3,5),(7,1)]. This doubles your ring‐entanglement degrees of freedom to 2 per block (versus 1) while staying in the coarse‐grained sharing regime, and tests whether letting the two interleaved rings optimize independently can push raw accuracy above 0.93.  

2. Augment with a second set of long-range “diagonal” chords.   
   Add four CRZ gates per block on wires [(0,5),(1,6),(2,7),(3,4)] all sharing a new key “chi_diag.” This adds only one parameter per block and injects extra long-range coupling beyond the opposite-qubit chords. By mirroring the proven chord pattern but at a different graph distance, you probe whether additional sparse connectivity unlocks further expressivity gains.  

3. Tie entangling strengths globally across all blocks.   
   Replace the per-block “chi_ring” and “chi_chord” with single, global parameters (remove them from PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK and reference a shared “chi_ring” and “chi_chord” in every block). This reduces N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK by 2 (and N_PARAMS by 12 total) to test whether stronger parameter sharing of entanglers preserves the 0.93 raw score while boosting parameter efficiency and further shrinking the generalization gap.  

4. Add hierarchical single-qubit offset parameters.   
   Keep the existing “ry_h” and “ry_h2” banks but introduce small per-bank offset keys “Δry_h” and “Δry_h2” in each block, and apply RY gates as RY(ry_h + Δry_h) on wires 0–3 and RY(ry_h2 + Δry_h2) on wires 4–7. This hierarchical scheme preserves coarse-grained sharing yet allows each block to locally adjust its rotation bias, testing whether slight relaxation of the two-bank constraint can lift performance without overparameterizing.  

5. Experiment with block-internal gate ordering.   
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, reorder each block to apply RZ_pre → CRZ entanglers (rings + chords) → RY → RZ_post, rather than RY → RZ_pre → entangler → RZ_post. By moving the shared RY layer to post-entanglement, you alter the information flow through the block at identical depth and gate count. This simple structural tweak may enhance feature mixing before the final Z rotations and yield faster convergence or higher raw accuracy.
