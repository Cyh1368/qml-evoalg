# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three feature‐map reuploads × two ansatz repeats of a minimal RY→pre-RZ→CZ‐chain→post-RZ block – as in the Seed “Feature-Reupload Ansatz” (depth 99, gates 270, n_params 146) – yields the top raw score of 0.85 with a moderate generalization gap (0.07).  
- Linear CZ chains interleaved with two-stage RZ rotations plus per-qubit RY mixers maximize expressivity per parameter: the Seed (0.85) outperforms more elaborate entanglers in Gen 16 (Phase-Entangled, 0.84, depth 142) and Gen 17 (ShinkaEvolve with CRZ bridges, 0.84, depth 117).  
- Fixed IsingZZ feature encoding combined with a two-parameter gain/bias readout calibration stabilizes training and is present in all top‐performing classifiers (Seed, Gen 13–17).  
- Keeping the per-block parameter count low (~24) while preserving local phase flexibility yields faster convergence to high accuracy (Seed converges by ~750 steps versus ~240 in Gen 16 but with no raw‐score gain).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Inserting long-range controlled rotations (CRY/CRZ) or additional two-qubit parameterized gates (Gen 16, 17) increased depth to 117–142 and gate counts to 294–300 without improving beyond 0.84, reducing parameter efficiency.  
- Aggressive overparameterization and hierarchical structures (Gen 18, n_params 506, depth 237) led to a lower raw score (0.80) and a larger generalization gap (0.10).  
- Circular or sparse CZ entanglers and extra mid-circuit RX layers (e.g., Gen 16’s circular entangler, depth 142; Gen 19’s rich sparse/CNOT mixers, depth 124) inflate gate counts (300+) but do not surpass the 0.85 benchmark.  
- Shallow reductions in repeat counts or dramatic parameter sharing (as in earlier “collapsed_rz_param_sharing”) drop raw accuracy below 0.78–0.84, indicating expressivity loss when sharing is too aggressive.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Centralizing gate definitions in ANSATZ_SPEC with validate_ansatz_spec + apply_ansatz_block ensures consistency and simplifies swap-in/swap-out of candidate blocks (Seed program).  
- Using qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue and tape.graph.get_depth() for on-the-fly depth and gate counts allows precise cost/benefit analysis when iterating ansatz designs.  
- The Seed’s choice of exactly 24 trainable angles per block (8 RY, 8 pre-RZ, 8 post-RZ) yields the optimal depth-vs-performance trade-off; more parameters per block (e.g., added RX or CRZ) drove depth above 117 with no score gain.  
- Uniformly applying a fixed IsingZZ feature map and two readout calibration parameters across models standardizes the training pipeline and decouples expressivity tuning from feature encoding.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Depth vs. Score: Optimal raw combined_score_raw (0.85) occurs at depth ≈ 99; depths ≥ 117 plateau at 0.84 or below (Gen 13–17), and depths ≥ 142 never exceed 0.84.  
- Gate Count: Top performance clusters at ~270 gates (Seed); architectures ≥ 294 gates (Gen 16, 17) do not improve raw accuracy.  
- Parameter Count: n_params ≈ 146 balances expressivity and efficiency; fewer parameters (98 in Gen 13) match 0.84 but cannot surpass the 0.85 seed, while excessive parameters (506 in Gen 18) degrade performance.  
- Generalization Gap: Moderate gaps (~0.07–0.09) accompany the best raw scores (Seed gap 0.07; Gen 17 gap 0.09); too large (> 0.10 in Gen 18) correlates with lower accuracy (0.80).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Axis-alternating mixers per block  
   In your `apply_ansatz_block`, replace the initial RY layer with RX on every other block (e.g. if `block_index % 2 == 1`, use RX rotations instead of RY). This preserves the 8-gate single-qubit mixer budget while probing whether mixing rotation axes boosts expressivity without increasing depth or gate count, building directly on the Seed’s minimal-param RY→RZ→CZ structure.  

2. Central shared CRZ augmentation  
   After the third CZ in the linear chain (wires `[3,4]`) insert a single CRZ gate whose angle is a new parameter `"crz_shared"`, but share this one parameter across all blocks. This introduces a controlled phase flexibility at the heart of each entangler with only one extra trainable variable and minimal depth overhead, testing if a global two-qubit parameter can raise performance beyond pure CZ chains.  

3. Mid-circuit feature re-encoding  
   Between uploads 2 and 3 in `circuit_z_expectations`, invoke `feature_map(records)` once more—without an intervening ansatz block—to re-inject data‐driven nonlinearity. This adds only N_FEATURES IsingZZ gates (~28 two-qubit operations) but leverages the proven stability of fixed feature maps to deepen the circuit’s ability to capture complex correlations.  

4. Block-level global phase rotations  
   At the end of each ansatz block, append an 8-qubit parallel RZ rotation using a single new parameter `rz_block_{i}` (one per block). Implement this by looping over all wires:  
     for q in range(N_QUBITS): qml.RZ(params[offset + extra_index], wires=q)  
   This adds one trainable phase per block (six total) and experiments with coarse global phase control while preserving local RZ flexibility.  

5. Upload-specific repeat counts  
   Replace the constant `N_REPEATS` with a list, e.g. `REPEATS = [3,2,1]`, and in your upload loop use `for repeat_index in range(REPEATS[upload_index])`. This uneven re-upload schedule concentrates more ansatz depth where early feature embeddings are richest, then tapers parameters later to curb overfitting—testing a simple nonuniform depth allocation within the proven 3×2 framework.
