# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mid-circuit two-axis mixers breaking up commuting CZ layers  
  • Both the current best program (combined_score_raw 0.95, test_accuracy 0.97, depth 135) and Gen 28 (“two_axis_midpoint_mixer,” combined_score_raw 0.95, test_accuracy 0.97) interleave shared RY/RX layers halfway through the seven perfect-matching CZ schedule. This non-commutativity lifts test accuracy by ≈1% over similar designs without mid mixers (e.g., Gen 36/40 at test_accuracy 0.96).  
- Edge-colored complete-graph entanglement via seven perfect matchings  
  • The current best, Gen 28, Gen 32 (“midmixer_full_su2_crossover”), and Gen 37 (“Permutation-Equivariant Complete-Graph Ansatz”) all deploy a seven-matching K₈ decomposition. They achieve high expressivity at an optimal depth (135) and gate count (540), outperforming cyclic or ad hoc CZ schedules (e.g., Gen 29’s cyclic coupling, combined_score_raw 0.90).  
- Heavy parameter tying for permutation equivariance  
  • Shared single-qubit rotations (collective_ry, collective_rx_in, collective_ry_mid, collective_rx_mid, collective_rz, collective_rx) reduce each block to 6 parameters. The current best and Gen 37 both use 38 total parameters (3 uploads × 2 repeats × 6 + 2 readout), yielding parameter efficiency 0.03 without sacrificing accuracy.  
- Three feature-upload cycles × two ansatz repeats  
  • All top performers (current best, Gen 34/37/40) use 3 data reuploads and 2 ansatz repeats per upload. This pattern consistently stabilizes feature encoding and keeps depth at the 135/gate-count-540 sweet spot.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Omitting non-commuting mid circuits  
  • Gen 36 (“8-Qubit Layered CZ Classifier,” no mid two-axis stage) and Gen 40 (“Collective Mixer with Full Feature Interaction,” no mid mixers) both plateau at test_accuracy 0.96 and combined_score_raw 0.94, despite deeper or fuller-graph designs.  
- Increasing depth/gate count without added mixers  
  • Gen 36 (depth 147, 636 gates) and Gen 40 (depth 153) incur hardware cost without accuracy gains vs. 135-layer circuits; deeper is not better unless structural expressivity (mid mixers) is introduced.  
- Over-parametrization without structural novelty  
  • Gen 38 (“8-Qubit Phase-Mixer Ansatz,” 80 parameters) sees test_accuracy drop to 0.94 with a larger generalization gap (0.03) and very low parameter efficiency (0.01), illustrating that simply adding parameters can worsen overfitting.  
- Full-graph CZ coupling alone is insufficient  
  • Designs that implement the complete interaction graph but omit mid-circuit two-axis mixing (Gen 31 in earlier studies; Gen 40 here) underperform relative to designs that split the CZ schedule with non-commuting layers.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + dynamic key-to-position mapping  
  • Encoding the entire block in a Python list of gate dicts and indexing parameters via a `key_to_position` lookup makes it trivial to splice in or reorder mixers (e.g., mid-circuit RY/RX) without manual index bookkeeping.  
- Parameter tying via repeated `"param"` strings  
  • Reusing the same param key across all wires (e.g., `"collective_ry"`) automatically enforces permutation equivariance and caps `N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` at 6, directly contributing to high parameter efficiency (0.03).  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript for metadata extraction  
  • The current best’s `circuit_metadata()` uses `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue()` and `QuantumScript.from_queue` to report exact depth (135) and gate_count (540), enabling precise cost–benefit comparisons when adjusting the ansatz.  
- Modular feature_map + apply_ansatz_block within a backprop-able QNode  
  • Separating fixed IsingZZ uploads (`feature_map`) from parametrized ansatz blocks (`apply_ansatz_block`) within a PennyLane `@qml.qnode(..., diff_method="backprop")` keeps the codebase clean and the training loop stable across variants.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Top combined_score_raw cutoff at 0.95 only with mid-circuit mixers  
  • All evaluated variants without mid mixers cap at combined_score_raw 0.94; only the current best and Gen 28 reach 0.95.  
- Parameter efficiency versus absolute accuracy trade-off  
  • 38 parameters (efficiency 0.03) with structured mixers deliver test_accuracy 0.97; doubling parameters to 80 (efficiency 0.01) in Gen 38 drops test_accuracy to 0.94.  
- Depth/gate sweet spot confirmed at 135/540  
  • Circuits shallower (e.g., fewer than four matchings per upload) or deeper (>147) underperform unless mid-circuit non-commutativity is introduced.  
- Convergence speed versus final accuracy  
  • Mid-circuit mixer designs converge in ~150 global steps to a validation threshold of 0.90, slightly slower than non-mid designs (~90 steps), but achieve the highest final test accuracy (0.97).
