# Independent review of the training pilot

Reviewed `research/metrics/training_pilot.py` and its saved JSON. The pilot uses disjoint S3 orbits, training-only candidate selection, paired random proposals and eight optimizer seeds. It does not evaluate the five reserved orbits. These are useful correctness and feasibility checks.

**The current outcome does not establish that symmetry repair improves learning.** Repaired beats original in 6/8 seeds at every eta; development MSE improvement falls from 0.001554 at eta=0 to 0.001007 at eta=1. But the sham's mean improvement is larger at every eta (0.003693, 0.002780, 0.001735), with mixed signs. Eight seeds describe random-search variation on one dataset split and one teacher, not variation over independent tasks.

Concrete confounds:

- The targets are generated by the compared ansatz families themselves. At eta=0 the repaired family contains the teacher exactly; at eta=1 the original family does. This is a deliberately realizable sanity check, not evidence of transfer or superiority on independent tasks.
- Repaired uses 12 gates versus 9 and halves every CRZ angle. Parameter counts and proposal counts match, but expressivity, parameter-induced function distribution, circuit execution cost and search geometry differ. A fixed 128 uniform draws is weak optimization, so approximation error and optimization error are unresolved.
- Adding .3 to a shared RY angle is a phase shift of the same periodic parameter family. With uniform full-period sampling it preserves the marginal search distribution, but changes paired samples. The sham is informative about finite-search noise and should not be interpreted as a causal structural control.
- Complete orbits have different sizes (1, 3 or 6). Report both sample-weighted and orbit-weighted risks; uncertainty should acknowledge orbit dependence.
- Eta mixes two teacher functions without keeping the invariant target component fixed. The broken teacher's Reynolds projection is not shown to equal the repaired teacher. Consequently eta changes both invariant signal and symmetry violation.

A direct diagnostic computed from the existing development states (no reserved orbit accessed) shows the broken target's minimum MSE over **all** invariant predictors is only **0.000411427** on development, versus target variance **0.140724**. On training the analogous invariant floor is **0.000446957**, versus variance **0.135722**. The repaired-versus-original teacher MSE is only 0.000806677 on development. Thus even eta=1 is weakly symmetry-breaking relative to the dominant target signal, and a repaired advantage there is compatible with finite-search error. The floor is computed exactly by grouping all permutations of each unordered triple and averaging squared deviations from each orbit mean; it is a distribution-specific oracle diagnostic, not a fitted predictor.

**Next decisive test:** retain a fixed invariant function h and use `y_eta=h+eta*b`, where b is obtained by subtracting the complete group average from a separate teacher and then normalizing its orbit variance to a prespecified scale. Measure the analytically known invariant-risk floor eta² E[b²]. Add a gate-budget control with repeated same-direction CRZ half-angles (same gate count as repair, exactly equivalent to the original operator), retain the sham, and run increasing optimization budgets with common random numbers and local refinement. Use multiple frozen teacher draws and orbit splits; select parameters only on training and report development results separately by teacher/split. Finally run the frozen protocol on the independent graph and ring benchmarks in `research/datasets/`, keeping final holdouts sealed until choices are fixed.

A convincing result would show repair approaching the invariant oracle floor as the controlled breaking strength increases, plus gains on independent invariant tasks that survive gate-count and optimization controls. Until then, describe this result as a small development pilot demonstrating executable interventions and orbit-disjoint training.
