symmetric_shared_params_ansatz

<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses 24 independent parameters (one RY/RZ per qubit plus a second independent RZ layer), which massively overfits on the tiny training set (train acc 0.88 vs test acc 0.56, a 0.31 generalization gap) and scores poorly on parameter economy, which the metric rewards monotonically.

I propose replacing the per-qubit independent rotation layers with heavily parameter-shared rotations (a single shared RY angle and a single shared RZ angle applied identically to all 8 qubits), an entangling ring of parametrized ZZ interactions sharing a single angle, and a final shared RZ layer. This reduces the circuit's distinct trainable parameters from 24 down to just 4 (ry, rz, zz, rz2), for a total of 6 parameters including gain/bias — a >4x reduction.

The rationale:
1. Since the circuit block is repeated 3 times with tied parameters, even a small number of shared angles produces a non-trivial (non-commuting, Trotter-like) effective unitary with real expressive power, while drastically cutting the parameter count that the score explicitly rewards without diminishing returns.
2. Full permutation-symmetric rotation layers act as a strong inductive bias / regularizer appropriate for a tiny training set, directly targeting the observed large train-test generalization gap.
3. Keeping one tunable entangling parameter (shared ZZ angle around a ring) retains the ability to correlate qubits (needed for the two-qubit readout pairs) without qubit-specific overfitting, and avoids the fixed-CZ option which offers no tunability at all.

This trades some raw fitting capacity for much better held-out and worst-group robustness, which the scoring function weights most heavily.
</DESCRIPTION>
