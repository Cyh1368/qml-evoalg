shared_param_ring_ansatz

<DESCRIPTION>
The seed ansatz uses 24 independent parameters (one RY/RZ per qubit twice), which is heavily over-parameterized for the tiny training set and explains the large train/validation/test gap (0.88 vs 0.63 vs 0.56). Since the scoring rule explicitly rewards fewer distinct trainable parameter names "with no point of diminishing returns", and robust generalization on held-out/worst-group samples is the dominant score term, I replace the per-qubit independent rotations with globally-shared parameters.

New design:
- A single shared RY angle applied to all 8 qubits (parameter "ry").
- A single shared RZ angle applied to all 8 qubits (parameter "rz").
- A ring of IsingZZ entanglers (8 edges, including the wrap-around 7-0) all sharing one angle "zz", giving a translation-invariant entangling layer.

This yields only 3 distinct trainable circuit parameters (plus the fixed gain/bias), down from 24. Because the block is repeated 3 times with tied parameters, the effective circuit is a Trotterized evolution under a translationally-symmetric Hamiltonian-like generator (local field + ring ZZ coupling), which is still expressive enough to build entanglement and separate classes, while the parameter-sharing symmetry acts as a strong inductive bias that should reduce overfitting on the small training set, shrink the train/test gap, and improve worst-group margins. The ring topology (rather than the seed's open CZ line) also ensures that every consecutive pair of qubits, including qubit 0 and qubit 7, is coupled at least once per block, which is important since the fixed readout pairs are unknown in advance and could span any two qubits.
