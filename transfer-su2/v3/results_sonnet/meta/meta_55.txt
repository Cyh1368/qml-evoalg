# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Two-Parameter Multi-Shell Shared-CRZ Ansatz**  
  • Generations 50 (“8-Qubit Shared-Parameter CRZ Ansatz Classifier”) and 54 (“Minimal Two-Parameter Multi-Shell Ansatz Classifier,” the current best) both collapse the entire variational circuit to two distinct angles—θ_ry for global RY rotations and θ_z reused for all RZ and CRZ gates on shells at distances 1, 2, 4—repeated 3 times.  
  • Both achieved the top combined score of 0.61 (train 1.00, val 0.90, test 0.85), generalization gap 0.15, parameter efficiency 0.21, depth 45, gate count 108.  
  • Omitting the distance-3 shell and avoiding a third “long-range” parameter concentrated entanglement on the most informative edges, yielding strong expressivity under a minimal parameter budget.  

- **Tied Repeats via Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC**  
  • Defining ANSATZ_SPEC once and looping N_REPEATS=3 over the same parameter keys (“theta_ry”, “theta_z”) enforces exact parameter sharing and rotational symmetry.  
  • This pattern preserved parameter economy (2 circuit parameters + 2 readout) while retaining sufficient circuit depth (45) to achieve perfect training convergence in ~1 440 steps.  

- **Selective Output Mixer Usage**  
  • Earlier variants (Gen 48/49) showed benefits from reusing θ_ry as a final global RY mixer without adding new parameters.  
  • In the current best (Gen 54), empirical tests revealed that adding that extra mixer on top of a minimal two-shell structure degraded training (train accuracy fell to 0.75). The insight: mixer utility depends on upstream entanglement capacity.  

- **Constrained Shell Selection**  
  • Using only shells at distances 1, 2, 4 (nearest, skip, antipodal) maximizes useful correlations. Attempts to add a 3-distance ring or to split shells anisotropically did not improve scores.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Cross-Axis Entanglers Without Extra Parameters (Gen 51)**  
  • Introduced non-commuting CRY on the skip shell while reusing θ_ry/θ_z. This overfit the few-shot training (train 0.88) but collapsed validation to 0.55 and test 0.48 (combined 0.35), with a generalization gap 0.40 and near-zero worst-group margins.  
  • Mixing axes without additional parameters broke the circuit’s capacity to generalize.  

- **Dual-Axis or Multi-Scale Shell Splitting (Gens 45 & 46)**  
  • Splitting skip-shell entanglers into two parameters (CRX+CRZ or dual-generator) introduced four circuit parameters yet only reached combined scores ~0.43 (val 0.64/test 0.56), generalization gaps ~0.19.  
  • Depth increased (e.g., 84) and per-gate expressivity diluted, leading to under-fitting of complex state correlations despite the higher parameter count.  

- **Unjudged Final Mixer on Minimal Designs**  
  • Appending a reused-RY output mixer to the two-shell variant (dropping one entangling shell) collapsed train accuracy to 0.75 (current best’s ablation). The extra mixer can interfere if the entangling backbone is already minimal.  

## Implementation Insights

- **Programmatic ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec**  
  • Centralizing gate definitions in a list of dicts and validating them catches mis-indexed wires or unsupported gates at program load time. The mapping from param key to index ensures exact parameter sharing across repeats.  

- **Wide Uniform Initialization & Decoupled Readout Calibration**  
  • Initializing all circuit angles uniformly over [–π, π] prevented dead-zone initializations that bias ranking. Separately initializing gain=1 and bias=0 decoupled readout calibration from circuit learning, stabilizing early convergence across seeds.  

- **AnnotatedQueue Profiling for Depth/Gate Counts**  
  • Using qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue and tape.graph.get_depth() concretely confirmed the 45-depth/108-gate “sweet spot” for the two-parameter multi-shell architecture. This profiling guided pruning of less effective shells.  

- **Symmetry Preservation Drives Robustness**  
  • Tying the same angles across all qubits and repeats preserved the ring’s rotational symmetry. Designs that broke symmetry (anisotropic splits, cross-axis shells) consistently underperformed or overfit.  

## Performance Analysis

- **Score Comparison**  
  • Best two-parameter models (Gen 50, 54): combined 0.61 vs. dual-generator (Gen 47) 0.52, shared-three-shell (Gen 55) 0.57, multi-shell without final mixer (Gen 53) 0.43, cross-axis (Gen 51) 0.35.  
  • Parameter efficiency peaked at 0.21 for 2-param designs; four-param variants never exceeded 0.19.  

- **Generalization Gap Trends**  
  • Two-parameter multi-shell: gap 0.15, worst-group margin 0.33. Dual-generator: gap 0.09 but lower combined score. Cross-axis: gap 0.40 and near-zero margins.  

- **Depth & Gate Count Correlation**  
  • Circuits with depth 45 and ~108 gates outperformed deeper (≥84) or sparser variants, confirming that an optimal connectivity pattern underlies the performance gains, not just raw depth.  

- **Convergence Efficiency**  
  • Top-performing programs consistently reached validation ≥0.90 in ~1 440 steps. Less effective variants either never converged or required more epochs without matching that threshold.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce fixed per-shell scaling factors on the shared “theta_z” to softly break perfect shell symmetry without adding parameters. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, wrap each CRZ angle lookup in apply_ansatz_block with a hard-coded multiplier (e.g. 1.0 for distance-1, 0.8 for distance-2, 1.2 for distance-4). This preserves the 2-parameter economy while diversifying entanglement strengths, leveraging the success of static symmetry distortions to enrich expressivity without over-parameterization.

2. Vary the order of the three entangling shells across the three repeats to diversify circuit pathways under the same angles. In apply_ansatz_block, use the repeat_index to choose a permutation of the shell loops (e.g. repeat 0: shells 1→2→4; repeat 1: 2→4→1; repeat 2: 4→1→2) while still reading “theta_z.” This introduces depth-wise asymmetry that can improve gradient flow and mixing, building on the tied-repeat pattern that yielded strong convergence.

3. Swap in a final global RZ mixer reusing “theta_z” instead of appending RY gates, to better align the last rotation with the CRZ entanglers. In ANSATZ_SPEC, remove any final RY entries and append eight {"gate":"RZ","wire":i,"param":"theta_z"} entries after the entanglers. This tests axis-alignment effects observed in shared-parameter mixers without inflating the parameter count.

4. Replace the skip-shell CRZ entanglers (distance 2) with Ising-XX gates that reuse “theta_z” (i.e. qml.IsingXX). Simply change the gate field for those ANSATZ_SPEC entries from "CRZ" to "XX". This swaps the generator axis on the skip shell to break residual commutation patterns while retaining the 2-parameter budget, inspired by the need to diversify entanglement without extra trainables.

5. Apply small, fixed per-repeat scaling offsets to “theta_z” to break perfect repeat symmetry. In apply_ansatz_block, multiply the shared angle by a constant array [1.00, 0.95, 1.05][repeat_index] before any CRZ calls. This introduces gentle heterogeneity across repeats—preserving parameter sharing but enriching representational capacity in line with the proven robustness of slight symmetry perturbations.
