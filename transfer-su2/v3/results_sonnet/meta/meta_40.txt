# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Two-parameter multi-shell CRZ ansatz (current best)  
  • Achieved combined score 0.61 (train 1.00, val 0.90, test 0.85, gap 0.15) with only two distinct circuit angles (“theta_ry”, “theta_z”) plus gain/bias.  
  • Reuses the same RZ angle for CRZ entanglers across three connectivity shells (nearest-neighbor, skip, antipodal), yielding depth 45 and 108 gates.  
  • Block-reuse (3×) with tied parameters amplifies expressivity without expanding the search space, ensuring zero seed-to-seed variance.  

- Global single-qubit rotations + shared entanglers  
  • Minimal shared-parameter designs in Gen 30 (score 0.57) and Gen 32 (score 0.57) follow the same pattern: one RY and one RZ/CRZ angle repeated 3×.  
  • Consistently produce perfect training accuracy and high validation (~0.88–0.90) and test (~0.85) accuracy, with low generalization gaps (~0.14–0.16).  

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC shell enumeration  
  • Enumerating each CRZ shell in a simple list lets one-line additions/removals of connectivity and enforces generator symmetry, speeding iteration and avoiding indexing errors.  
  • The current best program’s clear spec plus `validate_ansatz_spec` guarantees correct parameter key extraction (N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK = 2).  

- Wide-range parameter initialization  
  • Uniform sampling of all circuit angles in [–π, π] (instead of near-zero) yields robust convergence across candidate ansätze, preventing dead-zone plateaus seen in low-variance starts.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Extreme parameter collapse  
  • Gen 34 (“8-Qubit Shared-Parameter Ring Ansatz”) tied RZ, CRZ, CRX, CRY into a single angle → combined score 0.37, underfitting (train 0.69) and large generalization artifacts.  
  • Over-sharing removed necessary entangling diversity, collapsing margins and lowering test accuracy to ~0.48–0.61.  

- Adding dual-axis entanglers without extra parameters  
  • Gen 31 (“Axis-Mixed Multi-Shell Ansatz”) introduced CRY alongside CRZ at zero cost → combined score 0.37, generalization gap 0.26.  
  • Extra non-commuting rotations without added parameter capacity hampered training, leading to under-trained local minima.  

- Parity-split mid-range entanglers  
  • Gen 33 split distance-2 links into even/odd controls (6 circuit parameters) → combined score 0.40, parameter efficiency 0.09.  
  • Increased parameter count did not capture harder group correlations and slowed convergence.  

- Replacing CRZ with other two-qubit generators  
  • Gen 39 (long-range XX shell) scored 0.43; Gen 40 (shared ZZ) scored 0.38.  
  • Mixing in IsingXX or IsingZZ exclusively underperformed: these generators offered less synergy with global RZ sharing and failed to reach >0.60 combined score.  

## Implementation Insights

- validate_ansatz_spec + ordered parameter keys  
  • Centralized validation catches mis-wiring and missing “param” fields before training. The current best program reports exactly two keys in order: [“theta_ry”, “theta_z”].  

- apply_ansatz_block that ignores block_index  
  • Enforces tied parameters cleanly across repeats. By mapping each param name to its position once, the block-application loop remains concise and bug-resistant.  

- AnnotatedQueue for depth/gate profiling  
  • Using `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` and `QuantumScript.from_queue` gives reliable depth = 45 and gate_count = 108. Top performers cluster at this “sweet spot.”  

- Explicit init of gain/bias separate from circuit angles  
  • Initializing gain = 1, bias = 0 alongside wide-range angles ensures the readout calibration does not interfere with circuit entanglement learning.  

## Performance Analysis

- Best combined score tied to 2-distinct-parameter designs  
  • Only the current best (score 0.61) and its near-clones (Gen 25) surpass 0.58. All other variants plateau at ≤0.57 or fall below 0.56.  

- Parameter efficiency peak ~0.21  
  • The 2-parameter multi-shell CRZ models achieve test accuracy 0.85 with N_PARAMS = 4, yielding efficiency 0.21. Designs with 5–6 params drop to ≤0.15.  

- Generalization gap stabilized at ~0.15  
  • High-scoring models with shared RZ/CRZ entanglers hold generalization gaps in the 0.14–0.17 range. In contrast, over- and under-parameterized models show gaps of 0.04 (low accuracy) or >0.20.  

- Connectivity matters more than raw gate count  
  • All top performers use three connectivity shells with controlled-phase entanglers. Merely adding depth or mixing generators (without respecting the multi-shell CRZ pattern) never reaches the 0.61 threshold.
