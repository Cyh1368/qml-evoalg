# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Repeating a minimal one-parameter block three times multiplies expressivity without extra weights.  
  • Gen 29 (One-Parameter Collective-Correlation Ansatz) uses N_REPEATS = 3 of the same “collective_correlation” mixer+CZ+ZZ block and tops the leaderboard with combined 0.54 (train/val/test = 0.94/0.82/0.79).  
  • Builds on earlier Gen 22 patterns where 3 identical Möbius/CZ repeats reached combined 0.53.  

- Nonlocal Möbius-ladder CZ plus parameterized ZZ correlations boosts margins.  
  • Gen 29 adds translation-symmetric IsingZZ gates (tied to the same parameter as RY mixers) on top of the Möbius entangler, improving combined score from 0.51 (Gen 23, 26) to 0.54.  

- Extreme parameter tying yields high parameter efficiency and regularization.  
  • Gen 29 achieves parameter efficiency 0.26 with only one distinct circuit parameter (plus gain/bias), outpacing Gen 23’s 0.18 (2 distinct) and Gen 28’s 0.25 (1 distinct) while raising test accuracy by +0.11.  

- Trainable gain/bias calibration on averaged two-qubit observables stabilizes margins.  
  • Consistent in top performers (Gen 22, 23, 29), the final “gain*(mean(correlators)–bias)” readout yields robust worst-group margins (~0.19) and prevents trivial solutions.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Tying to only local XX entanglers underfits completely.  
  • Gen 30 (collective_xx_mobius) shares one parameter over RY/XX with Möbius CZ but scores chance-level (combined 0.43; test 0.50), showing that XX alone lacks the cross-qubit correlation strength of ZZ.  

- Relying solely on fixed CZ webs without parameterized correlators yields modest test gains but high gaps.  
  • Gen 28 (distance_three_cz_ring) uses only un-parameterized long-range CZ plus one “collective_angle” for RY/RZ, achieving combined 0.49 but with a large generalization gap (0.19) and modest train 0.56.  

- Adding small numbers of distinct parameters without richer entanglers gives diminishing returns.  
  • Gen 23’s 2 distinct angles (collective RY/RZ + shared CRZ readout) reached only combined 0.51 and test 0.73, matching simpler one-parameter models but with more parameters.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Parameter tying via key_to_position mapping and `del block_index` is zero-overhead and enforces global symmetry.  
  • Gen 29’s `apply_ansatz_block` drops `block_index` and uses a single dict lookup per gate to bind all mixers and ZZs to “collective_correlation,” enabling N_REPEATS = 3 with no extra params.  

- Combining Möbius CZ (12 gates) with IsingZZ (8 gates) yields a balanced entanglement/correlation layer: depth 54, gate_count 84.  
  • The deeper block in Gen 29 (vs. depth 33 in Gen 26) pays off with a +0.03 combined-score lift, illustrating that moderate depth cost can be traded for expressivity.  

- Wide uniform initialization in [–π,π] plus gain=1/bias=0 avoids barren-plateau traps and stabilizes early convergence.  
  • Adopted by all top-ranked models (Gen 22, 23, 29), this consistent init practice underpins reliable convergence to high test accuracies (~0.74–0.79).  

- Circuit metadata extraction via `AnnotatedQueue` and `tape.graph.get_depth()` enables precise depth/gate-count reporting for cost/performance analysis.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined scores span 0.43→0.54 across the latest generations.  
  • Gen 30 (0.43) at the low end—chance performance—versus Gen 29 (0.54) highest, highlighting the critical role of parameterized ZZ correlations.  

- Parameter efficiency correlates with distinct-parameter minimalism.  
  • Gen 29: 0.26 (test 0.79/3 params), Gen 28: 0.25 (test 0.76/3 params), Gen 23: 0.18 (test 0.73/4 params). Fewer distinct weights + richer entanglers maximize efficiency.  

- Accepting a moderate generalization gap (≈0.15) unlocks higher test accuracy.  
  • Gen 29 gap 0.15 yields test 0.79; in contrast, Gen 26’s low gap 0.06 caps test at 0.68 due to under-expressivity.  

- Incremental addition of parameterized layers gives diminishing but consistent gains.  
  • Moving from only Möbius CZ (Gen 26: 0.51) → Möbius CZ+ZZ (Gen 29: 0.54) shows a +0.03 boost per correlated layer added, validating layering strategies.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the ZZ correlation layer into “local” and “global” parameters.  
   In ANSATZ_SPEC, keep the single “collective_correlation” param on the Möbius cross-chords (edges [0,4],[1,5],…) but assign a new param key (e.g. “local_corr”) to the nearest-neighbor ZZ gates. This adds one extra weight, letting the optimizer independently modulate short- vs long-range correlations—a small param increase grounded in the observed +0.03 boost when adding parameterized ZZ layers.  

2. Alternate two Möbius-ladder patterns across repeats with zero new parameters.  
   In apply_ansatz_block, use `if block_index % 2 == 0` to apply the standard ladder CZ+ZZ pattern and `else` to apply a shifted ladder (e.g. cross-chords [0,5],[1,6],[2,7],[3,4]), reusing “collective_correlation” for both RY and ZZ gates. This injects richer nonlocal connectivity along depth without increasing the parameter count, building on the depth-amplification insight.  

3. Insert a shared CRZ layer on the readout pairs before averaging.  
   After all repeats but before computing expvals, loop over READOUT_PAIRS and apply `qml.CRZ(phi, wires=[a,b])` with a new “readout_phase” param. This gives the circuit fine-tuned control of the measurement basis—echoing Gen 21’s CRZ-readout benefit—while only adding one extra parameter.  

4. Interleave an IsingYY layer tied to the existing collective parameter.  
   In each block, immediately after the ZZ gates add IsingYY rotations on the same eight NN edges using the same “collective_correlation” key. This leverages the proven power of orthogonal two-qubit correlators for boosting expressivity without introducing extra parameters.  

5. Decouple single-qubit mixing and two-qubit correlations into two distinct global angles.  
   Rename the RY mixer param to “mixer_angle” and the ZZ param to “zz_angle” (update PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK and ANSATZ_SPEC accordingly). This two-parameter variant preserves full parameter tying within each type but lets the optimizer separately tune basis rotation versus coupling strength—an evidence-based tradeoff between the one-param regularity of Gen 29 and the expressivity gains seen when adding richer entanglers.
