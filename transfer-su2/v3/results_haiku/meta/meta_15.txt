# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parity‐key rotation sharing combined with mixed entanglers  
  • Generation 9 (score 0.54) ties RY/RZ by even/odd parity (4 rotation keys) and layers nearest-neighbor XX, staggered YY, diagonal ZZ in each 5-gate block. This delivered perfect train (1.00), very high validation (0.96) and test (0.90) accuracies, and a small generalization gap (0.10) with only 12 distinct circuit parameters.  
- Shallow, repeated block structure  
  • Applying the same 5-layer ansatz block three times (depth 15, gate count 75) struck the best balance: Gen 9 outperforms deeper designs (e.g., Gen 12 depth 18, score 0.45) and shallower or differently repeated blocks (Gen 10 depth 12, score 0.36).  
- Decoupled readout calibration  
  • A two-parameter gain/bias stage on averaged two-qubit correlators (common across top performers Gen 1, Gen 7, Gen 9) stabilizes margins (Gen 9 validation_margin 0.58, worst-group_margin 0.31) without inflating circuit complexity.  
- Wide uniform initialization + Adam + early stopping  
  • Initializing all angles uniformly in [–π,π], then training with Adam and restore-best-weights early stopping, yielded stable convergence (~780 steps for Gen 9) and avoided dead-zone artifacts.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-tying parameters → under‐expressivity  
  • Gen 10 (reduced_depth_2xrepeat, score 0.36, gap 0.34, efficiency 0.06) and Gen 13 (zz_bridge_enrichment, score 0.33, efficiency 0.04) share parameters too aggressively, fitting training perfectly but under-expressing on test data (~0.66 vs 0.90).  
- Increasing depth without entangler diversity  
  • Gen 12 (modular_depth_optimized_ansatz, depth 18, score 0.45) and Gen 14 (reduce_depth_add_yy_link, depth 18, score 0.32) both add layers/cross-links but only achieve test 0.85 or worse, with gen gaps ~0.15–0.34, indicating mere depth growth cannot substitute for sparse, varied connectivity.  
- Sole reliance on global or homogeneous entanglers  
  • Gen 11 (quartet_sharing_lean, score 0.46) uses symmetric global IsingXX/YY/ZZ sharing but test only ~0.82; demonstrates that diversity in entangler placement (nearest-neighbor, staggered, diagonal) is crucial for capturing state features.  

## Implementation Insights  
- ANSATZ_SPEC abstraction + validate_ansatz_spec  
  • Defining gates/params in a Python list and extracting `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` via `validate_ansatz_spec` simplifies tied-parameter management across repeats, as seen in Gen 9’s concise `apply_ansatz_block`.  
- Direct use of qml.IsingXX/YY/ZZ  
  • Gen 9 implements 4 XX, 3 YY, 2 ZZ gates per block directly, reducing decomposition overhead (75 gates total) versus decomposed variants in other programs (e.g., Gen 12’s 81 gates).  
- Clear separation of circuit vs readout parameters  
  • Storing circuit angles in `params[:N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS]` and gain/bias in the final two entries keeps feature extraction and calibration modular, aiding both code clarity and training stability.  
- Uniform‐scale initialization prevents dead zones  
  • Initializing circuit angles uniformly in [–π,π] (with gain=1, bias=0) avoids identity regions that can misrank candidate circuits, a pattern shared by top performers.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gen 9 vs other top programs  
  • Gen 9’s combined score 0.54 (test 0.90) leads over Gen 7 (score 0.49, test 0.85) and Gen 12 (score 0.45, test 0.85), demonstrating the impact of adding diagonal ZZ couplings to nearest-neighbor XX and staggered YY.  
- Entangler diversity reduces generalization gap  
  • Transitioning from only nearest-neighbor XX (Gen 7 gap 0.15) to mixed XX+YY+ZZ (Gen 9 gap 0.10) tightened the gap by ~0.05, confirming that varied two-qubit connectivity improves robustness.  
- Parameter efficiency sweet spot ~12 keys  
  • Gen 9 uses 12 distinct circuit parameters (14 total), achieving efficiency 0.06. Under-parameterized circuits (<9 keys) underperform (efficiency ≤ 0.04), while more parameters yield diminishing gap reductions.  
- Depth 15 optimal for expressivity vs overfitting  
  • Circuits at depth 15 (3×5 layers) outperform both shallower (depth 12) and deeper (depth 18) designs, indicating this depth best balances feature extraction and regularization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a sparse CRZ layer immediately after the existing staggered YY gates in each 5‐gate block. Concretely, insert two CRZ gates—e.g. between qubits (1, 3) and (5, 7)—both sharing a new parameter key `"crz_cross"`. This draws on Gen 2’s long‐range phase‐type entanglers (+2 gates, +1 param) to inject non‐local correlations without exceeding the ~14-parameter sweet spot.  

2. Break the strict 3× repetition symmetry by defining two ansatz-block variants A and B with swapped diagonal ZZ pairs. For example, block A uses ZZ on (0, 3) & (4, 7) while block B uses ZZ on (1, 4) & (5, 0), then apply them in an A–B–A sequence. This alternation preserves ~12–14 total circuit parameters but introduces heterogeneity across repeats, mirroring Gen 3’s improved generalization from broken symmetry.  

3. Refine parity-based rotation sharing to quartet sharing for RY and RZ layers. Replace the two keys `"ry_even"/"ry_odd"` and `"rz_even"/"rz_odd"` with four distinct keys—`"ry_01"`, `"ry_23"`, `"ry_45"`, `"ry_67"` (and similarly for RZ)—so each adjacent pair has its own rotation parameter. This controlled increase from 4→8 per layer injects targeted expressivity where Gen 9 showed modest param injections yielded test‐accuracy gains.  

4. Re‐tie the four nearest‐neighbor XX parameters into two parity groups to free capacity for new gates. Define `"xx_even"` for edges (0-1, 4-5) and `"xx_odd"` for (2-3, 6-7), halving from 4→2 XX keys. The freed parameter budget can then fund the CRZ or quartet rotations above, preserving NN entangling power while avoiding the under‐expressivity of over-sharing.  

5. Append a compact “star” IsingXX layer at the end of each block to capture global correlations. Connect qubit 0 to qubits 2, 4, 6 with three IsingXX gates under one new key `"xx_star"`. This +3 gates/+1 param layer complements the nearest-neighbor and diagonal couplings of Gen 9, extending entangler diversity with minimal depth and parameter overhead.
