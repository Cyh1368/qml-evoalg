# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the long-range ZZ coupling into two separate parameters. In your `_build_entanglement_layer()`, replace the shared `"zz_diag"` key on wires `[0,3]` and `[4,7]` with two distinct keys—e.g. `"zz_03"` for `[0,3]` and `"zz_47"` for `[4,7]`—and update `ANSATZ_SPEC` and `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` accordingly. This minimal +1 parameter tweak preserves parity-sharing and entangler diversity while giving the optimizer finer control over each diagonal link, which has boosted test accuracy in prior runs.  

2. Add an odd-qubit YY loop to balance non-local cycles across both parity sublattices. Append four new `{"gate": "YY", "wires": [...], "param": "yy_loop_odd"}` entries on wires `[(1,3),(3,5),(5,7),(7,1)]` in `_build_entanglement_layer()`. Mirroring the existing even-qubit YY couplings with this new key expands entangler variety (as seen in Gen 25/26) without breaking the ~7–10 parameter sweet spot.  

3. Introduce per-repeat entangler pattern shifts to diversify layer structure. In `apply_ansatz_block`, compute `shift = block_index % N_QUBITS` and, just before applying any two-qubit gate from `ANSATZ_SPEC`, replace its wires with `[(w + shift) % N_QUBITS for w in wires]`. Tying parameters across repeats but varying the wire indices implements a lightweight regularizer—akin to layer-wise rotational symmetry breaking—that can tighten margins without adding new keys.  

4. Implement deterministic entangler “dropout” across repeats. Inside `apply_ansatz_block`, collect all parametric two-qubit items into a list, compute `skip_idx = block_index % len(param_two_qubit_items)`, and skip the gate at that index during each repetition. Cycling through the XX/YY/ZZ gates per block mimics structural perturbations (as in Gen 9) and can sharpen worst-group margins while keeping the parameter count fixed.  

5. Extend the readout calibration to a trainable weighted sum over correlators. In `predict_batch`, slice out `M = len(READOUT_PAIRS)` new parameters (e.g. `params[N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS : N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS + M]`), apply a softmax to obtain weights `wₖ`, then compute `raw = sumₖ wₖ * zₖ` instead of the simple mean. Retaining the final gain and bias, this evidence-backed modification (inspired by readout‐weighting patterns) lets the model emphasize its most informative correlators, tightening classification margins with a modest param increase.
