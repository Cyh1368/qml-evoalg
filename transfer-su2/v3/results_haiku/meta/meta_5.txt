# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Shared rotations with sparse, learnable entanglers boosted out-of-sample performance  
  • Generation 2 (“8-Qubit Entangled Classifier with Shared Rotations”) achieved the top combined score 0.43 (test acc 0.78, gap 0.22) using just 11 parameters (9 distinct).  
  • Tying RY/RZ by qubit parity (two RY and two RZ keys) plus four nearest-neighbor XX and a single cross-layer YY parameter provided enough expressivity to fit and generalize.  
- Mixed Ising gates outperform fixed CZ chains  
  • Replacing linear CZ chains (Gen 0, score 0.29, gap 0.31) with parametrized IsingXX/YY entanglers (Gen 2) reduced depth (20→12) and gate count (93→66) while raising validation/test accuracy by ~0.2.  
- Low parameter counts with targeted symmetry  
  • Gen 2’s 11 total parameters gave a parameter efficiency of 0.07 versus 0.02 in Gen 0 (24 angles) and still matched training accuracy = 1.00.  
- Wide uniform initialization and early stopping stabilized training across candidate structures  
  • All top performers (Gen 2, Gen 3) used full-period uniform init and Adam with early stopping, preventing barren-plateau artifacts and ensuring meaningful comparisons.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Over-tying that collapses expressivity  
  • Gen 5 (“aggressive_param_sharing_ising_couplings”) tied all global rotations to seven parameters, yielding only test acc 0.50 (score 0.33) and near-zero margins.  
- Increasing entangling depth without breaking symmetry  
  • Gen 3 (shared_params_deep_entangle) stacked three repeat blocks of shared RY/RZ plus XX/YY/ZZ across all neighbor and long-range pairs. Despite depth 18 and 99 gates, test acc plateaued at 0.70 (score 0.34) and gap remained 0.30.  
- Relying solely on CZ chains  
  • Gen 0’s linear CZ entanglers with pre/post RZ rotations (24 parameters) overfit hard (train 0.88→test 0.56) and under-expressed dataset complexity.  
- Purely global rotations without localized couplings  
  • Gen 5’s global RY/RZ layers lacked the local structure needed, harming both margins (worst-group ~0) and test accuracy.

## Implementation Insights
- Parameter-key abstraction simplifies tied-param architectures  
  • The `validate_ansatz_spec` → `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` mapping in the best program cleanly handles shared-parameter indexing across repeats.  
- qml.IsingXX/YY gates reduce gate-count versus decomposed multi-gate constructs  
  • The Gen 2 ansatz uses `qml.IsingXX`/`qml.IsingYY` directly, shaving ~30% off gate count vs. CZ+single-qubit rotations in Gen 0.  
- Structured sparsity in entanglement rescues expressivity  
  • Focusing learnable XX on even–odd pairs plus a single cross-coupling (YY) strikes a balance between connectivity and parameter economy.  
- Parity-based rotation sharing preserves qubit heterogeneity  
  • Sharing RY/RZ parameters by even/odd parity retains minimal asymmetry needed for the classifier to distinguish state-vector features.

## Performance Analysis
- Score uplift correlates with mixed entanglers and shallow depth  
  • Moving from CZ-only Gen 0 (score 0.29, depth 20) → Gen 2 IsingXX/YY (score 0.43, depth 12) delivered +0.14 combined score.  
- Generalization gap shrank as targeted entanglers were introduced  
  • Gen 2 gap 0.22 vs. Gen 0 gap 0.31: XX/YY couplings improved out-of-sample margins (validation_margin 0.42 vs. 0.24).  
- Parameter efficiency peaks at sweet-spot around 10–12 params  
  • Gen 2 achieved 0.07 efficiency; going below (Gen 5, 7 params) under-expressed (efficiency 0.07 but test 0.50), while above (~14 params in Gen 3) increased gap without gain.  
- Worst-group margins track with sparse cross-coupling  
  • Gen 2’s worst-group margin 0.25 exceeded that of more heavily tied (Gen 5 ~0) or overly deep (Gen 3 ~0.20) models, highlighting the value of a single cross-YY link.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a sparse CRZ layer to complement your XX/YY entanglers. Add one or two CRZ gates (e.g. between qubits (1, 3) and (5, 7)) with a shared parameter key “crz_bridge”. This follows Gen 2’s pattern of mixed‐entangler benefits—CRZ injects phase correlations at minimal depth and gate count, potentially boosting out‐of‐sample margins without blowing up parameter budget.  

2. Alternate entanglement patterns across repeats instead of re-using the exact block. Define two sets of entangler parameters (e.g. “ent_0_a”…“ent_3_a” for even–odd XX in repeats 1&3, and “ent_0_b”…“ent_3_b” in repeat 2) and cycle them. This breaks the strict periodic symmetry of N_REPEATS=3, preserving parameter efficiency (~12–14 params) while giving the circuit the extra heterogeneity that helped Gen 3 generalize better when not purely over-shared.

3. Test N_REPEATS=2 with an added second cross-YY link to find a depth sweet spot. Reduce the number of block applications to 2 and simultaneously add an IsingYY gate between qubits (1, 3) under a new key “cross_b”. Gen 2 showed depth 12 was optimal; cutting repeats to 2 (depth ≈8) and introducing one more targeted cross-coupling may maintain expressivity and further shrink the generalization gap.

4. Increase rotation‐sharing granularity from parity to quartets for RY/RZ. Instead of two keys (“ry_a/ry_b”, “rz_a/rz_b”), define four keys—for wires [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7]—for both RY and RZ. This slight rise in distinct parameters (from 4 to 8 per layer) builds on parity-sharing’s success by adding controlled heterogeneity, which could lift test accuracy and worst-group margins without exceeding ~15 total params.

5. Add a targeted IsingZZ cross-coupling to rescue edge‐case correlations. Include a single IsingZZ gate between qubits (3, 4) under a new param “zz_bridge” in each block. Gen 2’s lone cross-YY link improved worst-group margins; adding a complementary ZZ link maintains sparsity while learning additional non-stoquastic structure, likely improving out-of-sample robustness with only +1 param.
