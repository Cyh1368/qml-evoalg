Analysis of the performance metrics shows:

**Current program (being improved):**
- Combined score: 0.54
- n_params: 13 (vs 9 in prior)

- validation_accuracy: 0.91 (vs 0.97 in prior)
- worst_group_margin: 0.30 (vs 0.32 in prior)
- generalization_gap: 0.14 (vs 0.04 in prior)

**Problem:** The addition of the mid-circuit rotation layer (_build_mid_rotation_layer) increased parameter count from 7 to 11 distinct parameters, but degraded performance significantly:
- Validation accuracy dropped from 97% to 91%
- Worst-group margin barely changed (0.32 → 0.30)
- Generalization gap worsened from 0.04 to 0.14
- The scoring penalty for extra parameters (-4 distinct params) outweighs any theoretical expressivity gain

**Root cause:** The metric explicitly states "Parameter economy matters at every count: fewer distinct trainable parameter names ALWAYS scores higher, with no point of diminishing returns." The mid-block rotation introduced 4 new parameter keys (ry_mid_even, ry_mid_odd, rz_mid_even, rz_mid_odd) that increased complexity without improving the worst-group separation.

**Solution:** Revert to a simpler, leaner architecture that maintains parameter economy while keeping proven entanglement structure:
1. Remove the mid-rotation layer entirely
2. Keep the initial RY, first entanglement (XX), cross-entanglement (YY), long-range (ZZ), and final RZ
3. Revert parameter naming to match the prior successful approach (ry_even/ry_odd naming without prefixes)
4. This restores the 7-parameter baseline that scored 0.60

The prior program with 7 distinct parameters achieved better validation accuracy (0.97 vs 0.91), better worst-group margin (0.32 vs 0.30), and lower generalization gap (0.04 vs 0.14), confirming that parameter economy is the dominant scoring factor.
