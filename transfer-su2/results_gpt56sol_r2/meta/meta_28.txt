# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Two-axis global rotations per block + linear CZ chain (ANSATZ_SPEC with `global_ry` and `global_rz`) × 6  
  • Current best (Seed program): n_params=14, depth=29, gate_count=138, test_loss≈0.01, perfect accuracy, converged in ≈90 steps.  
  • Matches Generation 9 seed and outperforms one-axis variants by halving test loss.  
- Single-axis global rotation per block + CZ chain × 6  
  • Gen 39 (“Shared-Theta”), Gen 49 (“Tied-RY-CZ-RZ”): n_params=8, depth=29, gate_count=138, test_loss≈0.02–0.03, converged in ≈60 steps, 100% accuracy.  
  • Delivers perfect generalization with reduced parameter count and faster convergence.  
- Fixed two-qubit ZZ-correlator readout + trainable gain/bias  
  • Used in all top performers (Seed, Gen 39, Gen 47, Gen 49) to stabilize losses (validation/test ≲0.05) and enforce zero generalization gap.  
- Six repeats of the ansatz block  
  • Present in Seed and all high-scoring individuals; balances expressivity and hardware complexity.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Omitting one rotation axis (pure single-axis) increases test_loss by ~2×  
  • Gen 39/Gen 49: test_loss≈0.02–0.03 vs. Seed’s ≈0.01, showing that adding RZ recovers missing expressivity.  
- Replacing CZ chain with ring-topology ZZ-Ising entanglers  
  • Gen 47 (“One-Parameter Ring”): depth increased to 54, convergence slowed to ≈420 steps, test_loss≈0.07 despite perfect accuracy.  
- Fully unshared per-qubit rotations (146+ params)  
  • Early generations: converged in ~30 steps but only ≈81% accuracy—parameter count alone fails without structured sharing.  
- Zero-parameter CZ-only ansatz  
  • Plateaued at ≈50% accuracy, confirming necessity of at least one tunable rotation axis.

## Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + param-key mapping  
  • Seed’s SPEC defines two gate entries per qubit (`"param": "global_ry"` and `"global_rz"`), yielding N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK=2 and simplifying design swaps.  
- Separation of StatePrep from ansatz  
  • Ensures consistent input loading and avoids accidental entanglement, as in all evaluated programs.  
- Pennylane QNode with backprop + qml.AdamOptimizer  
  • Universal across top performers—robust convergence for both one-axis (Gen 39/49) and two-axis (Seed) designs.  
- Early stopping with best-weight restoration (patience=75, min_delta=1e-4)  
  • Used in Seed and Gen 49 to halt at optimal validation loss, preserving lowest test_loss and preventing overfitting.

## Performance Analysis

- Test loss vs. parameter trade-off  
  • Two-axis Seed (14 params): test_loss≈0.01, efficiency=0.07  
  • Single-axis Gen 39/49 (8 params): test_loss≈0.02–0.03, efficiency=0.12  
  • Ring-ansatz Gen 47 (8 params): test_loss≈0.07, efficiency=0.11  
- Convergence scaling  
  • Seed (14 params): ~90 steps to threshold  
  • Single-axis (8 params): ~60 steps  
  • Ring topology: ~420 steps  
- Circuit depth/gate count optimum  
  • Depth=29, gate_count=138 (Seed and single-axis CZ) yields best balance; deeper (54) or sparser entanglers underperform.  
- Zero generalization gap across shared-parameter ansätze  
  • Seed, Gen 39, Gen 47, Gen 49 all achieve train=validation=test accuracy=1.00, confirming structured parameter sharing plus readout calibration drives robust generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace each fixed CZ in the chain with a parameterized controlled-phase gate (e.g. qml.CPhase) using one new “global_cphase” parameter per block, shared across all CZ wires in that block.  
   Evidence: the CZ chain is critical for entanglement depth (Seed), and prior small tests of tunable ZZ couplings suggest adjustable entanglement strength can further tighten the test loss without increasing circuit depth or gate count.

2. Introduce a two-cluster rotation sharing: split the 8 qubits into left (0–3) and right (4–7) halves, and in each block use separate (global_ry_L, global_rz_L) and (global_ry_R, global_rz_R) parameters.  
   Rationale: retains the Seed’s two-axis expressivity while giving each half-system modest independent flexibility; this coarse per-region unsharing adds only four parameters per block and may capture local state features better than fully global rotations.

3. Append a lightweight final calibration layer after the sixth block: apply two more shared rotations (e.g. one global_RY and one global_RZ across all qubits) before measurement.  
   Justification: the fixed readout with trainable gain/bias is proven to stabilize losses; a small extra global rotation layer can act as a final “basis tweak,” potentially shaving off residual test_loss with minimal extra depth.

4. Alternate block designs between full two-axis and single-axis rotations: in even repeats use both global_ry/global_rz, in odd repeats use only global_ry.  
   Benefit: harnesses the faster convergence of single‐axis blocks (Gen 39/49) and the superior expressivity of two-axis blocks (Seed), cutting total parameters by ~1/3 while preserving depth=29 and the CZ chain structure.

5. Replace the per-qubit RY→RZ pair with a single qml.Rot(φ, θ, λ) gate per qubit, but share only φ and λ as “global_rot1” and “global_rot2” per block (fix θ=0).  
   Insight: qml.Rot encodes SU(2) rotations more compactly; by tying two Euler angles globally you maintain two parameters per block but gain richer rotation flexibility than separate RY/RZ sequences, which could lower the ≈0.01 test_loss further.
