# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Shared even/odd single-qubit rotations plus a fixed CZ chain (Generation 2 “Shared-parameter single-qubit rotations … fixed CZ chain”) yielded perfect train/validation/test accuracy (1.00), zero generalization gap, mean validation/test loss ≃0.02/0.01, only 26 parameters, depth 35, 186 gates, convergence in ≃90 steps, and parameter efficiency ≃0.04.  
- Nearest-neighbor shared entanglers with even/odd RY/RZ (Generation 11 “ising_zz_nearest_neighbor_minimal”) matched perfect accuracy, zero gap, mean loss ≃0.03, with 26 parameters, reduced depth 24 and 138 gates, converging faster (~60 steps), and the same efficiency ≃0.04.  
- Selective layered ansatz with shared ZZ couplings (Generation 13 “zz_nearest_neighbor_minimal”) again hit 1.00/1.00/1.00 metrics, zero gap, mean test loss ≃0.02, with 26 parameters, depth 18 and only 90 gates—showing compact shared-parameter blocks can preserve expressivity.  
- Consistently, N_REPEATS=6 of a small shared-parameter block balanced expressivity and economy—every top design used six repeats and a two-parameter readout (gain/bias) to stabilize training and avoid overfitting.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-pruning the entangling layer to slash gate count (e.g., reducing to 90 gates in Gen 13) slowed convergence drastically (≈300 steps vs. 60–90 steps), indicating too-thin connectivity starves gradient information despite maintaining final accuracy.  
- Mixing in extra parametric two-qubit gates (CRX/CRY/CRZ or YY) as in Generation 6 (“add_longrange_entanglement,” 38 parameters, 138 gates) yielded a slightly lower combined score (0.99 vs. 1.00) and reduced parameter efficiency (~0.03), showing that extra gate types can bloat the circuit without performance gain.  
- Expanding single-qubit rotation axes beyond RY/assigned RZ phases (e.g., adding global RX layers in Gen 12) increased parameters (32) and gates (168) but did not lower test loss (≈0.03–0.04) or improve efficiency, indicating diminishing returns from axis enrichment.  
- Introducing long-range entanglers beyond nearest neighbors (Generation 9) inflated parameter count (62) and depth (30) yet delivered no accuracy or loss improvements over simpler topologies.

## Implementation Insights

- Formal ANSATZ_SPEC validation and a param-key→index lookup (as in the Generation 2 seed) prevent indexing errors and minimize runtime overhead when looping through repeated blocks.  
- Encapsulating the ansatz in apply_ansatz_block(params, block_idx) with explicit dispatch for single- and two-qubit gates yields concise, flexible code that cleanly supports ALLOWED_* gate sets.  
- Vectorizing readout measurements via pnp.stack over circuit_pair_expectations enables batched expectation computation for all ZZ pairs, significantly speeding up predict_batch throughput.  
- Initializing the calibration layer to gain=1 and bias=0 decouples quantum feature extraction from linear scaling, ensuring the ansatz focuses on shaping correlators before output adjustment.

## Performance Analysis

- All top circuits converge to perfect accuracy once parameter count crosses the ≃26-parameter threshold; adding parameters (up to 38 or 74) does not further reduce loss or improve generalization.  
- Depth and gate-count trade-offs govern convergence speed: the 35-depth CZ-chain design (Gen 2) converged in ≃90 steps, the 24-depth NN-ZZ design (Gen 11) in ≃60 steps, and the 18-depth compact design (Gen 13) in ≃300 steps—highlighting that moderate connectivity plus parameter sharing best balances gradient richness and training time.  
- Parameter efficiency (test accuracy / #params) peaks at ≃0.04 for all 26-param shared-parameter designs and drops to ≃0.03 when parameter count rises, confirming a sweet spot at aggressive parameter reuse.  
- Zero generalization gap across all successful programs underscores that combining shared-parameter ansätze with a trainable gain/bias readout and early stopping reliably prevents overfitting on this eight-qubit state-classification task.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate two-qubit entanglers between repeats  
   In odd‐indexed repeats keep the fixed nearest-neighbor CZ chain, and in even repeats replace each CZ with a shared‐parameter IsingZZ entangler (one “zz_shared” per block). This preserves the 4‐param/per‐block budget and injects richer two-qubit couplings every other layer—leveraging the perfect accuracy of ZZ‐based minimal ansätze while maintaining the proven convergence of the CZ chain.

2. Prepend a global RX mixing rotation in every block  
   Add a single trainable parameter “rx_mix” at the start of each ANSATZ_SPEC block, applying RX(rx_mix) on all eight qubits before the existing RY_even/odd layers. This low-overhead mixer has improved gradient flow in hybrid circuits and complements the even/odd RY pattern, potentially shaving 10–20% off convergence steps without increasing total parameters.

3. Insert one long-range XX entangler per block  
   After the CZ/ZZ layers in each block, append a single shared IsingXX gate between qubits 0 and 7 with its own “xx_long” parameter. Adding this one long-range coupling per block costs only one extra param and minimally increases gate count, yet taps into the modest convergence speedups seen when a single CRX/XX gate was introduced in earlier generations.

4. Split the readout gain into two group-wise gains  
   Instead of a single global gain, learn two gains—“gain_even_pairs” and “gain_odd_pairs”—that separately scale the average of ZZ correlators on even-starting versus odd-starting qubit pairs before applying the bias. This adds one parameter but lets the model differentially weight the most informative correlator subsets, building on the zero-gap stability of the calibration layer.

5. Tie blocks symmetrically to deepen stacking  
   Reduce the number of unique parameter sets by mirroring ANSATZ_SPEC params across repeats: share block 0⇄5, 1⇄4, and 2⇄3, so only three distinct parameter vectors are learned but still applied six times. This halves the unique-param footprint (from 26→14) while preserving full circuit depth, testing whether deeper repeated structure with aggressive sharing can match or exceed expressivity.
