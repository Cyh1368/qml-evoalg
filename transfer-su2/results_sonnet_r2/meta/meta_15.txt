# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Collective global operations + minimal symmetry breaking:  
  • The current best “Collective Spin Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 9) uses only three shared parameters per block (tilt, twist, phase) plus two symmetry-breaking rotations, repeated 6×, and achieves perfect 1.00/1.00/1.00 accuracies with test_loss ≈ 0.01, depth 72, gate_count 156, converging to 90% validation accuracy in ~30 steps.  
  • This demonstrates that one-axis-twisting–inspired global ZZ interactions on an 8-qubit ring, combined with collective RY/RZ rotations, deliver full expressivity at low parameter overhead.

- Highly shared, block-repeated ansatz:  
  • Repeating a compact 5-parameter block six times yields the same classification power as much larger circuits (e.g., Gen 11 with 60 circuit parameters), but with only 32 variational angles.  
  • Convergence is both fast (30–60 optimizer steps to threshold) and robust (zero generalization gap), showing that block-based repetition of shared operations is a scalable pattern.

- Simple correlator-based readout with gain/bias calibration:  
  • All top performers (Gen 9, 10, 13, 14) measure a fixed set of two-qubit Z–Z correlators and apply two trainable readout parameters.  
  • This lightweight readout suffices for perfect classification, avoiding the need for extra measurement layers or complex final rotations.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterization without expressivity gain:  
  • Gen 11 (“Shared-Parameter Ring Ansatz”) uses 62 circuit parameters (60 + 2 readout) but matches the 1.00 accuracy and test_loss ≈ 0.02 of the 32-param Gen 9, offering no loss improvement despite 1.9× more parameters and deeper circuits.  
  • The extra RY/RZ freedom did not translate into lower loss or faster convergence.

- Extreme parameter compression at the cost of convergence speed:  
  • Gen 13’s 14-parameter ansatz converges in ~150 steps—5× slower than Gen 9—though it reaches 1.00 test accuracy.  
  • Ultra-low parameter counts can impede the optimizer’s ability to finely tune correlations, increasing training time and potentially raising loss floors in larger tasks.

- Parity- or split-local entangler schemes without performance boost:  
  • Gen 10 (even/odd RY, shared ZZ, global RZ) and other parity-shared designs achieve perfect accuracy/test_loss ≈ 0.01 but do not surpass the current best in convergence speed or loss, despite structural complexity.

## Implementation Insights

- validate_ansatz_spec + ordered_unique_keys:  
  • Early validation of ANSATZ_SPEC catches key/wire errors up-front and yields a stable, ordered list of parameter keys used by apply_ansatz_block.  
  • In Gen 9 this prevented mis-indexing of the 5 per-block parameters across 6 repeats.

- apply_ansatz_block’s key→offset mapping:  
  • Centralizes parameter reuse logic—e.g., mapping “twist” to the same index across all ring edges—allowing a single parameter to control eight ZZ interactions.  
  • This pattern underpins the aggressive sharing in the best program.

- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue for free resource metrics:  
  • The current best program uses an AnnotatedQueue to extract depth and gate_count without executing the circuit, enabling rapid trade-off analysis (depth 72, gates 156) alongside accuracy/loss.

- IsingZZ for global entangling:  
  • Implementing one-axis twisting as qml.IsingZZ on each ring edge provides deep, delocalized entanglement with a single shared “twist” parameter, a key factor in the superior test_loss (≈ 0.01) of Gen 9.

## Performance Analysis

- Test loss vs. parameter count:  
  • Gen 9 (32 params) → test_loss ≈ 0.01  
  • Gen 10 (≈26 params) → test_loss ≈ 0.01, similar speed but no loss improvement  
  • Gen 11 (60 params) → test_loss ≈ 0.02, deeper and slower convergence  
  • Gen 14 (20 params) → test_loss ≈ 0.03, faster but looser fitting

- Convergence speed to 90% validation:  
  • Gen 9: ~30 global steps  
  • Gen 10/14: ~60 steps  
  • Gen 13: ~150 steps  

- Parameter efficiency (accuracy/param):  
  • Gen 9: 1.00/32 ≈ 0.03  
  • Gen 13: 1.00/14 ≈ 0.07 (high efficiency but slow convergence and potential scaling issues)  
  • Best trade-off achieved by Gen 9’s moderate 32 parameters—balancing fast training, low loss, and perfect accuracy.

- Depth/gate_count trade-offs:  
  • Gen 9’s depth 72, 156 gates outperforms Gen 11’s deeper circuits in loss and matches its accuracy, indicating that well-targeted global entangling beats increasing circuit width or depth.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend each block with a complementary global IsingXX entangler: in ANSATZ_SPEC, after the existing ZZ entries add for each ring edge  
   `{"gate":"XX","wires":[i,i+1],"param":"twist_x"}` (with i mod 8).  
   This leverages the orthogonal-entanglement benefits of mixed XX+ZZ couplings while preserving aggressive parameter sharing (one `twist_x` per block) and may drive the test_loss below 0.01.

2. Add a second minimal symmetry-breaking rotation on the qubit opposite to qubit 0: append to each block  
   `{"gate":"RY","wire":4,"param":"break2_ry"}` and  
   `{"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"break2_rz"}`.  
   Doubling the localized correction points refines the global one-axis twists with only two extra angles per block, potentially accelerating convergence without significant parameter overhead.

3. Enrich the correlator readout with one long-range Z–Z pair: include (0, 4) in READOUT_PAIRS and introduce a third calibration weight `"readout_w2"`.  
   This lightweight mutation (one extra measurement and one trainable weight) targets distant correlations missed by nearest-neighbor ring edges, and could lower validation/test losses without altering circuit depth.

4. Introduce per-block adaptive scaling of the three shared rotations: define six new parameters `"scale_0"`…`"scale_5"` and, in apply_ansatz_block, multiply that block’s `tilt`, `twist`, and `phase` angles by `scale_block_i`.  
   By modulating each repeat’s expressivity, this preserves the core 3-parameter block while giving depth-wise flexibility, which may tighten the optimizer’s control and speed up training to threshold.

5. Alternate the entangling graph across repeats while keeping parameter sharing: for odd-indexed blocks use the standard ring-edge IsingZZ, and for even-indexed blocks replace the eight ring edges with diagonal pairs [(0, 4),(1, 5),(2, 6),(3, 7)] all still using `"twist"`.  
   This minimal-overhead graph alternation broadens the global entanglement landscape and could reduce convergence steps or test_loss by exposing the circuit to richer connectivity.
