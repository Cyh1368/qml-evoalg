# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Global single-qubit parameter sharing + linear CZ chain → best score  
  • Generation 10 (Shared-Parameter RY-RZ Ansatz with Linear CZ Entanglement): combined score 0.72, 14 params, depth 35, 186 gates, perfect accuracies, zero generalization gap.  
  • Building on Gen 2/Gen 11’s shared-angle CZ-sandwich (score 0.68 with 20 params), reducing to 14 params in Gen 10 boosted the combined score by 0.04.  
- Pre- and post-entanglement RZ layers sandwiching a CZ chain is a robust “expressivity module”  
  • Appears in Gen 2, Gen 11, and Gen 10 — always yields 100% training/validation/test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
- Consistent circuit depth/gate count isolates parameter economy as the driver of efficiency  
  • All top shared-parameter ansätze hold depth = 35, gate_count = 186; score gains came solely from collapsing parameters.  
- Parameter reduction correlates with higher combined score despite slower convergence  
  • Gen 2/11 (20 params) converged in ~60 steps (score 0.68); Gen 10 (14 params) converged in 90 steps (score 0.72), demonstrating that a leaner parameter set can yield better overall performance even if optimization takes longer.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Full per-wire rotations vastly inflate parameter count with poor parameter efficiency  
  • Generation 0: 146 params → combined score 0.61, parameter efficiency 0.01; converged in 30 steps but scored lowest.  
- Introducing individually parameterized two-qubit Ising/XX gates without aggressive sharing  
  • Generation 1 (38 params, XX/YY chain): score 0.64; Generation 3 (29 depth, XX entanglers, 0.63 score). Gate-count savings didn’t offset the higher param count.  
- Partial or mixed parameter sharing with complex entanglement patterns underperforms  
  • Generation 4 (38 params alternating RY-RZ-CZ): score 0.64, 90 steps to converge; Generation 8 (extensive sharing + hybrid entangler) failed all tests (score 0.0), indicating expressivity collapse when sharing and entanglement aren’t well balanced.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Collapse multiple identical rotations into one trainable weight via repeated `"param"` keys  
  • E.g. ANSATZ_SPEC entries `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"ry_shared"}` for i=0…7. Gen 10 uses exactly two shared keys: “ry_shared” and “rz_shared.”  
- Sandwiching pattern:  
  1. Shared-RY layer across all qubits  
  2. Shared-RZ “pre” layer  
  3. CZ chain entangler  
  4. Shared-RZ “post” layer  
  This canonical block (Gen 2, 11, 10) consistently yields perfect generalization.  
- Minimal parameter sets can drive high expressivity if the entangler block is retained  
  • Gen 10 achieves higher combined score with only 14 params vs. Gen 2/11’s 20 params, showing that two well-chosen shared rotations suffice when paired with the CZ sandwich.  
- Trade-off: fewer parameters → slower convergence  
  • Gen 10 takes ~90 steps vs. ~60 in Gen 2/11, suggesting that extremely shared architectures may require more gradient steps but ultimately reach a better optimum.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Parameter count vs. combined score:  
  • 146 params (Gen 0) → 0.61  
  • 62 params (Gen 7) → 0.63  
  • 38 params (Gen 1/4) → ~0.64  
  • 20 params (Gen 2/11) → 0.68  
  • 14 params (Gen 10) → 0.72  
- Convergence trend: fewer parameters lengthen training  
  • Gen 0: 30 steps; Gen 2/11: 60 steps; Gen 10: 90 steps.  
- Gate count/depth held constant for shared-parameter designs (35 depth, 186 gates), isolating parameter economy as the key performance lever.  
- Generalization gap remains zero across all shared-parameter ansätze, confirming that parameter sharing not only improves efficiency but also promotes robust generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace each CZ in the linear chain with a parametric CRZ gate that all share a single new parameter `"phi_ent"`.  
   • In ANSATZ_SPEC, change every `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,i+1]}` to `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,i+1],"param":"phi_ent"}`.  
   • This keeps the nearest‐neighbor entangler topology intact while giving the optimizer control over entanglement strength, adding only one extra trainable weight (n_params→3).  

2. Double‐stack the canonical [shared‐RY]→[shared‐RZ]→[CZ‐chain]→[shared‐RZ] block end-to-end, but continue sharing `"ry_shared"` and `"rz_shared"` across both repeats.  
   • Simply append a second copy of the 35-depth block to ANSATZ_SPEC. Depth rises to ~70 and gate_count to ~372, but n_params stays at 2.  
   • This probes whether deeper expressivity (two repeats) with the same ultra-lean parameter set accelerates convergence or boosts the combined score beyond 0.72.  

3. Swap the shared RY layer for RX: use `{"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"rx_shared"}` on all qubits in place of RY, keeping the shared-RZ sandwich and CZ chain unchanged.  
   • This isolates the impact of rotation axis choice on expressivity and may uncover an RX/RZ variant that matches or exceeds the current best. n_params remains 2.  

4. Augment the entangling topology by closing the CZ chain into a ring: add `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[7,0]}` immediately after the existing linear CZs.  
   • Retains the same `"ry_shared"`/`"rz_shared"` layers; n_params=2, depth~36, gate_count~187.  
   • The extra connectivity could reduce convergence steps or lower validation loss by providing an alternative entangling pathway.  

5. Collapse all single-qubit rotations into one ultra-shared parameter `"theta_shared"`—i.e. replace every RY and RZ with either `{"gate":"RY","param":"theta_shared"}` or `{"gate":"RZ","param":"theta_shared"}`—to drive n_params down to 1.
