<DESCRIPTION>
The evidence across trials shows that fewer distinct trainable parameters consistently score higher, with the 4-distinct-parameter seed (ry_a, ry_b, rz, zz_ring) clearly outperforming the current 6-parameter variant (0.70 vs 0.67) despite very similar accuracy/margin numbers. Since parameter economy dominates the score with no diminishing returns, we push further by merging the two RY groups (ry_a, ry_b) into a single shared "ry" parameter, and simplifying back to the RZ-ZZring-RZ sandwich structure (drop the extra XX ring and rz_a/rz_b split) that already proved effective. This yields only 3 distinct parameters (ry, rz, zz_ring) while keeping the same qualitative circuit motif: a global RY layer, a phase layer around a fully-shared ZZ entangling ring. Reducing gate count and depth also helps convergence speed and train/test gap. The intended effect is to trade a small amount of raw expressiveness for a substantially better parameter-efficiency term and faster/more stable convergence, which the scoring function rewards heavily.
</DESCRIPTION>
