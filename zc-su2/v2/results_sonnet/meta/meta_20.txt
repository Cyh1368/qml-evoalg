# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-group RY sharing combined with a full-ring ZZ entangler and CNOT mixing  
  • Generation 16 (“Asymmetric Shared-Parameter Entangling Ansatz”) groups wires 0–3 vs 4–7 for RY, wraps a single shared zz_ring around all edges (including 7–0), and inserts forward/backward CNOT ladders. This design achieved the top combined score (0.70), perfect train/val accuracy, 99 % test accuracy, and converged in 30 steps.  
- Pre-/post-entangling reuse of a single global RZ parameter  
  • The “rz_p” parameter in Gen 16 is applied to all qubits before and after the ZZ ring. This reuse preserves phase flexibility without increasing the distinct-parameter count and contributes to a high validation margin (0.68).  
- Minimal distinct parameters with rich gate variety accelerates training  
  • By using only four distinct trainable names (ry_a, ry_b, rz_p, zz_ring) across six total parameters and mixing them with unparameterized CNOTs, Gen 16 halves convergence time (30 vs 60 steps) compared to purely shared-parameter ZZ-ring ansätze (Gen 14/18/19).  
- Asymmetric single-qubit rotation groups preserve problem structure  
  • Both Gen 14 and Gen 16 employ two RY groups to encode class-relevant asymmetry. Gen 16’s addition of CNOT ladders on top of that pattern yields the best generalization (0.01 gap) among evaluated circuits.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-collapsing all RZ gates into one global parameter without sufficient mixing  
  • Generation 15 collapsed pre- and post-layers into a single “rz” and scored 0.0, losing critical phase degrees of freedom.  
- Breaking the ring topology by omitting the wrap-around edge  
  • Generation 17 omitted the ZZ(7, 0) gate and failed validation entirely (score 0.0), demonstrating that full-ring connectivity is mandatory.  
- Pure shared-parameter ZZ entanglers without complementary mixing  
  • Circuits relying only on a single zz_ring and lacking forward/backward CNOTs (Gen 17) or richer single-qubit rotations (Gen 18/19) underperformed or converged slowly, indicating expressivity trade-offs.  
- Fixed CZ/CNOT chains without parameterized two-qubit phases  
  • Earlier CZ chain designs (e.g., Gen 7) reached only 0.64 combined score and exhibited low parameter efficiency (0.10) and worst-group margins (~0.34).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Clear ANSATZ_SPEC structure with named parameters  
  • Gen 16’s static list uses exactly four distinct names to control six parameterized gates, enforcing symmetry while limiting trainables.  
- Leveraging unparameterized gates for connectivity  
  • Forward/backward CNOT ladders in Gen 16 provide additional entangling depth at zero parameter cost, sharpening the optimizer’s ability to explore rich subspaces.  
- Dual application of “rz_p” enhances expressivity  
  • Reusing the same RZ parameter before and after the ZZ ring infuses extra phase tunability without increasing `n_distinct_params`, which boosts validation margin.  
- Two-group RY sharing maintains asymmetry and reduces overfitting  
  • Naming wires 0–3 as “ry_a” and 4–7 as “ry_b” captures class structure; this pattern consistently yields zero generalization gap when paired with sufficient entangling (Gen 14 and Gen 16).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score uplift from 0.67 → 0.70 by adding CNOT mixing  
  • Gen 14/18/19 (ZZ ring + shared RY/RZ, no CNOT ladders) all scored 0.67; Gen 16’s inclusion of forward/backward CNOTs raises the combined score to 0.70.  
- Halved convergence steps (60 → 30) with minimal extra gates  
  • Despite increasing depth from ~23 to 60 and gate count from ~117 to 138, Gen 16 converges in half the iterations of prior high-scoring designs.  
- Improved parameter efficiency (0.12 → 0.16) correlates with higher test accuracy  
  • Gen 16’s 6 parameters (4 distinct) yield a parameter efficiency of 0.16 versus ~0.12 in earlier shared-ZZ designs, supporting its superior 99 % test accuracy.  
- Validation margin trade-offs reflect richer entangling  
  • While Gen 12 (pure RZ-ZZ sandwiches) reached a 0.73 margin, Gen 16 balances a slightly lower margin (0.68) against a higher combined score and faster convergence, underscoring the value of mixing gates.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the sandwich motif to three blocks. After the final RZ_post layer in ANSATZ_SPEC, append a second copy of the entire RY→forward CNOT ladder→RZ_pre→ZZ_ring→backward CNOT ladder→RZ_post sequence, reusing the same parameter names (ry_a, ry_b, rz_p, zz_ring). This deepens the circuit boundary complexity while preserving the proven four-name parameter symmetry, which should tighten decision margins without doubling convergence time.  

2. Split the ZZ-ring into two alternating parameters. In each ZZ entangler, replace “zz_ring” with “zz_even” on edges (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7) and with “zz_odd” on edges (1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–0). This 1 → 2 increase in distinct two-qubit parameters enriches ring flexibility along even vs odd bonds—an expressivity boost that has raised margins in prior gens.  

3. Interleave a shared XX ring after each ZZ ring. Immediately following the last ZZ edge in the ring (before the backward CNOT ladder), insert for every neighbor pair [i, (i+1) mod 8] an XX gate with a single shared parameter “xx_ring.” Orthogonal Ising mixing has empirically lifted validation margins in related ansätze without inflating distinct-parameter count.  

4. Decouple pre- and post-ZZ phase rotations. Change the eight RZ gates before the ZZ ring to use “rz_pre” and the eight after to use “rz_post” instead of reusing “rz_p.” This adds one distinct parameter (n_distinct_params → 5) but gives independent phase control around the entangler, which can improve worst-group margin.  

5. Inject a mid-circuit asymmetric RY remix. After the backward CNOT ladder (just before measurement), insert another RY layer on wires 0–3 parameterized by “ry_a” and on wires 4–7 by “ry_b.” Reusing these two names re-emphasizes class structure at zero extra distinct parameters and can sharpen boundary refinement, boosting test accuracy.
