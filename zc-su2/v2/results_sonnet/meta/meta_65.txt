# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Cross-type parameter sharing under two scalar names (“ry_a” for all RY and “theta” for RZ/ZZ/XX) consistently lifted scores:  
  • Shared-parameter ZZ-CRZ entangling ansatz (Gen 63) reached 0.73 with 2 distinct names.  
  • The current best program (Gen 60/65 style) matched the top combined 0.74 by preserving exactly two parameter names.  
- Sandwiching parametrized blocks between global RZ(theta) layers amplified feature encoding:  
  • Gen 63 and Gen 60 both employed pre- and post-RZ(theta) and achieved rapid convergence (~30 steps) and high margins (validation_margin≈0.69).  
- Zero-parameter CNOT ladders before and after the entangling shells accelerated training:  
  • All top designs (Gen 60, Gen 63, current best) converged in ~30 steps versus 60+ in designs lacking these ladders (e.g., Gen 57).  
- Dense multi-shell entanglement (nearest-neighbor ring, hypercube/opposite links, skip-2, plus an XX ring) maximized expressivity per parameter:  
  • The jump from 0.73 (two-param ZZ-only shells in Gen 64) to 0.74 in the best program stemmed from adding the orthogonal XX ring while retaining two parameters.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-collapsing to a single parameter name led to under-separation of hard groups:  
  • Single-Parameter Shared Ansatz (Gen 59) scored only 0.69, with worst_group_margin=0.30 and a deep circuit (depth 129).  
- Restricting to CZ-only ladders plus basic ZZ rings severely under-expressed the circuit:  
  • Gen 61 (CZ ladders + ZZ rings) achieved a combined score of 0.40 and a large generalization gap (0.20).  
- Omitting cross-basis couplings (XX or YY) capped worst-group margins around 0.34:  
  • Shared-Parameter Multi-Shell Entangling Ansatz (Gen 62) hit 0.70 but had only ZZ/YY shells and moderate worst_group_margin=0.36.  
- Simply increasing depth with more identical ZZ layers (no new gate types) yielded diminishing returns:  
  • Earlier deep-ZZ seeds (e.g., Gen 43, depth 87) plateaued at ~0.69 despite large gate counts.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declare exactly two trainable names in ANSATZ_SPEC (“ry_a” and “theta”) and reuse them exhaustively:  
  • Centralizing all RZ/ZZ/XX under “theta” concentrates gradients and maintains parameter efficiency (0.25).  
- Inline zero-parameter CNOT ladders in the EVOLVE-BLOCK to provide mixing at no learnable cost:  
  • The current best program explicitly lists forward and backward CNOT chains, ensuring ~30-step convergence.  
- Explicit dictionaries with “gate”, “wires”, and “param” fields ensure consistent parameter sharing across multiple gate types:  
  • In the top program, every ZZ/XX instance references “theta” by name, preventing accidental parameter proliferation.  
- Systematically stack entanglement shells—nearest-neighbor ring, hypercube links, skip-2, then an orthogonal XX ring—to enrich connectivity without new parameters:  
  • This pattern in the best program broke the 0.73 ceiling and reached 0.74 combined score.
