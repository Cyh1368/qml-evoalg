## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two global, non-commuting mixer parameters (“global_mix” for all RY layers and “global_tilt” for all RX layers) combined with a three-layer CZ butterfly entanglement (distances 1, 2, 4) consistently yields top performance.  
  • Current Best (“Seed program”): combined 0.73, convergence 60 steps, gen-gap 0.02, worst-group margin 0.35.  
  • Gen 23/Gen 30 (“global_butterfly_mixer”): also 0.73 combined, depth 15, gate 84.  
- Shallow circuit depth (~15) and moderate gate count (~84) strike the optimal expressivity–trainability balance.  
  • Current Best: depth 15, gates 84, conv 60 steps.  
  • Deeper variants (Gen 28, Gen 33: depth 30, gates 168) match accuracy but double resource use with no speed gain.  
- Aggressive parameter tying across all qubits drives high parameter efficiency (~0.25) without sacrificing generalization.  
  • Current Best: n_distinct_params = 2, parameter_efficiency 0.25, validation_accuracy 0.98.  
  • Contrast: Gen 39 (“shared_long_range_xx”, 3 distinct) efficiency 0.20, combined 0.70.  
- Explicit, hard-coded CZ butterfly pairings guarantee the required locality/long-range mixing.  
  • Present in all top‐scoring circuits (Gen 23, Gen 30, Current Best).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-constraining all mixers into a single shared parameter (no second non-commuting axis) underfits and fails validation.  
  • Gen 38 (“tied_butterfly_mixer”): combined 0.0.  
- Schema/key inconsistencies in ANSATZ_SPEC break parsing and lead to 0-score outcomes.  
  • Gen 42 (“global_mix_augmented_butterfly”): used wrong “wire” vs “wires” → combined 0.0.  
- Increasing the distinct parameter count beyond two yields diminishing returns or worse.  
  • Gen 41 (“phase_aligned_butterfly”, 3 distinct): combined 0.71, depth 18 vs 0.73 at depth 15.  
  • Gen 33 (“compact_shared_xx”, 4 distinct): combined 0.69, parameter_efficiency 0.17.  
- Doubling circuit depth/gate count without reducing parameters simply inflates resource cost without improving accuracy or convergence.  
  • Gen 28/Gen 33 (depth 30, gates 168): combined 0.73/0.69, conv ~120 steps vs 60 steps at depth 15.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Maintain a flat list-of-dicts ANSATZ_SPEC: eight identical RY entries, three CZ layers of 4 gates each, then eight RX entries. This clarity avoids parser errors.  
- Use “wire” for single-qubit gates and “wires” for two-qubit gates consistently. The Current Best strictly follows this, unlike Gen 42.  
- Hard-code the CZ butterfly connectivity as explicit pairs ([0,1],[2,3],…, distances 1,2,4) to reproduce the exact 12 entanglers found in top circuits.  
- Assign only two distinct parameter names (“global_mix” and “global_tilt”) and reuse them across all qubits—this enforces a strong symmetry prior and yields parameter_efficiency 0.25.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Distinct-parameter count vs. combined score:  
  • 2 distinct → 0.73 (Current Best, Gen 23/30)  
  • 3 distinct → 0.70–0.71 (Gen 39: 0.70; Gen 41: 0.71)  
  • 4 distinct → 0.69 (Gen 33)  
- Convergence speed correlates inversely with parameter count and depth:  
  • 2-param, depth 15 → ~60 steps (Current Best)  
  • 3-param, depth 18–15 → ~60–120 steps (Gen 41: 60; Gen 39: 120)  
  • 4-param, depth 15 → ~90–120 steps (Gen 33: ~90)  
- Generalization gap remains minimal (~0.02) for the 2-parameter designs and slightly improves (<0.01) in some 3-parameter variants, but combined score and worst-group margin peak at 2.  
- Worst-group margin peaks (0.35) in 2-parameter circuits, dips in richer parameterizations (Gen 39: 0.32), indicating that extreme tying also bolsters subgroup robustness.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Partial symmetry breaking in the RY mixer layer: split your eight RY mixers into two groups (qubits 0–3 use “global_mix_a”, qubits 4–7 use “global_mix_b”) but keep the RX layer tied to “global_tilt.” This raises n_distinct to 3 in a minimal way, probing whether a mild relaxation of full symmetry boosts expressivity without blowing up parameter count.  

2. Interleave long‐range entanglement within the mixer block: apply RY(“global_mix”) on qubits 0–3, then all four distance-4 CZs, then RY(“global_mix”) on qubits 4–7, followed by the remaining distance-2 and distance-1 CZs, and finish with RX(“global_tilt”). This front-loads nonlocal correlations while preserving depth 15, leveraging early long-range mixing for faster convergence.  

3. Tunable medium-range phase coupling via “global_mix”: replace each distance-2 CZ with a CRZ gate parametrized by “global_mix” (no new distinct parameter). This recycles your high-efficiency mixer to control medium-range entanglement, potentially sharpening convergence as seen in other tuned entangler experiments.  

4. XX entanglers on medium links using the existing tilt param: swap out the distance-2 CZ layer for XX gates on the same qubit pairs, all using “global_tilt.” Prior trials with an XX-type “global_xx” showed promise; reusing your second mixer parameter keeps n_distinct=2 but explores non-Ising two-qubit interactions.  

5. Compare butterfly vs. ring local connectivity: after the main distance-4 and distance-2 layers, replace the distance-1 CZs with a nearest-neighbor CZ ring (e.g. [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7]) all using “global_tilt.” This swap keeps depth and gate count fixed and directly tests whether a ring-shaped local entangler outperforms the butterfly’s short-range pattern.
