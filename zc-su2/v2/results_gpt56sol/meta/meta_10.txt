# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Multi-scale “butterfly” entanglement layers drive expressivity without excessive depth.  
  • Tied-Mixer CZ Butterfly Ansatz (Gen 8) uses CZ layers at distances 1, 2, 4, depth 15, 84 gates → combined score 0.70.  
  • Parity-Tied 8-Qubit Ansatz (Gen 10) interleaves local CRZ/CRY and long-range CZ matchings, depth 28 → combined 0.66.  
- Moderate parameter tying fosters regularization yet preserves trainability.  
  • Gen 8 shares two RY mixer parameters (“mix_even”/“mix_odd”) + one global RX tilt → only 5 total, 3 distinct params, 100% train, 98% val/test.  
  • Gen 10 uses parity-shared RY (2 params), two distinct CRZ/CRY, one RX readout → 7 total, 5 distinct params.  
- Final global mixing layer converts phase entanglement into measurable populations.  
  • Both Gen 8 and Gen 10 apply a shared RX on all qubits after CZ layers, enhancing observable differentiation.  
- Balanced gate count and parameter efficiency correlate with top scores.  
  • Gen 8 achieves parameter_efficiency 0.20; Gen 10 at 0.14 vs per-qubit fully parameterized Gen 0 at 0.04.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-aggressive sharing with minimal parameters collapses expressivity (score 0.0).  
  • Parity Rotation CZ Ring Mixer (Gen 1): 2 shared RY + 1 RX mixer (3 params) → fails to converge or validate.  
  • 8-Qubit Layered RY-CZ-RX Ansatz (Gen 9): similar heavy tying + spec key errors → 0.0.  
- Omitting post-entanglement parameterized rotations starves the optimization landscape.  
  • 8-Qubit Layered CZ Ansatz (Gen 7) left out post-CZ RZ → 0.0 and no validation progress.  
- Shallow single-layer designs without parameterized two-qubit interactions yield zero training signal.  
  • Generations 2–6 variants all scored 0.0 despite various sharing schemas.  
- Inconsistent spec formatting blocks execution.  
  • Mixing `"wire"` vs `"wires"` fields caused schema violations in multiple patches.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Consistent gate-spec conventions are non-negotiable.  
  • The best programs uniformly use `"wire"` for single-qubit gates and `"wires"` for two-qubit gates, eliminating schema errors.  
- Even/odd shared mixers strike a balance between flexibility and compression.  
  • In Gen 8, alternating RY(“mix_even”) and RY(“mix_odd”) grants per-site phase control with only two parameters.  
- Multi-distance CZ topology captures both local and global correlations in few layers.  
  • The butterfly sequence (d=1,2,4) connects nearest, next-nearest, and half-chain qubits in 12 CZ gates.  
- A uniform RX “global_tilt” across all qubits post–entanglement enables efficient readout encoding.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Nonzero combined scores only occur when circuits blend multi-scale entanglement with at least one mixing layer before and after.  
  • Gen 0 (per-qubit RY–RZ–CZ–RZ) → 0.60; Gen 8 (butterfly + mixers) → 0.70; Gen 10 (parity + CRZ/CRY + CZ) → 0.66.  
- Depth vs. performance trade-off: deeper ≠ better past a threshold.  
  • Gen 8 (depth 15) outperforms Gen 10 (depth 28) and per-qubit Gen 0 (depth 20) despite fewer layers.  
- Parameter efficiency tracks generalization and convergence speed.  
  • Higher efficiency (Gen 8 at 0.20) yields 60-step convergence and a 0.02 generalization gap; lower efficiency (Gen 10 at 0.14) requires ~120 steps.  
- Worst-group margins remain a challenge even at top accuracy.  
  • Best observed worst-group margin is 0.35 (Gen 8), suggesting room for targeted robustness improvements.
