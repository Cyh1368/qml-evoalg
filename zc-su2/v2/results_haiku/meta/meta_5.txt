# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Shared single-qubit rotation parameters combined with grouped phase shifts.  
  • Generation 5 (Shared-Parameter RY-RZ-CZ Ansatz) uses one “ry_shared” for all RY’s plus four “rz_pre”/four “rz_post” groups. Achieves combined score 0.64 with only 11 gates and 9 distinct parameters.  
  • Generation 0 (Three-Layer Linear Entanglement) similarly uses pre/post RZ layers around a linear entangling block and scores 0.60 with 24 parameters.  
- Dual-layer phase compilation (pre- and post-entanglement RZs) consistently boosts validation accuracy.  
  • Both Gen 0 and Gen 5 implement RZ layers before and after CZ chains, achieving 0.99 validation accuracy and <0.20 loss.  
- Sparse long-range CZ entanglement augments nearest-neighbor chains for robustness.  
  • Gen 5 adds CZ gates on pairs [0–2,1–3,3–5,4–6,5–7] atop the linear backbone, improving worst-group margin to 0.34.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with generalization.  
  • Reducing to 9 distinct parameters in Gen 5 (param efficiency 0.09) yields a minimal generalization gap (0.01) while matching Gen 0’s perfect train accuracy.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-sharing of parameters cripples expressivity.  
  • Generation 1 and Gen 3 share only 3–4 unique parameters across RY/RZ/Ising layers, yielding combined score 0.0 and failing validation.  
- Shallow circuits lacking diverse two-qubit gates underfit the data.  
  • Generation 2’s single ZZ layer plus RY/RZ rotations provides too little entanglement variety, also scoring 0.0.  
- Typographical errors in entanglement blocks break coverage.  
  • Gen 3 mispairs qubits in its ZZ layer (duplicate [6,3], omits qubit 7), leading to failed validation despite multi-layer Ising gates.  

## Implementation Insights  
- JSON-style ANSATZ_SPEC definitions make it easy to iterate on gate/wire/param assignments without touching training logic.  
- Grouping qubits into blocks for shared-phase rotations (e.g., wires 0–1 share rz_pre_0, 2–3 share rz_pre_1, etc.) balances parameter count against local expressivity.  
- Explicit enumeration of CZ entanglers—first linear chain then select skips—yields stronger worst-case margins without exploding depth.  
- A fixed convergence schedule (60 steps) is sufficient for circuits of varying depth (20 in Gen 0, 27 in Gen 5), suggesting depth trade-offs don’t slow optimization.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score improvement from 0.60 (Gen 0) to 0.64 (Gen 5) came alongside a 2× reduction in distinct parameters (24→9) and modest depth increase (20→27).  
- All “0.0” scorers (Gen 1–3) violated validation by either over-sharing or missing two-qubit parametric gates, underscoring the necessity of both expressivity and correctness.  
- Perfect train accuracy (1.00) appears easy to achieve; the differentiator is validation/test performance (0.99) and minimal generalization gap, driven by balanced parameter sharing and entangling patterns.  
- Worst-group margin remains around ~0.34–0.35 for top performers, indicating further robustness gains may require new entanglement geometries or deeper layers.

For each of your long-range pairs (e.g. [0–2, 1–3, 3–5, 4–6, 5–7]), swap CZ→CRZ and assign them a single new parameter “crz_shared.” This draws on ALLOWED_PARAM_TWO_QUBIT_GATES to boost expressivity while keeping n_distinct_params near 10.

This consolidation halves the distinct phase parameters (e.g., from 8→4) and is predicted to reduce generalization gap, building on the dual-phase layer’s proven benefit.

5. Close the linear chain into a ring by appending CZ on [7, 0] at the end of the existing entangler block. This simple cyclic connectivity enhances symmetry and qubit mixing (a standard trick in QAOA/QNN ansätze) with just one extra gate and no new parameters, potentially improving both validation accuracy and worst-group robustness.
