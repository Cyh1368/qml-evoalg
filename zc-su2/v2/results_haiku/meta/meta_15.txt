# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Layered entanglement with three distinct blocks  
  • Shared-Parameter Layered 8-Qubit Ansatz (Gen 11, score 0.66) interleaves nearest-neighbor XX, offset YY, and long-range CRZ gates. This “XX→YY→CRZ” motif boosts worst-group margin to 0.35 and validation margin to 0.72.  
- Multi-phase rotations bracketing entanglers  
  • Gen 11’s initial RY(even/odd), mid-layer RY on half the qubits, and final global RZ create rich non-commutativity, driving perfect train/test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
- Minimal distinct parameters (~7–9) for expressivity  
  • Gen 11 uses 7 distinct parameters (93 gates, depth 20) and converges in ~30 steps. Gen 12 (8 params, score 0.65, 60 steps) and Gen 9 (9 params, score 0.63, 90 steps) follow the same trend—fewer distinct parameters tuned in layered structures yield faster convergence and higher scores.  
- Even/odd qubit sharing balances asymmetry and parsimony  
  • Across top scorers (Gen 11 & Gen 12), sharing RY across even wires vs. odd wires retains local variability while slashing parameter count, underpinning both rapid convergence and robust generalization.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Over-sharing across heterogeneous gates collapses expressivity  
  • Generations 13 and 15 (both score 0.0) applied aggressive global sharing (one parameter for all rotations or entanglers) and inconsistent spec keys (“wire” vs. “wires”), leading to validation failure.  
- Linear CZ-only entanglement under-entangles  
  • Shared-RY Linear CZ Ansatz (Gen 9) achieved only 0.63 and needed ~90 steps to converge. The lack of varied two-qubit interactions limited gradient richness and increased generalization gap to 0.01.  
- Gate-count bloat without new parameter diversity  
  • Gen 12’s addition of long-range ZZ couplers drove gate count to 84 but added no new distinct parameters (efficiency 0.10), yielding only 0.65 score. Depth/gate increases alone did not match the 0.66 peak.  
- Typographical/naming inconsistencies break the spec  
  • Generation 15’s mix of “wire” vs. “wires” and improper use of non-allowed two-qubit gates caused outright validation errors (score 0.0), highlighting the importance of a consistent declarative spec.

## Implementation Insights
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC list-of-dicts for rapid iteration  
  • All top programs use an explicit JSON-like gate specification; tweaking is as simple as adding/removing dict entries without touching training code.  
- Block-level parameter naming accelerates optimizer convergence  
  • Labels like “ent_nn”, “ent_off”, “ent_lr” in Gen 11 yield clearer gradient signals compared to generic “theta1,…,thetaN,” cutting convergence from 60 (Gen 12) to 30 steps.  
- Even/odd and phase-layer sharing patterns  
  • Gen 11 shares “ry_even” vs. “ry_odd” for the first layer and reuses “ry_mid” on alternate qubits—this structured asymmetry preserves expressivity with only 3 rotation parameters.  
- Uniform final RZ enhances margin without extra cost  
  • A single “rz_final” applied to all 8 qubits in Gen 11 boosts validation margin to 0.72, demonstrating that global phase adjustments can fine-tune class separations cheaply.

## Performance Analysis
- Score progression mirrors layering and parameter reduction  
  • Combined scores rose from 0.63 (Gen 9, 9 params) → 0.65 (Gen 12, 8 params) → 0.66 (Gen 11, 7 params) as entanglement variety and rotation layering increased.  
- Convergence speed correlates with block structuring  
  • Gen 9 needed ~90 steps, Gen 12 ~60 steps, and Gen 11 ~30 steps—each addition of a well-placed rotation or entanglement block strengthened gradients.  
- Depth and gate count plateau at optimal expressivity  
  • Both Gen 11 and Gen 12 settle around depth 20–18 and ~84–93 gates. Pushing beyond (e.g., depth 27 in other generations) without improved sharing did not increase scores.  
- Zero generalization gap is the hallmark of balanced sharing  
  • Gen 11 and Gen 12 both record 0.00 generalization gap; Gen 11’s extra layering edge raises combined score by 0.01. This confirms that well-tuned shared parameters prevent over- and under-fitting simultaneously.
