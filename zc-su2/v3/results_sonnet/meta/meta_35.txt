# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Dual-ring CNOT + disjoint CRZ entanglement  
  • Seed program (“current best”, Combined 0.41, gap 0.03) and Gen 20 best both interleave  
    – Ring 1: full 8-qubit CNOT cycle  
    – Disjoint CRZ(d) on (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7)  
    – Ring 2: offset CNOTs  
  • This pattern at depth 42/144 gates with 5 distinct parameters yields top val 0.58 and minimal gap.  
- Moderate circuit depth (~42–48) with 5 distinct parameters  
  • Seed (depth 42, n_distinct = 5) → Combined 0.41, gap 0.03  
  • Gen 31 (“Global-Shared Rotations with CRZ”, depth 48, n_distinct = 4) → Combined 0.40, gap 0.12  
  • Staying in this depth band maximizes expressivity per parameter without overfitting.  
- Final global RY layer sharpens decision boundaries  
  • Seed’s concluding RY(e) on all qubits aligns with Gen 20 and boosts validation by ~2–4% over circuits lacking it.  
- Explicit per-wire shared rotations for true global gates  
  • Seed lists RX(a), RY(b), RZ(c), RY(e) on each qubit rather than loop abstractions, ensuring consistent parameter reuse and circuit clarity.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive depth with heavy parameter sharing  
  • Gen 32 (“Shared-Parameter Ring-Entangler”, depth 87) → Combined 0.35, gap 0.27  
  • Gen 30 (“8-Qubit ZZ-CRZ Layered”, depth 34 but 117 gates) → Combined 0.34, gap 0.32  
  • Overlong ansätze drive overfitting without boosting validation.  
- Too few distinct parameters (<4) limits expressivity  
  • Gen 33 (“Parameter-shared 8-qubit entangling”, n_distinct = 3) → val 0.54, gap 0.15, efficiency 0.09  
  • Gen 29 (3-param, depth 36) hit Combined 0.40 but collapsed margins.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a parallel CRX(f) block immediately after the existing CRZ(d) gates, using the same disjoint pairs [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7] and a new shared parameter f. Cheap two-qubit rotations like CRX have proven high expressivity per depth cost, and combining them with CRZ phases can further boost validation without exceeding the 42–48 depth sweet spot. This directly builds on the seed’s successful CRZ(d) layer by enriching its non-Clifford entanglement profile.  

2. Split the single CRZ(d) parameter into two shared parameters—d₁ on pairs [0,1] & [4,5] and d₂ on [2,3] & [6,7]. Global insights show that adding just one extra phase parameter can give the circuit more nuanced control over opposite-qubit correlations, enhancing adaptability to diverse data features. This mutation adds zero depth but increases distinct parameters from 5 to 6, a minimal trade-off for potentially tighter generalization.  

3. Replace the second fixed CNOT ring with a “long-hop” CNOT pattern: CNOTs on [0→2], [2→4], [4→6], [6→0] and [1→3], [3→5], [5→7], [7→1]. You retain the four-gate depth of the offset ring while introducing medium-range mixing that can capture correlations the standard ring misses. This entangling variation stays within the seed’s depth/gate-count budget and may further shrink the generalization gap.  

4. Prepend a global ZZ(g) Ising layer before the RX(a) rotations, acting on opposite-qubit pairs [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] with a shared parameter g. Front-loading long-range interactions has been shown to amplify the efficacy of subsequent global rotations and entangling rings, sharpening the classifier’s decision boundary early in the circuit. This adds only one depth unit and one parameter, keeping the total within the optimal ≈42-gate-depth band.  

5. Refine the concluding RY(e) layer by splitting it into two shared rotations: RY(e₁) on qubits 0–3 and RY(e₂) on qubits 4–7. The seed’s final global RY(e) was critical for boosting validation by ~2–4%; this slight de-sharing (one extra parameter) lets the ansatz model potential left/right asymmetries in the data. You gain flexibility with minimal risk of overfitting, preserving the overall depth and gate count.
