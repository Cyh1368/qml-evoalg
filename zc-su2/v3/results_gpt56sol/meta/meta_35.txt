# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Single-shared mixer on alternating axes (RY on even, RX on odd) yields top performance. The current best (“shared_axis_brickwork”, Gen 24) hits combined 0.51 (train 0.94/val 0.78/test 0.78, gap 0.16, depth 12, 60 gates, param eff 0.26).  
- Three-block CZ entanglement (brickwork → staggered → antipodal) provides full-qubit connectivity with no extra parameters. Both Gen 24 (0.51) and Gen 21 (“parity_cz_global_mix”, 0.50) use exactly this pattern.  
- Shallow depth (≈12 layers) and moderate gate count (≈60 gates) balance expressivity and overfitting. Gen 24 and the Parity-Tied Two-Parameter Ansatz (Gen 30, combined 0.50 at depth 12) illustrate that deeper designs add complexity without score gains.  
- Avoiding RZ rotations preserves parameter efficiency and test accuracy. None of the top circuits (Gen 24, Gen 21, Gen 26) include RZ yet reach test ≈0.78; RZ-inclusive designs cap at ≤0.65 test accuracy.  

Ineffective Approaches  
- Introducing a second mixer parameter (Gen 30, combined 0.50) or adding XX two-qubit rotations (Gen 32/35, combined 0.49) yields only marginal improvements in training fit but worsens generalization (gap 0.17 vs 0.16) and lowers parameter efficiency (0.19 vs 0.26).  
- Over-tying parameters across dense entangling layers leads to underfitting. Gen 33 (“tunable_antipodal_crz”) shares only three distinct parameters over 60 gates and scores 0.41 with random-guess accuracies (0.50) and zero gap.  
- Excessive depth and added RZ/CRZ gates fail to boost scores. Gen 29 (depth 33, included CRZ) peaks at combined 0.45; Gen 34 (“tun​able_multiscale_ring”) with single shared CRZ attains only 0.40 and a 0.25 gap despite efficient entanglement.  

Implementation Insights  
- Define the mixer via a single named parameter (“shared_mix”) and apply it alternatingly:  
  • RY on qubits 0,2,4,6 and RX on 1,3,5,7 all tied to “shared_mix.”  
  • This front-loads superposition and doubles parameter usage without sacrificing expressivity.  
- Structure CZ entanglers in three explicit loops:  
  1) nearest neighbors [(0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7)]  
  2) staggered pairs [(1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,0)]  
  3) antipodal links [(0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7)]  
  This yields full connectivity at depth 12 with exactly 12 CZ gates.  
- Omit RZ gates entirely unless a clear expressivity deficit emerges. Top performers achieve high test accuracy and low depth/gate count by skipping RZ.  
- Limiting distinct parameters maximizes efficiency: Gen 24 uses n_params = 3 but only one distinct variable (eff 0.26), while Gen 30/32 use two distinct (eff 0.19) with no significant score gain.  

Performance Analysis  
- Distinct parameter count vs. combined score:  
  • 1 distinct → 0.51 (Gen 24)  
  • 2 distinct → 0.50 (Gen 30), 0.49 (Gen 32/35)  
  • 3 distinct → 0.41 (Gen 33)  
- Depth correlation:  
  • Depth 12 circuits (Gen 24, Gen 30) top at ≥0.50  
  • Depth 15 circuits (Gen 32/35) fall to ≈0.49  
  • Depth ≥18 circuits stagnate at ≤0.45  
- Parameter efficiency (distinct params / total params) tracks combined score:  
  • 0.26 (Gen 24) → 0.51  
  • 0.19 (Gen 30/32) → 0.49–0.50  
  • ≤0.14 (Gen 33/34) → ≤0.41
