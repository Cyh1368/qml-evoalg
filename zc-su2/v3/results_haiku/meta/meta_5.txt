# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating, subset-wise parameter sharing + global RZ pre/post with full Ising entanglers drove the highest combined score (0.58) in the 8-Qubit Shared-Parameter Ising Ansatz. Starting from the Alternating RY-RZ Entangling Ansatz (Combined 0.43), swapping in six distinct XX/YY/ZZ couplings raised test accuracy from 0.67 to 0.96.  
- Limiting distinct parameters to ~10 while using 12 total allowed rich expressivity without overfitting: the best circuit (depth 15, 90 gates) hit perfect train (1.00), near-perfect val/test (0.97/0.96) and a small gap (0.04).  
- Interleaving two RY parameters (ry_0 on qubits 0,2,4,6; ry_1 on 1,3,5,7) preserved heterogeneity in rotations with minimal overhead—a pattern shared by both the Gen 3 and Gen 4 top performers.  
- Wrapping entangling layers in shared RZ rotations (rz_pre, rz_post) consistently tightened decision margins and stabilized training (val margin 0.57, worst-group 0.28), compared to designs lacking these global layers.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Deep, unshared CZ-heavy circuits (Layered RY-RZ-CZ Ansatz: depth 20, 93 gates) severely overfitted (gap 0.31) and achieved a low combined score (0.29) despite maximal entangling.  
- Over-tying parameters to just three distinct values with CZ entanglers (Parameter-Tied CZ Ansatz) produced poor expressivity (test 0.59) and a moderate gap (0.16), even though it had the highest parameter efficiency (0.12).  
- Extreme parameter sharing in a shallow RY-XX-RZ design (3 distinct params, depth 12) capped combined performance at 0.42, confirming that too few trainable dimensions starve the ansatz of capacity.  
- Relying on a single two-qubit gate type (only CZ or only XX) failed to capture the problem’s full correlation structure, as seen in Gen 1 and Gen 5’s sub-60% test accuracies.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Assigning the same RY parameter alternately to even/odd qubits (ry_0 & ry_1) maximizes coverage of single-qubit rotations with just two variables.  
- Flanking entangling blocks with a shared RZ layer before (rz_pre) and after (rz_post) enhances phase flexibility and yields a tighter generalization gap (0.02–0.04 across top models).  
- Diversifying two-qubit interactions with XX, YY, and ZZ on both nearest-neighbor and cross pairs increases the ansatz’s expressivity more effectively than stacking additional layers of the same gate.  
- Capping distinct parameters around ten keeps convergence quick (≈150 steps) while maintaining high accuracy; expanding beyond that (as in the unshared CZ/RZ layering) led to overfitting.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Moving from Gen 3’s Alternating RY-RZ entangler (Combined 0.43, test 0.67) to Gen 4’s full Ising set (Combined 0.58, test 0.96) demonstrates that enriching entanglement types yields the largest single jump in performance.  
- Models with generalization gaps ≤0.07 (Gen 1, Gen 3, Gen 4) always outperformed high-gap circuits, but only Gen 4 paired a low gap (0.04) with high test accuracy.  
- Highest parameter efficiency (.12 in Gen 5) did not translate to top accuracy; the best trade-off was at efficiency 0.08 in Gen 4.  
- Depths beyond ~15 layers or gate counts above ~90 provided no accuracy benefit and exacerbated overfitting, indicating an optimal complexity sweet spot.
