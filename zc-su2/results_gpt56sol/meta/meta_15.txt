# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Single-layer parallel CZ matching + global-shared RY rotations yields top score (0.80):  
  • Program “single_matching_entangler” (Gen 10, close to current best) and the current best both use eight RY gates tied to a single logical parameter and one CZ layer on disjoint pairs.  
  • Depth 12, 72 gates, converges in ~60 steps with perfect train/validation/test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
- Global uniform RY parameterization provides sufficient expressivity with minimal overfitting:  
  • Reusing one RY parameter per qubit (“collective_ry”) across all eight wires matches performance of multi-parameter schemes.  
- Parameter efficiency ~0.12 correlates with maximal combined score (0.80):  
  • Eight-parameter ansätze (Gen 7 “collective_ry_colored_chain,” Gen 9 “globally_shared_ry_chain,” Gen 10 current best) uniformly reach 0.80.  
- Circuit depth reduction accelerates convergence without loss of accuracy:  
  • Moving from chain entanglers (depth 18–23; 90–138 gates; 90–120 steps) to one parallel CZ layer (depth 12; 72 gates; 60 steps) halves training steps while preserving perfect accuracy.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization with extra single-qubit layers yields low efficiency and score:  
  • Gen 2 “parity_shared_compact” (26 params, depth 29, 138 gates) scores only 0.66 despite perfect accuracy, parameter efficiency 0.04.  
- Adding shared RZ layers increases depth without gains:  
  • Gen 5 “shared_parity_layer” appends a global RZ on top of parity-shared RY, depth jumps to 29, combined score falls to 0.68, efficiency 0.05.  
- Ring-CZ topologies double convergence time versus matchings:  
  • Gen 11/13 “close_sparse_ring” and “close_cz_ring” achieve 0.80 but require ~210 steps (vs. 60 steps for one-layer matchings).  
- Under-coverage of qubits in rotation layers limits expressivity:  
  • Gen 12 (4-qubit shared RY) reaches only ~0.94 accuracy and 0.77 combined score, demonstrating a small generalization gap (~0.01).

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-wire repetition of identical params for collective rotations:  
  • Listing eight `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"collective_ry"}` entries in ANSATZ_SPEC creates uniform single-qubit coverage with minimal code.  
- One-layer parallel CZ matching minimizes depth:  
  • Specifying CZ gates on pairs `[0,1]`, `[2,3]`, `[4,5]`, `[6,7]` yields an entangler block of depth 1 and total depth 12.  
- Avoid unnecessary RZ or extra rotation layers:  
  • Simplifying the ansatz to just RY + one CZ layer (as in the current best) cuts gate count to 72 and reduces convergence steps.  
- Minimal logical parameter sets (eight independent RY angles) strike the balance between expressivity and hardware cost.

## Performance Analysis  
- Top combined scores (0.80) are reached only by eight-parameter, depth ≤ 18 designs:  
  • Current best (depth 12, 60 steps), Gen 7 (depth 18, 210 steps), Gen 9 (depth 23, 90 gates, 120 steps).  
- Parameter efficiency vs. score:  
  • Efficiency ~0.12 (8 params) → 0.80 combined; efficiency ~0.07 (14 params, Gen 8) → 0.72; efficiency ~0.04–0.05 (26–? params) → ≤ 0.68.  
- Depth vs. convergence:  
  • Depth 12 designs converge in ~60 steps; depth 18–23 chain entanglers take ~90–120 steps; depth 18 ring entanglers take ~210 steps.  
- Single parallel CZ layer suffices for zero generalization gap on this task, matching performance of more complex multi-layer entanglers at lower cost.
