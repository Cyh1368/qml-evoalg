# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parity-shared single-qubit rotations plus linear CZ chain entanglement (Alternating RY Linear-CZ Ansatz, Gen 3 & Gen 6) consistently hit combined 0.72, perfect accuracy, zero generalization gap, with just 14 parameters, depth 23, 90 gates.  
- Switching from ring to chain entanglement preserves accuracy (0.72) while halving convergence steps (chain: 90 vs ring: 180) and reducing depth/gate count (chain depth 23/gates 90 vs ring depth 54/gates 96).  
- Minimal parameter sets (two shared “ry_even”/“ry_odd” angles) suffice for expressivity—parameter efficiency of 0.07—matching or exceeding far larger ansätze.  
- Eliminating extra rotation layers around entanglers (vs. the 146-parameter RY–RZ sandwich, Gen 0) yields higher combined score (0.72 vs 0.61) by cutting circuit depth and gate overhead.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-sharing all single-qubit rotation parameters (Shared-parameter RY-CZ-RZ, Gen 1) collapsed expressivity, scoring 0.0 and failing validation.  
- Deep, high-gate-count designs (8-Qubit RY-RZ CZ Chain, Gen 0) achieved perfect accuracy but suffered from poor parameter efficiency (0.01) and lower overall score (0.61) due to unnecessary complexity.  
- Ring entanglement with only two rotation parameters (Alternating RY with Ring CZ, Gen 4) matched accuracy but doubled depth and training time versus chain, indicating over-entanglement can slow convergence.

## Implementation Insights  
- Define ANSATZ_SPEC with alternating RY gates using two shared parameters (“ry_even”/“ry_odd”) on qubit indices mod 2—this strikes the optimal balance of expressivity and compactness, as in the current best program.  
- Use a linear CZ ladder (wires [0,1],[1,2],…,[6,7]) rather than full ring to minimize circuit depth (23 vs 54) and gate count (90 vs 96) without accuracy loss.  
- Avoid sandwiching RZ layers around entangling gates unless task complexity demands it; in this problem, extra RZ/RY pre- and post-entanglers (Gen 0’s 35-depth, 186-gate circuit) yielded no accuracy gain.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score scales inversely with circuit complexity beyond a minimal threshold: 14-parameter/23-depth chains hit 0.72, while 146-parameter/35-depth circuits only reached 0.61.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates strongly with generalization: 0.07 efficiency (Gen 3/6) yields zero generalization gap and perfect test accuracy, whereas 0.01 efficiency (Gen 0) ties up resources without benefit.  
- Convergence steps reflect entanglement topology: chain ansatz converged in 90 steps vs ring in 180, reinforcing that sparser connectivity can accelerate training while maintaining performance.
