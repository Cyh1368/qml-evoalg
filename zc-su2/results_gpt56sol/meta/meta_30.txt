# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Dual-layer shared‐RY with parallel CZ matching  
  • Implemented in the current best program (Generation “stacked_shared_ry_cz”) and earlier Gen 16 (“shared_ry_cz_echo”), this pattern ties two full-register RY rotation layers to a single parameter “collective_mode,” interleaved with one or two disjoint CZ matchings. Both achieve combined score 0.80, perfect accuracies, zero generalization gap, and converge in 30–60 steps.  
- Parameter reuse via second RY layer (“phase echo”)  
  • Reusing the same “collective_mode” for a second RY layer (rather than introducing new angles) doubles circuit expressivity without increasing n_params. The current best program converges in 30 steps (vs. ~60 for single-layer) while maintaining top score.  
- Minimal‐depth CZ entanglement on disjoint pairs  
  • A single layer of four CZ gates on pairs [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7] suffices for full-register entanglement. Repeating this matching after the second RY layer (stacked_shared_ry_cz) retains score 0.80 with depth 24 and gate_count 144.  
- Eight‐parameter sweet spot  
  • All top‐scoring ansätze use exactly eight trainable parameters (parameter efficiency ≈0.12). Adding more parameters (14 in Gen 22/25) yields no accuracy gain and lowers combined scores to 0.71–0.72.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Excessive parameter tying without entanglement variety  
  • Gen 20 (“Shared RX/RY Ansatz with CZ Chain”) zeroed out (score 0.0) because using only two shared parameters for all RX and RY rotations overly restricts expressivity; a linear CZ chain alone can’t compensate.  
- Over‐minimal single‐layer ansatz  
  • Gen 29 (“Collective-Mode RY and CZ Ansatz”) scored 0.0 with only one shared RY layer and a single CZ layer. Feedback cites under-expressivity—deeper or additional trainable two-qubit gates are needed.  
- Heavy ring CRZ sharing and depth without efficiency  
  • Gen 25 (“Shared-Parameter RY+CRZ Ring Ansatz”) reached perfect accuracies but with 14 parameters, depth 18, convergence at step 990, and combined score 0.71. Although generalization was perfect, parameter efficiency suffered and optimization stalled near max steps.  
- Over-parameterized dual-axis schemes  
  • Gen 22/24 with 14–16 parameters (RX+RY or RY+XX layers) and depth 18 produced only combined 0.72. Extra axes didn’t translate to higher scores, suggesting diminishing returns beyond eight shared angles.

## Implementation Insights

- Gate‐list dictionaries for collective modes  
  • Repeating entries such as {"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"collective_mode"} for i∈0…7 succinctly ties rotations across the register. This pattern appears in all top programs (Gens 16, 19, 28, 30 and the current best), linking code brevity to high performance.  
- Strict spec syntax (“wire” vs “wires”)  
  • All successful ansätze use "wire" for single-qubit gates and "wires" for two-qubit gates. Misuse in lower‐scoring patches (e.g., Gen 17) led to spec parsing failures.  
- Depth extension via repeated blocks without new parameters  
  • Increasing circuit depth by simply repeating the same RY+CZ block (as in the current best program) boosts expressivity and halves convergence time (30 steps vs. ~60) without altering n_params.  
- Delegation to fixed backend  
  • All high‐performing programs rely on `_run(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` with no modifications to encoding or training logic, underlining that ansatz design alone drives the observed performance gains.

## Performance Analysis

- Top score plateau at combined 0.80 for eight‐parameter ansätze  
  • Both the current best program and previous single‐matching entangler designs (Gen 19, Gen 30) reach combined 0.80 with n_params = 8. No design has surpassed this score, underscoring the eight‐parameter threshold.  
- Convergence speed tied to block repetition  
  • The current best stacked design converges in 30 steps, half the 60–90 steps typical of single-layer variants, demonstrating that a second RY+CZ block accelerates optimization.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with score  
  • Ansätze reporting efficiency ≈0.12 hit combined 0.80; those at ≈0.07 (14 parameters) peak at 0.72. This confirms that fewer, well-shared parameters outperform more numerous, loosely shared ones.  
- Depth and gate count secondary to topology  
  • Depth varies from 12 (single‐match) to 24 (stacked), and gate counts from 72 to 144, yet all eight‐parameter, CZ‐matching topologies maintain perfect accuracy. Entanglement pattern and parameter sharing dominate performance outcomes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Stack a third RY–CZ block reusing “collective_mode.”  
   • After the existing second CZ layer, append another eight `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"collective_mode"}` followed by the same four parallel CZ matchings.  
   • Evidence: Depth extension via block repetition halved convergence time (30 vs. 60 steps) in the current best ansatz without adding parameters; a third block may accelerate it further.

2. Insert a shifted-CZ matching between the two RY–CZ blocks.  
   • Immediately after the first layer of CZs (and before the second RY echo), add four CZ gates on wire pairs [1,2], [3,4], [5,6], [7,0].  
   • Evidence: Two-edge-colored entanglers cover more graph edges while keeping depth-2 coupling; this was recommended in earlier insights to capture inter-block correlations with no param overhead.

3. Replace the second CZ layer with shared-parameter CRZ entanglers.  
   • Swap each `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,j]}` in the second matching for `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"crz_shared"}`, introducing exactly one new trainable angle.  
   • Evidence: Parameterized two-qubit gates can boost expressivity with minimal param increase; testing a single “crz_shared” probes whether tunable phases outperform fixed CZs.

4. Prepend a global collective RX rotation layer.  
   • At the very start, insert eight `{"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"collective_rx"}` before the first RY block, adding one new parameter.  
   • Evidence: Dual-axis control (RX+RY) in a shared fashion adds rotational flexibility to the landscape with only one extra angle, preserving high parameter efficiency.

5. Split the RY echo into two distinct shared parameters.  
   • Rename the first RY block’s “collective_mode” to “collective_mode1” and the second to “collective_mode2,” yielding two shared angles across wires.  
   • Evidence: Maintaining per-layer parameter reuse (phase-echo) doubles expressivity with just two parameters; a modest increase may push the combined score past the 0.80 plateau.
