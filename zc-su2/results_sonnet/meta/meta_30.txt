# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Tri-axis parameter reuse  
  • Generation 28 (“Single-Parameter Dense-CZ Ansatz for 8 Qubits”) applies the same “theta” in RX, RY, and RZ layers per qubit, extending the RY/RZ axis-switch trick to three non-commuting rotations. This yields a combined score of 0.80 with perfect train/validation/test accuracies and zero generalization gap.  
- Degree-3 CZ entangling graph  
  • The current best program’s two-loop construction (nearest-neighbor ring + antipodal CZ links) delivers high connectivity without extra parameters, compensating for extreme parameter sharing and converging in just 60 steps (depth 72, 216 gates).  
- Single-parameter expressivity  
  • Together with Generations 23 and 25, Generation 28 confirms that a solitary trainable θ reused across all qubits and axes suffices for full 1.00 accuracy (parameter_efficiency = 0.12), outpacing multi-parameter or tunable two-qubit gate designs.  
- Depth vs. convergence trade-off  
  • Compared to Generation 27’s dual-ring CZ ansatz (depth 84, 168 gates) and Gen 25’s ring+antipodal scheme (depth 66, 168 gates, 120 steps), Gen 28 balances a moderate gate‐count increase for a shallower depth and halves convergence time.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Parameter collapse without dense connectivity or correct specs  
  • Generation 30’s “Single-Parameter 8-Qubit Ring-Diameter Ansatz” collapses to one θ but fails validation (combined score 0.0), showing that extreme sharing must be paired with rigorous gate-spec formatting and sufficient entangler complexity.  
- Under-parametrized XX entanglers  
  • Generation 19’s shared-parameter RY/XX ansatz (combined score 0.68, 20 params) underperforms compared to CZ-based dense graphs, indicating that tunable XX gates alone don’t recover expressivity when rotation-layer parameters are limited.  
- Depth increase without topology enhancement  
  • Generation 16’s double-stacked shared-parameter block (depth 97, score 0.65) and Generation 17’s 20-parameter ring entangler (score 0.68) demonstrate that merely deepening or adding parameters without enriching connectivity yields diminishing returns.  
- Excessive coarse sharing  
  • Generation 26’s 2-parameter coarse-grained ansatz fails (score 0.0), confirming that too aggressive parameter reuse without distinct entangling angles cripples learning capacity and correctness.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Loop-driven tri-axis rotations  
  • Three successive loops over `range(N_QUBITS)` appending  
    {"gate": "RX"/"RY"/"RZ", "wire": wire, "param": "theta"}  
    succinctly encodes axis-switch reuse without bloating `n_params`.  
- Two-step CZ entangler assembly  
  •  
    for i in range(N):  
      ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,(i+1)%N]})  
    for i in range(N//2):  
      ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,i+N//2]})  
    yields degree-3 connectivity with zero additional parameters.  
- Consistent gate-spec keys  
  • Always use `"wire": int` for single-qubit and `"wires": [i,j]` for two-qubit gates to avoid silent validation failures (as seen in Gen 21/22).  
- Axis-switch across three non-commuting gates  
  • Extending the shared-θ pattern to RX, RY, and RZ multiplies the effective rotation space per parameter, a key factor behind Generation 28’s top performance.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Single-parameter ceiling at 0.80  
  • Both Generation 27 (dual-ring CZ) and Generation 28 (dense CZ) achieve the maximal combined score of 0.80 with parameter_efficiency = 0.12.  
- Convergence speed vs. gate count  
  • Generation 28 converges in 60 steps (216 gates, depth 72), outperforming Gen 25’s 120 steps (168 gates, depth 66) and Gen 27’s deeper dual-ring (depth 84). This suggests that modestly increasing gate count can drastically accelerate training.  
- Parameter count vs. topology  
  • Two-parameter designs (Gen 14) reach only 0.72 despite fast convergence (30 steps, 138 gates), while 20-parameter or tunable-XX designs (Gen 19) score 0.68. Top expressivity emerges from the interaction of minimal parameters with a rich, parameter-free entangler rather than from parameter quantity alone.  
- Zero generalization gap across top performers  
  • All programs scoring ≥ 0.68 exhibit perfect train/val/test accuracy and a 0.00 generalization gap, confirming that the successful patterns (extreme sharing + dense CZ graph) deliver both learning and generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Expand the entangling graph to degree-4 by adding “skip-2” CZ links. After your existing CZ loops, insert:
   for i in range(N_QUBITS):
       ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i, (i+2)%N_QUBITS]})
   This zero-parameter augmentation builds on the ring+antipodal motif in Generation 28, further enriching connectivity without increasing n_params, and may accelerate convergence via stronger long-range correlations.

2. Stack a second tri-axis rotation + dense-CZ block. Immediately after your final RZ loop, repeat steps 0–2 (RX loop, RY loop, ring+antipodal CZ loops) and close with another RZ loop reusing “theta.” Global insights show that a modest gate-count increase (two moderate-depth blocks) can slash convergence time, leveraging extreme parameter sharing twice.

3. Inject a shared-θ parametric two-qubit rotation on antipodal pairs. Replace—or append to—the antipodal CZs:
   for i in range(N_QUBITS//2):
       ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i, i+N_QUBITS//2], "param":"theta"})
   This probes whether adding a non-commuting entangling parameter, still shared globally, boosts expressivity beyond pure CZ connectivity without blowing up n_params.

4. Introduce two block-shared parameters (θ₁, θ₂) to decouple pre- and post-entanglement rotations. In the RX+RY loops use “theta1,” and in the final RZ loop use “theta2.” This raises n_params from 1→2—still extreme sharing—and tests if a tiny extra degree of freedom captures asymmetries, inspired by the marginal gains seen when moving from 1 to 2 parameters in earlier generations.

5. Mix CZ and CNOT in the nearest-neighbor ring. Modify your first entangler loop to:
   for i in range(N_QUBITS):
       gate = "CNOT" if i%2==0 else "CZ"
       ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":gate, "wires":[i,(i+1)%N_QUBITS]})
   By alternating gate types without extra parameters, you perturb the entanglement pattern in line with dense-graph principles, potentially unlocking faster convergence or increased robustness.
