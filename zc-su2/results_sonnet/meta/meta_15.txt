# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Extreme parameter sharing (one global RY + one global RZ) combined with a **closed-ring CZ entangler** yielded the highest combined score (0.72) in the “Global-Shared Rotations with CZ Ring Entangler” (Gen 11). Collapsing all single-qubit rotations to two parameters preserved full expressivity via the wrap-around entangling topology.
- Retaining the **RY→CZ→RZ** structure but replacing the open chain with a ring recoups expressivity lost in earlier global-sharing trials (e.g. Gen 15’s XX/CZ ring at 0.68) while maintaining only 14 parameters.
- Using distinct axis-switch parameters (“ry_global” vs. “rz_global”) across all qubits ensures a nontrivial entangled map despite extreme sharing—this axis reuse mirrors the Gen 7 pattern that halved parameters (from 52 to 26) and boosted score from 0.63 to 0.66.
- The closed-ring pattern evenly distributes two CZ interactions per qubit, which compensates for aggressive reduction in rotation parameters and underpins perfect train/validation/test accuracy with zero generalization gap.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Pairwise parameter sharing** (Gen 10 “Shared-Param RY-RZ CZ Ansatz”, 26 params) capped out at a 0.66 score despite 30-step convergence—indicating diminishing returns when grouping is too coarse.
- **Three-parameter group sharing** (Gen 12, 3 params) and **20-parameter global with extra XX injections** (Gen 15) achieved only 0.68, showing that adding multi-qubit gates or intermediate grouping fails to surpass the score unlocked by the ring-entangled global scheme.
- **Overparameterized designs** (>50 params, Gen 0) or **high-depth/up-circuit-count constructs** (e.g. Gen 2’s depth-108 ring) stagnated at ≤0.63, confirming that neither raw parameter excess nor depth alone yields top performance.
- Removal of pre-entanglement rotations without topology change (Gen 5) held the score at 0.62, underscoring that layer elimination alone is insufficient.

## Implementation Insights

- Explicitly listing each qubit’s gate in `ANSATZ_SPEC` with a shared `"param": "ry_global"` (and similarly for `"rz_global"`) makes **global sharing** transparent and easy to audit.
- Enumerating CZ edges `{"wires":[i,i+1]}` for i=0…6 plus `{"wires":[7,0]}` codifies a **closed-ring entangling backbone** that uniformly boosts expressivity.
- Naming the two axes separately (`ry_global` vs. `rz_global`) preserves the beneficial **axis-switch reuse** seen in Gen 7, while pushing parameter count down to 14.
- The compact ANSATZ_SPEC format (flat list of dicts) facilitates rapid iteration on grouping and entangling patterns without altering training or measurement code.

## Performance Analysis

- The global-shared ring (14 params, depth 60, conv 150) jumps from the 0.66 plateau of pairwise/three-group schemes to a 0.72 combined score, illustrating that **topology (ring vs. chain) can outweigh convergence speed (150 vs. 30 steps)** in final performance.
- Parameter efficiency peaks at 0.07 for the 14-parameter ring design, compared with 0.04–0.05 for higher-param or multi-gate variants—highlighting an optimal parameter window around 10–20 shared angles.
- Depth increases (29→60) and gate counts (138→144) relative to the shallow Gen 7 design are tolerated because the ring entangler fully leverages the extreme sharing to secure perfect accuracy and zero generalization gap.
- Across all perfect-accuracy circuits, **closing the entanglement loop** consistently boosts combined scores by ~0.02–0.06 over their chain-based counterparts, confirming the ring’s critical role in regaining expressivity under heavy parameter economization.
