<DESCRIPTION>
Replace the over-parameterized line-entangler block with a compact, symmetry-preserving cube mixer.
This ansatz uses only four shared trainable parameters per block: a collective RY input mixer, a collective RZ phase, a second collective RY mixer after full 3D cube entanglement, and a final collective RX readout mixer.

Why this should help:
- Much fewer parameters: 4 per block instead of 24, improving parameter efficiency and reducing overfitting.
- Better inductive bias: shared parameters enforce permutation-like symmetry across qubits, which is often beneficial when labels depend on many distributed binary features.
- Stronger entanglement coverage: the 3 perfect-matchings CZ pattern connects all qubits with short depth and better mixing than a single nearest-neighbor chain.
- Noncommuting single-qubit layers before and after entanglement increase expressivity while keeping the optimization landscape manageable.

This structure is aligned with the best-performing prior candidate and should improve validation/test accuracy while lowering the train-test gap and reducing the total circuit parameter count.
</DESCRIPTION>
