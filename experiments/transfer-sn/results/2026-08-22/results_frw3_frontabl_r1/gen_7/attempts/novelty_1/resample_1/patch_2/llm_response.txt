<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz utilizes a perfectly symmetric, collective parameterization (all 8 qubits share the exact same weights in each layer). While highly parameter-efficient and strongly regularized (resulting in a tiny train-test gap), it limits the model's capacity to recognize specific features. The encoded dataset couples binary features to distinct qubit pairs with varying information relevance. Forcing symmetric rotations prevents the ansatz from prioritizing more informative qubits.

By replacing the first collective `RY` layer with individual parameters per qubit, the ansatz can break permutation symmetry and dynamically scale/rotate the state on a per-qubit basis before entanglement. The phase shifts (`RZ`) and output readout mixers (`RY`, `RX`) are kept collective. This perfectly aligns with the final mean-Z readout logic—ensuring the output measurements remain unbiased and heavily regularized, while only requiring 11 parameters per block (68 total). This strikes an optimal balance between expressiveness, parameter efficiency, and generalization.
</DESCRIPTION>
