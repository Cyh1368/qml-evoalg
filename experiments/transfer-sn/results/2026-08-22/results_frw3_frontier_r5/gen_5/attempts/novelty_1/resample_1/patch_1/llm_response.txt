Looking at the performance data, the best design (combined_score=0.45) uses shared parameters across all qubits with full hypercube CZ entanglement. The current program uses per-qubit parameters (24 unique per block → 146 total params), leading to overfitting (gen_gap=0.07) and lower score.

My strategy: Take the proven shared-parameter approach and extend it with a **double entanglement sandwich** — two rounds of full hypercube CZ with an RY rotation between them. This creates richer multi-body interference while keeping parameters minimal (5 shared params vs 4).

<DESCRIPTION>
The key insight is that CZ-RY-CZ creates non-trivial multi-body correlations that a single CZ round cannot produce. With shared parameters, we get strong regularization (low generalization gap) while the doubled entanglement depth significantly increases expressiveness. 

Structure per block:
1. Shared RY input (1 param) — non-diagonal mixer
2. Shared RZ phase (1 param) — phase fine-tuning  
3. Full hypercube CZ (12 edges, 0 params) — entanglement round 1
4. Shared RY mid (1 param) — mid-circuit mixer creates interference
5. Full hypercube CZ (12 edges, 0 params) — entanglement round 2
6. Shared RY out + Shared RX out (2 params) — output measurement preparation

Total: 5 params × 6 blocks = 30 circuit + 2 readout = 32 params.
This is only 6 more than the best design but with dramatically richer entanglement structure.
