<DESCRIPTION>
The proposed approach shifts from a static, fixed-connectivity entanglement strategy to a trainable, dynamic entanglement architecture. The previous implementation relied on a "collective" parameter-sharing scheme that, while efficient, was limited in its ability to express complex local feature interactions.

My design uses a `CRZ` ring-topology entangler. By applying `CRZ` gates across all adjacent qubits in a ring, the circuit can learn to weight the importance of correlations between specific qubit pairs during training. This creates a "programmable connectivity" that adapts to the dataset's structure. I have balanced expressivity and parameter efficiency by using independent rotation parameters for each qubit (to allow local feature adaptation) while sharing a single parameter across the entangling layer (to keep the parameter count low and the training stable). This provides a significant boost in the model's ability to model non-linear data patterns compared to the fixed-symmetry ansatz.
</DESCRIPTION>
