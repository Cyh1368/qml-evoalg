# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Shared‐parameter rotational layers plus shared CRZ entanglers on alternating qubit pairs  
  • 8-Qubit Shared-Parameter Ansatz Classifier (Gen 9) uses fully shared RY (ry_0/ry_1), RZ (rz_0), and two CRZ parameters (crz_0/crz_1) per block. Achieved combined_score_raw = 0.92, test_accuracy = 0.93, generalization_gap = 0.00, depth = 87, gate_count = 276.  
  • 8-Qubit Shared-Parameter CRZ Ansatz (Gen 8) used the same pattern with separate blocks (122 params) and reached combined = 0.84, gap = 0.07.  
- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats  
  • Present in all top performers (Gen 2’s Re-uploading Controlled-RY Ansatz with combined 0.89; Gen 9 with 0.92) to enrich embedding while retaining generalization.  
- Trainable output gain and bias calibration  
  • All high-scoring circuits (Gen 2: combined 0.89; Gen 9: 0.92) include final affine scaling, reducing variance in raw expectation means and tightening test/train gap.  
- Moderate depth (~80–90) and gate count (~230–280)  
  • Circuits clustered in this “sweet spot” (Gen 9: depth 87; Gen 2: 81; Gen 5: 83) outperform both shallower and deeper variants.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Large unfactored parameter counts without sharing  
  • 8-qubit ansatz-based binary classifier (Gen 6) with 146 params, depth 136 achieved only combined_score_raw = 0.70 and generalization_gap = 0.07.  
  • Deep, wide multi-parameter CRY/CRX layers increased expressivity but led to low parameter efficiency (0.01) and sub-0.75 test accuracy.  
- Overly deep circuits and high gate counts without focused entanglement  
  • 8-Qubit Parity Ansatz Binary Classifier (Gen 3) used depth 93, gate_count 324, 32 params—scored only 0.87 combined with gap 0.04—suggesting parity-based entangling is less efficient than alternating shared CRZ.  
  • 8-Qubit Entangled Layered Classifier (Gen 7) with two shared-parameter repeats also depth 93, gate_count 324 but lower combined = 0.85 and gap = 0.08 due to mixed CRX/CRZ pairs.  
- Unfocused two-qubit gate mixing  
  • Circuits combining CRX, CRY, CNOT, CZ without parameter reuse (e.g., Gen 1 and Gen 4 from prior evaluations) showed strong train accuracy but poor test generalization (gaps 0.10–0.16).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Parameter‐key indexing and block offsets simplify shared reuse  
  • Current best (Gen 9) defines PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK = ["ry_0","crz_0","rz_0","crz_1","ry_1"] and uses `key_to_position` plus `block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` to apply shared params across all qubits and repeats.  
- Alternating diagonal and shifted entanglement maximizes correlation with minimal depth  
  • CRZ on (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7) then (1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0) in each block spreads interactions in two layers, matching the 87-depth/276-gate profile of Gen 9.  
- Full‐qubit shared rotations enforce global coherence  
  • Using one shared RY per layer (`ry_0` and `ry_1`) and one shared RZ (`rz_0`) reduces parameter dimensionality from ~30/qblock in unfactored designs to 5/qblock without sacrificing expressivity.  
- Embedding via three fixed IsingZZ uploads remains essential  
  • All top models (Gen 2, Gen 9) interleave three feature_map calls, supporting rich nonlinear feature encoding without overfitting when paired with parameter sharing.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Parameter count vs. combined score correlation  
  • Gen 9: 32 params → combined 0.92;  
  • Gen 3: 32 params (parity entanglement) → combined 0.87;  
  • Gen 6: 146 params → combined 0.70.  
  Indicates that low param counts alone are insufficient; structured sharing with effective entanglement is critical.  
- Depth/gate sweet spot around 87–93 depths and ~276 gates  
  • Gen 9 (87/276) outperforms Gen 7 (93/324) and Gen 3 (93/324), confirming that focused entanglement patterns beat brute-force depth increases.  
- Generalization gap driven to zero by strict sharing + CRZ patterns  
  • Only Gen 9 achieved gap = 0.00; Gen 2 had gap = 0.03, Gen 8 gap = 0.07, underscoring that fully shared parameters with CRZ yield optimal overfitting control.  
- Parameter efficiency (test_accuracy / n_params) peaks with Gen 9 at 0.03  
  • Ties with parity Ansatz (Gen 3) but yields far higher combined score (0.92 vs. 0.87), demonstrating quality of parameter utilization via shared CRZ blocks.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Append a static CZ “ring” entanglement at the end of each ansatz block.  
   After the final RY layer in ANSATZ_SPEC, add CZ gates on wires (0,1), (1,2), …, (7,0). This costs zero extra parameters, leverages the proven efficacy of CZ entanglers in spreading correlations, and should boost expressivity while keeping depth in the 87–93 sweet spot.

2. Introduce upload-specific CRZ parameters for richer interleaved entanglement.  
   Replace the shared keys “crz_0” and “crz_1” with “crz_0_upload{k}” and “crz_1_upload{k}” for k in {0,1,2}. This only adds 6 parameters (2 per upload) but lets the circuit learn distinct coupling strengths at each of the three feature-reupload stages, building on the success of shared-parameter CRZ and further reducing any residual generalization gap.

3. Alternate rotation axes in the shared single-qubit layers.  
   Rename the first shared rotation param to “rx_init” and apply RX on all qubits there, keep the middle RZ layer as “rz_0”, and change the final layer to a shared “rx_final” RX gate. By adding just two parameters you diversify rotation axes—an evidence-based expressivity boost—while still enforcing global coherence and low parameter count.

4. Trade ansatz repeats for more frequent feature embeddings.  
   Set N_UPLOADS=4 and N_REPEATS=1 so you still apply four ansatz blocks (total depth ≈ 87–90) but interleave embeddings four times instead of three. All top performers used ≥3 re-uploads to enrich the embedding without overfitting; this variation maintains parameter efficiency while exploring whether a denser re-upload pattern yields higher combined scores.

5. Make the input-encoding scale trainable.  
   Replace the fixed FEATURE_SCALE = π/2 in feature_map() with a scalar parameter “feature_scale” that is optimized alongside circuit params. This single new parameter allows the ansatz to adjust overall embedding strength per IsingZZ upload, potentially tightening feature nonlinearization and further improving generalization.
