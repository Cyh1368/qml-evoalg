<DESCRIPTION>
The current program achieves excellent performance (test_acc=0.93, generalization_gap=0.00, only 32 parameters) through aggressive parameter sharing. However, to improve further, I propose a hybrid approach that balances parameter efficiency with per-qubit expressiveness.

**Rationale:**
1. Current design uses mostly shared parameters (ry_init, rz_mid, etc.), which is great for generalization but may limit qubit-specific adaptation.
2. Prior programs with fully individual parameters (188/146 params) had worse generalization, suggesting we need moderate sharing.
3. Solution: Introduce **per-qubit RY rotations** (more expressive than shared) while keeping **shared RZ and entanglement layers** (efficient). This increases parameters per block from 5 to 11 unique types.

**Expected impact:**
- Slight increase in parameters (32 → ~68 total) but still highly efficient
- Better per-qubit tuning capability while maintaining good generalization
- Faster convergence due to more direct parameterization
- Mix of individual and shared parameters balances expressiveness and regularization

**Changes:**
- Replace shared `ry_init` with 8 individual RY gates (ry_0 through ry_7)
- Replace shared `ry_final` with 8 individual RY gates (ry_final_0 through ry_final_7)
- Keep shared RZ parameter (rz_mid) and CRZ entangling pairs for efficiency
- Add one extra RX layer with shared parameter for additional rotation coverage
