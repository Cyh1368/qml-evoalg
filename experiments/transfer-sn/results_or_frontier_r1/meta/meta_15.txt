# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Permutation symmetry via collective rotations  
  • Sharing single-qubit RY/RX mixers at the input and collective RZ/RX at the output across all 8 qubits (e.g. Permutation-Invariant Gen 9: combined_raw 0.94; Permutation-Equivariant Gen 14: combined_raw 0.95) delivers top accuracy (test 0.96) and minimal generalization gap (0–0.02) with only 26 parameters.  
- Full-graph CZ entanglement organized in perfect-matching layers  
  • Seven perfect-matching CZ layers (Gen 12 and Gen 14) exploit gate commutativity for parallel execution, boosting expressivity in low-parameter circuits (20–26 params) and achieving combined_raw ≥ 0.93.  
- Repeated feature-map uploads with small ansatz repeats  
  • N_UPLOADS = 3 interleaved with N_REPEATS = 2 ansatz blocks stabilized training and enforced consistent data encoding in all top performers.  
- Trainable scalar output calibration (gain, bias)  
  • Calibrating the mean Z-expectation via gain and bias parameters consistently improved alignment to ±1 labels, present in every >0.93-score program.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Large per-block parameter counts (>50)  
  • Layered and hypercube ansätze with 194–242 params fell to combined_raw 0.76–0.79 and exhibited generalization gaps of 0.09–0.11, indicating overfitting despite moderate depths (≈97–135).  
- Hardware-efficient or non-symmetric entanglers  
  • Custom evolved or chained two-qubit layers without global symmetry (Evolved Ansatz: combined_raw 0.83) under-utilized parameter sharing and failed to match complete-graph expressivity.  
- Excessive block repeats or bespoke gate layers  
  • Deep circuits with no parameter sharing often did not converge before early stopping (score < 0.90), revealing training instability in high-param regimes.  
- Sparse connectivity trade-offs  
  • Degree-3 cycle-graph CZ designs (combined_raw 0.90) cut gate count but plateaued below full-graph performance, suggesting limited expressivity.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec  
  • Encoding the gate sequence as a Python list and auto-validating parameters/key ordering enabled rapid iteration on shared-parameter designs (used in Gen 9, 12, 14).  
- Pennylane autograd QNode with backprop  
  • Leveraging backprop differentiation drove fast convergence (~150 steps for Gen 12; ~120 steps for Gen 14) under Adam optimization.  
- AnnotatedQueue for metadata extraction  
  • Programmatic depth and gate-count measurement guided topology choices (e.g. trading 396→444 gates for +0.02 combined_raw in Gen 14).  
- Early stopping with restore-best-weights  
  • Monitoring validation loss with patience = 75 prevented overfitting in low-param circuits, ensuring the highest test accuracies.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score progression vs. entanglement topology  
  • Cycle-graph (combined_raw 0.90) → Collective Mixer + Full-Graph CZ (0.93) → Permutation-Equivariant full CZ + extra mixers (0.95) maps a clear path: adding symmetry and minimal additional rotations yields +0.02–0.05 gains.  
- Depth/gate-count trade-off  
  • Gen 12 (depth 117, gates 396) vs. Gen 14 (depth 141, gates 444) shows that increasing circuit depth by ~20% with +6 parameters can lift combined score from 0.93 to 0.95 while keeping generalization gap at 0.02.  
- Parameter efficiency vs. absolute accuracy  
  • 20-parameter models peaked at 0.05 accuracy/param (combined_raw 0.93), but the highest absolute accuracy (combined_raw 0.95) required 26 params (efficiency 0.04), illustrating a Pareto frontier between economy and expressivity.  
- Consistent low generalization gaps  
  • All high-scoring symmetric designs maintain gaps ≤ 0.02, confirming that parameter sharing and symmetry are key to robust generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace each fixed CZ layer with a parameterized perfect-matching CRZ layer.  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, group the full-graph CZs into the seven perfect-matching sets (as in Gen 12/14) and swap each CZ for CRZ using a single shared key per matching (e.g. `"crz_match_0"`, …, `"crz_match_6"`). This exploits gate commutativity for parallelism, keeps N_PARAMS_per_block≈26, and adds tunable entanglement strength—leveraging the success of collective mixers plus full-graph connectivity.  

2. Introduce upload-specific feature-map scales.  
   Replace the global FEATURE_SCALE with three trainable scalars (`"scale_u0"`, `"scale_u1"`, `"scale_u2"`) and modify feature_map to use `FEATURE_SCALE = feature_scales[upload_index]`. Prior work showed N_UPLOADS=3 stabilizes training; adding per-upload scaling lets the circuit adapt encoding strength over successive reuploads with only +3 parameters.  

3. Share ansatz parameters across repeats within each upload.  
   In apply_ansatz_block, set `offset = upload_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` (ignoring repeat_index) so both repeats draw from the same 26-parameter block. This enforces intra-upload permutation symmetry, halves total unique circuit parameters (from 26×3×2→26×3), and tightens generalization gaps as seen in highly symmetric designs.  

4. Insert a minimal two-axis mixer between entanglement layers.  
   After each perfect-matching CRZ set, add collective RY and RX rotations (one shared RY and one shared RX key) before proceeding to the next block. This echoes the proven pattern of two-axis mixers boosting expressivity with minimal overhead (only +2 parameters per intermediate mixer) and can capture phase information more effectively than a single post-entanglement mixer.  

5. Learn qubit-wise readout weightings instead of uniform averaging.  
   Replace the scalar mean of Z-expectations in predict_batch with a weighted sum using 8 trainable weights (one per qubit), followed by the existing gain and bias calibration. This small extension (+6 parameters) builds on the benefit of output calibration and allows the model to emphasize high-signal qubits—potentially raising test accuracy beyond 0.96 while keeping generalization gap ≤0.02.
