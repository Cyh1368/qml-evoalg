# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-axis permutation-equivariant input mixing plus collective output rotations  
  • Permutation-Equivariant 8-Qubit Binary Classifier (Gen 19) shares a single RY and RX angle across all qubits at the input, and collective RZ/RX at the output, achieving combined_raw 0.95 (train 0.99/val 0.97/test 0.96) with only 26 parameters.  
  • This two-axis mixer improves over Gen 17’s single-axis RY input design (combined 0.93, 14 params).  
- Full-graph CZ entanglement via perfect-matching layers  
  • Gen 19 and Gen 18 employ full 8-qubit connectivity in seven CZ matchings (Gen 19: depth 123/gates 444 → 0.95; Gen 18: depth 147/gates 492 → 0.94), balancing expressivity with parallelism.  
  • Switching from bipartite K4,4 (Gen 16: combined 0.93, 20 params, depth 99) to full-graph layering yields +0.01–0.02 in combined score.  
- Repeated three-upload, two-repeat data re‐upload  
  • All top performers (Gen 16–19) use N_UPLOADS = 3 with N_REPEATS = 2, stabilizing encoding and pushing consistent gains.  
- Trainable gain/bias readout  
  • Every ≥ 0.93 model includes two scalar output calibration parameters, systematically reducing bias and improving alignment to ±1 labels.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Large per‐block parameter counts (> 50)  
  • Gen 15’s 194-parameter multilayer CZ ansatz severely overfits (train 0.97/val 0.86/test 0.84, gap 0.13) and scores negative combined (−1.03).  
- Deep, high-gate designs with insufficient sharing  
  • Gen 18’s 32 params and 492-gate circuit yields only 0.94 combined—marginally above Gen 17—while parameter efficiency drops to 0.03 and convergence slows (~180 steps vs. ~120 for Gen 19).  
- Single-axis or no mixing on inputs  
  • Gen 17’s use of only shared RY in-mixers (no RX) caps performance at 0.93 combined, underperforming the two-axis approach in Gen 19.  
- Shallow sparse entanglers  
  • Cycle-graph or hardware-efficient non-symmetric entanglers (earlier gens) plateau at ≤ 0.90–0.92, highlighting expressivity loss.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC for permutation equivariance  
  • Gen 19’s spec lists eight identical RY/RY gates (“collective_ry”/“collective_rx_in”), 28 CZ matchings, and eight shared RZ/RX output gates, enabling concise shared-param definitions and automated validation.  
- Efficient parameter indexing via key_to_position  
  • Mapping parameter keys to positions and applying a block_offset in apply_ansatz_block avoids manual indexing and scales naturally with N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS.  
- Pennylane backprop QNode with early stopping  
  • All top models leverage PennyLane’s autograd QNode and AdamOptimizer, reaching convergence thresholds (~0.90 val accuracy) in ~120 steps (Gen 19), with patience = 75 preventing overfitting.  
- AnnotatedQueue for automated depth/gate metrics  
  • Extracting depth and gate_count programmatically informed the trade-off decisions between Gen 18’s deeper 492 gates and Gen 19’s more efficient 444 gates.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score progression vs. entanglement & mixing  
  • Gen 16 (bipartite K4,4): 0.93 combined, 20 params, depth 99 → Gen 17 (complete-graph one-factorization): 0.93, 14 params, depth 111 → Gen 18 (full-graph CZ block): 0.94, 32 params, depth 147 → Gen 19 (full-graph + two-axis mixing): 0.95, 26 params, depth 123.  
- Parameter count sweet spot around 20–30  
  • Peak combined score (0.95) achieved at 26 params (Gen 19) vs. diminishing returns beyond 32 (Gen 18) or under-parametrization at 14 (Gen 17).  
- Depth/gate trade-off  
  • Increasing depth by ~10% and gates by ~28% from Gen 17 → Gen 19 lifts combined by +0.02, while an additional +20% depth (Gen 18) yields only +0.01.  
- Robust generalization maintained (gap ≤ 0.02)  
  • Symmetric designs from Gen 16 to Gen 19 keep generalization gaps ≤ 0.02, confirming parameter sharing’s role in controlled overfitting despite deeper circuits.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace static CZ layers with parameterized perfect-matching CRZ blocks  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, remove the 28 individual CZ entries and instead define seven perfect-matching groups, each using a shared CRZ parameter (e.g. “crz_match_0” … “crz_match_6”) applied in parallel to the four edges of that matching. This preserves full-graph connectivity, exploits gate commutativity for parallelism, and adds just seven tunable entanglement strengths per block—leveraging the proven gains of perfect-matching entanglers and collective mixers.  

2. Introduce per-upload feature‐map scaling  
   Replace the fixed FEATURE_SCALE constant with a trainable vector feature_scales of length N_UPLOADS, add parameters “scale_u0”, “scale_u1”, “scale_u2”, and modify feature_map to use FEATURE_SCALE = feature_scales[upload_index] for each reupload. This +3-parameter change lets the circuit adapt encoding strength across the three uploads, building on the stability of N_UPLOADS=3 and offering finer control over feature injection.  

3. Share ansatz parameters across repeats within each upload  
   In apply_ansatz_block compute offset = upload_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK (ignoring repeat_index) so that both repeats under a given upload draw from the same 26-parameter block. This halves the total circuit parameters (from 26×3×2=156 to 26×3=78), enforces intra-upload symmetry, and tightens generalization gaps as observed in highly shared permutation-equivariant designs.  

4. Interleave two-axis mixers between entanglement layers  
   After each perfect-matching entanglement (whether CZ or CRZ) in ANSATZ_SPEC, insert collective RY and RX operations—using new shared keys like “collective_ry_mid_k” and “collective_rx_mid_k” for the k-th insertion. This distributes the proven two-axis mixing power throughout the circuit, increases nonlocal expressivity with minimal overhead (+2 parameters per insertion), and can capture more complex phase relationships than a single end-of-circuit mixer.  

5. Upgrade readout to trainable qubit-wise weighting  
   In predict_batch, append a trainable weight vector of length 8 (e.g. “readout_w0”…“readout_w7”), compute raw = dot(readout_weights, z_expectations) instead of mean(z), then apply the existing gain and bias. This +6-parameter extension gives the model the flexibility to emphasize high-signal qubits—building on the success of output calibration and aiming to push test accuracy beyond 0.96 while preserving a tight generalization gap.
