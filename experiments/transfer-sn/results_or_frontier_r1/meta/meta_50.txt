# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Split perfect-matching CZ entanglement around a single shared two-axis mid-circuit mixer:  
  • Current Best (“8-Qubit Re-Uploading Quantum Classifier”): combined_score_raw = 0.96, test_acc = 0.98, N_PARAMS = 32, depth = 135, gate_count = 540.  
  • Gen 46 Phase-Coupled Ansatz: combined_score_raw = 0.95, test_acc = 0.97 with the same 4+1+3 matching schedule and one shared mid-layer parameter.  
- Global collective RY/RX mixer parameter tying at both input and output stages:  
  • Current Best and Gen 46 both tie “collective_ry”/“collective_rx_in” for all qubits, yielding smoother convergence (~270 steps) and zero generalization gap.  
- Three feature-reuploads × two ansatz repeats:  
  • All top designs (Current Best, Gen 46, Gen 44) converge by ≈120–270 steps with N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2; this layout stabilizes training and underpins the depth-540 sweet spot.  
- Minimal extra degrees of freedom for non-commutativity:  
  • Using a single “collective_mid” parameter for both RY and RX lift expressivity, outperforming full two-axis mid layers without ballooning the parameter count.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Omission of any mid-circuit two-axis mixer:  
  • Gen 44 Layered CZ Mixer (no mid mixer) reached only combined_score_raw = 0.94 and test_acc = 0.95 despite depth = 147 and 636 gates.  
- Over-parameterized brick-wall entanglement:  
  • Gen 47 Brick-wall Ansatz used 194 parameters, depth = 93, gate_count = 324, but grossly overfit (train_acc = 0.90 vs test_acc = 0.76, gap = 0.14, combined_score_raw = 0.67).  
- Dense untied CRZ couplings:  
  • High-param CRZ variants (80 params, depth = 135/540 gates) in earlier gens peaked at combined_score_raw ≈ 0.93–0.94 and showed generalization gaps of 0.03–0.04, indicating overfitting from lack of parameter sharing.  
- Shallower or deeper circuits without structural innovation:  
  • Gen 41 Ring-Chord (depth = 111, 372 gates) capped at combined_score_raw = 0.91; adding CZ layers beyond 147 depth without mid mixers (depth > 147, 600+ gates) also fails to improve accuracy.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC with key-to-position mapping simplifies parameter tying and mid-layer insertion:  
  • Current Best’s succinct spec cleanly interleaves CZ matchings and tied rotations by indexing PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK.  
- Single shared “collective_mid” parameter for both RY and RX reduces N_PARAMS per block from 6→5 while preserving non-commutativity:  
  • Compared to separate mid-RY/RX, this halves the extra degrees of freedom per block without loss in combined_score_raw.  
- Modular separation of feature_map() and apply_ansatz_block() fosters clarity and reusability:  
  • All top programs isolate fixed IsingZZ uploads from the parametrized ansatz, ensuring a uniform training pipeline.  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript metadata extraction gives exact depth and gate counts for precise resource accounting:

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate CZ and shared-CRZ matchings in the entangling layers. For example, swap the CZ gates in matching layers 2 and 5 for CRZ gates using new param keys like `"crz_match_2"` and `"crz_match_5"`, each tied across their four two‐qubit wires. This preserves the depth-135/540-gate schedule while introducing fine-grained, globally shared entangling angles—leveraging the mid ground between hard CZs and over-parameterized two-qubit rotations shown to boost combined_score_raw without inflating the parameter count.  

2. Introduce per-upload mid-circuit mixer parameters. Replace the single `"collective_mid"` key with three keys `"collective_mid_u0"`, `"collective_mid_u1"`, and `"collective_mid_u2"`, each tied across all qubits but unique to upload 0, 1, or 2. This adds only two extra degrees of freedom yet lets the ansatz adjust its two-axis non-commuting mixer per data re-upload, building on the stability of N_UPLOADS=3 and the expressivity gains of a shared mid mixer.  

3. Aggressively tie the mid-circuit mixer globally across all blocks. Collapse every block’s `"collective_mid"` into a single `"collective_mid_global"` key, shared across both uploads and repeats. By slashing 5 block-level parameters, you test whether further parameter efficiency can be achieved—potentially pushing parameter_efficiency above 0.03—while maintaining the zero generalization gap and combined_score_raw ~0.96 that mid-mixer designs afford.  

4. Augment the feature map with a tied pre-rotation per upload. Before each `feature_map`, insert a global RY rotation on every qubit parameterized by `"pre_mix_u{upload_index}"`, e.g. `"pre_mix_u0"`/`"pre_mix_u1"`/`"pre_mix_u2"`. This +3-parameter tweak enriches the initial data embedding basis—letting the circuit learn to align input features in Bloch space ahead of the proven IsingZZ layers—without altering depth or gate count.  

5. Vary perfect-matching order per ansatz repeat. In your ANSATZ_SPEC builder, cyclically shift the list of the seven perfect matchings by `repeat_index` (mod 7) for the two repeats within each upload. This dynamic re­indexing preserves the K8 covering structure and 4+1+3 mixer schedule but breaks residual commutativity between repeats, boosting expressivity at zero extra parameters—mirroring the success of upload-level matching cycling.
