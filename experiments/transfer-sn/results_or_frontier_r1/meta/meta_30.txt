# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Multi-stage permutation-equivariant mixing (input RY/RX → split CZ matchings → mid-circuit RY/RX → output RZ/RX)  
  • Seen in the current best program (Gen 28 “two_axis_midpoint_mixer”): combined_score_raw 0.95, test accuracy 0.97, generalization_gap 0.01.  
- Edge-colored complete-graph CZ entanglement via seven perfect-matchings, broken by a mid-circuit two-axis mixer  
  • Boosted expressivity: Gen 28 lifted test accuracy by +0.01 over Gen 25 (both raw 0.95), at a modest depth increase (123→135) and gate count (444→540).  
- Shared (collective) single-qubit rotation parameters  
  • Enforces permutation equivariance with few parameters; used in Gen 21, Gen 25, and Gen 28 to hit raw ≥ 0.95 while keeping N_params per block small.  
- Three feature-upload cycles × two ansatz repeats  
  • Uniform across all top scorers (Gen 16–23, Gen 28), stabilizing encoding of 28 binary features without over-deepening circuits.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Omitting a mid-circuit non-commutativity breaker  
  • Gen 25 (no mid mixer) stalled at test accuracy 0.96, gap 0.02, raw 0.95; Gen 27 (sparse_ring_ansatz) only raw 0.88.  
- Adding depth/gates without new mixing layers  
  • Gen 26 (depth 195, 1 020 gates) fell to raw 0.82 despite full connectivity—resource bloat without expressivity gain.  
- Cyclic CZ schedules in lieu of perfect-matchings  
  • Gen 29 (“cyclic_staged_mixer”) achieved raw 0.90 vs 0.95 for perfect-matching + mid-mixer, showing that edge-coloring plus mixers is more effective than cyclic staging alone.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + key_to_position mapping  
  • Facilitates insertion of mid-circuit mixers (`collective_ry_mid`, `collective_rx_mid`) between CZ layers, as in Gen 28.  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript introspection  
  • Yields exact `depth`/`gate_count` for each candidate, enabling data-driven acceptance of Gen 28’s +12% depth for +1% test accuracy.  
- Block-indexed parameter layout  
  • `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` × `N_UPLOADS` × `N_REPEATS` automatically scales circuit-parameter slicing in `apply_ansatz_block`, simplifying ansatz variants.  
- PennyLane Autograd QNode + Adam + early-stopping with restore-best-weights  
  • Delivered stable convergence (~150 steps for Gen 28 vs ~120 for non-mid designs) and reproducible generalization performance.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Parameter count vs test accuracy  
  • Increasing from 26 params (Gen 25 test 0.96) to 38 params (Gen 28 test 0.97) delivered the top test accuracy, at the cost of a slight drop in parameter efficiency (0.04→0.03).  
- Generalization gap reduction  
  • Inserting the mid-circuit mixer cut the gap from 0.02 (Gen 25) to 0.01 (Gen 28), improving robustness.  
- Depth/gate trade-off  
  • Gen 28’s 135-depth/540-gate circuit outperformed Gen 21/23’s 123-depth/444-gate designs by +1% test accuracy, justifying the extra layers.  
- Mixing necessity for deep designs  
  • Purely deeper entangling layers (Gen 26) without additional mixing underperformed, whereas depth paired with non-commuting mixers (Gen 28) yielded top scores.

1. Replace each CZ in the seven perfect‐matchings with a parameterized CRZ gate, sharing one parameter per matching (“crz_match_0”…“crz_match_6”).  
   • In your ANSATZ_SPEC, swap every {"gate":"CZ", "wires":[i,j]} in matchings 1–7 for {"gate":"CRZ", "wires":[i,j], "param":"crz_match_k"}.  
   • This preserves the edge-coloring parallelism while giving the optimizer fine-grained control over entanglement strengths—leveraging the commutativity of each perfect-matching for depth efficiency, as in Gen 24’s successful matching-based expressivity boost.

2. Introduce trainable per-upload feature scaling in feature_map.  
   • Add three new parameters “scale_u0”, “scale_u1”, “scale_u2” and multiply FEATURE_SCALE by scale_u{upload_index} inside feature_map before each IsingZZ.  
   • This +3-parameter change retains N_UPLOADS=3’s proven stability while letting the circuit learn to weight early vs. late classical data injections, sharpening encoding where it most improves boundary separability.

3. Tie parameters across the two repeats within each upload to enforce intra‐upload symmetry.  
   • In apply_ansatz_block, compute offset = upload_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK (ignore repeat_index) so both repeats draw from the same slice of parameters.  
   • Halving the number of block parameters builds on the proven benefits of shared “collective_*” rotations for permutation equivariance and should reduce overfitting by shrinking the search space without sacrificing the mixer–entangler structure that yielded test 0.97.

4. Add a second mid-circuit two-axis mixer between the final CZ matching and the output mixer.  
   • In ANSATZ_SPEC, after Matching 7 but before the “collective_rz” block, insert shared {"gate":"RY",…,"param":"collective_ry_mid2"} on all qubits followed by {"gate":"RX",…,"param":"collective_rx_mid2"}.  
   • Mirroring the success of the first mid-mixer in Gen 22 and Gen 28, this extra non-commutativity breaker further disrupts commuting entangling layers and can improve expressivity for minimal added depth and just +2 parameters.

5. Enrich the final output mixer to a three-axis SU(2) ladder.  
   • Replace the last shared RX(collective_rx) in ANSATZ_SPEC with a sequence RX(“mix_out_rx1”), RY(“mix_out_ry”), RX(“mix_out_rx2”), all on each qubit.  
   • This +3-parameter composite mixer augments the simple RZ–RX readout with a richer local rotation, enabling more flexible classification boundaries without increasing two-qubit gate depth, in line with the two-axis mixer’s proven benefits.
