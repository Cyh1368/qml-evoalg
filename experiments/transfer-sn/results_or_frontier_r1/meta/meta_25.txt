# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Two-axis permutation-equivariant mixing at both input and output  
  • Seen in Gen 19, Gen 21, Gen 23, and the current best Gen 22 (“Complete-Graph CZ Mixer”)  
  • Each applies shared RY and RX across all qubits before entangling, and shared RZ/RX after, yielding combined scores of 0.95 and maintaining strong generalization (gap ≤ 0.02, down to 0.01 in Gen 22)

- Split complete-graph CZ entanglement with a mid-circuit two-axis mixer  
  • Gen 22 divides the 28-edge CZ block into two commuting halves, inserting a second shared RY/RX mixer (“mid_ry”, “mid_rx”) to break commutativity  
  • This doubles effective entangling depth without inflating parameter count (38 params), achieves combined 0.95, test accuracy 0.97, and shrinks generalization gap to 0.01

- Three feature-uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  • Uniform across all top-scoring models (Gen 16–23), stabilizes encoding of 28 binary features  
  • Ensures expressivity without over-depth, consistently hitting ≥ 0.94 combined

- Trainable gain and bias readout calibration  
  • Present in every ≥ 0.93 model, reduces output bias, tightens decision boundary  
  • In Gen 22, contributes to a stable 0.25 test loss with 0.97 accuracy

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterized two-qubit gates (CRZ-only entangler)  
  • Gen 24’s 68-parameter “parameterized_crz_perfect_matchings” fell to 0.90 combined, generalization gap 0.03, parameter efficiency 0.01  
  • Deep, fully entangling CRZ layers without strategic commutativity breaking leads to resource bloat and overfitting

- Omitting mid-circuit commutativity breakers  
  • Gen 20’s “Collective Mixer” (26 params, depth 123) plateaued at 0.94 combined and gap 0.02  
  • Full-graph CZ in one block cannot match the expressivity gain of the split-plus-mixer approach

- Excessive depth/gates without new mixing  
  • Gen 18 (32 params, depth 147, 492 gates) improved only to 0.94 combined and slowed convergence (~180 vs. ~120 steps)  
  • Additional CZ layers alone yield diminishing accuracy returns

## Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC with named shared parameters  
  • All gates (“collective_ry”, “mid_rx”, etc.) keyed in a list-of-dicts enables rapid prototyping of new mixers/entanglers  
  • PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK + block-offset indexing automatically scales with N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS

- key_to_position mapping in apply_ansatz_block  
  • Decouples gate ordering from parameter layout, making it trivial to insert mid-circuit mixers (as in Gen 22) or swap entanglers

- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript introspection  
  • Programmatic depth/gate_count extraction guided the decision to accept Gen 22’s 135-depth/540-gate trade-off over Gen 21’s 123/444  
  • Ensures accurate resource accounting during search

- Stable training with PennyLane autograd and early stopping  
  • AdamOptimizer (η≈0.03), patience = 75, convergence threshold = 0.90 consistently yields convergence in ≈150 steps for Gen 22, with full restore-best-weights logic

## Performance Analysis

- Score progression  
  • Gen 20: combined 0.94 (depth 123, 444 gates)  
  • Gen 21: combined 0.95 (123, 444), gap 0.02  
  • Gen 22: combined 0.95 (135, 540), gap 0.01, test acc 0.97

- Test-accuracy uplift vs. parameter count  
  • Moving from 26 params (Gen 21/23) to 38 params (Gen 22) raises test accuracy from 0.96 to 0.97, at a modest cost in parameter efficiency (0.04 → 0.03)

- Generalization gap reduction  
  • Introducing the mid-circuit mixer in Gen 22 cut the gap to 0.01, improving robustness over Gen 19/21 (gap 0.02)

- Convergence trade-off  
  • Gen 22’s extra mixer and CZ edges slowed training (~150 steps) vs. ~120 for Gen 19/21, but yielded the highest test accuracy and lowest gap among evaluated candidates

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace each static CZ half‐block with perfect‐matching CRZ blocks while preserving the split-and-mixer structure. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, group the 16 and 12 edges into seven perfect matchings per half (four in the first, three in the second) and replace CZ with CRZ gates keyed “crz_match_0”…“crz_match_6”. This introduces only seven tunable entangling angles per half, exploits commutativity for parallelism (as in Gen 24’s matching idea) but avoids over-parameterization by sharing one parameter per matching, and can sharpen entanglement strength control without inflating depth or parameter count.  

2. Add trainable per-upload feature scaling to the feature_map. Define a vector of three new parameters “scale_u0”, “scale_u1”, “scale_u2” and in the feature_map multiply FEATURE_SCALE by the appropriate scale_u{upload_index}. This +3-parameter change lets the circuit learn to weight early vs. late uploads differently—leveraging the proven stability of N_UPLOADS=3 while giving the model fine-grained control over information injection at each stage.  

3. Tie ansatz parameters across the two repeats within each upload to enforce intra-upload symmetry. In apply_ansatz_block, compute offset = upload_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK (ignore repeat_index) so that both repeats use the same block of parameters, halving total circuit parameters from 6×6=36 to 3×6=18. This enforces the permutation-equivariance that has proven beneficial (two-axis mixers shared across qubits) and should reduce overfitting by shrinking the parameter space while retaining expressivity across uploads.  

4. Introduce a second mid-circuit two-axis mixer between the existing CZ second half and the output mixer. In ANSATZ_SPEC, after the final CZ matchings insert shared “mid2_ry” and “mid2_rx” RY/RX on all qubits before the output RZ/RX block. This mirrors the success of the first mid-mixer in Gen 22—further breaking residual commutativity between entangling segments without adding many parameters (+2) and boosting nonlocal expressivity.  

5. Upgrade the readout to qubit-wise trainable weighting before gain and bias calibration. In predict_batch, replace raw = mean(z) with raw = dot(w_read, z) using a new length-8 parameter vector w_read (“read_w0”…“read_w7”), then apply the existing gain/bias. This +8-parameter readout module builds on the proven benefits of calibration in ≥0.93 models, letting the circuit emphasize the most informative qubits and potentially lift test accuracy above 0.97 while preserving a tight generalization gap.
