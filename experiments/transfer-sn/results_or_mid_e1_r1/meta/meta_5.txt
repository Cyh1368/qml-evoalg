# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Parameter sharing across qubit pairs (shared RY/RZ rotations) with a balanced CZ‐chain connectivity, as in the current best “Shared‐Parameter 8-Qubit Quantum Classifier” (Gen 4), yielded a combined_score_raw = 0.86, a test accuracy of 0.84, and a minimal generalization gap of 0.02 over only 74 parameters, depth 87, 270 gates.  
- Moderate ansatz compression—reducing from 146 parameters (Gen 0, Gen 1) down to 62–74 (Gen 2, Gen 4)—preserved accuracy (train/val ≈0.86) while shrinking overfitting (gap ≤0.03 vs 0.07–0.10).  
- Balanced local entanglement (parallel plus diagonal CZ pairs) improved expressivity per parameter: Gen 4’s two-layer CZ pattern outperformed Gen 5’s ring/CNOT structure (combined 0.75) despite fewer gates (270 vs 300).  
- Trainable output calibration (gain and bias) combined with early stopping was essential across all high-performers (Gen 0, Gen 2, Gen 4) to stabilize convergence and boost validation/test accuracy by ~0.02–0.04.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Overparameterized circular entanglement (Gen 1: 146 params, depth 162) achieved high train accuracy (0.85) but poor validation/test (0.68/0.75) and a large gap (0.10), indicating wasted capacity.  
- Deep, symmetric ring blocks with grouped rotations (Gen 5: depth 126, 300 gates) resulted in only moderate performance (combined 0.75) and parameter efficiency remained low (0.01), showing that complexity without targeted connectivity harms resource use.  
- Excessive repeats or uploads (e.g., pushing to depth 162 in Gen 1) drove up gate count without improving generalization, whereas Gen 4 reached convergence by step 270 vs ~750 for Gen 0.

## Implementation Insights
- Using a validate_ansatz_spec routine to auto‐extract parameter keys enabled clean sharing of rotation angles (e.g., reusing “ry_0” on qubits 0&4), directly cutting block‐parameter count from ~24 to 12.  
- The alternating feature map + ansatz layout is robust; performance gains came not from changing upload count (fixed at 3) but from tailoring the ansatz block’s parameter sharing and connectivity.  
- Mapping shared parameters via a dict lookup (key_to_position) keeps the apply_ansatz_block implementation concise and supports rapid ansatz prototyping.  
- The best program’s small block (12 params) repeated six times, plus two readout params, strikes an optimal balance: enough trainable angles for expressivity, few enough to converge quickly under Adam with backprop.

## Performance Analysis
- Reducing N_PARAMS from 146 → 74 yielded test accuracy roughly stable at 0.84–0.87 but slashed generalization gap from 0.07–0.10 to 0.02, raising combined_score_raw from 0.85 (Gen 0) to 0.86 (Gen 4).  
- Depth reduction (162 → 87) and gate_count trimming (318/300 → 270) correlated with faster convergence (global_step at convergence: 270 vs ~750) and no loss in test performance.  
- Parameter efficiency (test_accuracy/N_PARAMS) remained ~0.01 across all models, suggesting that further improvements hinge on better expressivity per parameter rather than mere parameter count cuts.  
- Shared‐parameter ansätze (Gen 2: combined 0.80; Gen 4: combined 0.86) consistently outperformed fully independent schemes in generalization, underscoring the value of symmetry constraints in mid-sized circuits.
