# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mirror-symmetric single-qubit rotations with parameter tying consistently yield top performance.  
  • Seed program (“8-Qubit binary classifier”) uses shared RY gates across qubit pairs and shared RZ post-rotations, achieving the highest combined_score_raw 0.88 (train 93%, val 91%, test 87%) with only 74 parameters and depth 87.  
  • Gen 41 (“Mirror-Symmetric Mixed-Axis Controlled-Rotation Ansatz”) replicates this RY/RZ tying and scores 0.87 (train 91%, val 88%, test 86%) with 86 params, illustrating that symmetry plus compact parameter count drives stable convergence.  
- Mixed-axis controlled-rotation cores (CRX/CRY/CRZ combinations) balance expressivity and efficiency.  
  • Seed’s core (CRX, CRY, CRZ, CRY) outperforms single-axis variants, matching the raw 0.88 top score.  
  • Gen 41’s mixed-axis CRX/CRY/CRZ core also scores well (0.87), while any CRZ-only core (see earlier Gen 32) lagged by >0.10 in test accuracy.  
- Fixed long-range + local CZ entangling scaffold ensures sufficient connectivity without depth explosion.  
  • All top performers (seed, Gen 41, Gen 42) use CZ couplings on wires (0–4,1–5,2–6,3–7) plus nearest-neighbor CZs, capping depth at 87–93 and delivering ≥ 0.86 test accuracy.  
- Three data re-uploads × two ansatz repeats remains the sweet spot.  
  • Seed’s 3 uploads × 2 repeats (depth 87) converges in ~270 steps to 0.88 combined score.  
  • Shallower variants (e.g., Gen 36 at depth 81) lose ~0.02 in raw score, quantifying the expressivity cost of fewer repeats.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization beyond ~80 trainable parameters yields diminishing returns.  
  • Gen 41/42 use 86 params and reach 0.87/0.86, below seed’s 0.88 with 74 params. Larger ansatze (Gen 31 with 104 params, Gen 30 with 110) plateau at ≤ 0.86 and converge more slowly (~300–570 steps).  
- Increasing circuit depth past ~93 gates without pruning fails to boost accuracy.  
  • Gen 43 (“Mirrored Controlled-Rotation Ansatz Classifier”) depth 93, 258 gates, scores only 0.80; Gen 41 depth 93 scores 0.87 vs. seed’s 87-depth 0.88.  
- Low-diversity entangling cores degrade expressivity.  
  • CRZ-only cores in earlier generations scored ≤ 0.78 test accuracy (e.g., Gen 32), confirming multi-axis rotations are essential.  
- Removing or weakening mirror symmetry destabilizes convergence and yields no accuracy gain (seen in asymmetric variants outside current top-5).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Deterministic parameter ordering via `ordered_unique_keys = list(dict.fromkeys(parameter_keys))` ensures consistent mapping across QNode evaluations and contributes to stable Adam updates, as in the seed’s `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`.  
- Readout calibration separated into two classical parameters (gain, bias) initialized at (1.0, 0.0) stabilizes early training; the seed’s low validation loss (0.39) vs. Gen 40’s higher loss (0.56) reflects more accurate final calibration.  
- Automated resource reporting with `AnnotatedQueue` + `QuantumScript.from_queue` provides reliable depth/gate counts (seed: depth 87, gate_count 252), enabling direct comparison and rapid pruning of over-complex designs.  
- Structured `apply_ansatz_block` loop keyed by `block_index = upload_index*N_REPEATS + repeat_index` cleanly implements repeated mirror-shared blocks, as demonstrated in the seed program and replicated across Gen 40–44.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined_score_raw trends closely track parameter count and depth: seed (74 params, depth 87) leads at 0.88; Gen 41/42 (86 params, depth 93) follow at 0.87/0.86; deeper or larger circuits fall further behind (Gen 43 at 0.80, Gen 40 at 0.78).  
- Generalization gaps remain low (seed 0.06, Gen 41 0.05, Gen 44 0.03) when employing mirror symmetry and mixed-axis cores, highlighting robust training vs. overfitting trade-offs.  
- Parameter efficiency (test_acc/params ≈ 0.012 for seed vs. ≈ 0.01 for all others) underscores the seed’s optimal packing of expressivity into 74 parameters.  
- Validation_loss correlates inversely with final test accuracy: seed’s 0.39 validation loss → 0.87 test accuracy, whereas higher losses in Gen 40 (0.56) and Gen 43 (~0.52) map to lower test accuracies (0.83, 0.87).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce per-upload feature-scale trainable parameters  
   • In your `feature_map`, multiply `FEATURE_SCALE` by a distinct scalar αᵤ for each upload (u=0,1,2), initialized at 1.0.  
   • This leverages the proven benefit of 3× re-uploads while giving the circuit low-cost control over input amplitudes, potentially improving convergence without altering depth or CZ layout.  

2. Parametrize the long-range entangling scaffold with mirror-tied CRZs  
   • Replace each fixed CZ on (0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7) with two mirror-shared CRZ gates: one parameter “crz_lr0” applied to wires (0,4)&(3,7) and another “crz_lr1” on (1,5)&(2,6).  
   • This builds on the seed’s success with multi-axis controlled rotations and mirror symmetry, allowing the circuit to learn optimal long-range phase couplings without increasing overall depth.  

3. Prepend a mirror-symmetric RZ pre-rotation layer  
   • Before the existing RY pre-rotations, insert RZ gates on each qubit using four new shared parameters (“rzp_g0”–“rzp_g3”) tied across the same mirror pairs as your RY pre-rotations.  
   • Early-layer phase adjustments have shown zero run-to-run variance in the seed; this adds only +4 parameters and +8 gates but could lift feature representations and boost the combined score.  

4. Insert a local mixed-axis entanglement mini-core  
   • After your main 4-pair CRX/CRY/CRZ block and before post-rotations, add two additional controlled rotations: a mirror-shared CRX on (0,1)&(6,7) with param “crx_loc” and a CRY on (2,3)&(4,5) with param “cry_loc.”  
   • This taps into the seed’s demonstrated benefit of mixed-axis two-qubit gates on local neighbors, enriching short-range entanglement without materially increasing circuit depth.  

5. Tie ansatz parameters across repeats to test redundancy  
   • Modify `apply_ansatz_block` so that for each upload index, the same `N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` parameter vector is reused in both repeats (i.e., share parameters between repeat_index 0 and 1).  
   • If expressivity is preserved, this could halve the total trainable parameters (from 74 to 38+2 readout), further improving parameter efficiency while retaining the successful mirror-symmetric, mixed-axis structure.
