# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Layered CZ-RY ansatz with mid-layer RZ rotations:  
  – Best exemplified by the “8-Qubit Reuploading CZ-RY Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 2), yielding combined_score_raw = 0.88 (versus 0.85–0.86 for others).  
  – The inclusion of trainable RZ gates between CZ and output RY boosts expressivity without excessive parameters.  
- Three reuploads × two ansatz repeats:  
  – All top-performers use N_UPLOADS = 3 and N_REPEATS = 2, with Gen 2 achieving zero performance variance and validation_accuracy = 0.91.  
- Trainable readout gain and bias:  
  – Every high-scoring circuit calibrates the mean Z readout via gain/bias, stabilizing convergence (Gen 2 converged in 570 steps).  
- Pennylane QNode + Adam + early stopping:  
  – Autograd backprop with Adam and validation-loss early stopping (patience = 75) yields fast, stable convergence across runs.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized two-qubit rotations:  
  – “8-Qubit Re-uploading CRY Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 3) used 140 parameters and CRY entanglers but scored only 0.81 combined, despite depth = 81.  
  – Low parameter efficiency (0.01) and no accuracy gain vs. CZ-only variants indicate diminishing returns.  
- Dense CZ meshes without trainable two-qubit phases:  
  – “Multi-layer CZ Ansatz” (Gen 5) with 146 parameters, depth = 93, gate_count = 300 achieved only 0.84 combined; pure CZ entanglement lacked fine-grained control.  
- Deep circuits with ring-CRX entanglers:  
  – “8-Qubit Re-uploading CRX Ring Ansatz” (Gen 4) depth = 114, 74 parameters, combined = 0.86. High depth failed to offset limited expressivity of CRX rings.  
- Excessive gate count vs. accuracy:  
  – Both Gen 4 (228 gates) and Gen 5 (300 gates) consumed more two-qubit interactions but underperformed Gen 2’s 276 gates, highlighting that count alone isn’t predictive of accuracy.

## Implementation Insights  
- Parameter-key mapping via ANSATZ_SPEC:  
  – The dynamic dict.fromkeys ordering and offset arithmetic in apply_ansatz_block cleanly scales to N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS.  
- Balanced single- vs. two-qubit layers:  
  – Gen 2’s block of 8 RY → 6 CZ → 4 CZ (mesh) → 8 RZ → 8 RY per repeat optimizes entanglement bandwidth with local rotations.  
- Circuit metadata introspection:  
  – Using pennylane.queuing.AnnotatedQueue to compute depth/gate_count in circuit_metadata ensures accurate resource reporting.  
- Readout calibration integrated in predict_batch:  
  – Splitting circuit_params and readout_params simplifies gradient flow and isolates quantum vs. classical parameter effects.

## Performance Analysis  
- Sweet spot at ~98 parameters & depth ≈ 87:  
  – Gen 2 (98 params, depth 87) leads with 0.88 combined.  
  – Slightly higher params (140–146) or depth (93–114) correlate with lower combined scores (0.81–0.86).  
- Consistent generalization gaps ~0.06–0.07:  
  – All circuits yield train–test gaps ≤ 0.07, so absolute test accuracy drives ranking.  
- Zero variance in Gen 2 vs. small std in others:  
  – Stability (std = 0.00) indicates robust landscape; other ansätze had no variance reported but lower means.  
- Trade-off of expressive entanglement vs. parameter efficiency:  
  – Despite similar gate_count (Gen 2: 276 vs. Gen 5: 300), targeted CZ-RY layering outperforms brute entanglement meshes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce sparse trainable two-qubit phases  
   Replace one fixed CZ gate per entangling mesh (e.g. the central CZ in the 6-CZ block) with a CRZ gate that has its own parameter. This adds fine-grained entanglement control—akin to the successful mid-layer RZ rotations—without substantially increasing depth or parameter count, and may boost expressivity where pure CZ proved too coarse.

2. Add a pre-upload single-qubit phase layer  
   Prepend each feature upload cycle in the `circuit_z_expectations` QNode with one RZ rotation on every qubit (with its own trainable parameter). This mirrors the proven benefit of mid-ansatz RZ gates, offering early circuit calibration before data encoding and potentially smoothing the optimization landscape.

3. Explore an extra data reupload  
   Change `N_UPLOADS` from 3 to 4 while keeping `N_REPEATS` at 2 (for eight total ansatz blocks) so that `N_PARAMS` stays near 98. This tests whether more frequent data injections—without blowing up the parameter budget—can further improve validation accuracy, building directly on the optimal 3×2 reupload×repeat pattern.

4. Alternate CZ connectivity to diversify entanglement  
   In every second ansatz block, swap the current 4- and 6-CZ mesh with a complementary pairing (e.g. wires [0,5],[1,4],[2,7],[3,6]). This diversity of entanglement pathways leverages CZ efficiency but broadens the circuit’s ability to capture different qubit correlations without adding gates or depth.

5. Selectively untie one shared RY parameter  
   Duplicate a single shared RY key (for example split `"ry_out_0"` into `"ry_out_0a"` on wire 0 and `"ry_out_0b"` on wire 7) to add one extra degree of freedom. This controlled break in symmetry tests whether a tiny increase in parameter uniqueness can lift accuracy while retaining the compact, layered CZ-RY structure that underpins Gen 2’s success.
