# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mirror-shared single-qubit rotations plus mixed-axis two-qubit core  
  - Current best (seed) and Gen 20 (“Mirrored-Rotations”) both use 8 shared RY pre-rotations (ry_g0–ry_g3) mirrored over qubit pairs and 8 shared RZ post-rotations. With only 74 total circuit parameters they achieve combined_score_raw = 0.88 (train/val/test 0.93/0.91/0.87) and zero run-to-run variance.  
- Fixed CZ scaffold combining long-range (0–4,1–5,2–6,3–7) and local couplings  
  - Both the seed and Gen 20 used exactly eight CZ entanglers, yielding a consistent depth of 87 and gate_count of 252, maximizing information mixing without over-entangling.  
- Mixed-axis CRX/CRY/CRZ “core” on four designated wire pairs  
  - The seed’s controlled-rotation core (CRX on 0–7, CRY on 1–6, CRZ on 2–5, CRY on 3–4) provides expressivity and accounts for the top raw score, outperforming CRZ-only variants.  
- Three re-uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  - The optimal layout N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2 appears repeatedly (seed, Gen 18, Gen 20), balancing depth (87) against expressivity to hit ~0.90 validation accuracy in ~270 steps.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-entangling or dense connectivity  
  - Gen 21 added extra long-range CZs and local CNOTs (146 params, depth 93, 300 gates) but fell to combined_score_raw = 0.80 and slower convergence.  
- Extra CRZ-only couplings without mixed-axis lifts  
  - Gen 23/24 expanded the symmetric CRZ scaffold (110–122 params, depth 93–120) yet combined_score_raw dropped to 0.77–0.80 and gaps widened to 0.10–0.12.  
- Mirror symmetry alone (no mixed-axis core)  
  - Gen 22 used 98 params and depth 87 but test_acc fell to 0.85, showing symmetry must be paired with multi-axis gates for expressivity.  
- Excessive parameter pruning  
  - Ultra-lean designs (≈50 params) boosted parameter efficiency but sacrificed ~0.03–0.05 in raw score and raised generalization gaps to ~0.09.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Deterministic parameter-key ordering via dict.fromkeys  
  - Both the seed and Gen 20 use validate_ansatz_spec + dict.fromkeys to build PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK, ensuring correct offsets in apply_ansatz_block and reproducible mapping.  
- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript metadata extraction  
  - Automates precise depth/gate_count reporting (seed: depth 87, 252 ops), enabling tight coupling of resource metrics with performance.  
- Separate readout calibration (gain, bias) as last two params  
  - Decouples classical and quantum gradient paths; seed hits 0.90 validation in ~270 steps vs ~570 in deeper 98-param designs, with zero test-accuracy variance.  
- Structured loop over uploads and repeats  
  - apply_ansatz_block indexed by block_index (upload_index × N_REPEATS + repeat_index) allows rapid prototyping of mirrored/shared blocks across 6 placements.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Peak performance at 74 params, depth 87, 252 gates  
  - Current best and Gen 20 both hit combined_score_raw = 0.88, generalization gap = 0.06, converging in ~270 steps.  
- Increasing params or depth degrades or plateaus performance  
  - Gen 27 (86 params, 276 gates) achieved combined_score_raw = 0.86; Gen 25 (122 params, depth 120) only 0.80 despite richer connectivity.  
- Convergence speed inversely correlates with complexity  
  - 74-param circuits converge in ~270 steps; 98-param/276-gate designs take ~570 steps; 146-param circuits often stall before threshold.  
- Tight correlation of parameter efficiency and raw score  
  - Designs at 74 params reached both highest raw score and doubled parameter efficiency over 98+-param variants, confirming minimal redundancy drives optimal generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce trainable re-upload scaling factors. Add three new scalar parameters α₀, α₁, α₂—one per upload—and multiply the existing FEATURE_SCALE by αᵢ inside the feature_map for upload_index = i. This leverages the proven value of the 3× re-uploads while giving the model low-cost control over data amplitude, potentially improving expressivity and convergence without significantly increasing depth or gate count.  

2. Replace the four fixed long-range CZ gates with mirror-shared CRZ entanglers. Swap each CZ on (0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7) for CRZ, using two shared parameters (e.g. “crz_lr0” on (0,4)&(3,7), “crz_lr1” on (1,5)&(2,6)). This builds on the success of mirror symmetry and mixed-axis two-qubit gates, allowing the circuit to learn optimal long-range entanglement strength while preserving the depth-87, eight-entangler scaffold.  

3. Augment the pre-rotation stage with mirror-shared RZ layers. Prepend an RZ gate on each qubit before the existing RY pre-rotations, using four shared parameters (“rzp_g0”–“rzp_g3”) mirrored across the same qubit pairs. Early-layer phase tuning has zero run-to-run variance in the seed design; this small (+4 params, +8 gates) addition could boost feature lifting expressivity with minimal overhead.  

4. Inject a local mixed-axis core on nearest neighbors. After the existing mixed-axis CRX/CRY/CRZ core but before post-rotations, insert two mirror-shared controlled rotations on local pairs: one CRX on (0,1)&(6,7) and one CRY on (2,3)&(4,5). This taps the superior expressivity of mixed-axis couplings in the seed’s core, testing whether augmenting local entanglement alongside the fixed CZ scaffold raises combined_score_raw beyond 0.88.  

5. Probe slight asymmetry in the post-rotation layer. Release one pair of shared RZ post-rotations (e.g. the “rz_g2” pair on qubits 2&5) to use two independent parameters instead of one, while keeping the other three pairs mirror-shared. This controlled symmetry break tests whether a small degree of parameter asymmetry can widen the model’s hypothesis space and further narrow the generalization gap without over-entangling.
