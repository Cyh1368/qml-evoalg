# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mirror-symmetric parameter tying across qubit pairs in each ansatz block (e.g. “ry_in_0” applied to qubits 0 & 7, “rz_mid_1” to 1 & 6, etc.) halved block parameters from 32→16 and preserved expressivity.  
  • Current best program: 98 params, depth 87, combined_score_raw 0.88 (converged in ~570 steps)  
  • Gen 2 (“8-Qubit Ring-Entangled Binary Classifier”): 98 params, depth 87, combined_score_raw 0.88  
- Three feature re-uploads × two ansatz repeats remains the sweet spot for expressivity vs. resource use. All top performers (current best, Gen 2) use N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2.  
- Balanced RY–CZ–RZ–RY layering per block (8 RY → 8 CZ → 8 RZ → 8 RY) yields high accuracy with moderate depth. The current best and Gen 2 follow this 16-parameter, four-stage block.  
- Always include trainable readout gain and bias after the circuit. Every high-scorer (current best, Gen 2, Gen 8) uses gain/bias calibration and sees accelerated convergence and stable test accuracy.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Replacing CZ entanglers with parametric two-qubit rotations degrades generalization:  
  • Ring CZ-CRX Shared Ansatz Classifier (Gen 9): depth 126, n_params 86 → combined_score_raw 0.78; generalization_gap 0.11.  
- Dense or full 8-qubit CZ meshes without parameter sharing waste gates and parameters:  
  • Ring-Star Reuploading Quantum Ansatz (Gen 12): depth ~120, n_params 98 → combined_score_raw 0.77; parameter_efficiency 0.01.  
- Oversized depths (>100) with heavy entanglement inflate overfitting and fail to improve test accuracy beyond ~0.85.  
- Over-pruned symmetric designs with too few parameters limit expressivity:  
  • 8-Qubit Symmetric Ansatz Classifier (Gen 16): depth 81, n_params 62 → combined_score_raw 0.85.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Using dict.fromkeys on PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK plus an offset index in apply_ansatz_block allows arbitrary N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS without rewriting gate logic. Current best leverages this to scale 16 keys→(3×2×16)=96 circuit params.  
- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue combined with qml.tape.QuantumScript.from_queue provides accurate depth and gate_count metadata, enabling tight correlation of resources to empirical performance.  
- Separating quantum circuit parameters and classical readout calibration (gain, bias) in predict_batch isolates their gradient flows, improving stability and convergence speed: seen in all top-performers.  
- Mirrored assignment of RZ and RY output parameters in ANSATZ_SPEC (e.g. “ry_out_0” on wires 0 & 7) demonstrates that tying non-commuting rotations across symmetry axes preserves model capacity while halving trainable angle count.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Top combined_score_raw = 0.88 is achieved at depth ≈ 87 and n_params ≈ 98 (current best and Gen 2). Shallower (< 80) or deeper (> 100) circuits underperform by 0.02–0.10.  
- Parameter efficiency (test_accuracy / n_params) remains ~0.01 for 98-param designs, balancing expressivity and overparameterization. Designs with 50 params (Gen 14) raise efficiency to 0.02 but lose ~0.02 in combined score.  
- Generalization gaps cluster around 0.06–0.07 for 3×2 layouts with early stopping; wider gaps (>0.10) occur in overparameterized high-depth variants (Gen 9).  
- Convergence steps correlate with ansatz depth/param count: the current best converges in ~570 steps; models that never reach the 0.90 accuracy threshold within allotted epochs (e.g. Gen 11 at 104 params) highlight the need for balanced parameter counts and block structures.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. In each ansatz block, replace only the central CZs on wires [0,1] and its mirror [6,7] with trainable CRZ gates—e.g. param keys “crz_mid_0” on (0,1) and “crz_mid_1” on (6,7)—while leaving the other six CZs fixed. This sparse CRZ injection leverages the proven benefit of mid-layer Z rotations for fine-grained control without the generalization loss seen in fully parametric entanglers. Monitor the generalization gap to ensure you’re not over-parameterizing the circuit.  

2. Prepend each feature re-upload cycle with a mirrored RZ calibration layer. In `circuit_z_expectations`, before calling `feature_map(records)` insert RZ gates on qubits 0–7 with shared params “rz_pre_0”…“rz_pre_3” applied to mirror pairs (0&7, 1&6, 2&5, 3&4). Grounded in the efficacy of mid-ansatz RZs, this early corrective phase can smooth the loss landscape and accelerate convergence. Track convergence steps to quantify training speed-ups.  

3. Probe a 4×2 upload/repeat layout by setting N_UPLOADS=4 and keeping N_REPEATS=2 with the same 16-param mirrored block. This raises circuit parameters to 128 and depth to ≈115, testing whether an extra data injection further boosts validation accuracy within the overfitting threshold observed around depth≈87. Compare combined_score_raw and test generalization against the 3×2 baseline to identify any net gains.  

4. Alternate between two mirror-symmetric CZ meshes across blocks. Define pattern A (current ring plus local pairs) and pattern B (complementary long-range pairs [0,5],[1,4],[2,7],[3,6] plus the same local CZs), and apply A in even-indexed blocks, B in odd. This preserves gate count/depth while diversifying entanglement pathways, leveraging CZ efficiency to capture broader qubit correlations. Evaluate expressivity gains via parameter efficiency and test accuracy.  

5. Introduce a controlled symmetry-break in the output layer by splitting “ry_out_0” into two separate params: “ry_out_0a” on wire 0 and “ry_out_0b” on wire 7. This single-parameter untie tests whether a minimal lift in parameter uniqueness can improve capacity without sacrificing the compact mirrored structure. Track changes in combined_score_raw and generalization gap to verify net accuracy gains.
