# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mirror-symmetric parameter sharing across qubit pairs (tying RZ_mid and RY_out parameters on wires 0↔7, 1↔6, etc.) halved per-block parameters (32→16) without loss of accuracy.  
  • Evidenced by the current best/Gen 18 (combined_score_raw 0.88, depth 87, n_params 98, zero std)  
- Three feature-map re-uploads × two ansatz repeats per upload (total 6 blocks) consistently hits peak performance.  
  • Both the seed program and Gen 18 use N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2 → depth 87, combined_score_raw 0.88  
- Four-stage balanced blocks (RY_in→CZ_long-range+local→RZ_mid→RY_out) yield high expressivity at moderate depth.  
  • Mirrored Ring Rotation Ansatz (Gen 15) omitted the full CZ→RZ_mid pattern and scored only 0.72  
- Explicit trainable readout calibration (gain and bias applied post-circuit) accelerates convergence (~570 steps to 0.90) and stabilizes test accuracy.  
  • All top performers (current best, Gen 18) include gain/bias and saw zero run-to-run variance  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-pruning parameters lowered depth and gate count but reduced accuracy and widened gap.  
  • 8-Qubit Echo-Calibrated Classifier (Gen 13): 50 params achieved parameter_efficiency 0.02 but combined_score_raw fell to 0.85 and gap rose to 0.09  
- Overparameterization with deep circuits failed to improve expressivity and slowed convergence.  
  • Ring-Entangled Ansatz (Gen 17): 146 params, depth 126 → train_acc 0.81, no early convergence, low param_efficiency  
- Substituting fixed CZ entanglers with parametric CRX/CRZ degraded generalization.  
  • Mirrored Ring Rotation Ansatz (Gen 15) with CRZ couplings: combined_score_raw 0.72, gap 0.06  
- Inflating gate count without structured sharing wasted resources.  
  • Reuploading Ring Ansatz (Gen 19): 110 params, depth 120, combined_score_raw 0.87 (only +0.01 vs. best)  

## Implementation Insights  
- Using dict.fromkeys on PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK + block‐index offsets allows a generic apply_ansatz_block loop over N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS.  
  • Seed program’s apply_ansatz_block cleanly scales 16 keys→96 circuit params  
- Leveraging qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript.from_queue yields exact depth/gate_count metadata for tight resource–performance analysis.  
- Isolating classical readout parameters (gain, bias) in predict_batch decouples gradient flows, improving stability—seen in both current best and Gen 19.  
- Mirror-symmetric entries in ANSATZ_SPEC (tying RZ_mid_i and RY_out_i) deliver zero train/validation/test std while halving parameter count.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Top combined_score_raw = 0.88 achieved at depth ≈ 87 and n_params = 98 (seed program & Gen 18). Deeper (> 100) or shallower (< 80) circuits underperform by 0.02–0.16.  
- Parameter efficiency peaks at 0.02 for 50-param Gen 13 but frontloads a 0.03–0.05 raw-score penalty. Designs around 98 params strike the best trade-off (efficiency ~0.01, highest accuracy).  
- Generalization gaps of ~0.06–0.07 correlate with balanced 3×2 layouts and mirror sharing; aggressive pruning (Gen 13, gap 0.09) or over-entangling (Gen 15, gap 0.06 but low base score) upset this balance.  
- Convergence steps scale with parameter count/depth: the 98-param, 87-depth ansatz converges in ~570 steps to 0.90, whereas 146-param/126-depth Gen 17 never reached threshold within the same budget.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. In each block, replace only the four long-range CZ gates with mirror–shared CRZ rotations.  Concretely, swap CZ(0,4)&CZ(3,7) for CRZ(param “crz_long_0”, wires=[0,4]) & CRZ(“crz_long_0”, wires=[3,7]) and CZ(1,5)&CZ(2,6) for CRZ(“crz_long_1”, wires=[1,5]) & CRZ(“crz_long_1”, wires=[2,6]).  This sparse injection of tunable Z–couplings builds on the proven power of mid-layer rotations without fully overparameterizing the entangling mesh—monitor the validation-test gap to guard against overfitting.  

2. Insert a mirror-symmetric RX mini-layer immediately after your CZ entanglers and before the RZ_mid stage.  Define four new params “rx_mid_0”…“rx_mid_3” applied to qubit pairs (0&7),(1&6),(2&5),(3&4) to introduce noncommuting rotations at low depth overhead.  This extra mid-block flexibility may boost expressivity and accelerate convergence while retaining the overall four-stage balance.  

3. Tie each block’s parameters across its two repeats to halve the circuit parameter count while preserving depth.  Implement by mapping block_index 1→0, 3→2, 5→4 in your key-to-position offset logic, so blocks 0&1 share the same 16 keys, as do 2&3 and 4&5.  This tests whether deeper circuits with parameter reuse can match accuracy and improve param-efficiency.  

4. Upgrade the readout calibration to mirror-shared per-pair gains and biases.  After computing Z-expectations, average them over each mirror pair (0&7),(1&6),(2&5),(3&4), then apply “gain_0”/“bias_0” through “gain_3”/“bias_3” before taking the final mean.  This finer-grained calibration builds on the success of global gain/bias layers and may yield faster convergence or tighter generalization.  

5. Alternate long-range entanglement patterns block-by-block to diversify correlations.  In even-indexed blocks keep the standard pairs [0,4],[1,5],[2,6],[3,7], and in odd blocks use their complement [0,5],[1,4],[2,7],[3,6], while leaving local CZs unchanged.  This change preserves depth and gate count but exposes the circuit to two distinct CZ meshes—measure its impact on expressivity via combined_score_raw and parameter_efficiency.
