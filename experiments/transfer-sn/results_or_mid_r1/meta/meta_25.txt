# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mirror-shared pre- and post-rotations plus mixed-axis controlled-rotations (Gen 20 “Mirrored-Rotations”): achieved combined_score_raw 0.88 (train/val/test 0.93/0.91/0.87), with just 74 parameters, depth 87, 252 gates, and zero run-to-run variance.  
- Fixed CZ scaffold between mirrored pairs (0↔4, 1↔5, etc.) plus local CZs: delivers strong spatial mixing at low cost, stabilizing validation performance in both Gen 20 (0.91 val_acc) and prior bests with mirror symmetry.  
- Three re-uploads × two repeats per upload: depth 87 consistently hits peak expressivity; Gen 20, seed program and Gen 18 all use N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2 and top scores ~0.88.  
- Trainable readout calibration (gain & bias) decoupled from circuit gradients: accelerates convergence (~270 steps to threshold in Gen 20 vs ~570 in deeper designs) and yields zero test-accuracy variance across seeds.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-entangling with dense or long-range CZ layouts (Gen 21): 146 params, depth 93, gate_count 300, but combined_score_raw only 0.80 and slower convergence; richer connectivity did not translate to better generalization.  
- Adding extra parametric CRZ-only couplings (Gen 23/24 “hybrid_symmetric_crz”/“complement_entanglement_crz”): circuits of depth 93 and 110–122 params saw combined_score_raw drop to 0.77, generalization gaps widen to 0.10–0.12.  
- Mirror symmetry alone without mixed-axis core (Gen 22): reduced to 98 params and depth 87 but test_acc fell to 0.85 (combined_score_raw ~ –0.81 in summary), indicating that symmetry plus fixed CZ isn’t sufficient without the targeted CRX/CRY/CRZ lifts.  
- Excessive parameter pruning (e.g., Gen 13 echo-calibrated 50 params): achieved higher parameter efficiency but at a cost of 0.03–0.05 raw-score penalty and generalization gap increase to ~0.09.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC with mirrored parameter keys (e.g. ry_g0 shared on wires 0/1) and computing PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK via dict.fromkeys yields a compact block size (12 keys → 74 total params) and zero-variance results in Gen 20.  
- Leveraging qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript.from_queue to extract exact depth and gate counts enables tight feedback loops on resource vs. accuracy trade-offs (e.g. 252 vs. 276 gates).  
- Isolating readout calibration in predict_batch (gain, bias as last two params) decouples classical and quantum gradients, leading to faster convergence (~270 steps at 0.90 acc) and stable test performance.  
- Consistent loop over N_UPLOADS and N_REPEATS with apply_ansatz_block parametrized by block_index supports rapid prototyping of mirrored/shared patterns across the six blocks.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Peak combined_score_raw 0.88 correlates with depth 87 and a lean 74 parameters (Gen 20). Circuits with ≥110 params or ≥93 depth uniformly underperform (0.77–0.80).  
- Parameter count vs. performance: dropping from 98 params (prior best) to 74 params did not degrade accuracy; in fact, param efficiency doubled while maintaining the top raw score.  
- Generalization gaps: optimal designs (Gen 20, seed) hold gap at 0.06–0.07. Over-entangled or over-pruned designs see gaps ≥0.10.  
- Convergence speed scales inversely with circuit complexity: the 74-param Gen 20 hits 0.90 validation accuracy in ~270 steps, whereas 98-param/276-gate designs took ~570 steps, and 146-param circuits often failed to converge within budget.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Implement parameter sharing across repeats by mapping all first-repeat blocks (indices 0, 2, 4) to one shared ANSATZ_SPEC parameter set and all second-repeat blocks (1, 3, 5) to another. This reduces the circuit-parameter count from 72 to 24 (plus 2 readout), preserves the full depth-87 structure, and tests whether deeper repetition with heavy parameter reuse can match the 0.88 combined_score_raw achieved by mirror sharing in Gen 20.

2. Enrich inter-upload mixing with a single mirror-shared RX mini-layer inserted after each feature_map and before the ansatz blocks: define four new params (“rx_int_0”…“rx_int_3”) applied to qubit pairs (0&7),(1&6),(2&5),(3&4), and reuse those same keys across all three uploads. This injects non-commuting rotations between data re-uploads at low cost (+4 params, +24 gates total) while leveraging proven mirror symmetry for expressivity.

3. Hybridize the local entangling scaffold by replacing the four CZ gates on nearest-neighbor pairs ([0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7]) with two mirror-shared CRZ rotations: one param “crz_loc_0” on (0,1)&(6,7) and another “crz_loc_1” on (2,3)&(4,5). This sparse tunable coupling tests whether granting the circuit selective local Z-entanglement further boosts generalization—as seen with long-range CRZ injections—without altering depth or gate count significantly.

4. Append a second mixed-axis core immediately after the mirrored post-rotations, reusing the same four wire pairs but cycling the axis order (e.g. CRZ→CRX→CRY→CRX) with mirror-shared parameters. This deepens the entangling strategy by +4 gates and +4 params (keeping total depth ≤93) and evaluates if a two-stage mixed-axis interaction outperforms the single core in combined_score_raw and convergence speed.

5. Perform axis-order ablations on the mixed-axis core by generating variants that permute the gate sequence (for example, CRZ→CRX→CRY→CRX or CRY→CRZ→CRX→CRY) while maintaining mirror sharing and the same parameter budget. Empirically comparing these sequences will reveal the most expressive axis ordering, directly leveraging the critical role of mixed-axis controlled rotations in the current best program.
