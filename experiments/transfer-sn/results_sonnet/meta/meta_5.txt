# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Layered data re-uploads with fixed IsingZZ feature maps plus dense single-qubit rotations (RY/RZ) and CZ entanglers (Generation 0 “initial_program”) achieved the top combined score of 0.85 with 0.87 test accuracy, a modest depth (99) and a relatively low parameter count (146).  
- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with two repeats of a compact ansatz block (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2) delivered stable convergence by ~750 steps and a small generalization gap (0.07).  
- Early-stopping on validation-loss with best-weight restoration (used in Generation 0 and Generation 3) reliably prevented overfitting and locked in peak test performance.  
- Calibrating the readout via trainable gain and bias terms (initialized to 1 and 0) consistently improved raw Z-expectation scaling without increasing circuit complexity.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Replacing fixed CZ gates with trainable two-qubit rotations (CRZ) increased expressivity but required 15–33% more parameters and deeper circuits (depth ≥ 120), yet failed to improve combined score (Generation 3: 170 params → 0.84; Generation 4: 194 params → 0.84).  
- Over-deepening the circuit (e.g., six-repeat CRZ brick-wall in Generation 4, depth 136) led to larger generalization gaps (0.10–0.12) and no gain in test accuracy.  
- Under-expressive ansatz designs lacking sufficient entangling diversity (Generation 5 “RY-CRZ Reuploading”) catastrophically failed to converge (combined score 0.0), highlighting that mere addition of parametrized couplings without proper layering can collapse training.  
- Richer connectivity patterns (e.g., ring-CRZ in Generation 6) raised training accuracy (0.98) but also amplified overfitting (generalization gap 0.12) compared to the more balanced fixed-CZ baseline.

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ansatz blocks via a declarative ANSATZ_SPEC and validating wire/parameter mappings enables rapid iteration of single- and two-qubit gate layouts without code duplication, as in the current best program.  
- Mapping each block’s parameter keys into a contiguous slice of the full parameter vector simplifies gradient updates across repeated blocks and keeps parameter indexing consistent.  
- Using Pennylane’s backprop QNode with pnp arrays and AdamOptimizer permits efficient end-to-end differentiation even with hundreds of parameters; initializing readout gain to 1 and bias to 0 jump-starts calibration.  
- Recording circuit metadata through an AnnotatedQueue and extracting depth/gate counts ensures accurate resource accounting, which correlated strongly with generalization performance in these experiments.

## Performance Analysis  
- The highest combined score of 0.85 was achieved by two programs: Generation 0 (fixed CZ, 146 params, depth 99) and Generation 6 (ring-CRZ, 158 params, depth 120), but only Generation 0 attained the best test accuracy (0.87 vs. 0.86) and smallest generalization gap (0.07 vs. 0.12).  
- Programs with parameter counts above ~150 (Gen 3–6) uniformly plateaued at combined score ~0.84–0.85, indicating diminishing returns from added parameters or entangling layers.  
- Circuit depth beyond ~100 gates correlates with increased overfitting: depths of 120–136 saw generalization gaps of 0.10–0.12, whereas the 99-layer fixed-CZ design held a gap of 0.07.  
- Early stopping at ~750–810 optimizer steps stabilized all top performers; delaying stopping did not yield further accuracy gains, underscoring the importance of timely convergence criteria.

This leverages the extra expressivity of CRZs in a controlled fashion—successfully adding two-qubit rotation capacity without the full-circuit overfitting seen when all CZs were replaced.

Adjust your loop in circuit_z_expectations to upload features and apply one ansatz block per cycle. This increases feature-injection frequency—mirroring the convergence benefits of three uploads—while keeping gate depth under the overfitting threshold.

3. Fuse single-qubit rotations using U3 gates: replace the per-qubit RY→RZ→RZ sequence in ANSATZ_SPEC with a single qml.U3(θ, φ, λ, wires=i) entry (mapping to three parameters per qubit). Gate fusion in this form preserves local expressivity but reduces the overall gate count and circuit depth, which correlated with the best generalization in Generation 0.

Initialize each β_b to 1. This lightweight sharing tests whether globally controlling block strengths can boost parameter efficiency and curb overfitting, building on the success of the compact 146-parameter design.
