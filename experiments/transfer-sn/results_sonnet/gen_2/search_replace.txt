<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz block uses only a linear chain of CZ gates for entanglement, which limits information mixing between distant qubits (e.g., qubit 0 and 7 are only connected through 6 intermediate hops per block). This can slow convergence and limit expressivity, contributing to the train-validation-test gap.

I propose:
1. Closing the entangling chain into a ring by adding a CZ gate between qubit 7 and qubit 0, giving every qubit exactly two entangling neighbors and improving connectivity uniformity.
2. Replacing the fixed CZ ring with parametrized CRZ gates that share parameters in pairs (even-indexed edges share one parameter, odd-indexed edges share another), similar in spirit to a "brick" pattern. This keeps the parameter count low (adds only 2 new parameters per block, from 24 to 26 per block, i.e. total circuit params from 144 to 156, total from 146 to 158 — still small) while giving the model the ability to learn how strongly to entangle neighboring qubits rather than using a fixed maximal CZ, potentially improving both trainability and generalization.

This should help the circuit mix information more symmetrically around the ring and give the entangling gates learnable strength, which often improves trainability (avoiding gradient issues from fixed maximal entangling operations) and final accuracy without significantly increasing parameter count or circuit depth (only 1 extra gate per block, from CZ ring being open to closed, plus converting existing CZ to CRZ).
