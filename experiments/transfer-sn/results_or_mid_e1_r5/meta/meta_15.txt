# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Symmetric parameter sharing on mirrored wires combined with targeted entanglement:  
  • “Symmetric Shared-Param RY Ansatz with CRY Entanglement” (Gen 15) ties RY rotations across (0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7), uses a fixed CZ cross-ring plus even/odd CRY layers, yielding combined_score_raw 0.88, test 0.90, gap 0.04 with only 74 parameters.  
  • Compared to the earlier “Symmetric CRY-Entanglement Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 8, raw 0.86, test 0.88, gap 0.08, 74 params), adding diversified post-entanglement RX/RY and maintaining symmetry cut the gap in half.  
- Three re-uploads × two repeats of the same compact ansatz block:  
  • Best performance achieved at depth 87, gate_count 252 (Gen 15), outperforming deeper (≥93) or wider (≥120 param) circuits.  
- Fixed CZ cross-ring entanglement as a backbone with two layers of parametrized CRY couplings:  
  • This mix (cross-ring CZ + CRY_even + CRY_odd) yields stable gradients and high expressivity per parameter, as seen in Gen 15 versus over-connected variants in Gen 11–13.  
- Post-entanglement axis mixing rotations (RX on some wires, RY on others) to diversify single-qubit expressivity:  
  • Present in both Gen 9 and Gen 15; in Gen 15 it aligns with symmetry to push test accuracy from 0.88→0.90.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Overparameterized multi-scale or deep ansätze:  
  • “8-Qubit Reuploading Variational Classifier” (Gen 11) used 218 params, depth 93, gates 300 but only raw 0.74, test 0.83 (gap 0.16).  
  • “8-Qubit Re-Uploading Quantum Classifier” (Gen 12) with 146 params, depth 126 had raw 0.73, test 0.77 despite mixed entanglement.  
- High gate count (>300) without symmetry or parameter tying:  
  • “Ring-Entangled Re-uploading Classifier” (Gen 13, 122 params, depth 132, gates 348) stalled at raw 0.80, test 0.83, gap 0.06.  
- Dense two-qubit connectivity without structured sharing:  
  • Pure CZ ladders or skip-connection entanglers (Gen 6, Gen 10) increased depth to 120+ but test accuracy stagnated at ~0.72–0.78.  
- Adding exotic entanglement hierarchies (multi_scale_adaptive_entanglement) led to overfitting:  
  • Gen 7 hit train 0.96 but test 0.84 (gap 0.12, raw 0.75), confirming diminishing returns from unsystematic depth.

## Implementation Insights
- Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC with validate_ansatz_spec → ordered PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK:  
  • Enables block_index × offset addressing and consistent key→index mapping in apply_ansatz_block.  
- Mirrored single-qubit rotations keyed by shared param names (“ry_a”, “ry_b”, …) halve the number of rotation parameters while preserving expressivity.  
- Layer ordering matters:  
  • First apply fixed CZ entanglement, then two CRY layers on well-chosen pairs (even then odd), finally axis-mixed RX/RY rotations. This yielded the best expressivity/depth trade-off in Gen 15.  
- Gain/bias readout calibration on mean-pooled Z expectations stabilizes small-data generalization:  
  • Present in all top performers (Gen 9, Gen 15) and crucial for zero-std validation/test across seeds.

## Performance Analysis
- Raw combined score lift from 0.86→0.88 by switching from Gen 8’s symmetric CRY entangler to Gen 15’s augmented block with post-entanglement axis mixing.  
- Test accuracy scaling with parameter count:  
  • 74 params (Gen 15) → test 0.90;  
  • 120–146 params (Gen 9/13) → test 0.83–0.88;  
  • 218 params (Gen 11) → test 0.83 despite >2× params.  
- Depth reduction from ~93 (high-param models) to 87 (Gen 15) cut generalization gap from ~0.06–0.08 to 0.04.  
- Consistent parameter efficiency (~0.01) across models masks absolute performance: lowest-param symmetric designs deliver the highest test scores, underscoring the balance of symmetry, modest depth, and targeted entanglement.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Collapse block offsets to enforce global parameter tying  
   Remove the `block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` offset in `apply_ansatz_block` and set `N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS = N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK`. This will make every upload/repeat reuse the same mirrored‐parameter set (as in Gen 9), halving the total variational weights without changing the circuit topology and likely further reducing the generalization gap.  

2. Swap fixed CZs for trainable, mirrored CRZ couplings  
   In your `ANSATZ_SPEC`, change each `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,j]}` in Layer 2 to `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"crz_a"}` (and similarly `"crz_b"` for a second key), then mirror these keys across the other cross‐ring pairs. Prior low‐param two‐qubit rotations (e.g. Gen 5) boosted validation accuracy per extra angle while keeping depth under the ~350‐gate sweet spot.  

3. Prepend a symmetric RZ pre‐rotation layer  
   Before the existing RY layer, insert two mirrored RZ gates with shared parameters—e.g. `{"gate":"RZ","wire":0,"param":"rz_a"}`, `{"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"rz_a"}`, and similarly `"rz_b"` on wires (1,5) and their mirrors (2,6),(3,7). This orthogonal rotation expands the initial state manifold per parameter, echoing the benefits of the post‐entanglement axis mixing in Gen 15.  

4. Mix CRY and CRX in the odd entanglement layer  
   Modify Layer 4 so that half the odd‐pair controlled rotations use CRX instead of CRY—e.g. `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[1,2],"param":"cry_odd_a"}` and its mirror, while keeping the other two pairs as CRY with `"cry_odd_b"`. Axis‐heterogeneous entanglement boosts expressivity at zero extra parameter cost, leveraging the stability of targeted two‐qubit couplings.  

5. Add a soft symmetry‐enforcement regularizer  
   In your training loop, augment the L2 loss with λ · Σ_over_mirrored_pairs (θ_i – θ_j)^2, where each rotation parameter on wire i is paired with its mirror j. This gently encourages the proven benefits of exact symmetry (Gen 15’s low gap) while still permitting small asymmetries if they improve fit, acting as a lightweight overfitting guard.
