# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Diversified entanglement ＋ axis mixing per block: Generation 17’s “diversified_entanglement_and_mixing” ansatz—alternating RY→CNOT chain→RZ→CRY→CRX→RY—lifted the combined score to 0.88 (train/val/test = 0.92/0.91/0.88) versus 0.87 in Gen 14/16, demonstrating that adding a CRX “mixing” layer after CRY entanglement yields a measurable accuracy gain (~1 ppt).  
- Three re-uploads × two repeats layout: All top performers (Gen 15, 17) use N_UPLOADS = 3 and N_REPEATS = 2, consistently reaching ≥0.90 validation accuracy with modest generalization gaps (0.04–0.06), confirming the stability of this staging.  
- Shared symmetric rotations stabilize training: Models that tie RY/RZ parameters across mirrored wires (e.g., Gen 15, 16) and Gen 17’s block-structured rotations both achieve low accuracy variance (std = 0.00), underscoring the value of parameter symmetry or structured block definitions.  
- Gain/bias readout calibration: Every top‐scoring circuit—including Gen 17—incorporates two trainable readout parameters and obtains zero‐std test/validation results, reaffirming that mean-Z scaling and biasing is crucial for small-data generalization.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized deep circuits without mixing: Gen 14 used 110 shared params, depth 99, 348 gates but only 0.87 raw; Gen 18’s shared_mirror_mixed_block (74 params, depth 87) scored 0.85, showing that neither mere depth nor mirror sharing alone matches the expressivity of combined CRY + CRX mixing.  
- Sparse CRY-only entanglement limits expressivity: Gen 16 (“symmetric_butterfly_compact”) with only CRY coupling after a CZ ring reached 0.87 raw and gap 0.06, indicating that a single parametrized entanglement layer underfits the decision boundary compared to dual-entangler blocks.  
- Gate count bloat (>320) without targeted pruning: Circuits exceeding ~320 two-qubit gates (Gen 14/17) show only marginal improvements over leaner 252-gate designs (Gen 15), confirming diminishing returns from unstructured gate additions.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Formal ANSATZ_SPEC → key-to-index mapping: The validate_ansatz_spec + ordered PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK pattern simplifies block indexing and ensures consistent parameter placement across N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS, critical for reproducible multi-block layouts.  
- Block ordering: Placing a phase adjustment (RZ) immediately after the CNOT ladder, then alternating CRY and CRX on distinct qubit pairs before a final RY, maximizes per-block expressivity. Gen 17’s explicit RY–CNOT–RZ–CRY–CRX–RY sequence outperforms flatter or unsorted layer stacks.  
- Early stopping with best-weights restoration: Across top models, monitoring validation loss with patience = 75 and restoring the lowest‐loss parameters yields small generalization gaps (Gen 17 gap = 0.04) and zero‐variance test accuracies.  
- Adam backprop convergence: Gen 17 reaches the 0.90 accuracy threshold by ~750 steps, roughly half the steps of deeper/shared block designs (e.g., Gen 18 at ~1560), highlighting that diversified mixing accelerates optimizer progress.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Accuracy vs. parameter count: Gen 17 (122 params) edges out Gen 16 (74 params) by 1 ppt raw score (0.88 vs. 0.87) but at identical parameter efficiency (~0.01), reaffirming that additional parameters must be purposefully structured (e.g., CRX mixing) to yield gains.  
- Gate count trade‐offs: Reducing two-qubit operations to ~252 gates in Gen 15 achieved the same 0.88/0.90 test accuracy with a smaller generalization gap (0.04) versus Gen 17’s 324 gates, suggesting targeted gate pruning could reclaim efficiency without sacrificing performance.  
- Generalization gap trends: Circuits combining multi-axis mixing and parameter sharing (Gen 15 gap = 0.04, Gen 17 gap = 0.04) halve the gap of over-simplified (Gen 16 gap = 0.06) or over-deep (Gen 14 gap = 0.09) designs, confirming a sweet spot in block complexity.  
- Convergence speed correlations: The addition of dual entanglement layers (CRY + CRX) not only lifts accuracy but also accelerates convergence (Gen 17 at ~750 steps vs. Gen 14/16 at ~300–1560 steps), correlating richer block expressivity with faster optimizer progress.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Reduce per-block gate count by pruning the end-of-chain CNOTs: in your circular CNOT ladder (step 2 of ANSATZ_SPEC), remove the links on wires [7,0] and [3,4] across all blocks. This cuts ~2 two-qubit gates per block (≈16 gates total)—mimicking Gen 15’s leaner 252-gate design—while preserving connectivity, to test if you can retain 0.88/0.90 accuracy with a smaller generalization gap.  

2. Alternate CRY/CRX ordering between repeats: for `repeat_index == 1` in each upload, swap the entanglement sequence to apply CRX before CRY (i.e. RY→CNOT→RZ→CRX→CRY→RY). This per-block axis-mixing variation builds on the proven benefit of dual entanglers and may yield further accuracy or convergence-speed gains via diversified expressivity.  

3. Append a lightweight CRZ mixing layer after CRX in each block: extend your block to RY→CNOT→RZ→CRY→CRX→CRZ→RY, using four new mirrored parameters `crz_a,…,crz_d`. Prior low-param two-qubit rotations (e.g. Gen 5’s trainable CRZs) boosted validation per extra angle without breaching the ~350-gate sweet spot, so this adds targeted phase mixing.  

4. Redistribute uploads vs. repeats: experiment with N_UPLOADS=2 and N_REPEATS=3 (keeping total blocks = 6) so you perform deeper local stacking per feature upload. Insights show that richer intra-upload mixing can speed convergence; testing this layout may further push the optimizer past the ~750‐step threshold.  

5. Enforce soft mirror symmetry during training: add to your L2 loss a term λ·Σ (θ_i – θ_j)² over each mirrored parameter pair in initial/final RY and in CRY/CRX couplings. Gentle symmetry regularization has been shown to stabilize training and minimize variance (std≈0.00) without rigid tying, balancing flexibility and the low-gap benefits of Gen 15/17.
