# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Incorporation of long-range parametric two-qubit rotations into a CZ backbone drove the jump from raw 0.98 to 0.99. The 8-qubit layered CZ-CRZ hardware-efficient ansatz classifier (Gen 7, 122 params, 252 gates, depth 87) outperformed the 140-param ShinkaEvolve (raw 0.98, depth 81, 222 gates) by adding just four CRZ links per block.  
- Alternating RY and RZ single-qubit layers around a compact entangler yielded both rapid convergence (~240 steps) and zero generalization gap. Gen 7’s 8+4+8 rotation pattern per 20-param block matches this recipe.  
- Preserving the three-upload × two-repeat feature-upload layout maintained expressivity while bounding circuit resources, as seen in Gen 7 vs. deeper RY-RZ-CZ-CRY variants (depth 144, 360 gates) that only hit raw 0.98.  
- A balanced gate mix (∼32% single-qubit, ∼68% two-qubit per block) maximized expressivity per parameter, delivering perfect train/validation/test accuracies without over-entangling.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-entanglement without CRZ flexibility: the RY-RZ-CZ-CRY ansatz classifier (206 params, 360 gates) matched raw 0.98 but at 44% more gates than Gen 7, yielding no accuracy gain and lower parameter efficiency.  
- Excessive parameter sharing or reduction hurt expressivity: the Even-Odd Shared-Rotation (44 params, raw 0.95) and Shared-Parameter Re-Uploading (32 params, raw 0.97) classifiers converged in >480–1,380 steps and never reached perfect test accuracy.  
- Aggressive pruning of single-qubit layers or CZ links to cut depth below ~80 increased convergence time and dropped combined scores below 0.98, indicating a lower bound on entanglement density for this task.  
- Sole reliance on fixed CZ entanglers (no long-range CRZ) capped raw scores at ~0.97–0.98 even when depth exceeded 100, showing that expressivity requires parameterized two-qubit gates.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC as a flat list of dicts with explicit `"param"` keys enabled seamless extension from CZ to CRZ: the Gen 7 block adds four CRZ entries to the backbone without restructuring the code.  
- The `validate_ansatz_spec` → `apply_ansatz_block` pattern decouples gate specification from parameter indexing, making it trivial to adjust the RY/RZ count or insert CRX/CRY variants for future sweeps.  
- Leveraging PennyLane’s backprop Adam optimizer with per-epoch validation checks and best-weight restoration (early stopping, patience = 75) ensured stable convergence in ~240 steps and zero generalization gap, mirroring ShinkaEvolve’s training stability.  
- The hardware_efficient_checkerboard’s CZ ladder (0–1,2–3,…,7–0) plus CRZ cross-links evenly covers both local and long-range correlations in only two entangling layers per block, minimizing depth while maximizing expressivity.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Moving from ShinkaEvolve (raw 0.98, 140 params, depth 81, 222 gates) to Gen 7 (raw 0.99, 122 params, depth 87, 252 gates) yielded a +0.01 raw score boost with 13% fewer parameters and only a 13% gate-count increase.  
- Gen 7’s convergence (~240 steps) was only marginally slower than ShinkaEvolve (~210 steps) but achieved perfect test accuracy (1.00) vs. raw 0.98, demonstrating the value of strategic CRZ insertion.  
- Parameter efficiency (test_accuracy / n_params) remained at 0.01 for both top circuits, indicating that mid-sized ansätze (~120–140 params) hit the sweet spot for this dataset.  
- Circuits with <50 params never broke raw 0.97, and those with >200 params yielded no further accuracy gains, underscoring that balanced mid-range parameter counts plus targeted two-qubit flexibility deliver optimal performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parameterize the local CZ backbone by swapping select CZs for CRZs. For example, in ANSATZ_SPEC replace the CZs on wires [2,3] and [6,7] with  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[2,3],"param":"crz_back_b_0"}` and  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[6,7],"param":"crz_back_b_1"}` (with block‐indexed names). This adds four extra two‐qubit parameters per block directly into the hardware‐efficient ladder, testing whether local entanglement flexibility further boosts expressivity without a large depth increase.  

2. Add a second long-range CRZ layer per block to capture mid-range correlations. Immediately after the existing four CRZ entries in ANSATZ_SPEC, append four more CRZs on the “anti-diagonal” pairs  
   (0,7), (1,2), (4,3), (5,6) with new params `crz2_b_0…crz2_b_3`. This ups the two-qubit‐to-single-qubit gate ratio slightly toward the proven ~68% mark, letting you probe whether denser long-range links shave convergence steps or edge the raw score above 0.99.  

3. Turn the fixed feature-map scale into a small trainable vector of length N_UPLOADS. Replace the scalar FEATURE_SCALE with `FEATURE_SCALE_U = pnp.array([...], requires_grad=True)` of shape (3,), then in feature_map use `FEATURE_SCALE_U[upload_index]` instead of π/2. You only add three parameters but enable the model to learn optimal encoding amplitudes per upload—a strategy that often speeds convergence without altering circuit depth.  

4. Consolidate each qubit’s two single-qubit rotations into a single U3 gate to reduce gate count. In each block’s ANSATZ_SPEC, swap the RY and RZ entries for qubit q with  
   `{"gate":"U3","wires":[q],"param":["θ_b_q","φ_b_q","λ_b_q"]}`. U3 captures full SU(2) freedom in three parameters versus two sequential rotations and can cut the single-qubit gate count by ~25%, testing if richer gates yield faster or more parameter-efficient training.  

5. Alternate controlled-rotation axes across blocks to diversify entanglement. In the long-range layer, for even block indices use CRZ gates, and for odd block indices swap those four CRZs to CRY (with new param names like `cry_b_0…cry_b_3`). This block‐wise axis mixing builds on Gen 7’s success with CRZ flexibility and probes whether orthogonal two-qubit rotations further tighten generalization or speed up convergence.
