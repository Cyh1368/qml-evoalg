# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Multi‐layer feature re‐uploading combined with layered per‐qubit rotations and a linear CZ chain delivered the top performance. The “8-qubit Quantum Binary Classifier” (depth 99, 270 gates, 146 params) using 3 uploads × 2 repeats of per-qubit RY, pre-RZ, CZ, post-RZ blocks achieved combined_score_raw 0.97, test_acc 0.98, gap 0.01 in ~180 steps.
- Dense RY–RZ rotation patterns interleaved with ring CNOT entanglement (Generation 3 “optimize_ansatz_topology_and_efficiency”) yielded near-perfect accuracy (test_acc 0.99), combined_score_raw 0.96, and converged in ~90 steps—showing that rich single-qubit rotations plus global entanglement stabilize training.
- Including pre- and post-entanglement Z-rotations around two-qubit gates (as in the current best program) consistently improved expressivity over single-rotation layers seen in brickwall and ring variants.
- Trainable gain and bias calibration of the mean Z-expectation, together with Adam optimization and validation-based early stopping, stabilized convergence and controlled overfitting across high-parameter circuits.

Ineffective Approaches
- Uniform collective rotations (“Collective Rotation Ansatz Classifier,” Generation 2) with only 20 parameters achieved combined_score_raw 0.74 and test_acc 0.75 despite high parameter efficiency (0.04), indicating insufficient expressivity.
- A simple RY+CNOT ladder (“8-Qubit RY+CNOT Ansatz Binary Classifier,” Generation 4) underfit the data (train_acc 0.71) and overfit slightly (generalization_gap 0.12), producing a combined_score of –2.87.
- Brickwall CNOT patterns with fewer layers (“8-Qubit Repeated Brick-Wall Ansatz Classifier,” Generation 1) reached a respectable combined_score_raw 0.96 but fell short of the best 0.97, suggesting that linear CZ chains and extra Z-rotations add valuable representational power.
- Reductions in gate count or entanglement complexity below a certain threshold led to rapidly diminishing accuracy and generalization performance.

Implementation Insights
- Validating the ansatz via a formal validate_ansatz_spec step ensures error-free construction of QNode tapes and clean mapping of parameter keys—critical for reliably repeating blocks.
- The best program’s explicit split of each block into RY on every qubit, per-qubit pre/post RZ, and a contiguous CZ chain (146 params, depth 99) maximizes expressivity while remaining within the fixed 3×2 upload/repeat framework.
- Early stopping on validation loss with best-weight restoration (patience 75, min_delta 1e-4) prevents overtraining in deeper circuits and keeps the generalization_gap at ~0.01.
- The combination of batch‐mode Adam updates, a small parameter initialization range (±0.05), and calibrated readout (gain initialized to 1, bias to 0) provides stable, reproducible convergence across seeds.

Performance Analysis
- A clear trend shows that increasing circuit parameters from 20 to 146 and depth from 71 to 99 correlates with combined_score_raw rising from 0.74 to 0.97 and test_acc improving from 0.75 to 0.98, though parameter efficiency falls from 0.04 to 0.01.
- Higher gate_count (270 vs. 228) and longer depth allow richer entanglement and rotation patterns, supporting faster convergence relative to medium-sized designs (brickwall converged in ~390 steps, current best in ~180).
- Ring vs. linear CZ entanglement yields similar peak accuracies (0.99 vs. 0.98) but slightly lower combined_score for ring variants, highlighting the marginal benefit of linear chains plus extra Z-rotations.
- All high-parameter designs maintained a small generalization_gap (~0.01), confirming that repeated feature uploads plus early stopping effectively control overfitting.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend each ansatz block with a parameterized RX layer after the post-RZ rotations (i.e. RY → RZpre → CZ chain → RZpost → RX). Dense single-qubit rotations (RY–RZ patterns) have stabilized training and adding an extra RX per qubit will increase expressivity in the same lightweight fashion that improved performance in Generation 3.  

2. Alternate your linear CZ chain with a ring-CNOT entanglement in every second block (e.g. even-indexed blocks use CZ chain, odd-indexed use a CNOT ring). Ring CNOT plus rich rotation patterns converged in ~90 steps with test_acc 0.99, so interleaving them may combine rapid convergence with the marginal expressivity benefit of linear CZ+pre/post Z.  

3. Increase the number of repeats per upload from 2 to 3 (set N_REPEATS = 3) while keeping N_UPLOADS = 3. Global insights show that deeper circuits (higher depth and parameter count) consistently raised combined_score from 0.96 to 0.97; testing an extra repeat could further boost representational capacity without fundamentally altering the upload/repeat framework.  

4. Insert parameterized two-qubit rotations (e.g. CRZ or CRX) immediately after every second CZ in the chain. Pre- and post-entanglement Z-rotations boosted expressivity in the best program, so adding trainable two-qubit rotations will enrich nonlocal couplings and may reduce the number of blocks needed to reach peak accuracy.  

5. Introduce trainable per-feature scaling factors in the feature_map (i.e. replace fixed FEATURE_SCALE with a learnable vector of length N_FEATURES initialized to π/2). Just as readout gain/bias calibration stabilized convergence, learning which features to emphasize during each upload should improve mapping flexibility and help control overfitting in these high-parameter circuits.
