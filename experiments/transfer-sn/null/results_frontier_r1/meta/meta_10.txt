# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating per-qubit RY–RX rotations with ring CZ entanglement (Generation 7 “ry_cz_rx_efficient”) achieved the top combined_score_raw of 0.99, perfect train/validation/test accuracy, zero generalization gap, and convergence in ~150 steps.  
- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats (used in Generations 5–9) consistently drove high accuracy (≥0.97) and minimal generalization gaps (≤0.01).  
- Wrapping CZ layers with local Z rotations (Generation 5 “commutation_tied_rz” and Generation 9 “tied_phase_cz_brickwall”) boosted expressivity—each reached combined_score_raw ≈ 0.97 with train/validation ≈ 1.00 and test ≈ 0.98.  
- Trainable gain/bias readout calibration plus Adam optimization and validation-based early stopping stabilized convergence and kept overfitting in check across all high-performing circuits.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Very low-parameter collective rotations (Generation 2 “Collective Rotation Ansatz Classifier,” 20 params) underfit severely (combined_score_raw 0.74, test_acc 0.75).  
- Simple RY+CNOT ladders (Generation 4) achieved only 0.71 train accuracy and suffered a 0.12 generalization gap, indicating insufficient expressivity.  
- Shallow brickwall CNOT patterns with fewer layers (Generation 1) plateaued at combined_score_raw 0.96 (test_acc 0.99) despite good efficiency, falling short of richer CZ-based chains.  
- Overly dense entanglement without parameter reduction (Generation 8 “ring_cross_cz_rich_entangle,” 146 params, 288 gates) increased circuit depth and gate count but did not outperform leaner designs, yielding the same perfect accuracy but lower parameter efficiency.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Formal spec validation via validate_ansatz_spec and ordered_unique_keys ensured correct parameter indexing for Generation 7’s 16-parameter block, preventing gate-parameter mismatches.  
- Vectorized batch predictions using pnp.array and pnp.stack of qml.expval outputs (predict_batch) accelerated forward passes and gradient computations.  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript metadata extraction enabled precise measurement of circuit depth and gate count, linking structural changes directly to performance shifts.  
- The current best program’s lean block (per-qubit RY, ring CZ, per-qubit RX) yields 98 total trainable parameters—versus 146 in richer ansätze—demonstrating that a streamlined design can maximize accuracy and minimize overfitting.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined_score_raw rose from 0.97 (Generations 5 & 9 with 98 params but linear CZ + extra Z-rotations) to 0.98 (Generations 6 & 8 with brickwall/rich entanglers) to 0.99 (Generation 7 with ring CZ + RX/RY). This shows judicious choice of entangling topology and rotation layers matters more than sheer parameter count.  
- The current best program (Gen 7) uses depth 81 and gate_count 228, outperforming deeper circuits (e.g., Gen 5’s depth 99/270 gates) in both convergence speed (~150 vs. ~180 steps) and accuracy consistency (zero gap).  
- All high-parameter designs share a low parameter efficiency (~0.01), but reducing from 146 to 98 parameters in Generation 7 delivered the highest effective score per parameter and fastest convergence.  
- Zero generalization_gap in Generation 7 versus ~0.01 in other top performers underscores that a balanced, moderate-complexity ansatz can eliminate overfitting while maintaining perfect classification.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Wrap each CZ‐ring in per‐qubit Z rotations. Global insights from Generations 5 and 9 showed that commutation‐tied RZ layers around CZ chains boosted expressivity without overfitting. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, insert a parameterized RZ on every qubit immediately before and after the CZ‐ring (e.g. “RZpre_i→CZ chain→RZpost_i”), adding 16 extra Z‐parameters per block to enrich phase flexibility while preserving the lean RY–CZ–RX structure.  

2. Alternate CZ topologies across blocks. Generation 6 (“ry_cz_rx_brickwall”) and Generation 7 (“ry_cz_rx_efficient”) respectively achieved 0.98 and 0.99 combined scores by leveraging brickwall and ring entanglers. In your block‐loop, use the existing ring‐CZ pattern for even block indices and swap in a brickwall CZ pattern (e.g. entangling pairs [0,2],[1,3],[4,6],[5,7],[2,4],[3,5],[6,0],[7,1]) for odd block indices to combine the rapid convergence of ring CZ with the marginal expressivity boost of brickwall layouts.  

3. Enrich nonlocal coupling with sparse CRZ gates. Adding trainable two‐qubit rotations after CZs has been hypothesized to reduce block count needed for peak accuracy. Modify every second CZ in the ring (for example, the CZ between qubits (1,2),(3,4),…) to be followed by a CRZ on the same wires; this injects extra adjustable entanglement phases without dramatically increasing gate count.  

4. Increase feature re‐uploads to four rounds. The best program’s three re‐uploads drove zero generalization gap, but deeper feature mixing may allow the ansatz to encode more complex decision boundaries with the same per‐block structure. Set N_UPLOADS = 4 and keep N_REPEATS = 2, then retune parameter indexing so you have four feature‐map→ansatz sequences; this tests whether richer data re‐injection can further accelerate convergence.  

5. Make the feature scale learnable per upload. Trainable gain/bias readout calibration stabilized convergence across all top circuits, suggesting upstream feature‐scaling could also adapt to data. Replace the fixed FEATURE_SCALE with a trainable vector feat_scale of length N_UPLOADS (initialized to π/2) and multiply each upload’s IsingZZ angles by the corresponding feat_scale[upload_index], giving the circuit control over how strongly each feature‐reupload is imprinted.
