The current ansatz already achieves excellent performance (98% test accuracy, 1% generalization gap, parameter efficiency of 0.01). However, the connectivity is currently limited to nearest-neighbor linear CZ gates only. 

To improve further while maintaining efficiency, I propose adding parametrized controlled-RZ gates that create longer-range entanglement patterns. Specifically, I'll add CRZ gates in a "skip-1" pattern (connecting qubits separated by 1 position: 0-2, 1-3, 2-4, etc.). This:

1. **Enhances expressive power**: Longer-range entanglement allows the circuit to capture more complex correlations between distant qubits
2. **Maintains efficiency**: Only 6 additional parametrized gates per block (modest increase from 24 to 30 unique parameters per block)
3. **Improves information flow**: Provides alternative pathways for quantum information propagation beyond nearest-neighbor coupling
4. **Respects circuit structure**: Adds gates after the existing linear chain, preserving the learned benefits of the current structure

The skip-1 pattern (0-2, 1-3, 2-4, 3-5, 4-6, 5-7) is a well-established entanglement topology in quantum machine learning that bridges the gap between local and global connectivity, often improving generalization without excessive complexity.

This modest enhancement should yield:
- Slight test accuracy improvement (potentially 0.98 → 0.99+)
- Maintained or improved generalization (gap stays ≤0.02)
- Reasonable parameter efficiency despite adding ~36 circuit parameters
