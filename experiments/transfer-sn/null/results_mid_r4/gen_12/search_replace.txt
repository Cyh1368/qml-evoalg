<DESCRIPTION>
The current top-performing ansatz uses an RY-layer, a CRZ-ring, and an RX-layer (24 parameters per block, 146 total). It achieves 100% accuracy with a validation loss of 0.09 and fast convergence (120 steps).

To improve the combined score, I am proposing a design that maintains the 24-parameter-per-block budget but increases the entanglement complexity and rotational coverage. By replacing the CRZ-ring with a more diverse set of controlled rotations—specifically, a combination of CRZ for phase entanglement and CRY for state-population mixing across different qubit distances—we can potentially lower the loss further.

The proposed block structure:
1.  **RY Layer** (8 params): Initial basis rotation.
2.  **CRZ Ring** (8 params): Nearest-neighbor phase entanglement.
3.  **CRY Long-Range** (8 params): Long-distance (skip-3) population entanglement (0-4, 1-5, 2-6, 3-7, 4-0, 5-1, 6-2, 7-3).

This structure increases the "mixing" of information across the register, which is beneficial for the 8-qubit binary classification task where features are encoded as qubit-pair phase couplings.
