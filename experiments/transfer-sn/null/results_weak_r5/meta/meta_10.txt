# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Shared‐parameter phase shifts: The current best program (Parameter‐Efficient 8-Qubit Ansatz, Gen 5) uses just two shared RZ parameters before entanglement and one shared RZ after, cutting tunable circuit parameters to 84 (plus gain/bias) and still reaching a combined raw score of 0.99 with perfect train/validation/test accuracy.  
- Sparse, targeted two-qubit entanglers: Gen 5’s four CRZ gates per block (wires [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7]) provided enough coupling to achieve zero generalization gap, matching the 100% accuracy of much heavier designs (e.g. Gen 4’s 188-parameter ansatz) while keeping parameter count and optimization steps low (~210).  
- Multi-upload data re-injection with modest repeats: All top performers—including Gen 5—use N_UPLOADS = 3 interleaved with N_REPEATS = 2. This layout consistently converged in ~210 steps to perfect accuracy, demonstrating that three re-uploads strike the right balance of expressivity and trainability.  
- Trainable readout calibration (gain + bias): As in Gen 5 and preceding best models (Gens 1 and 6), adding two scalar parameters on the mean‐Z measurement dropped final test loss to ~0.06 and stabilized convergence, ensuring zero generalization gap.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Overparameterized entanglement layers without sharing: Gen 4’s layered_entanglement_ansatz used 188 parameters, reached only a 0.98 combined score and required 300 steps—demonstrating diminishing returns and lower parameter efficiency (~0.01).  
- Fully independent CRZ couplings: Gen 7 (“make_crz_independent”) expanded to 140 parameters by giving each CRZ its own angle, yet only achieved a 0.98 combined score and test accuracy 0.99, indicating overfitting risk and hardware impracticality.  
- Deep staircase designs with redundant gates: Gen 8’s CRZ staircase ansatz (146 parameters, depth 136) matched the 0.99 score of leaner circuits but suffered the same low efficiency (0.01) and added needless gate overhead.  
- Excessive pruning of entanglers: The earlier Parity-Shared ansatz (Gen 3) cut parameters to 38 but collapsed expressivity, falling to 0.75 combined score and a 0.06 generalization gap—showing there is a lower bound (~86–110 parameters) below which multi-upload classifiers lose capacity.

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC plus generic apply_ansatz_block: Defining each gate as a dict and automating parameter‐to‐vector mapping (via `validate_ansatz_spec` and `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`) allowed rapid swapping between shared vs. independent gates, making it trivial to prototype Gen 5’s shared-RZ/sparse-CRZ pattern.  
- Shared parameter mapping via dict.fromkeys: In Gen 5, using a single key “rz_shared_pre” (and “rz_shared_post”) across eight wires ensures compact storage and consistent update, directly translating to reduced parameter count and improved efficiency.  
- Consistent multi-upload loop structure: Embedding `feature_map` then two repeats of `apply_ansatz_block` in the QNode for each of three uploads ensured uniform capacity across models, and highlighted that expressivity gains come from parameter sharing and sparse entanglement rather than additional repeats.  
- Automated metadata extraction: Using `AnnotatedQueue` and `QuantumScript.from_queue` in every variant gave reliable depth and gate counts, enabling direct comparisons (e.g. depth 138/gates 336 for Gen 5 vs. depth 81/gates 222 for Gen 7) that informed which circuits were unnecessarily heavy.

## Performance Analysis  
- Score plateau at 0.99 with ~86–110 parameters: Gen 1 (110 parameters, depth 81) and Gen 5 (86 parameters, depth 138) both hit combined raw 0.99, showing that sharing plus sparse entanglement can compress earlier high-performing designs without accuracy loss.  
- Parameter efficiency peaks with Gen 5: Test_accuracy / n_params ≈ 1.00 / 86 ≈ 0.0116, outpacing Gen 1’s ~0.0091 and Gen 6’s ~0.0063, confirming that the shared-RZ + sparse-CRZ block is the most resource-efficient to date.  
- Convergence stability around 210 steps: All top‐scoring models using multi-upload and shared parameters (Gens 1, 5, 6) converged near 210 optimizer steps, compared to 300 steps for the overparameterized Gen 4—highlighting that leaner parameterizations also train faster.  
- Depth vs. parameter trade-off: Although Gen 5’s circuit (depth 138) is deeper than Gen 1’s (depth 81), it uses 24% fewer parameters to attain the same 0.99 score, indicating that deeper circuits with shared gates can be preferable to shallower, heavily parameterized ones.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce per-upload shared pre-phase shifts. Replace the single “rz_shared_pre” key with three upload-specific keys (“rz_pre_u0”, “rz_pre_u1”, “rz_pre_u2”), each applied to all eight wires before the entanglers in upload 0, 1, and 2. This adds only two extra parameters but leverages the proven multi-upload structure by giving each data re-injection its own global phase degree of freedom, which may boost expressivity without bloating the circuit.  

2. Enrich two-qubit rotations with mixed axes in one ansatz block. In, say, upload 1 repeat 0, swap the four CRZ gates for two shared CRX gates on wires [0,1] and [4,5] (key “crx_shared”) and two shared CRY gates on wires [2,3] and [6,7] (key “cry_shared”). This adds just two parameters while diversifying the rotation axes in the sparse entangler pattern—building directly on Gen 5’s success with shared-parameter CRZ but improving gradient flow.  

3. Insert a sparse diagonal CRZ entangler between the second CZ backbone and the final RZ. After the fixed CZs on [(0,2), (1,3), (2,4), (3,5), (4,6), (5,7)], append four CRZ gates on diagonal pairs [(1,2), (3,4), (5,6), (7,0)] all sharing a new key “crz_diag.” You gain one extra parameter and a shallow depth increase, extending long-range coupling in the same shared-parameter, sparse style that underpins Gen 5’s high efficiency.  

4. Augment the feature-map with a fixed IsingXX re-injection layer. Between upload 1 and upload 2, call qml.IsingXX(FEATURE_SCALE * records[..., k], wires=[a, b]) over FEATURE_PAIRS—mirroring the existing ZZ encoding but along the XX axis. This costs zero trainable parameters, enriches data nonlinearity across uploads, and follows the multi-upload insight that expressivity derives more from interleaved couplings than sheer parameter count.  

5. Explore a 4×1 upload-repeat layout while keeping total blocks constant. Change to N_UPLOADS = 4 and N_REPEATS = 1 (still six ansatz blocks total), so you interleave four data encodings with one shared-parameter ansatz block each. This tests the trade-off between more frequent re-injection and fewer repeats, directly extending the proven multi-upload strategy to discover whether a higher upload cadence can match or exceed Gen 5’s convergence in ~210 steps.
