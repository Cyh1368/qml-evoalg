# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Multi-upload with modest repeats (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2):  
  • Seen in the current best (“shared_rz_czring_sparsecrz_reuploadfriendly,” Gen 14) achieving combined_score_raw 0.99, perfect accuracies, zero generalization gap, and convergence in ~210 steps.  
  • Also in Gen 17 (0.99 combined, 80 params) and Gen 16 (0.98 combined, 74 params).  
- Shared global pre- and post-RZ phases:  
  • Collapses eight per-qubit RZ parameters into two scalars per block.  
  • Enabled the 86-param best design to match the 1.00 test accuracy of heavier models (e.g. 104 params in Gen 11) at fewer parameters.  
- Sparse parametrized CRZ entanglers plus a second fixed CZ layer:  
  • Four CRZ angles per block on disjoint pairs (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7) combined with an extra CZ “mixing” layer densify correlations.  
  • The best program (86 params) hits 1.00 accuracy with depth 138/336 gates, matching Gen 9’s 170 params but at half the trainable count.  
- Parameter “sweet spot” ≈80–86:  
  • Gen 17 (80 params) and the current best (86 params) both achieve combined_score_raw 0.99 and perfect accuracies.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized entanglement layers (>110 params):  
  • Gen 18 (116 params) converged in ~300 steps with the same 1.00 test accuracy but lower parameter efficiency (≈0.008), and Gen 19 (248 params) saw combined_score_raw drop to 0.96.  
  • Extra trainable two-qubit rotations beyond sparse CRZ gave no accuracy gain but slowed convergence.  
- Under-parameterization (<80 params):  
  • Gen 16 (74 params) fell to combined_score_raw 0.98, validation accuracy 0.99, and took ~240 steps, indicating expressivity loss below ~80 params.  
- Fully independent or mixed-axis controlled rotations:  
  • Designs with per-edge CRX/CRY (Gen 11, Gen 18) matched 1.00 accuracy but with more parameters (104–116) and deeper circuits, yielding worse efficiency and no accuracy benefit.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec → ordered unique parameter keys:  
  • dict.fromkeys on the collected PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK automatically collapses repeated “rz_shared_pre” and “rz_shared_post” entries.  
- Key-to-position mapping in apply_ansatz_block:  
  • A single loop over ANSATZ_SPEC dispatches gates by name and wire(s), so swapping shared vs. independent parameters or adding/removing gates is confined to ANSATZ_SPEC.  
- Dual fixed CZ layering (“backbone” CZ ring + additional CZ links):  
  • Adds nonlocal correlations at zero parameter cost. The best program’s 16 fixed CZ gates per block yield expressivity previously achieved only by extra controlled rotations in heavier designs.  
- Re-uploading Pattern:  
  • The repeated feature map → ansatz block loop (3× uploads × 2 repeats) systematically amplifies expressivity, enabling perfect classification with only 86 trainable parameters.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined_score_raw plateau at 0.99 for 80–116 params:  
  • 80 params (Gen 17) and 86 params (current best) both hit 0.99, but 74 params drop to 0.98 and 248 params to 0.96.  
- Parameter efficiency peaks around 80–86 params (~0.011–0.012):  
  • Current best (86 params) yields ≈0.0116; Gen 17 (80 params) ≈0.0125. Over-parameterized models fall below 0.009.  
- Convergence speed inversely correlates with parameter count:  
  • 86 params → ~210 steps; 74 params → ~240 steps; 116 params → ~300 steps.  
- Depth/gate trade-off:  
  • Although Gen 18 is shallower (depth 81, 222 gates), its higher param count slows training. The best balance occurs at depth 138/336 gates with sparse sharing, maximizing both speed and efficiency.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the single “rz_shared_pre” and “rz_shared_post” with upload-specific RZ scalars: define keys “rz_pre_u0”, “rz_pre_u1”, “rz_pre_u2” before each of the three uploads and likewise “rz_post_u0”, “rz_post_u1”, “rz_post_u2” after each block. This adds only four parameters (bringing you to ~90) but gives each data re-injection its own global phase, directly extending the proven benefit of multi-upload phase diversity without bloating the circuit.

2. In the second repeat of each upload, swap the four independent CRZs for a mixed-axis sparse layer: two shared CRX gates on [(0,1),(4,5)] keyed “crx_shared” and two shared CRY gates on [(2,3),(6,7)] keyed “cry_shared.” This injects orthogonal control rotations as in Gen 5, diversifying entangler axes to improve gradient flow and expressivity at only two extra parameters.

3. Immediately after each feature_map and before the first ansatz block, interleave a fixed IsingXX layer on every FEATURE_PAIRS wire—i.e. qml.IsingXX(FEATURE_SCALE * x_k, wires=pair_k). This zero-parameter coupling axis boost leverages the same fixed-gate insight that the extra CZ backbone provided, enriching nonlinear mixing across uploads without increasing trainable count.

4. Reduce the four sparse CRZ parameters to two by sharing them across symmetric pairs: use “crz_even” for edges (0,1) and (2,3) and “crz_odd” for (4,5) and (6,7). This symmetry-driven sharing tests whether you can maintain perfect accuracy and converge in ~210 steps with only ~84 variational angles, further probing the 80–86 parameter sweet spot.

5. Swap to an N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3 schedule (still six ansatz blocks total) to explore deeper intra-upload stacking versus more frequent injections. Keeping the same block design and parameter budget will reveal whether extra repeats per upload can match or exceed the convergence speed and test accuracy of the 3×2 layout.
