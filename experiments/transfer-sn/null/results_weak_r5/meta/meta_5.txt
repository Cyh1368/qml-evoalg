# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Shared‐parameter entangling layers: the Shared-CRZ Ansatz (Gen 1) reduced two-qubit parameters from 28 distinct CRZs to just “crz_even” and “crz_odd,” cutting total parameters from 146→110 and depth from 99→81, while boosting combined score from 0.97→0.99 with perfect accuracies and zero generalization gap.  
- Multi-upload data re-injection: all top performers (Gen 0, Gen 1, Gen 6) used N_UPLOADS=3 interleaved with N_REPEATS=2 and achieved 100% test accuracy; the current best (Gen 6, “8-Qubit Data Reupload Classifier”) converged in ~210 steps to combined raw 0.99.  
- Calibrated readout (gain + bias): every program that achieved stable perfect classification incorporated trainable gain and bias on the mean‐Z readout, reducing residual test loss (e.g. validation/test loss ~0.06 in Gen 6).  
- Early-stopping with best-weight restoration under Adam backprop: consistent rapid convergence (≈180–210 steps) and zero overfitting across Gen 0→Gen 6, demonstrating this training regime reliably yields strong generalization.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-pruned entanglement (Gen 3 “Parity-Shared” with 38 parameters) lost expressivity: train accuracy fell to 86%, test to 80%, combined raw 0.75 and a 0.06 generalization gap.  
- Naïvely increasing parameters without sharing (Gen 2’s 188 parameters) did not improve combined score beyond 0.97→0.97→0.99 plateau and suffered low parameter efficiency (0.01), signaling diminishing returns past ~110–158 parameters.  
- Rigid, unshared two-qubit rotations (full distinct CRZ per wire) inflated parameter count with no measurable accuracy gain over shared-CRZ designs.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Centralized ansatz validation (`validate_ansatz_spec`) and automatic parameter‐to‐vector mapping enable flexible gate sharing: e.g. mapping “crz_odd” → single index across four odd-pair gates.  
- Generic `apply_ansatz_block` driven by a declarative `ANSATZ_SPEC` permits rapid experimentation with gate ordering, making it trivial to swap CZ↔CRZ or share parameters.  
- Metadata extraction via Pennylane’s `AnnotatedQueue` and `QuantumScript.from_queue` provides reliable depth and gate counts for performance tracking.  
- Mini-batch Adam with backprop and early-stopping (patience=75, min_delta=1e-4) consistently yields convergence near 90% validation accuracy threshold by ~210 steps, balancing training speed against overfitting.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined raw score evolution: 0.97 (Gen 0, full RY-RZ-CZ) → 0.99 (Gen 1 shared-CRZ) → plateau at 0.99 (Gen 6 Data Reupload).  
- Parameter vs. depth trade-off: Gen 1 achieved 0.99 with 110 parameters and depth 81, while Gen 6 matched 0.99 with 158 parameters and depth 99—suggesting shared entanglement layers deliver depth and parameter savings without accuracy loss.  
- Perfect test accuracy and zero generalization gap occur consistently once parameter count exceeds ~110 with multi-upload entanglement patterns; below that (Gen 3) expressivity collapses.  
- Parameter efficiency (test_accuracy/n_params) peaks at Gen 1 (~0.0091) versus Gen 6 (~0.0063), illustrating that beyond ~110 parameters, additional gates yield minimal performance returns.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Share the eight final RZ rotations into just two parameters—“rz_final_even” and “rz_final_odd”—instead of eight distinct keys. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the eight final RZ entries with four wired to “rz_final_even” (wires 0,2,4,6) and four wired to “rz_final_odd” (wires 1,3,5,7). Gen 1’s shared‐parameter entangling layer cut both depth and parameter count with no loss in accuracy, so applying the same sharing idea to the final rotations should further boost parameter efficiency without sacrificing expressivity.  
2. Introduce a third entangling layer acting on “diagonal” pairs—e.g. wires [(0,2),(1,3),(4,6),(5,7)]—using a single shared CRZ parameter “crz_diag.” Append these four CRZ(“crz_diag”)→CZ gates between your existing even and odd layers in ANSATZ_SPEC. This adds only one parameter and a shallow depth increase, leveraging the proven benefit of shared‐CRZ gates to enrich expressivity at minimal overhead.  
3. In one of the existing entangling layers, replace the fixed CZ gates with shared‐parameter CRX gates (“crx_even”/“crx_odd”). For example, swap the CZs in the even layer for CRX gates using the same “crz_even” wiring pattern but with a new shared key “crx_even.” This adds an extra controllable rotation axis in the two-qubit interactions, building on the success of shared‐CRZ designs while potentially improving gradient flow and convergence.  
4. Upgrade the readout calibration from a single global gain and bias to per-qubit gains/biases: introduce two parameters (gain_i, bias_i) per qubit (total 16) and compute predictions as mean_i [ gain_i ·(z_i − bias_i) ]. Every top model used calibrated readout to reduce residual loss; moving to a finer‐grained per-wire calibration should further lower test loss while preserving perfect accuracy.  
5. Layer a cyclical learning-rate schedule atop Adam optimizer: for instance, oscillate the step size between 0.01 and 0.05 every 50 global steps. All best programs converged in ~180–210 steps under fixed 0.03; a CLR schedule can sharpen early progress and potentially drive convergence below 180 steps without changing the evaluation code or architecture.
