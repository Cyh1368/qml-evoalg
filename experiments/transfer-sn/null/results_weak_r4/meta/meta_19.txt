# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mixed fixed/parameterized ring entanglement  
  • Staggered CZ/CRZ Ring Classifier (Gen 14) interleaves fixed CZ on even bonds with trainable CRZ on odd bonds, yielding perfect accuracies (1.00) and the lowest validation/test losses (0.03/0.02). This drove its combined score up to 1.51, well above the 0.99 of pure CZ designs.  
- Balanced parameter count per block  
  • The best program uses 20 trainable parameters per block (8 RY + 4 CRZ + 8 RX), for a total of 122+2 readout params—enough extra expressivity over the 16-param shared-RX designs (74 params, score 0.99) to cut losses in half, yet avoids the trainability issues seen at 24 params/block (146 params, score 0.95).  
- Retaining the 3-upload × 2-repeat layout  
  • All top circuits (Gen 14, 17, 19) use N_UPLOADS=3 and N_REPEATS=2. They converge in ~150 optimizer steps, maintain depth 81 and gate_count 228, and deliver zero generalization gap.  
- Local RY–entangler–RX sandwich  
  • Wrapping each staggered ring layer between trainable RY input and RX output rotations remains a robust motif: it underpins both the shared-RX bests (Gen 17/19, score 0.99) and the CRZ-augmented champion (Gen 14, score 1.51).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Exhaustive parameterized two-qubit gates  
  • Gen 16’s six-block ansatz with CRZ on every bond (24 params/block, 146 total) ballooned depth to 105 and gate_count to 276 yet dropped to a combined score of 0.95 and required ~390 steps to converge.  
- Over-parameterized SU(2) blocks  
  • Gen 9’s 98-param Shared-Pair classifier (depth 150, gates 348) achieved only a 0.97 raw score, demonstrating diminishing returns beyond ~100 params.  
- Hybrid CZ + CNOT entanglement  
  • Mixed CZ/CNOT layouts spiked gate_count (~306) and depth (~111), slowed convergence (~390 steps), and fell to a combined score of 0.94.  
- Aggressive parameter sharing  
  • Collapsing all single-qubit rotations into ~50 unique keys produced a lean circuit but under-expressive performance (score 0.73, poor accuracy), showing a floor on permissible sharing.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Generic ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec  
  • Defining gates as dict entries with explicit “param” keys, then auto-extracting PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK, makes swapping CZ⇄CRZ trivial. The champion leverages this to mix fixed and trainable entanglers without code duplication.  
- Key-to-position mapping in apply_ansatz_block  
  • Building a dict of parameter key→index lets the same code path handle RY, RX, CZ, and CRZ uniformly, streamlining entanglement experiments (e.g., toggling CRZ on odd bonds).  
- Real-time depth/gate metrics via AnnotatedQueue  
  • circuit_metadata() immediately reports depth and gate_count, guiding designers to stick to the 81/228 “sweet spot” that correlates with top scores and fast convergence.  
- Decoupled feature_map vs. ansatz application  
  • Separating the fixed IsingZZ embedding from the trainable block logic let teams rapidly iterate local rotations and entanglers, culminating in the optimal RY–(CZ/CRZ)×2–RX structure.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Param count vs. combined_score_raw  
  • 74 params → 0.99; 98 params → 0.99; 122 params → 1.51; 146 params → 0.95. There is a clear sweet zone around 100–125 params: enough capacity to minimize loss, but not so many that training degrades.  
- Loss reductions drive score gains  
  • The champion’s validation/test losses (0.03/0.02) are ~2× lower than in shared-RX designs (0.07/0.05), accounting for the jump from 0.99 to 1.51 despite identical perfect accuracies and zero gap.  
- Depth and gate_count consistency  
  • All circuits hitting combined_score_raw ≥ 0.99 share depth 81 and gate_count 228. Deviations to deeper gates (105–150 depth) uniformly correlate with lower raw scores (0.94–0.97) and slower convergence.  
- Convergence behavior  
  • Top configurations stabilize at ≥ 0.90 validation accuracy in ~150 steps. Under-parameterized or over-entangled variants take ~120 or ~390 steps, respectively, underscoring the expressivity–trainability trade-off maintained by the champion’s balanced design.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Collapse the four odd‐bond CRZ angles into a single shared “ent” parameter per block. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the four entries  
   {“gate”:“CRZ”,“wires”:[i,j],“param”:“crz_…”}  
   with a single key “ent” on all odd bonds. This trims the block from 20→17 trainable params (total params ≃104) and probes whether a more parameter-efficient entanglement still delivers the sub‐0.05 losses that underpin the champion’s high score.  

2. Add a lightweight block‐wise phase offset layer after the RX output rotations. Append four RZ gates on qubits [1,3,5,7] sharing a new key “phi_block” in each block’s ANSATZ_SPEC. This +1 param/+4 gates per block (depth ≃85) gives the circuit limited per-block phase tuning without blowing past the 81–228 sweet-spot footprint.  

3. Alternate the entanglement axis between uploads to inject axis diversity at zero cost to depth or param count. In upload 0 keep CRZ on odd bonds, switch to CRX in upload 1 and CRY in upload 2 (all using the same per-block “crz”/“crx”/“cry” key). This tests whether mixing controlled-rotation axes can further reduce loss by capturing complementary correlations.  

4. Enrich intra‐block connectivity with fixed diagonal CZs. Insert four CZ gates on wires [(0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7)]—for example, immediately after the existing CZ–CRZ pair in each block. Adding these next-nearest-neighbor links (depth +4 gates/block) explores whether the champion’s ring skeleton benefits from a light mesh overlay.  

5. Tie local rotations across repeats to cut redundant parameters and speed convergence. Rename your RY (or RX) keys so that the two repeats under each upload share the same eight angles—e.g. both blocks in upload 0 use “ry0_0”…“ry0_7” and “rx0_0”…“rx0_7”. This slashes per-upload local-mixing params from 16→8 while preserving the proven RY–entangler–RX sandwich and tests if leaner local layers maintain zero gap and low loss.
