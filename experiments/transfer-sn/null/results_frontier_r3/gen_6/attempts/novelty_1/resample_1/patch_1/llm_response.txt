Looking at the current program, the main bottleneck is the **linear CZ entanglement chain** (0→1→2→...→7), which has a graph diameter of 7. Information from qubit 0 needs to pass through 7 intermediate gates to reach qubit 7. This limits convergence speed.

I'll replace the linear chain with a **ring + cross topology** that reduces the graph diameter to ~2, enabling much faster information propagation while keeping the same parameter count.

ring_cross_entanglement
</NAME>

<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses a linear CZ chain (7 gates: 0-1, 1-2, ..., 6-7) for entanglement. This has diameter 7, meaning information takes many ansatz blocks to propagate across all qubits.

I replace this with:
1. Even pairs CZ (0-1, 2-3, 4-5, 6-7) - 4 parallel gates
2. Odd pairs CZ (1-2, 3-4, 5-6, 7-0) - completes the ring, 4 parallel gates  
3. Cross connections CZ (0-4, 1-5, 2-6, 3-7) - long-range entanglement, 4 parallel gates

This creates a graph with diameter ~2, meaning any qubit can communicate with any other in just 2 hops. The total CZ count goes from 7 to 12, but parameter count stays at 24 per block (146 total) since CZ gates are parameter-free.

Expected improvements:
- Faster convergence due to better information flow
- Potentially higher accuracy from richer entanglement structure
- Same parameter count, so no loss in parameter efficiency
</DESCRIPTION>
