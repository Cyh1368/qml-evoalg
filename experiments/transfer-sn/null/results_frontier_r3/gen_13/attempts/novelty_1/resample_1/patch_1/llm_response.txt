An excellent way to improve both parameter efficiency and generalization is to prune redundant rotational layers. 

Since the fixed feature map uses `IsingZZ` (which encodes data into complex phases in the Z-basis) and our entangling gates are `CZ` (also diagonal in the Z-basis), the circuit naturally accumulates a massive amount of Z-rotations. Thus, adding parameterized `RZ` layers is theoretically redundant. The circuit only strictly needs a mixer that does not commute with Z—such as `RY`—to switch between the X and Z bases, making the whole structure act as a highly expressive, hardware-efficient QAOA-like sequence. 

By completely removing the `RZ` gates, we preserve the necessary local adaptability (via independent `RY` rotations) while stripping out overparameterization. This should accelerate optimizer convergence, push parameter efficiency much higher (reducing the total parameter count to just 50), and help maintain a perfect 1.0 test accuracy.
