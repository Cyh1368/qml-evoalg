# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Re-uploading with N_UPLOADS = 3 and N_REPEATS = 2  
    • Present in the current best (Gen 17 “efficient_hybrid_ring_ansatz,” combined_score_raw = 1.00, convergence_step ≈ 210) and earlier Gen 1/Gen 9 solutions.  
    • Ensures sufficient expressivity without exploding circuit depth.  

- Hybrid ring topology: fixed CZ ring + shared-parameter long-range CRZ  
    • Gen 17 (92 params, depth 126, 300 gates) achieved perfect train/val/test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
    • CRZ “long” parameter shared across diagonals concentrates entanglement power cost-effectively.  

- Two-stage RZ parameter sharing  
    • Pre-entanglement RZ in diagonal pairs (“rz_p0…rz_p3”) plus post-entanglement odd/even RZ (“rz_post_even/odd”) in Gen 17.  
    • Maintains high expressivity with only 92 total variables, matching or exceeding more heavily parameterized ansätze.  

- Independent per-qubit RY initialization  
    • Layer 1 RY_0…RY_7 grants qubit-wise state flexibility.  
    • Used in Gen 17 and Gen 13 (“efficient_circular_cry”), correlates with stable, rapid convergence.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mixing multiple entangler types without structured sharing  
    • Gen 18 (“ring_mix_cycle,” 72 params, depth 75, gate_count 180) combined CZ, CRY, local RY but only reached combined_score_raw = 0.99 and converged by step ≈ 510.  
    • Dense local RY+CRY entanglement increases parameter interactions but slows optimization and yields higher test loss (≈ 0.07).  

- Over-parameterized sparse hierarchies  
    • Gen 16 (“hierarchical_sparse_ansatz_with_feature_aware_init,” 122 params, depth 93, 276 gates) scored combined=1.49 but with low parameter efficiency (0.01) and no accuracy gain over leaner designs.  
    • Large parameter counts inflate training cost without improving expressivity on this dataset.  

- Under-parametrized single-gate entanglement  
    • Gen 15 (“Shared-Parameter Entangling Ansatz” variant) with only RY+CRY ring (74 params, 180 gates) scored combined_raw = 0.95.  
    • Lacks long-range coupling, resulting in insufficient state mixing for flawless classification.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC + auto-mapping of parameter keys  
    • Mapping PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK → vector indices (as in Gen 17) eases repeating blocks and sharing parameters across layers.  
    • Facilitates rapid prototyping of new ansatz topologies.  

- Separate, fixed IsingZZ feature_map  
    • Decouples data encoding (FEATURE_SCALE = π/2) from ansatz tuning, focusing optimization on variational layers (used uniformly across all top performers).  

- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue for metadata extraction  
    • Capturing depth/gate_count from a zero-input tape streamlines resource reporting and guided gate-count reductions in the CRZ-ring design.  

- Trainable readout calibration (gain=1, bias=0 init)  
    • Present in Gen 1, Gen 9, Gen 10, and current Gen 17.  
    • Consistently accelerates early convergence by aligning raw Pauli-Z means to label scale.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gen 17 vs. Gen 18  
    • Gen 17: combined_score_raw = 1.00, conv_step ≈ 210; Gen 18: 0.99, conv_step ≈ 510.  
    • Structured long-range CRZ + CZ ring dramatically cuts convergence time and slightly improves accuracy.  

- Parameter count vs. performance  
    • Gen 16 (122 params) matches Gen 13 (80 params) in perfect accuracy but is outperformed in efficiency by Gen 17 (92 params) with faster convergence.  
    • Parameter sharing (RZ stages + CRZ long) yields expressivity comparable to larger hierarchies with fewer steps.  

- Gate count and depth trade-offs  
    • Top accuracy correlates more with balanced entanglement structure than minimum depth: Gen 17 (depth 126, 300 gates) converged faster than Gen 13 (depth 114, 228 gates, conv_step 270).  
    • A slightly deeper circuit with richer coupling can optimize more rapidly than a shallower, narrowly entangled one.  

- Parameter efficiency plateau at ~0.01  
    • All best-in-class (Gen 1, Gen 9, Gen 10, Gen 17) achieve zero gap and perfect accuracy but sit at ~0.01 accuracy/param.  
    • Indicates further efficiency gains require novel ansatz paradigms beyond incremental sharing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the fixed CZ ring with a shared‐parameter CRZ ring. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, swap each CZ in Layer 3 for a CRZ gate and give them all the same param key (e.g. “crz_ring”). This leverages the proven power of tunable CRZ entanglement while adding only one extra variational degree of freedom for nearest‐neighbor coupling, and may accelerate convergence by letting the circuit adapt its local entanglement strength.  

2. Introduce a mid-range CRZ layer for distance-2 couplings. After the existing “crz_long” gates in Layer 4, insert four CRZ gates on wires [(0,2),(1,3),(2,4),(3,5)] (and their modulo-8 partners) all sharing a new key “crz_mid”. This adds just one parameter per block to test whether a multi-scale entanglement hierarchy (nearest, mid, and long range) can boost expressivity beyond the dual‐scale design in Gen 17.  

3. Share the “crz_long” parameter globally across all blocks. Instead of offsetting the crz_long index by block_index, map every block’s “crz_long” to the same position in circuit_params. This cuts down N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS by (N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS−1) and probes whether a uniform long-range coupling across layers retains the perfect accuracy and zero gap of the current best while improving parameter efficiency.  

4. Make the feature‐map scale trainable per upload. Define three new scalars “feature_scale_0”, “feature_scale_1”, “feature_scale_2” (initialized to π/2) and in each upload loop call qml.IsingZZ(feature_scale_k * records[...,k], …). This lets the model learn the optimal encoding strength for each re-upload separately, building on the decoupled IsingZZ map’s stability and potentially improving class separation early in training.  

5. Share full ansatz‐block parameters across repeats within each upload. Modify apply_ansatz_block so that both repeats for a given upload_index use the same slice of circuit_params (i.e. block_index = upload_index). This halves the block-level parameter count without changing depth or gate count, directly testing whether the second repeat truly needs independent angles or simply reinforces the same transformation.
