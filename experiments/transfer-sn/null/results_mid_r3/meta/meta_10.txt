# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Cyclic CRY entanglement rings with per-qubit RY rotations (Gen 1: “8-Qubit Re-Uploading CRY Variational Classifier”) achieved combined_score_raw = 0.99, perfect accuracies, zero gap, with only 180 gates and depth 108. This ansatz balances expressivity and resource use better than richer entanglers.  
- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2) proved essential across top performers (Gen 1: 0.99 raw; Gen 6 “efficient_circular_bridge_ansatz”: 0.98 raw; Gen 7 “add_parametric_entangling_gates”: 0.99 raw).  
- Trainable readout calibration (gain, bias) initialized to (1, 0) stabilized early training and was used by all perfect classifiers (Gen 1, Gen 6, Gen 7), ensuring rapid attainment of 100% accuracy.  
- Pennylane’s autograd QNode + Adam optimizer (stepsize≈0.03) reliably handled ~100 parameters, as demonstrated by Gen 1 (98 params), Gen 7 (116 params) and maintained zero generalization gaps.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Adding bridge-CZ layers to circular entanglement (Gen 6) increased gate_count to 252 and depth to 120 at the cost of a slight drop to combined_score_raw = 0.98, showing multi-layer CZs give diminishing returns.  
- Rich multi-gate entangling blocks (RY/RZ/CZ/CRZ in Gen 7) did not boost final accuracy beyond 0.99 but slowed convergence (~180 steps) and raised param count to 116, indicating over-complexity.  
- Combining long-distance CRY couplings with shared-phase RZ rotations (Gen 11) incurred the slowest convergence (~690 steps) and a lower raw score (0.96), suggesting that mixing distant and local entanglement with sharing can impede training.  
- Deep, high-gate-count circuits with parameter sharing (Gen 8: depth 126, 270 gates) saw only combined_score_raw = 0.97, underscoring that mere depth/gate increases without targeted entanglement design degrade parameter efficiency.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Mapping PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK to vector indices (used in Gen 1) enables clean replication of the same ansatz block across N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS without manual index bookkeeping.  
- Initializing readout gain to 1 and bias to 0 (Gen 1, Gen 6, Gen 7) aligns raw 〈Z〉 means to ±1, which both speeds up early convergence and yields stable 100% accuracy.  
- Leveraging qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue in the current best program provided exact depth (108) and gate_count (180) metrics, guiding ansatz simplification.  
- Retaining the fixed IsingZZ feature map (FEATURE_SCALE=π/2) across all top designs decouples embedding complexity from ansatz expressivity, ensuring consistent performance when tuning only the variational block.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gen 1’s CRY-ring ansatz (0.99 raw) used the fewest gates (180) among programs achieving perfect test accuracy, outperforming Gen 6 (252 gates, 0.98 raw) and Gen 8 (270 gates, 0.97 raw).  
- Although Gen 2’s shared-parameter ansatz (depth 81, 222 gates) also hit 100% accuracy, Gen 1 reduced gate_count by ~20% at only a 33% increase in depth, highlighting CRY ring’s efficient connectivity.  
- Convergence step trade-off: Gen 1 converged at ~450 steps—slower than richly entangled Gen 7 (~180) but much faster than over-shared Gen 11 (~690)—balancing training speed and circuit compactness.  
- Parameter efficiency remains plateaued (~0.01) across the best designs (Gen 1: 98 params; Gen 2: 92 params), indicating further efficiency gains likely require novel ansatz paradigms beyond incremental gate-type or sharing tweaks.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Collapse symmetric CRY edges into a shared parameter  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the eight distinct CRY keys (“cry_0”…“cry_7”) with a smaller set—e.g. four keys for opposite-edge pairs or even one key “cry_shared” applied on all ring edges. This maintains the cyclic CRY-entangler structure of the best program but cuts per-block parameters (from 16 to 9 or fewer), testing whether parameter tying preserves expressivity and zero gap at lower cost.  

2. Share ansatz-block parameters across the two repeats in each upload  
   Modify apply_ansatz_block’s indexing so that for each upload_index, both repeat_index=0 and 1 draw from the same slice of circuit_params (i.e. offset = upload_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK). This halves the total variational parameters (≈98→≈49 + 2 readout) while keeping depth and gate count identical, directly probing if repeat-wise parameter sharing sustains perfect accuracy.  

3. Inject a light-weight, shared CRZ diagonal entangler for long-range coupling  
   After the existing CRY ring in each block, append one parametrized CRZ gate “crz_diag” acting on the qubit pairs [(0,7),(1,6),(2,5),(3,4)] with the same angle. This adds a single long-range ZZ interaction per block (≈+6 gates, +1 param) without a heavy depth penalty and builds on evidence that modest distant coupling can boost expressivity over purely nearest-neighbor rings.  

4. Rebalance the feature-ansatz interleaving to N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3  
   Keep total ansatz blocks at six by setting N_UPLOADS=2 and N_REPEATS=3 in the global constants. This preserves the circuit’s overall depth (~108) and gate count (~180) but shifts the “feature↔ansatz” cadence—leveraging the proven importance of six blocks while exploring whether grouping three blocks per upload accelerates convergence.  

5. Use differentiated initialization ranges for single- vs. two-qubit rotations  
   In the seed init RNG, draw all RY angles from Uniform(−0.05,0.05) as before, but sample CRY (and any new CRZ) angles from a wider Uniform(−0.1,0.1). This break in initial symmetry for entangling parameters mirrors strategies that sped up training in higher-expressivity ansätze, potentially reducing convergence steps below the current ~450 without increasing circuit complexity.
