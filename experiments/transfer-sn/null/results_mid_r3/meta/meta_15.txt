# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- CRY-ring entanglement with per-qubit RY rotations (Gen 1 “8-Qubit Re-Uploading CRY Variational Classifier,” current best) yields the highest score (combined_score_raw = 0.99), perfect train/val/test accuracies, zero generalization gap, with only 180 gates at depth 108.  
- Three data re-uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2) consistently underpins top performance (Gen 1, Gen 9, Gen 10), ensuring sufficient expressivity without excessive depth.  
- Trainable readout calibration (gain initialized to 1, bias 0) stabilizes early training and is present in all perfect classifiers (Gen 1, Gen 9, Gen 10), enabling rapid attainment of 100% accuracy.  
- Decoupling embedding from the variational block via a fixed IsingZZ feature map (FEATURE_SCALE = π/2) standardizes input encoding and focuses optimization efforts on ansatz design (used by Gen 1 and all comparators).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mixing multiple entangling gate types (CZ ring + CRY cross-links + independent RZ rotations in Gen 12 “RY-CZ-CRY 8-Qubit Ansatz Classifier”) inflated gate count (204) and depth (114) but only reached combined_score_raw = 0.97 and converged slowly (~1080 steps).  
- Introducing shared long-range CRX gates atop a circular CZ chain (Gen 14 “Layered VQC with Shared-Parameter Long-Range CRX”) increased parameters (110), depth (120), and gate count (252) but did not surpass a 0.98 raw score.  
- Parameter-shared multi-gate blocks (Gen 9 “Shared-Parameter Entangling Ansatz”) reduced total parameters to 116 but preserved a low parameter efficiency (0.01) and scored only 0.98, demonstrating that sharing alone doesn’t guarantee superior expressivity.  
- Over-simplified RY-CRY ring without additional entanglers in Gen 15 achieved just 0.95 raw despite modest depth (108) and gate count (180), indicating under-parametrization can impair classification power.

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ansatz structure in ANSATZ_SPEC and auto-mapping parameter keys to vector indices allows seamless replication across uploads/repeats, as in the current best and Gen 10.  
- Using qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue to extract precise depth and gate counts guided the gate-count reduction in the CRY-ring design (final: 180 gates, depth 108).  
- Initializing readout gain to 1 and bias to 0 in the parameter vector consistently accelerated early convergence and improved stability in all top performers.  
- Retaining a fixed IsingZZ feature map isolates variational block tuning from data encoding, enabling fair comparison of ansatz topologies (applied uniformly in Gen 1, Gen 9, Gen 10).

## Performance Analysis  
- The CRY-ring ansatz (current best) outpaces richer entanglement schemes: Gen 9 scored 0.98 (116 params, 246 gates), Gen 10 scored 0.97 (80 params, 222 gates), Gen 12 scored 0.97 (74 params, 204 gates), Gen 14 scored 0.98 (110 params, 252 gates), and Gen 15 scored 0.95 (74 params, 180 gates).  
- A lower gate count (180 vs. 204–252) correlates more strongly with top accuracy than shallower depth; the best model trades minimal depth savings for fewer gates.  
- Simpler entanglement (single gate type in a ring) also accelerates convergence (~450 steps) compared to multi-gate blocks (~330–1080 steps), improving training efficiency.  
- Parameter efficiency remains at ~0.01 across the best designs (98–116 params), suggesting that further gains will require fundamentally new ansatz paradigms rather than incremental sharing or gate-type additions.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Collapse symmetric CRY edges into shared parameters. In your EVOLVE-BLOCK ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the eight distinct cry_i keys with four keys (e.g. cry_pair0…cry_pair3) and apply each to opposite ring edges:  
   • cry_pair0 on (0,1)&(4,5), cry_pair1 on (1,2)&(5,6), cry_pair2 on (2,3)&(6,7), cry_pair3 on (3,4)&(7,0).  
   This halves the two-qubit parameter count per block (16→8) while preserving the cyclic entangling pattern that underpins perfect generalization in Gen 1.  

2. Share ansatz-block parameters across the two repeats. In apply_ansatz_block’s indexing logic, map both repeat_index=0 and 1 within each upload to the same slice of circuit_params (i.e. offset = upload_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK). This reduces N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS by 50% (≈98→49) without altering depth or gate count, directly testing if the second repeat merely refines versus redundantly re-expressing the same variational transformation.  

3. Inject a lightweight shared-parameter cross-ring CRY entangler. After the existing ring of nearest-neighbor CRYs in each block, append one parametrized CRY gate “cry_cross” applied in broadcast to the opposite pairs [(0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7)]. This adds +4 gates and +1 param per block (minimal depth impact) and builds on evidence from shared long-range ZZ couplings (Gen 14) that modest distant two-qubit links can boost expressivity over purely nearest-neighbor rings.  

4. Promote FEATURE_SCALE to a trainable parameter. Replace the fixed FEATURE_SCALE = π/2 by registering a single new trainable scalar “feature_scale” (initialized at π/2) and use it in feature_map: qml.IsingZZ(feature_scale * records[..., k], wires=[a,b]). This adds one global parameter that lets the circuit learn optimal embedding strength, combining the stability of a standardized IsingZZ map with the flexibility to adapt encoding amplitude for improved class separation.  

5. Append a shared single-parameter RZ rotation after each ansatz block. In ANSATZ_SPEC, insert {"gate":"RZ","wire":i,"param":"rz_shared"} for all i∈[0…7], but tie them to a single key “rz_shared” so that one angle is applied to every qubit. This injects global phase flexibility (+8 gates, +1 param) that can refine the post-entanglement readout without substantially increasing depth, inspired by the success of trainable readout calibration in stabilizing early convergence.
