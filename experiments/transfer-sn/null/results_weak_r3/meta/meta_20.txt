# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with exactly two ansatz repeats remain indispensable. All top performers (Gen 13, 16, 17, 18 and the current best) use N_UPLOADS=3 and N_REPEATS=2, yielding perfect accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
- Parameter sharing on single-qubit rotations (e.g. shared RY/RZ across qubit pairs in Gen 16, Gen 18 and the current best) slashes unique parameters without loss of expressivity, supporting 100% test accuracy with ≤122 circuit parameters.  
- A “sandwich” ordering—initial RY, sparse CZ entanglers, intermediate RZ, followed by parametrized CRZ layers, then cross-CZs, and final RZ—consistently accelerated convergence. The current best program preserves this pattern, boosting the combined score to 1.49.  
- Jointly training a readout gain and bias calibration stabilizes early training across all high-scoring models (Gen 13, 16, 18, current best), delivering zero std in accuracy metrics.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized, uniformly dense CRZ/CRY blocks (Gen 13’s 170 params, Gen 16’s 318 gates) achieved no accuracy gain over leaner entanglers but drove parameter efficiency to ~0.01 and slowed convergence.  
- Naïve reduction to extremely sparse ansätze (e.g. Gen 18’s 74 params) plateaued at a combined raw score of 0.99 and could not surpass the expressivity ceiling of richer connectivity.  
- Dense CZ meshes with no matching parametric rotations (seen in failed Gen 14) underperformed and introduced small generalization gaps, highlighting the need for parametrized entanglers rather than fixed meshes.  
- Malformed gate specifications (Gen 19) prevented any evaluation—underscoring the importance of strict validation of gate entries and wire lists.  

## Implementation Insights  
- The current best program builds on the “4 CRZ + 2 CZ” minimal block by adding a second quartet of CRZ cross-links (crz_1_4, 3_6, 0_5, 2_7), elevating the combined score from Gen 10’s 1.46 to 1.49 with only a modest depth increase.  
- Mapping parameter keys to block indices via a dict (key_to_position) in apply_ansatz_block proved robust across varied gate patterns, as demonstrated by Gen 17 and the current best.  
- Leveraging qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue and tape.graph.get_depth for on-the-fly circuit metadata allows accurate depth and gate-count tracking, enabling direct correlation with performance metrics.  
- Initializing all circuit parameters in (–0.05, 0.05) with readout gain=1 and bias=0 (as in Gen 5, Gen 10, and the current best) yields rapid, stable convergence.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score rose from Gen 10’s 1.46 (depth=78, gates=216) to 1.49 in the current best (depth=99, gates=318), demonstrating that a targeted increase in cross-block CRZ connectivity can outweigh the depth/gate penalty.  
- Convergence slowed from ~240 steps (Gen 10) to ~390 steps in the current best, revealing a trade-off between expressivity (higher combined score) and training speed.  
- Parameter efficiency stagnated at ~0.01 for high-param circuits (Gen 17 and current best), indicating diminishing returns on adding more trainable angles without more aggressive parameter sharing.  
- Shallow designs with fewer than 100 gates (e.g. Gen 18’s 246 gates) achieved perfect raw accuracy but could not push the combined score above 1.39–1.46, reaffirming the value of a balanced, moderate-depth entanglement scheme.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Cyclically rotate the four “base” CRZ pairs across your three uploads. In your ANSATZ_SPEC generation, replace the hard-coded crz_0_1, crz_2_3, crz_4_5, crz_6_7 entries with a loop that for upload i uses pairs shifted by i qubits (e.g. upload 0: (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7); upload 1: (1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,0); upload 2: (2,3),(4,5),(6,7),(0,1)). This preserves the proven 4-CRZ block depth/gate count while enriching entanglement footprints (cf. Gen 10’s jump to 1.46).  

2. Share each CRZ parameter across the two ansatz repeats to free budget for extra connectivity. In your key_to_position mapping, map, for example, both block 0 and block 1 “crz_0_1” to the same index (and likewise for the other base CRZs), cutting four block parameters. Use the freed slots to append a fixed CZ—say between qubits [0,7]—after the existing cross-CZs. Prior work shows tying CRZ across repeats retains expressivity while trimming parameters, and a lightweight CZ enriches connectivity.  

3. Convert one of the fixed cross‐CZs into a trainable CRZ and rebalance with RZ pruning. Replace, for example, the CZ on wires [5,6] with {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[5,6],"param":"crz_5_6"}, then identify the two RZ rotations (initial or final) with the smallest average gradients and remove them. This +1 trainable entangler (parmetrized cross-link) driven by sparse CRZ success in Gen 10 may boost score while keeping N_PARAMS≈122.  

4. Introduce per‐upload learnable FEATURE_SCALE factors in your feature_map. Replace the scalar FEATURE_SCALE with a 3-element vector [s₀,s₁,s₂] (initialized to π/2) and multiply each IsingZZ upload by its sᵤ. This lets the circuit adaptively tune feature‐map strength per re-upload—leveraging the proven three‐upload expressivity without altering the ansatz depth.  

5. Inject lightweight inter‐upload CZ entanglers to link successive feature‐map blocks. After each feature_map (for upload 1 and upload 2), insert a single fixed CZ—e.g. qml.CZ([0,7]) after upload 1 and qml.CZ([2,5]) after upload 2—just before the first RY of the ensuing ansatz block. Evidence: minimal CZ cross-links in Gen 10 and the current best improved cross‐block coherence with only +2 gates.
