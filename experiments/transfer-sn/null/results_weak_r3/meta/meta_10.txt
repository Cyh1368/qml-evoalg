# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Sparse, targeted CRZ entangling with minimal fixed cross-links drove the top score. The current best “8-Qubit Re-Uploading VQC Classifier” (Gen 10, combined score 1.46, depth 78, gates 216) uses exactly four parametrized CRZ per block plus two CZs, mirroring the Gen 5 pattern but shaving depth from 90→78.  
- Three feature re-uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2) consistently produced perfect accuracy and zero generalization gap in Gen 0, Gen 2, Gen 5, and the Gen 10 best.  
- Sandwiching single-qubit RY rotations before entanglers and RZ rotations after (RY–CRZ/CZ–RZ) accelerated convergence (≈240 steps for Gen 10 versus ≈450+ in deeper designs) and improved gradient flow.  
- Jointly training readout gain and bias with circuit parameters stabilized early training and was present in all top performers (Gen 5 score 1.39, Gen 10 score 1.46).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized, uniform controlled rotations (CRY) failed to boost accuracy but inflated complexity. Gen 7’s “CRZ-Entangled” design (188 params, depth 87) and Gen 2’s CRY-heavy ansatz (170 params, depth 93) both plateaued at combined scores ≈0.97–0.99.  
- Excessive block repeats beyond two per upload yielded no accuracy gain. Gen 8’s six-block re-uploading (depth 102, gates 246) achieved perfect accuracy but only a 0.99 combined score, at the cost of longer training (~450 steps).  
- Dense CZ meshes without complementary parametrized rotations underperformed. Gen 9’s CZ grid (300 gates, depth 99) missed the 0.96 target combined score (raw 0.94) and showed a small generalization gap (0.01).  
- Simple parameter sharing without depth reduction still left circuits too deep. Gen 6’s paired-ansatz (74 params, depth 99) converged rapidly but only reached a 0.97 combined score.

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining the ansatz declaratively via ANSATZ_SPEC and then pruning to four CRZ plus two CZ per block (as in Gen 10) yields both code clarity and minimal gate count.  
- Leveraging key_to_position mapping for parameter indexing allows vectorized parameter application and straightforward parameter tying across repeats—critical in both Gen 5 and Gen 10.  
- The fixed IsingZZ feature map on 28 binary features combined with sparse two-qubit coupling keeps depth low (78 in Gen 10) while preserving expressivity.  
- Initializing circuit parameters in (–0.05,0.05) and fixing readout gain=1, bias=0 before training (used in all high-scoring programs) contributes to stable early convergence.

## Performance Analysis  
- Depth reduction from ≈90–102 down to 78 correlated with the largest jump in combined score (from ≈0.99 in Gen 6–9 to 1.46 in Gen 10).  
- Gate count reduction (300→216) tracked closely with score improvements once perfect accuracy was already achieved.  
- Parameter budgets around 120–124 (Gen 5:124 params, score 1.39; Gen 10:122 params, score 1.46) hit the sweet spot for expressivity vs. efficiency—further cuts to 74 params (Gen 6) lost raw score.  
- Convergence steps remained reasonable (210–240) for sparse-entangler designs, versus >450 steps for deeper or over-parameterized circuits.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Rotate the sparse-CRZ connectivity across uploads  
   Action: In your ANSATZ_SPEC, keep four CRZs per block but cyclically shift their qubit pairs on each upload (e.g. upload 0: (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7); upload 1: (1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,0); upload 2: (2,3),(4,5),(6,7),(0,1)). This preserves the per-block depth and gate count (4 CRZ + 2 CZ) while enriching entanglement patterns over the three re-uploads. Evidence: sparse, targeted CRZs drove the jump to 1.46 combined score; diversifying without adding gates may capture more feature correlations.  

2. Share CRZ parameters across the two repeats to free budget for extra entanglers  
   Action: Modify key_to_position so that within each upload both repeats index the same four crz_* parameters (e.g. use key `"crz_0_1_upload0"` for blocks 0 and 1), reducing N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK by 4. Reallocate the saved parameters to insert one extra CZ between qubits (e.g. between (2,6)) per block. Evidence: tying across repeats preserves expressivity but cuts parameters, and minimal extra CZs boost connectivity without blowing up depth.  

3. Tie single-qubit rotations across symmetric qubit pairs and reallocate savings  
   Action: In ANSATZ_SPEC, rename ry_0→ry_0_1 and ryd_1→ry_0_1 (likewise ry_2_3, ry_4_5, ry_6_7, and similarly for rz), halving single-qubit rotation params from 16 to 8. Use the recovered ≈8 parameters to add two more CRZ gates per block on next-nearest neighbors (e.g. (1,4),(3,6)). Evidence: parameter sharing under symmetry has raised efficiency in recent generations, and adding CRZs in sparse patterns aligns with the Gen 10 winner.  

4. Upgrade to per-qubit readout gain and bias with budgeted pruning  
   Action: Extend your readout layer in predict_batch to have separate gain_i and bias_i for each qubit (total 16 instead of 2 params), then prune two lowest-impact single-qubit rotations per block to keep N_PARAMS≈122. Implement by replacing gain,bias scalars with vectors of length 8, and drop the least sensitive ry/rz pairs (using their gradient magnitudes) from ANSATZ_SPEC before retraining. Evidence: joint gain/bias training stabilized convergence; more granular calibration can reduce final loss if you carefully swap in budget from negligible rotations.  

5. Integrate sensitivity-guided, block-wise parameter pruning  
   Action: After an initial training run on the current best ansatz, compute the average absolute gradient of each block’s parameters over the last 50 steps. For each upload, remove the lowest-impact gate (parametrized rotation or CRZ) in its ANSATZ_SPEC, rebuild PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK, and retrain from scratch. Evidence: Gen 5’s post-training pruning trimmed unused parameters without sacrificing accuracy, and dynamic block-wise removal targets underused gates while preserving the 1.00 generalization.
