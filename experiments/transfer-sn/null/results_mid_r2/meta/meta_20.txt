# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three feature-upload rounds interleaved with two ansatz repeats continues to deliver perfect classification and zero generalization gap.  
  • 8-Qubit Multi-Upload Ansatz Classifier (Gen 12, “trainable_coupled_ansatz”) scored a combined 1.51 with 100% accuracies, depth 93, 252 gates, converging in ~120 steps.  
  • 8-Qubit Re-uploading Ansatz Classifier (Gen 10) hit combined_raw 0.99 at depth 81 with the same upload/repeat layout.  
- Structured per-block mixing of local rotations, parametric ZZ/ZX couplings, then parallel CZ entanglers yields high expressivity in shallower circuits.  
  • In Gen 12’s EVOLVE-BLOCK: 8 RY → 4 CRZ (feature-pair couplings) → 8 RZ → 4 CRX → 4 CZ, achieving depth 93 vs. 130–136 in earlier designs.  
- Trainable readout calibration (gain & bias) plus early-stopping on validation loss stabilizes training and speeds convergence.  
  • All top performers (Gens 10, 11, 13, and current Gen 12) converged in ~120–150 steps with zero generalization gap.  
- Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC with key→index parameter mapping ensures consistent, error-free parameter slicing across uploads/repeats.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized circuits with >140 total parameters and unguided entanglement layering did not improve accuracy and increased resource use.  
  • Gen 14 (146 params, depth 136, 276 gates) matched 100% accuracy but incurred a 0.01 gen gap.  
  • Gen 15’s “axis_diversity_and_global_connectivity” (same param count) fell to combined_raw 0.97 and 0.02 drop in val/test accuracy.  
- Deep, repetitive multi-axis rotations without structured feature-pair couplings produced no convergence or slower training (>700 steps).  
  • Prior under-expressive Gen 4 with 158 params required ~210 steps for a 0.99 combined_raw, no accuracy boost.  
- Simply adding RZ layers post-CRZ without rebalancing entangling depth inflated gate counts without speed gains.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Validating ANSATZ_SPEC early and extracting ordered parameter keys avoids mis-indexing and accelerates debugging.  
  • The current best Gen 12 reports SPEC_ERRORS before circuit construction, ensuring spec correctness.  
- Offset-based parameter slicing combined with a key→position dict makes apply_ansatz_block both concise and robust.  
- Profiling with qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue and QuantumScript tape enables on-the-fly depth and gate_count measurement, guiding circuit design.  
- The Gen 12 block ordering (local RY→parametric CRZ on feature pairs→local RZ→CRX ring→parallel CZ) demonstrates how interleaving feature-aware couplings with local rotations sharply reduces depth while retaining full expressivity.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The Gen 12 ansatz improved the combined score from 0.99 (previous 98-param ring designs) to 1.51, achieving 100% train/val/test accuracy with zero gap.  
- Depth reduction from ~130 to 93 (–28%) did not compromise accuracy or convergence speed (~120 vs. ~150 steps).  
- Parameter efficiency remained at ~0.01 despite an increase to 146 params, indicating that block structure, not sheer param count, drives performance gains.  
- Consistent convergence in ~120–150 steps across top patterns reinforces that three uploads + two repeats + structured entanglement is optimal for this dataset.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate entangling‐axis on the ring couplers  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC generation, set CRX on ring edges for even block_index and CRY for odd block_index (i.e. toggle between CRX and CRY on wires [1,2], [3,4], [5,6], [7,0] based on block_index % 2). This simple parity switch preserves the existing block depth and param count but enriches the operator basis, leveraging the axis‐diversity insight from Gen 12 to sharpen decision boundaries without extra gates.  

2. Introduce tunable coupling in the parallel CZ layer  
   Replace every other CZ in the final layer of each block (e.g. wires [0,2] and [2,4]) with CRZ gates (“crz_p02”, “crz_p24”, etc.), updating PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK accordingly. By promoting fixed CZs to parameterized CRZs only on half the edges, you add minimal two‐qubit parameters while giving the optimizer fine‐grained control over key entanglement strengths, building directly on the success of mixed CRZ/CRX couplings in Gen 12.  

3. Collapse RY→RZ pairs into single Rot calls  
   In ANSATZ_SPEC, replace each pair of single‐qubit gates RY(wire, θ) followed later by RZ(wire, φ) with one qml.Rot(θ, λ, φ, wires=wire) or qml.U3, remapping the two original parameters into the three‐angle convention (you can fix the extra Euler angle to zero if needed). This fusion reduces two gates to one per qubit per block, cutting depth by 16 per block (–32 overall) while preserving full SU(2) expressivity, and aligns with the goal of depth reduction without accuracy loss.  

4. Make the feature‐map scale trainable per upload  
   Replace the scalar FEATURE_SCALE with a length‐3 trainable vector [σ₀,σ₁,σ₂], and in feature_map multiply records[...,k] by σ_upload_index before the IsingZZ. This adds only three parameters total and allows each upload round to learn its optimal embedding strength, combining the proven robustness of the three‐upload layout with data‐adaptive amplitude tuning to potentially speed convergence and widen classification margins.  

5. Share parameters across repeats within each upload  
   Modify apply_ansatz_block so that both repeats (repeat_index 0 and 1) within the same upload_index use the same slice of circuit_params (i.e. remove the repeat‐specific offset). This parameter‐tying cuts N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK in half for the repeats while retaining the three‐upload structure, leveraging the repeated structure’s redundancy to improve parameter efficiency without sacrificing the block’s expressivity as demonstrated by the zero generalization gap in Gen 12.
