# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Three feature-upload rounds interleaved with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2) consistently yields perfect classification and zero generalization gap.  
  • Seen in “8-Qubit Re-uploading Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 10, depth 81, combined_raw 0.99), “Hardware-Efficient Ansatz” (Gen 11, depth 130, combined_raw 0.99), “CRX-CZ binary classifier” (Gen 13, depth 130, combined_raw 0.99), and our Current Best Program (depth 130, combined_raw 0.99).  

- Ring-structured CRX/CRY entanglement plus parallel CZ couplers provides high expressivity with moderate circuit depth.  
  • Employed by Gen 11 and the Current Best Program: 8 two-qubit parameters per block, 98 total params, depth 130, gate_count 228, achieving 100% test accuracy in ~150 optimizer steps.  

- Custom single-qubit rotation–entanglement–rotation blocks can reduce depth without loss of accuracy.  
  • Gen 10’s RY–CZ(feature-pair + neighbor)–RX blocks per upload achieved combined_raw 0.99 at depth 81 (vs. 130) with identical parameter count.  

- Trainable readout calibration (gain & bias on mean Pauli-Z) stabilizes outputs and accelerates convergence.  
  • Present in all top performers (Gens 10, 11, 13, Current Best), consistently converging by ~150 steps with zero generalization gap.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterized circuits (>140 total params) increase resource use without accuracy gains.  
  • Gen 14 (146 params, depth 136, gate_count 276) matched combined_raw 0.99 but incurred a 0.01 generalization gap.  
  • Gen 15 (146 params, same depth/gates) fell to combined_raw 0.97 and 0.02 drop in validation/test accuracy.  

- Complex multi-axis repeats lacking ring CRX structure underperform more balanced designs.  
  • Gen 15’s “independent_ry_crx_cry_rz_ansatz” achieved lower accuracies (0.98 val/test) despite similar depth/gate_count to ring-based models.  

- Simply adding layers or parameters beyond the 98-param, depth ≈ 130 regime yields diminishing returns.  
  • Earlier Gen 4 with 158 params needed ~210 steps vs ~150 for 98-param models, with no accuracy improvement.

- Shallow ansätze without heterogeneous entanglement patterns (from prior rounds) failed to reach high scores or converged very slowly (>700 steps).

## Implementation Insights

- Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC with a key→index mapping enables systematic replication across uploads/repeats without manual offsets.  
  • The Current Best Program defines 16 ops/block (8 RY, 8 CRX/CRY, 8 CZ) and computes N_PARAMS = N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK×3×2 + 2.  

- Autograd-compatible pnp.array for parameters and qml.AdamOptimizer backprop ensures stable gradient flow through deep, entangled circuits.  

- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue plus QuantumScript tape for on-the-fly extraction of depth and gate_count allows automated resource profiling in run_experiment.  

- Validation-loss early stopping (patience = 75, min_delta = 1e-4) with best-weight restoration reliably prevents overfitting, yielding zero generalization gap in top models.

## Performance Analysis

- All 98-parameter designs (Gens 10, 11, 13, and Current Best) hit combined_raw = 0.99, train/val/test accuracies ≈1.00/0.99/1.00, generalization_gap = 0.00, despite depth varying from 81 to 130.  

- Over-parameterized variants (146 params in Gens 14 & 15) showed no accuracy benefit—Gen 14 had slight gen_gap = 0.01; Gen 15 dropped combined_raw to 0.97.  

- Depth reduction to 81 (Gen 10) matched performance of 130-depth circuits, underscoring that expressive block design (RY–CZ–RX) can compensate for fewer layers.  

- Convergence speed strongly correlates with parameter count and ansatz structure:  
  • 98-param ring and custom blocks converge in ~150 steps.  
  • Heavier 158-param from prior experiments required ~210 steps.  
  • Under-expressive or overly compact models failed or took >700 steps.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate two-qubit rotation axes between successive ansatz blocks  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC generation loop, set block_index % 2 == 0 to use CRX on each ring edge and block_index % 2 == 1 to use CRY on the same wire pairs, while preserving the RY pre-rotations and fixed CZ couplers. This simple parity switch introduces axis diversity proven to enrich expressivity (see Global Insight #2) without changing total parameters (98) or depth (~130), and can accelerate convergence by providing complementary entangling directions.  

2. Promote fixed CZ couplers to trainable CRZ gates  
   Replace each “CZ” entry in ANSATZ_SPEC with a parameterized “CRZ” gate (e.g. crz_0…crz_7) and update PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK accordingly. This adds only eight extra two-qubit parameters per block and leverages the optimizer’s ability to tune coupling strengths, building on the success of CRX/CRY ring entanglement (Insight #2) to tighten decision boundaries.  

3. Append a post-entanglement RZ layer in every block  
   Immediately after the last CZ/CRZ in each ansatz block, insert single-qubit RZ rotations on all eight wires with new parameters rz_post_0…rz_post_7. This symmetry-breaking layer adds just eight parameters per block and a small depth overhead, and taps the known benefit of extra single-qubit rotations for boosting expressivity without exceeding the optimal depth/parameter regime (see Gen 10 vs. Gen 11).  

4. Redesign each upload block as a compact RY–CZ(feature-pair + neighbor)–RX motif  
   Following Gen 10’s depth-efficient pattern, replace your 16-gate block of RY, CRX/CRY, and eight CZs with a custom sequence: for each feature pair (a, b), apply RY(a), RY(b), CZ(a,b), RY(a), RY(b), then neighbor CZs, finishing with RX(a), RX(b). Re-map PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK accordingly to maintain 16 parameters/block but collapse the depth from ~130 to ~80, preserving 100% test accuracy while halving circuit depth (Insight #3).  

5. Make the feature-map scale trainable per upload  
   Swap the constant FEATURE_SCALE with a trainable vector scale = [s₀,s₁,s₂] (one scalar per upload) and use qml.IsingZZ(scale[upload_index] * records[..., k], wires=[a,b]). This adds just three parameters total and allows the circuit to learn optimal embedding strengths at each re-upload, combining the robustness of the three-upload layout (Insight #1) with data-adaptive amplitudes to potentially improve convergence speed and classification margins.
