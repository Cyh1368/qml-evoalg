# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Pure CRX-Ring Entanglement (per-qubit RY → CRX ring → per-qubit RZ) consistently yields perfect accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
  • Program to Analyze (n_params=146, depth=114, combined_score_raw=1.00, conv≈210 steps)  
  • Gen 8 “crx_ring_evolution” (n_params=104, depth=114, combined_score=1.51, conv≈150 steps)  
  • Gen 15 “CRX Ring 8-Qubit Binary Classifier” (n_params=146, depth=162, combined_score=1.47, conv≈180 steps)  
- Three feature-map uploads interleaved with two ansatz repeats (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2) is a stable layout for full separability. All top programs (Gen 14–19, Program to Analyze) used this and converged in 150–210 steps.  
- Distinct per-qubit RY before and RZ after the CRX entanglers lowers loss to ~0.02 and speeds convergence compared to shared or omitted rotations.  
- Trainable gain/bias calibration on the averaged Z-expectation (used in Gen 15, Program to Analyze) stabilizes early training and prevents bias drift.  

Ineffective Approaches  
- Hybrid CZ/CRX Mixing Layers increased depth and parameters without improving accuracy.  
  • Gen 9 “crx_ring_plus_cz_mixing” (n_params=146, depth≈210) reached only combined_score_raw=0.98 with a small generalization gap.  
  • Gen 13 “hybrid_crx_cz_ring” (n_params=170, depth=168) stagnated at 0.98.  
- Shared-phase RZ or RY parameter schemes reduced expressivity. Gen 17 “hybrid_entanglement_shared_rz” achieved perfect accuracy but incurred higher loss (~0.03) and low parameter efficiency.  
- Excessive ansatz repeats per upload inflated circuit depth (>162) yet yielded no score gains (Gen 15 depth=162 vs Program to Analyze depth=114).  
- Under-parameterized schemes (<80 parameters) failed to fully separate classes (earlier re-uploading ansatz combined_score≈0.73 with a 0.07 gap).  

Implementation Insights  
- The validate_ansatz_spec→PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK pattern combined with a flat circuit_params vector and indexed offsets makes apply_ansatz_block fully generic and easy to swap entangling patterns (e.g., switching from CRZ to CRX ring).  
- Wrapping feature_map + apply_ansatz_block in a backprop-capable QNode with qml.AdamOptimizer and a small uniform ±0.05 init (gain = 1, bias = 0) yields stable convergence in ~150–210 steps.  
- Incorporating trainable gain and bias on the mean Z-expectation (rather than per-qubit weights) simplifies the final layer while still correcting systematic offsets.  
- Validation-loss early stopping with weight restoration prevents overfitting and ensures zero generalization gap across seeds in all top performers.  

Performance Analysis  
- The current best (Program to Analyze) matches the ring-CRX gold standard: combined_score_raw=1.00, test_loss≈0.02, depth=114, gate_count=228, n_params=146.  
- Leaner CRX-ring variants (Gen 8, n_params=104) also hit perfect accuracy with identical depth/gate_count and faster convergence (~150 vs 210 steps). This shows n_params≥104 suffices.  
- Hybrid or CZ-mixed circuits never surpassed combined_score_raw=0.98 despite higher n_params (146–170) and depth (>168), confirming pure CRX ring is more expressive per gate.  
- Depth ≳114 is necessary for combined_scores ≥0.97; deeper designs (>162) add no accuracy benefit and only reduce parameter efficiency (≈0.01 across all top circuits).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Implement an “alternating‐lean” rotation schedule by removing the post‐entangler RZ layer on every even‐indexed ansatz block. Concretely, in ANSATZ_SPEC only include the RY→CRX gates for blocks where block_index % 2 == 0, leaving the full RY→CRX→RZ triad on odd blocks. This cuts ~8 parameters per even block (targeting ~104–122 total) and directly tests whether the lean rotation set of Gen 8 suffices across a mixed block layout.

2. Tie ansatz parameters across all blocks to probe minimal overparameterization. Instead of computing offset = block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK, use offset = (block_index % 1) * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK (i.e. always zero) so every block shares the same parameter vector. This collapses N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS to N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK (≈24) while preserving the 3×2 interleaving, testing if repeated identical CRX rings still achieve perfect separability.

3. Introduce per‐upload feature‐map scaling factors so the circuit learns optimal encoding strengths. Replace the constant FEATURE_SCALE with a trainable vector feature_scale_upload of length N_UPLOADS, initialized to π/2, and multiply each IsingZZ angle by feature_scale_upload[upload_index]. This adds only 3 parameters and may accelerate convergence by adapting how strongly each data injection imprints on the state.

4. Replace the scalar mean readout with a small per‐qubit weighted sum: introduce 8 trainable readout weights w_i alongside the gain/bias calibration, and compute raw = ∑_i w_i z_i. Initialize w_i = 1/8 to mimic the mean, then optimize them together with gain and bias. This fourfold‐parameter final layer preserves simplicity but grants the circuit flexibility to emphasize qubits that capture the strongest class‐separating signal.

5. Alternate entanglement topology between ring and star CRX patterns to capture complementary correlations. For even blocks use the standard 8-qubit CRX ring, and for odd blocks replace ANSATZ_SPEC’s CRX list with CRX gates from qubit 0 to every other qubit ([0,1], [0,2], … [0,7]). This hybrid‐pattern remains pure CRX, keeps depth≈114, and may leverage both global‐ring and hub‐spoke interactions for richer expressivity.
