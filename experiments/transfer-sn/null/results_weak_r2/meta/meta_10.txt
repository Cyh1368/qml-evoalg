# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three feature‐map uploads interleaved with two repeats of a local ansatz block (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2) consistently yields perfect separability.  
  • The current 8-Qubit Ring CRX classifier (ANSATZ_SPEC: per-qubit RY→CRX-ring→RZ) converged to 100% train/val/test accuracy, zero gap, validation/test loss ≈0.02, combined_score_raw=1.00, depth=114, gate_count=228, in ~210 steps.  
  • Earlier models with this pattern—e.g. “8-Qubit RY–RZ–CZ–CRZ Repeat” and “8-Qubit Phase-Coupled Ansatz” (combined_score_raw 0.98–0.99)—also achieved flawless generalization.  
- Ring-topology parameterized two-qubit rotations (CRX) improved expressivity per gate versus linear CZ/CRZ chains.  
  • Swapping CZ for an 8-edge CRX ring (same total parameters, 146) reduced mean loss (~0.05→0.02) and boosted combined_score_raw (0.99→1.00) compared to the doubled-ring variant (depth↑, no score gain).  
- Full parametrization of single-qubit rotations before and after entanglers is critical.  
  • All top classifiers (current ring CRX, RY–RZ–CZ, CRX hybrid) apply per-qubit RY, entanglers, then per-qubit RZ, enabling local feature reorientation and global mixing.  
- Declarative ansatz specification (ANSATZ_SPEC + key-to-index mapping) allows rapid prototyping and gate-type swapping without rewiring core loops.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive parameter sharing across qubits undercuts expressivity.  
  • The “8-Qubit Re-uploading Ansatz Classifier” with shared even/odd RY/RZ (38 params) fell to combined_score_raw=0.73 and exhibited a 0.07 generalization gap.  
- Increasing linear CRZ chain length or adding extra entangling layers without richer topology yields diminishing returns.  
  • The “Linear CRZ Entanglement” model (188 params) matched a 0.97 combined score of 104-param CZ scaffolds but at higher resource cost.  
- Simplifying entangler structure by heavy sharing (single shared RZ) preserved accuracy but inflated depth/gate count and lowered parameter efficiency.  
  • The “8-Qubit Shared-RZ Ansatz” hit 100% accuracy but had 276 gates (depth 120) and parameter_efficiency≈0.01, worse than the ring CRX’s 228 gates.  
- Merely stacking more ansatz repeats beyond two (e.g. doubled CRX rings) increased depth (from 114→162) without score improvement (still 0.99–1.00).  

## Implementation Insights  
- validate_ansatz_spec + PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK mapping ensures correctness and automatic indexing of parameters per block.  
- Offsetting into the flat parameter vector by block_index×N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK cleanly supports any gate sequence in ANSATZ_SPEC.  
- Initialization of readout gain/bias (gain=1, bias=0) and small random circuit parameters (±0.05) with Adam and L2 loss yields stable training and zero generalization gap across high‐expressivity ansätze.  
- Embedding feature_map and apply_ansatz_block inside a pennylane.qnode with backprop enables seamless autograd and early stopping integration, as demonstrated in the ring CRX classifier’s rapid convergence (~210 steps).  

## Performance Analysis  
- The ring CRX ansatz (combined_score_raw=1.00, test_loss≈0.02, depth=114, gates=228) set a new high‐water mark over previous top models:  
  • +0.01 combined_score_raw versus the best CZ-scaffold and CRX-hybrid (~0.99), with 17% fewer gates than shared-RZ (228 vs 276).  
- All top classifiers share zero generalization gap; only under-parametrized or over-shared designs showed nonzero gaps or lower accuracy.  
- Depth thresholds: ≥99 depths deliver ≥0.97 combined scores, but beyond ~114 depth further increases yield no measurable accuracy or loss improvements.  
- Parameter efficiency remains ~0.01 across the high‐accuracy models, indicating current circuits are overparameterized but that ring CRX achieves the best loss–gate trade-off.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Experiment with alternative upload‐repeat schedules. For example, set N_UPLOADS=6 and N_REPEATS=1 (one ansatz block per upload) or N_UPLOADS=4 and N_REPEATS=1 or 2 (front‐loading feature re‐uploads), keeping the total number of blocks ≈6–8. This preserves the proven interleaving pattern while testing whether more frequent data injections accelerate convergence or tighten the generalization gap.

2. Enrich each ansatz block with full SU(2) single‐qubit rotations. In ANSATZ_SPEC, prepend for i∈0…7 a gate entry {"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"rx_i"} immediately before the existing RY→CRX-ring→RZ sequence, and extend PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK accordingly. Prior top classifiers show that adding RX before RY unlocks richer local reorientation and may further reduce loss or speed up training.

3. Hybridize the ring entanglement by alternating CRX and CZ gates. Mutate the eight-edge entangler layer so that even-indexed edges apply fixed CZ (no param) and odd-indexed edges remain parameterized CRX. This mixes the CZ-scaffold’s parameter efficiency with CRX-ring expressivity at constant depth and gate count, probing a sweet spot between resource cost and performance.

4. Double the CRX entangling sublayer without adding parameters. Duplicate the block’s eight CRX entries immediately after the first CRX ring in ANSATZ_SPEC—but reference the same param keys (“crx_0”…“crx_7”)—so you have two back-to-back CRX rings per block. This densifies two-qubit mixing in each block while keeping N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK unchanged, testing whether deeper entanglement further lowers loss.

5. Introduce trainable feature‐map scales per feature pair. Replace the constant FEATURE_SCALE with a learnable vector feature_scale[k], initialized to π/2, and modify feature_map to call qml.IsingZZ(feature_scale[k] * records[...,k], wires=[a,b]). This adds N_FEATURES lightweight parameters that adaptively weight the input encoding, leveraging the insight that richer parametrization around data injections can improve separability.
