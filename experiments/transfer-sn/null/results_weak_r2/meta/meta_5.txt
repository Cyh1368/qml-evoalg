# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Repeated feature‐map uploads with interleaved ansatz blocks (N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2) consistently yielded rapid convergence and high accuracy. Both the “8-Qubit RY-RZ CZ Ansatz” (combined raw 0.97, depth 99) and the best “8-Qubit Ring CRX Ansatz Classifier” (combined 1.52, depth 114) use this pattern.  
- Rich two-qubit entanglement topologies improved expressivity without adding parameters:  
  • Ring‐structured CRX (8-Qubit Ring CRX) achieved 100% train/validation/test accuracy and zero gap with 146 parameters.  
  • CZ‐scaffold entangler (8-Qubit CZ-Scaffold) matched 0.97 combined score using 104 shared-parameter blocks.  
- Full parametrization of single-qubit rotations (per-qubit RY and RZ) before and after entanglers provided the flexibility needed for perfect classification, as seen in both RY-RZ CZ and CRX-ring models.  
- Early stopping with best‐weight restoration and simple L2 loss consistently delivered stable generalization (generalization gap ≤0.01 in top models).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Aggressive parameter sharing across qubits (even/odd shared RY/RZ in “8-Qubit Re-uploading Ansatz Classifier”) slashed parameter count to 38 but yielded only 0.73 combined score and a 0.07 generalization gap, indicating under-expressivity.  
- Linear CRZ chains with high parameter counts (188 parameters in “Reuploading Classifier with Linear CRZ Entanglement”) achieved 0.97 combined score—on par with lower-param CZ‐based ansätze—demonstrating diminishing returns and low parameter efficiency.  
- Excessive circuit depth without richer entanglement (e.g., depth 130 in CZ-scaffold versus 114 in ring CRX with better score) increased gate count without proportional performance gains.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining the ansatz via a declarative ANSATZ_SPEC and mapping parameter keys to indices (as in apply_ansatz_block) offers modularity: swapping in CRX vs CZ entanglers required only spec edits, not core loop changes.  
- Leveraging parameterized two-qubit gates (CRX) on a closed-ring topology improved expressivity over fixed CZ chains, with identical parameter counts (146) and only a modest depth increase (+15).  
- Placement of per-qubit RY rotations before entanglement and RZ rotations after (as in ring CRX ansatz) appears critical: it ensures local feature rotation and global mixing, driving perfect separability.  
- Consistent use of Pennylane’s backprop interface and AdamOptimizer with small random initialization (±0.05) and calibrated gain/bias readout stabilized training across all high-performing models.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score progression: 0.73 (shared even/odd) → 0.97 (RY-RZ CZ, CRZ-linear, CZ-scaffold) → 1.52 (ring CRX), pinpointing ring CRX as the clear top performer.  
- All top classifiers share zero or near-zero generalization gap; only the under-parametrized shared even/odd model suffered overfit (gap 0.07).  
- Parameter efficiency remains low (~0.01 accuracy per parameter) even for the ring CRX and CZ-scaffold, indicating that further pruning or compression could maintain accuracy while improving efficiency.  
- Circuit depth correlates with accuracy up to a point: models with depth ≥99 achieved ≥0.97 combined score, but beyond ~114 (ring CRX) extra depth added little benefit relative to gate count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Experiment with alternative upload‐repeat schedules to sharpen feature mixing. For example, keep six ansatz blocks total but switch to N_UPLOADS=6, N_REPEATS=1 (one block per upload) or N_UPLOADS=4, N_REPEATS=1.5 (i.e. four uploads with some uploads applying two blocks). This preserves the proven six‐block pattern while front‐loading more frequent feature re‐uploads, testing whether more granular data injections yield faster convergence or tighter generalization gaps.

2. Enrich single‐qubit expressivity by adding RX rotations before the existing RY layer. Concretely, prepend each block in ANSATZ_SPEC with “RX wire i, param rx_i” for i∈0…7, followed by the current RY→CRX‐ring→RZ sequence. Full SU(2) parametrization around the entanglers has driven perfect separability in top models and may further reduce loss or accelerate convergence.

3. Hybridize the two‐qubit entangler topology by interleaving fixed CZ gates with parameterized CRX in the ring. For instance, in ANSATZ_SPEC replace every even‐indexed CRX with a CZ (no param) or a CRZ gate, achieving a mixed CRX–CZ ring. This draws on the CZ‐scaffold’s parameter efficiency and the CRX‐ring’s expressivity to probe sweet spots in depth versus parameter count.

4. Stack a second CRX‐ring entanglement sublayer inside each ansatz block using shared parameters. Duplicate the eight CRX entries in the block spec immediately after the first ring but point them to the same “crx_0…crx_7” keys. This densifies entanglement without increasing parameter count, leveraging ring‐CRX’s proven topology while exploring whether deeper two‐qubit mixing boosts accuracy.

5. Integrate a light‐weight pruning or L₁ regularization on CRX parameters post‐training to carve away negligible gates. After convergence, threshold or drive small‐magnitude crx_i to zero and re-evaluate on validation. Given the low parameter efficiency (~0.01 accuracy/param), modest pruning (10–20%) may maintain ≥0.97 combined score while substantially boosting compactness.
