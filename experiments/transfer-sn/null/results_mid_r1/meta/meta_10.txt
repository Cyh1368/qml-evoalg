# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Layered re-uploading with simple controlled-X couplings: The current best “8-Qubit Re-Uploading Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 9) interleaves 3 feature-map uploads with 2 repeats of a block that applies per-qubit Ry rotations plus two CRX layers on alternating even/odd pairs. This yielded combined_score_raw = 1.00, perfect train/val/test (1.00), zero generalization gap, depth = 75, gate_count = 180, and convergence in ~150 steps.  
- Moderate depth with repeated encoding: Retaining N_UPLOADS = 3 and N_REPEATS = 2 across top performers (Gen 1, Gen 4, Gen 9) hit the sweet spot for expressivity vs. trainability. Gen 9’s depth of 75 outperformed deeper circuits (depth ≥ 93) with no loss in accuracy.  
- Minimal two-qubit parameterization: Restricting parametric entangling gates to CRX only (no CRY/CRZ) in Gen 9 matched or exceeded the performance of richer ansätze (e.g., Gen 4’s CRZ network), showing that a targeted controlled-X pattern suffices for full separability.  
- Fast convergence via early stopping and best-weight restoration: All top programs (e.g., Gen 9 and Gen 1) employed validation-loss early stopping with restore-best-weights, stabilizing training and preventing overfitting while achieving zero generalization gap.  

Ineffective Approaches  
- Overparameterized two-qubit interactions: Gen 4 (“crz_entanglement_efficiency,” 140 params, depth 93, score 0.99) and Gen 6 (“hypercube_evolution_ansatz,” 122 params, depth 93, score 0.98) added CRZ/CRY layers and long-range couplings without improving test accuracy or gap, merely inflating resource costs.  
- Hierarchical hypercube/ladder entanglement: Gen 6’s hypercube-distance CZ network (258 gates) and Gen 1’s mixed CNOT-CZ-CRY brickwall (348 gates) both achieved near-perfect classification but at much higher depth and gate count, indicating diminishing returns from complex connectivity.  
- Orthogonal rotation brickwork without controlled-X focus: Gen 8’s alternating Ry–Rz + CNOT ansatz (depth 81, gate_count 228, params 98) converged more slowly (~390 steps) and scored lower (combined 0.96) than Gen 9, suggesting that single-qubit Rz layers add overhead without commensurate expressivity gains.  
- Aggressive parameter sharing alone: Gen 1’s shared-parameter RZ scheme reduced unique keys but still produced depth 99 and 348 gates, demonstrating that sharing must be paired with an optimized entanglement pattern to cut resource use effectively.  

Implementation Insights  
- ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec pattern: All evaluated programs define their block via a list of dicts and auto-validate/generate key-to-position maps. This modular design (as in Gen 9’s apply_ansatz_block) makes it trivial to swap in/out gates and ensures spec consistency.  
- Decoupled readout calibration: Splitting N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS from gain/bias simplifies the QNode signature and stabilizes gradient flow—used identically across Gen 1, Gen 4, Gen 8, and Gen 9.  
- Generic feature re-upload loop: Implementing `for upload_index in range(N_UPLOADS): feature_map(...); repeat(ansatz)` ensures uniform encoding of high-dimensional binary inputs. Gen 9’s streamlined 180-gate circuit highlights how combining this loop with a minimal ansatz block maximizes efficiency.  
- Automated circuit metadata capture: Leveraging `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` and `tape.graph.get_depth()` in circuit_metadata allowed precise tracking of depth and gate count, directly correlating resource metrics with performance outcomes.  

Performance Analysis  
- Depth vs. raw score: Only circuits with depth ≤ 81 (Gen 8 at 81→0.96, Gen 9 at 75→1.00) reached or exceeded combined_score_raw = 0.99. The best raw score (1.00) coincides with the shallowest design (depth 75), underscoring a strong inverse correlation between depth and generalization performance.  
- Gate count efficiency: Gen 9’s 180 gates delivered perfect accuracy metrics, while more complex designs (e.g., Gen 6 at 258 gates, Gen 1 at 348 gates) failed to surpass 1.00 combined_score_raw or required no fewer steps.  
- Parameter count vs. performance: Increasing parameter count from 98 (Gen 9) to 122 or 140 (Gen 6/Gen 4) did not improve combined_score_raw, demonstrating that overparameterization yields redundant degrees of freedom rather than better generalization.  
- Training dynamics: Gen 9 and Gen 1 both converge in ~150 steps to perfect accuracy, whereas brickwork-style Gen 8 slows to ~390 steps. This highlights how ansatz structure—not just depth or param count—drives training efficiency.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a “rotating” CRX connectivity across uploads. In your upload loop, offset the wires in `ANSATZ_SPEC` by `upload_index` mod N_QUBITS (e.g. on upload 0 use [(0,1),(2,3),…], on upload 1 use [(1,2),(3,4),…], etc.). This preserves the simple per-block Ry+CRX pattern and keeps depth at 75 while gradually sampling next-neighbor couplings, testing whether light-weight long-range interactions boost expressivity without extra parameters.  

2. Share block parameters across the entire circuit. Rename all Ry keys to a single set {ry0…ry7} and all Crx keys to {crx0…crx7} in every block, so each upload/repeat reuses the same 16 trainable angles. Gen 9’s perfect accuracy with just 98 parameters suggests the ansatz’s power comes more from its structure than distinct angles—this will cut N_PARAMS in half and probe true parameter efficiency.  

3. Swap control and target in half the CRX gates of the second entangling layer. In each block, replace two of the four `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[a,b]}` entries with `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[b,a]}` (e.g. invert (1,2)→(2,1), (5,6)→(6,5)). This injects inverse conditional rotations without adding gates, diversifying entanglement symmetry while preserving the minimal CRX-only design that converged fastest.  

4. Rebalance encoding versus processing: set N_UPLOADS = 4 and N_REPEATS = 1, but drop the second CRX layer in each block so that each upload still applies 8 Ry + 4 CRX gates (16 gates). This modification keeps the total depth ≈75 and gate_count≈180 but tests whether more frequent feature re-uploads with a lighter ansatz block accelerates convergence or enhances generalization.  

5. Append a final shared‐parameter RZ phase layer before measurement. After the last ansatz block, add `{"gate":"RZ","wire": i, "param":"rz_end"}` for i=0…7, reusing the same `"rz_end"` key. This 8-gate, 1-parameter addition leverages the success of simple single-qubit rotations to fine-tune the global measurement basis with minimal overhead.
