<DESCRIPTION>
I’m tightening the ansatz into a parameter-shared, low-redundancy block that still keeps the circuit expressive.

The main change is to reuse the same 8 trainable keys across multiple single-qubit layers, instead of allocating separate keys for every layer. This directly reduces the unique circuit parameters per block while preserving a strong inductive bias: a local RY warm-start, a ring CZ entangler, a shared RZ refinement, a long-range CRY bridge, a shifted CZ ring, and a final shared RX layer.

Why this should help:
- fewer unique parameters improves parameter efficiency and usually reduces overfitting
- shared weights across sublayers encourage smoother optimization and better generalization
- mixed-axis rotations plus two entangling topologies keep enough expressivity for the binary classification task
- reduced circuit size should also help convergence speed under the fixed Adam training loop

This follows the recommendation to tie parameters across repeated substructures, but in a stronger form: the same 8 keys are reused throughout the block.
</DESCRIPTION>
