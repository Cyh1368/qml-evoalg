# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Dense RY–RZ–CZ layering with repeated feature re-uploading drove top performance.  
  • The “8-Qubit Evolved Ansatz Quantum Classifier” (Gen 0) achieved combined_score_raw 0.97 (test_acc 0.98, gen_gap 0.01) using per-qubit RY, pre-/post-RZ, and a chain of CZ entanglers across 3 uploads×2 repeats.  
- Pre- and post-rotation Z phases enriched expressivity.  
  • Adding RZ before and after CZ (146 parameters, depth 99) outperformed the ring-entanglement model (Gen 2: 98 params, depth 81, raw 0.95).  
- Multiple feature-map re-uploads with early stopping yielded fast convergence.  
  • Gen 0 converged in ~180 steps to 0.99 validation accuracy, vs ~540 steps for the ring model, demonstrating the benefit of balanced depth and parameter count.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Uniform RY–CNOT blocks underfit despite similar depth.  
  • Both “Eight-Qubit Reuploading RY-CNOT Classifier” (Gen 3) and “8-Qubit RY-CNOT Hardware-Efficient Ansatz” (Gen 4) yielded raw –2.87 (test_acc 0.58, gen_gap 0.12) at depth 75 with 50 params, indicating insufficient two-qubit expressivity.  
- All-to-all CZ connectivity plus collective rotations incurred overhead without matching accuracy.  
  • “Collective Rotations and Full CZ Entanglement Ansatz” (Gen 1) used only 20 params but depth 135 and gate_count 396, yet scored raw 0.75 (test_acc 0.74, efficiency 0.04), suggesting pruning entanglers or richer single-qubit blocks is preferable.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Flexible ansatz specification via `validate_ansatz_spec` and parameter–key mapping eases experimentation with gate mixes (single-qubit rotations vs. controlled rotations).  
- Initializing circuit parameters in [–0.05, 0.05] with gain=1, bias=0 enabled stable Adam convergence, hitting the 0.90 accuracy threshold by step 180 in the best model.  
- Interleaving RZ layers around CZ gates increases nonlinearity without drastically increasing depth, as seen by the jump from raw 0.95 (Gen 2) to 0.97 (Gen 0) when adding those RZ layers.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Test accuracy scales with ansatz richness more than sheer gate count.  
  • Gen 0 (146 params, 270 gates) outperformed Gen 1 (20 params, 396 gates) and both RY–CNOT models, despite lower depth than Gen 1.  
- Parameter efficiency remains low (~0.01) across high-accuracy models, indicating diminishing returns per parameter but critical expressivity gains.  
- Faster convergence correlated with moderate circuit depth: best program’s depth 99 converged ~3× faster than the depth-81 ring model (Gen 2), highlighting a sweet spot in depth–expressivity balance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce parameterized two-qubit rotations (e.g. CRZ or CRY) in place of every other fixed CZ in the entangling chain. For instance, alternate CZ and CRZ gates in each block so that half of the entanglers carry trainable angles. This builds on the success of pre/post-RZ enrichment and injects more nonlinearity per block without dramatically increasing depth.

2. Prune the 1D chain of CZs into a “ladder” or “zig-zag” pattern (e.g. entangle qubit pairs (0,1),(2,3),(1,2),(3,4),(4,5),(6,7),(5,6)) to maintain full connectivity but reduce total two-qubit gates. By removing redundant or long-range CZs you lower gate count and depth overhead (avoiding Gen 1’s inefficiency) while preserving expressivity through staggered entanglement.

3. Enhance single-qubit blocks by sandwiching each RY between two RZs—i.e. replace RY(wire) with RZ(wire)→RY(wire)→RZ(wire) in every block. This quadruples the number of Z rotations around each Y rotation, leveraging the proven benefit of pre/post-RZ layers to boost expressivity without adding extra two-qubit gates.

4. Systematically vary the feature-upload vs. variational-repeat ratio: in addition to the baseline 3 uploads×2 repeats, test 4×1 and 2×3 configurations using the same per-block RY–RZ–CZ–RZ pattern. This will reveal whether more frequent data re-uploads (higher N_UPLOADS) or deeper local repetition (higher N_REPEATS) better trades off convergence speed and accuracy, building directly on the fast convergence seen in Gen 0.

5. Make the FEATURE_SCALE in the IsingZZ feature map trainable per feature, e.g. replace the fixed π/2 multiplier with a learnable vector of length N_FEATURES. By allowing the circuit to adapt the strength of each feature injection you increase the expressivity of the data encoding itself—akin to adding trainable layers before the variational ansatz.
