# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Hypercube-structured entanglement plus two-channel RY “preparation” and “decode” layers drove the top score (0.89).  
  • 8-Qubit Hypercube Ansatz Classifier (combined_score_raw = 0.89, test = 0.90) uses RY on two disjoint 4-qubit subsets (“prepare_a/b” and “decode_a/b”) around CZ couplings along the three binary-cube dimensions, outperforming the previous 6-block linear-CZ design (0.85).  
- Shared “bridge” RY layer between CZ matchings prevented collapse into purely diagonal stages and narrowed the generalization gap to 0.05.  
  • The bridge in the hypercube model maintained expressive mixing without overfitting, versus brickwork-only patterns (gap ≥ 0.07).  
- Multistage data re-uploading (3 uploads × 2 repeats) combined with rich phase structure yields stable convergence (~540 steps) and high train/validation/test (0.95/0.90/0.90).  
  • Reinforces that repeated IsingZZ feature maps plus interleaved ansätze offer expressivity gains over single upload or flat repeats.  
- CZ-only entanglement, when aligned with problem geometry (hypercube), outperforms global-rotation-only mixers (0.87) and mixed CNOT brickworks (0.80–0.85), highlighting the value of problem-inspired coupling.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Pure global single-qubit rotations with no trainable entanglers plateaued at 0.87 (8-qubit global-rotation classifier), despite very low depth (65) and fast convergence (~90 steps). Lack of entanglement expressivity limited top accuracy.  
- Deep, overparameterized RY–RZ–CNOT brickwork (8-Qubit Phase-Coupled Binary Classifier, 146 params) scored only 0.69 with depth 142; excessive two-qubit gates without structured mixing hurt both efficiency (0.005) and accuracy.  
- Sparse CNOT brickwork with tied phases (8-Qubit Re-uploading Ansatz, 98 params) reached 0.80 (gap 0.04) but parameter efficiency (0.01) suffered, indicating redundant parameters in loosely connected layouts.  
- Layered RX–RZ chains (8-Qubit Multi-Upload Re-Uploading Classifier, 146 params) yielded 0.85 but generalization gap remained 0.07—further evidence that unguided entanglement patterns require complementary phase layers or problem-specific couplings.  

## Implementation Insights  
- PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK mapping and a consistent pre→CZ→mix→bridge→CZ→post layout simplify parameter indexing and support code-gen of evolved blocks; the hypercube program implements 11 unique keys/block × 4 wires each = 44 params total.  
- The “bridge” RY layer across all qubits regularizes adjacent entanglers: in code, a single `"param": "bridge"` applied to wires 0–7 effectively adds inter-block phase diversity.  
- Two-channel RY parameters (“prepare_a”/“prepare_b” and “decode_a”/“decode_b”) exploit natural bipartition of the hypercube, doubling mixing richness with only 4→4 wire reuse rather than per-wire unique parameters.  
- Re-use of a fixed IsingZZ feature map (FEATURE_SCALE = π/2) across three uploads ensures nonlinearity is captured before hypercube-specific entanglement, as in all top-scoring designs.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The hypercube model’s combined raw score (0.89) exceeds the previous best (0.85) by 0.04, at the cost of ~50 additional gates (348 vs. 270) and modest depth increase (99 vs. 87).  
- Generalization gaps across top performers cluster: 0.05 (hypercube), 0.07 (linear CZ 6-block), and 0.07–0.10 (other re-uploads), confirming that phase-rich layouts narrow train–test discrepancies.  
- Parameter efficiency remains low (~0.02 for hypercube vs. 0.01–0.06 for others), underscoring that structural design (entanglement geometry + bridge) outweighs raw parameter count in driving accuracy gains.  
- Convergence steps scale with ansatz richness: hypercube (≈540 steps) vs. linear CZ 6-block (≈750) vs. global-rotation (≈90), suggesting that structured entanglement accelerates learning compared to flat repeats of simple mixers.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a small number of trainable two-qubit phase gates within the hypercube couplings. For instance, in each of the three CZ matchings replace one representative CZ (e.g. wires [0,1], [0,2], [0,4]) with a CRZ whose angle is a new per-block parameter “crz_dimX”. This preserves the hypercube geometry while allowing the model to learn fine-grained two-qubit phases, building on the success of phase-rich single-qubit bridges without drastically increasing depth.

2. Augment the global RY “bridge” with an alternating RZ bridge layer on every second block. After the existing RY “bridge” on wires 0–7 in blocks 2 and 4, insert an RZ(param_bridge_z_block) on all eight qubits. This complementary axis mixing can further prevent adjacent CZ layers from collapsing into diagonal stages, leveraging the bridge’s regularizing effect with added phase diversity.

3. Rotate the two-channel preparation/decoder bipartition across uploads to diversify mixing. Rather than using the same hypercube “coloring” for all three uploads, cycle through the three 4-qubit colorings induced by slicing the hypercube along dimensions 0, 1, and 2. This mutation injects data into differently mixed subregisters on each upload, potentially enriching the feature interaction patterns observed by the ansatz.

4. Make the FEATURE_SCALE of the IsingZZ data re-upload map trainable per upload stage. Replace the fixed π/2 multiplier with parameters scale_upload_0, scale_upload_1, scale_upload_2 that are optimized during training. By letting the circuit tune how strongly each re-upload injects nonlinearity, you can adaptively balance expressivity and convergence speed across the multistage feature embedding.

5. Insert sparse long-range CZ edges aligned to non-adjacent hypercube vertices after select blocks. For example, add CZ(wires=[0,7]) and CZ(wires=[1,6]) immediately after block 2 and block 5. These occasional entanglers capture higher-order correlations beyond nearest-neighbor hypercube edges with minimal gate and depth overhead, complementing the core geometry-inspired couplings.
