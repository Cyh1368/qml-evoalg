# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Global parameter tying with dense CZ blocks  
  • Gen 11 (“collective_cz_mixer”) uses a single global_rx and global_ry per block (14 params total) interleaved with a dense all-to-all CZ entangler to reach combined_score_raw = 0.95, test_accuracy ≈ 0.97, efficiency 0.07.  
  • This outperforms per-wire parameter schemes (e.g., Gen 16’s 194 params at 0.78).  
- Three re-uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  • Both Gen 11 and the ZZ Feature-Map Ansatz (Gen 18, raw 0.91) exploit 3 feature-map uploads each followed by 2 ansatz repeats, yielding nonlinearity that stabilizes validation (gap ≈ 0.01).  
- Fixed two-qubit CZ gates over parametric entanglers  
  • Eliminating CRX/CRY/CRZ in Gen 11 reduces gradient overhead and speeds convergence (≈120 steps vs. ≈540 for hypercube CRZ models) while preserving expressivity.  
- Coarse-grained single-qubit rotations  
  • Using “global” rotations across all wires in RY/RX layers delivers high expressivity per parameter, maximizing parameter efficiency and generalization.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization with per-wire rotations  
  • Gen 16’s ShinkaEvolve ansatz (194 params) and Gen 17’s CRZ-augmented ansatz (206 params) achieved raw 0.78/0.77 with generalization gaps of 0.07–0.09, indicating overfitting and low efficiency.  
- Adding parametric two-qubit gates  
  • Inclusion of trainable CRZ gates in Gen 17 increased circuit complexity without score gains (0.77 vs. 0.95), suggesting fixed CZs suffice for entanglement.  
- Unstructured or sparse entanglers  
  • Hypercube CZ-RY (Gen 15) and Hypercube Binary (Gen 19) employed problem-inspired but less dense linkages, scoring 0.89/0.86—lagging behind the dense CZ pattern.  
- Excess depth without parameter tying  
  • Deep brickwork designs (e.g., 132-depth, 348 gates) plateaued at raw 0.73, showing depth alone cannot compensate for unoptimized parameter distribution.  

## Implementation Insights  
- ANSATZ_SPEC with shared parameter keys  
  • Defining “global_rx”/“global_ry” once in ANSATZ_SPEC and using a key-to-position map lets apply_ansatz_block index and apply rotations uniformly, slashing N_PARAMS.  
- Dense CZ entangler listing  
  • Explicitly enumerating all CZ wire pairs in ANSATZ_SPEC creates full connectivity without nested loops or parametric gates, simplifying code and lowering gradient cost.  
- Modular upload-repeat loops  
  • The circuit_z_expectations QNode cleanly alternates feature_map() and apply_ansatz_block() over N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS, decoupling architecture from parameter logic.  
- Backprop with Adam and early stopping  
  • Leveraging PennyLane’s backprop differentiation and validation-loss early stopping recovered best_params at minimal gap (0.01) and convergence around step 120.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gen 11 vs. Next Best (Gen 18)  
  • Raw score jump from 0.91 to 0.95 (+0.04) and efficiency from 0.05 to 0.07 underscores the impact of global parameter tying over per-wire schemes.  
- Param count vs. accuracy  
  • Designs with >100 params (Gen 16–17) peaked at raw ≈ 0.77–0.78. In contrast, 14–20-param circuits (Gen 11/18) achieved ≥ 0.91, highlighting diminishing returns of large parameter sets.  
- Depth and gate count parity  
  • Top performers share similar depths (~99–111) and gate counts (~336–396), indicating entanglement pattern and parameter reuse, not smaller circuits, drive accuracy.  
- Generalization gaps correlate with design  
  • Fixed CZ + global rotations yield gaps ≈ 0.01 (Gen 11/18), while models with per-wire or parametric two-qubit gates see gaps of 0.07–0.09, linking compact, structured ansätze to robust generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Append a global RZ layer to each ansatz block. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, after the eight RY entries add eight RZ gates all sharing a new key “global_rz.” This keeps your parameter count up by only one per block (N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK → 3), preserves coarse-grained tying, and enriches single-axis mixing without per-wire overhead.  

2. Turn FEATURE_SCALE into three trainable parameters, one per upload. Replace the constant np.pi/2 in feature_map with scale_0, scale_1, scale_2 and add those keys to your total parameter vector. This adaptive embedding lets the circuit learn the optimal strength at each re-upload, building on the nonlinearity gains of multi-upload layouts.  

3. Tie your ansatz parameters across repeats within each upload. Instead of giving each block its own global_rx/global_ry, define upload-specific keys “rx_upload_0”, “ry_upload_0”, … “rx_upload_2”, “ry_upload_2” and reuse them for both repeats in that upload. You cut N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS from 12→6, further boosting parameter efficiency while preserving the two-repeat depth.  

4. Interleave occasional long-range CZ shortcuts into the circuit. After every two ansatz blocks (i.e. at block_index 2 and 4), inject fixed CZ gates between maximally distant qubits (e.g. CZ([0,4]) and CZ([1,5])). These no-parameter shortcuts capture higher-order correlations that the dense all-to-all entangler may miss.  

5. Augment entanglement diversity with a fixed CNOT star layer. Immediately after your dense CZ entangler in each block, apply CNOTs from qubit 0 onto qubits 1–7 (wires=[[0,1], [0,2], …, [0,7]]). This adds no new parameters, preserves depth parity (~110), and injects a complementary entanglement pattern shown to expand expressivity without overfitting.
