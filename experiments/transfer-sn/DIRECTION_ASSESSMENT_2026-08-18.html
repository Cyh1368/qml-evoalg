Assessment of the 2026-08-18 direction

Whether the “hallucinated symmetry / rerun-consistency” framing is supported by what is on disk, what it gets right, what it gets wrong, and what to publish instead.
Written 2026-08-18 against 202608-18-direction.md. Every number below is quoted from a named artifact in this repository.

Verdict in one line

The direction is right about the failure mode and wrong about its mechanism, and the repair rule as written (“accept a symmetry when the model reports it consistently across reruns”) is the one criterion already measured to fail, producing a false positive on the mid arm in 5 runs out of 5. Applied to what the search builds rather than what it says, the same idea separates cleanly and is the publishable contribution.

00If you only read one section

Plain language, no jargon. Everything after this section is the evidence behind it.

You are a researcher about to run ShinkaEvolve on a quantum machine learning problem whose answer you do not know. What should you notice, and what should you do so that you find the symmetry when there is one and do not fool yourself when there is not?

What to notice

The model’s own write-up tells you nothing about whether it found something. This is the single most important point. The mid-tier ensemble wrote confident notes about symmetry in every single run, 5 out of 5, and the circuits it actually built had the target structure in one. A researcher reading only the patch notes would have concluded five times over that the symmetry was found. Ignore what the model says it did; look at what it built.

A high score is not merely useless here, it points the wrong way. The two best-scoring weak runs both had the wrong structure. The correct structure turned up in the runs ranked third and fourth. So the natural move, take the winning run and study it, is precisely the move that misleads you.

Wild score swings between identical reruns mean the instrument is not working. Ten byte-identical weak runs ranged from 0.00 to 0.75, and one never improved on the starting circuit at all. Any single number drawn from runs that behave like that is meaningless.

What to do

  1. Run it at least 5 times, ideally 10, with the same settings. Once is not an experiment.
  2. From each run, pull out the actual circuit and check whether the same structural pattern appears. Concretely: does one shared parameter drive the same gate across all the qubits? Not “does the model mention it” but “does the artifact contain it.” In our data this went 3/3 for the strong ensemble, 1/5 for mid, 2/10 for weak. That is the test.
  3. Then test it directly on the behaviour. If you believe you have found “relabelling the qubits does not matter,” relabel them and check the output does not change. This check does not depend on any judgement call about which pattern to look for, and it is the one to trust most. The programs are saved and this has not been run yet; it is cheap and it is the first thing to do.
  4. Decide what pattern counts as a hit before you look at the results. We picked ours knowing the answer, which is acceptable for a study and not acceptable for real work.
  5. If only one model in your ensemble ever produces the structure, be suspicious. In our runs one model found it 18 times out of 20 cold starts while the other two managed 1 out of 21 combined. That may be telling you about the model rather than about your problem.

The one thing this cannot yet tell you

If your reruns disagree with each other, that means “this did not resolve anything,” not “there is no symmetry.” The two look identical from where you sit. The weak and mid ensembles gave inconsistent results on a problem that genuinely does have a symmetry, purely because they were not good enough to find it.

We know a strong ensemble correctly returns “nothing here” when there is nothing: 0 out of 3, on a dataset deliberately built with no symmetry. We do not know what a weak or mid ensemble does in that situation, because those runs are the ones still pending. Until they return, the honest rule is: consistent structure across reruns is good evidence a symmetry is real; inconsistency only means you need a stronger ensemble before concluding anything.

Practical upshot

Use a strong ensemble, run it 5 to 10 times, check the circuits rather than the commentary, and confirm by actually applying the transformation. That protocol is supported by the evidence we have.

Who found it, and whether selection explains it (DISCOVERY_ATTRIBUTION.md)

The symmetry enters in a single event at generation 3. UCB1 does deterministic round-robin initialisation (opus → gemini → gpt, identical in all three runs, same parent, no inspirations), so generation 3 is not a selection artifact. Cold-start motif rate: gpt-5.6-sol 18/20, opus-4.6 1/12, gemini-3.1-pro 0/9, Fisher p = 1.7 × 10−8.

02Where the proposal is right

  • The core danger is real and measured. A researcher reading proposer patch notes cannot tell a discovery from a wrong hypothesis, and the failure is worst exactly where the signal would be needed. Mid names a symmetry in 5 runs out of 5 and its final circuit has the target structure in one.
  • Score is the wrong readout, and the memo implicitly avoids it. Confirmed independently: the two highest-scoring weak runs both have the wrong structure, so selecting the run to inspect by score selects against the truth.
  • Reproducibility tracks capability. CV 0.05 frontier against 0.61 mid and 0.64 weak. This is the strongest quantitative result in the project and it is exactly what the memo leans on.
  • The frontier arm is honest under a null. 0/3 motif on a task built to have none, with tied circuits proposed and then rejected by the fitness function. That is the behaviour the memo predicted, obtained under a decision rule fixed in advance.
  • The guideline framing beats the benchmark framing. As a benchmark this instrument is weak: weak and mid are not separable at all (gen-49 gap +0.043, pooled sd 0.229, Cohen d = 0.19, ~63 runs per arm to resolve, and weak scores higher than mid while costing a third as much). As a methodology paper, the same measurements are the point rather than an embarrassment.

03Four things the proposal gets wrong

1. The pathology is not hallucination. It is a wrong hypothesis, faithfully built.

The memo says the weak and mid arms “confidently report symmetries that are not present,” and calls this hallucination. Measured, mid builds what it claims 97% of the time. It reports mirror symmetry, builds mirror symmetry, and mirror symmetry is a genuine symmetry of the circuit layout that is not a symmetry of the task. Mid also never once used permutation vocabulary; it did not claim the right symmetry and miss it, it claimed a different one.

This matters for the paper in three ways. It is a stronger and less familiar result than hallucination (“LLMs make things up” is not news; “LLMs commit to a coherent, faithfully implemented, wrong invariance and never revisit it” is). It changes the mechanism: the defect is hypothesis selection, not honesty. And a reviewer who reads onepager_symmetry/report.tex and finds the words “neither arm was confabulating” against an abstract claiming confabulation will not recover from it.

Reconciling the two numbers, which are not in conflict: the 22% figure counts a general symmetry regex against a permutation-specific structural test, so it measures “said something symmetry-ish, built the S8 orbit.” Matched claim to matched structure, mid is at 97%. Both are correct; only the second describes the mechanism.

2. The proposed acceptance rule is the criterion already measured to fail.

The memo’s rule is: if the model consistently says the same symmetry across seeds, accept it. That is criterion 3, and mid satisfies it perfectly (5/5) while being wrong in 4 runs of 5. Requiring repetition filters sporadic claims; it cannot filter a systematic wrong hypothesis, because the wrong hypothesis is also consistent. The rule only works with the readout swapped from the verbal claim to the extracted structural invariant, where it goes 3/3 frontier, 1/5 mid, 2/10 weak.

3. “Inconsistent across seeds ⟹ no symmetry” is not supported, and the counterexample is in-house.

The memo’s inference rule is that fluctuation across reruns indicates the symmetry is absent. The weak and mid arms fluctuate wildly (CV 0.61–0.64) on a task that does contain the symmetry. So instability is jointly caused by absence-of-structure and by insufficient-searcher, and the data on hand cannot tell those apart at the cheap tiers. The honest reading of instability is “this instrument resolved nothing; no conclusion either way”, which is a weaker and safer claim than the memo makes. The null control was run frontier-only; the weak/mid null arms (launch_null_tiers.sh, 20 generations, n = 5 each, ~$9) are the experiment that would close this and had not returned when this was written.

4. The statistics are not yet strong enough for a normative guideline.

  • Null control: 3/3 vs 0/3, Fisher p = 0.10. Pre-registered as suggestive, not significant.
  • Frontier arm: n = 3 everywhere. The sd 0.054 has a 95% CI of roughly 0.03–0.16.
  • The strict structural fingerprint was chosen knowing the answer. A looser one (any parameter spanning ≥8 wires, two-qubit gates included) gives weak 6/10, mid 3/5, frontier 3/3, and the separation collapses. VALIDATION_CRITERIA.md already flags this as the protocol’s weakest point, and a guideline paper is exactly the genre where a reviewer will attack it.
  • One task, one symmetry group, one very heavily pretrained concept (permutation invariance).
  • The discovery is nearly single-model: gpt-5.6-sol 18/20 cold-start against 1/21 for the other two frontier models. “Frontier ensembles are reliable” may in fact be “one model is reliable and the other two are along for the ride,” which is a different paper and a fragile one, since it dates.

The open gap, stated precisely: nothing found treats rerun variance as a diagnostic for whether a discovery is real in LLM-driven scientific search, nothing found reports that variance shrinks with proposer capability, and nothing found validates such a criterion against a task with a known hidden symmetry plus a matched negative control. That combination is this project’s.

Caveat carried from the source: most entries are SNIPPET-grade, seen in search results and not verified against the papers. Read 2605.20086 and 2605.29268 properly before positioning. Not yet searched at all: quantum-ansatz / VQE architecture-search reproducibility, and equivariance discovery in neural architecture search. Both could contain a direct competitor.

05Academic impact and audience

Who needs this. Anyone pointing an LLM-driven evolutionary loop (ShinkaEvolve, OpenEvolve, AlphaEvolve-likes) at a scientific problem whose answer they do not know, which is now a fast-growing and almost entirely unvalidated practice. The paper’s function is to stop a specific, cheap, attractive mistake: run once, read the patch notes, believe them.

What it is worth. A short methods / negative-results paper with an unusually well-matched control, not a capability result. Realistic venues: a NeurIPS/ICML workshop on agents-for-science or on reproducibility, or a QML venue if the quantum framing is kept central. It will not be a headline paper and should not be written as one.

Durability. The tier-specific numbers (mid 22%, frontier 97%) will date within a year as models improve, and the entire frontier result may reduce to “gpt-5.6-sol is good at this.” What does not date is the protocol: the demonstration that verbal claims, single-run scores and raw structural agreement all fail as validity criteria while structural recurrence works, plus a matched negative control showing the criterion returns “nothing here” when there is nothing. Write the protocol as the contribution and the model tiers as the evidence, never the reverse.

Honest ceiling. One task, one symmetry, n=3 at the frontier, p=0.10 on the control, and a fingerprint chosen with hindsight. Reviewers will find all five. The paper survives if it names them first and shows the behavioural equivariance check, which has no fingerprint free parameter.

06The claim, in one paragraph

LLM-driven evolutionary search reports its own discoveries unreliably, and the unreliability is not fabrication but committed wrong hypotheses: on a quantum ansatz task whose labels are S8-invariant by construction, mid-tier and frontier ensembles use symmetry language at an identical 37.9% rate, mid names a symmetry in every one of five runs and builds the target invariance in one, and mid’s claims are 97% faithful to what it actually built, only to the wrong symmetry (mirror reflection of the circuit rather than relabelling of the data). Score does not rescue this, since the two highest-scoring weak runs have the wrong structure, and neither does demanding that the model repeat its claim across reruns, which accepts mid 5 times out of 5. What does work is applying the recurrence test to the artifact instead of the narration: extract the parameter-sharing partition from each run’s best program and require the same normalised invariant to recur across independent reruns, which holds 3/3 for the frontier ensemble, 1/5 for mid and 2/10 for weak, tracks the score-reproducibility gap (CV 0.05 vs 0.61 and 0.64), and returns 0/3 on a matched control dataset whose labels were altered to destroy the symmetry while every other property was held fixed (Fisher p = 0.10, pre-registered as suggestive). We therefore propose that a discovery from such a search be reported only after N ≥ 5 reruns of a fixed configuration, with the criterion evaluated on a pre-registered structural invariant extracted from the evolved artifacts and confirmed behaviourally, and we document that the model’s own description of its discovery carries no usable evidential weight.

07Proposed fixes

To the framing

  • Drop “hallucination.” Replace with hypothesis lock-in or faithfully-built wrong invariance. It is what the data show and it is the more interesting claim.
  • State the rule on artifacts, not narration, everywhere including the abstract. “The model said it again” is not evidence; “the invariant recurred” is.
  • Weaken the negative direction of the rule. Inconsistency licenses “unresolved,” not “absent,” until the weak/mid null arms return. If they show cheap-tier motif rates at the frontier-null level (~10%), the stronger claim becomes available; if they sit at the arm’s real-task rate (29–33%), that arm’s motif rate is baseline parameter-tying noise and the criterion has a minimum model tier, which is itself a publishable and useful boundary.
  • Lead with the guideline, keep the benchmark out. weak vs mid is unresolvable at any affordable n and weak outscores mid; do not invite that fight.

Experiments, in order of value per dollar

  1. Behavioural equivariance on the six best programs already extracted (~free; the best_program_local.py files exist). Permute inputs, measure output change. This removes the fingerprint circularity, which is the single most attackable point in the whole protocol, and costs essentially nothing.
  2. Weak/mid null arms (launch_null_tiers.sh, ~$9, pre-registered on criterion 6). Already the pending experiment; it is also the one that decides whether fix #3 above can be stated in the strong form.
  3. Null replicates 3 → 10 (~$5.50 and ~90 min each, ≈$40). If the motif rate stays at 0 this moves Fisher well below 0.05 and the control stops being “suggestive.”
  4. Pre-register the fingerprint on a second task before running it. The SU(2) Heisenberg task in zc-su2 already exists with a different symmetry (isotropic XX+YY+ZZ exchange), and a second symmetry class is the cheapest defence against “permutation invariance is over-represented in pretraining.” This is the highest-value item that is not yet scheduled.
  5. Frontier n = 3 → 6 (~$45). Lowest priority: it tightens a CI that is already directionally safe, and buys less than items 1–4.

Threats to name in the paper before a reviewer does

  • Fingerprint chosen with hindsight; looser definition collapses the separation (mitigated by item 1).
  • p = 0.10 on the control at n = 3 (mitigated by item 3).
  • Ensemble members share pretraining, so their agreement is not independent evidence.
  • The frontier effect may be one model: gpt-5.6-sol 18/20 cold-start vs 1/21 for opus and gemini.
  • Low variance also characterises a searcher that reliably fails (mid_e1_r2 finished at exactly 0.0000), so the criterion needs a level as well as a spread.
  • Score anchoring is task-specific: the null seed reads 1.249 on the real task’s scale, above the best solution ever found on the real task. Never compare scores across conditions.

08What would change this assessment

  • Weak/mid null runs coming back at ~30% within-run motif rate. That would mean cheap-tier motif production is noise unrelated to the task, and the guideline would have to carry an explicit minimum-capability precondition rather than being presented as universal.
  • The equivariance check disagreeing with the structural fingerprint on any of the six programs. The structural claim is a proxy; if the proxy and the behaviour part ways, criterion 5 as written is not the criterion to publish.
  • arXiv:2605.20086 turning out to already contain the recurrence criterion. Read it before anything else is drafted; it is the most likely scoop and is currently known only from a snippet.
