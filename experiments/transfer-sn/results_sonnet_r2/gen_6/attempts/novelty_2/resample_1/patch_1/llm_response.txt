<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz block only has a linear chain of CZ gates (nearest-neighbor, open boundary), which limits information mixing between distant qubits and requires more depth for full entanglement. I propose:

1. Adding a closing CZ gate (7,0) to make the entangling layer a ring instead of a chain, improving connectivity with just one extra gate.
2. Replacing the fixed CZ entangling gates with parametrized CRZ gates that share parameters in pairs (even/odd) to keep parameter count controlled while giving the entangler trainable strength, which typically improves expressivity and trainability for classification tasks with structured data (as here, ZZ-based feature encoding pairs well with CRZ-style entanglers).
3. This keeps circuit depth and parameter growth modest (adds 8 more params per block, but reduces gap and improves accuracy based on known results that trainable entangling gates outperform fixed CZ in variational classifiers with re-uploading).

This should reduce train-test gap by giving the optimizer more capacity to shape entanglement strength per feature-upload stage, rather than relying on identical fixed CZ coupling every time, and the ring topology helps propagate correlations between qubits 0 and 7 which are otherwise only weakly coupled through the chain.
