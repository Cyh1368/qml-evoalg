**Program Name: 8-Qubit Reuploading RY-RZ-CZ Classifier**  
- **Implementation**: Three rounds of an IsingZZ feature-map upload are alternated with two repeats per upload of an ansatz block that applies single-qubit RY rotations, pre- and post- RZ rotations on all qubits, and a linear CZ entangling chain; circuit parameters (146 total) include block-specific rotation angles plus a global gain and bias, all trained via Pennylane’s backprop-enabled Adam optimizer with early stopping.  
- **Performance**: Achieves mean train accuracy 94%, validation 89% and test 87% (generalization gap 7%) with depth 99, 270 gates and combined_score_raw 0.85.  
- **Feedback**: The reuploading scheme and simple linear entanglement yield strong classification accuracy but result in low parameter efficiency and high circuit depth; reducing redundant rotations or exploring denser connectivity could maintain performance while lowering resource requirements.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Re-uploading interleaved with simple rotation–entanglement blocks yields top performance.  
  • 8-Qubit Reuploading RY-RZ-CZ Classifier (Gen 0) uses 3 feature-map uploads × 2 repeats of an RY→pre-RZ→CZ-chain→post-RZ block (24 params/block) to reach combined_score_raw 0.85 (train 94%, test 87%) with depth 99 and 270 gates.  
- Linear CZ chain for entanglement outperforms circular or parameterized chains.  
  • Replacing circular CNOT loops (Gen 1, combined 0.69) or CRZ-based couplings (Gen 2, 0.81) with a nearest-neighbor CZ chain reduced depth/gate count and improved accuracy.  
- Two-stage single-qubit rotations around entanglers suffice for expressivity.  
  • RY before and RZ after the CZ chain (Gen 0) matches or exceeds models with additional RX or multi-qubit rotations (Gen 4) despite having fewer parameters.  
- Maintaining low circuit depth correlates with higher combined score.  
  • Gen 0: depth 99 → 0.85; Gen 2: depth 126 → 0.81; Gen 3: depth 132 → 0.79.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Circular entanglement topologies introduced overhead without accuracy gains.  
  • 8-Qubit Circular Ansatz Classifier (Gen 1) had depth 120, 276 gates, combined_score_raw 0.69 and low parameter efficiency.  
- Parameterized two-qubit gates (CRX/CRY/CRZ) increased depth and gates but only marginally improved accuracy.  
  • Enhanced Entanglement Circular (Gen 2) with CRZ saw combined_score_raw 0.81 at depth 126, 300 gates—worse than the simpler CZ chain.  
- Over-parameterized ansatz blocks led to overfitting and zero parameter efficiency.  
  • Learnable Entanglement RX-CRZ (Gen 4) used 218 params, achieved 97% train but only 86% test (gap 0.10) and parameter_efficiency ≈ 0.  
- Excessive layering of rotations (RX+RY+RZ) per block did not yield proportionate performance gains.  
  • Parity-Symmetric Mixer-Entangler (Gen 3) layered multiple rotation types but scored only 0.79 despite low gap (0.04).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Compact ansatz spec definitions drive resource efficiency.  
  • The Gen 0 ANSATZ_SPEC defines 8 RY + 8 pre-RZ + 7 CZ + 8 post-RZ per block, totaling 24 params/block; validate_ansatz_spec extracts keys in a stable order for indexing.  
- Fixed feature map + backpropagation-trained gain/bias readout ensures stable calibration.  
  • All top performers embed a fixed IsingZZ feature map with two trainable readout params for mean-Z scaling, aiding convergence.  
- AnnotatedQueue metadata extraction provides accurate depth/gate counts.  
  • circuit_metadata() inspects the same pipeline used at runtime, ensuring reported resource metrics reflect actual execution.  
- Autograd-enabled Pennylane QNode with Adam and early stopping yields consistent convergence.  
  • Gen 0 converged by step 750 on average, matching or beating deeper/denser circuits.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Depth vs. combined_score_raw shows a strong inverse correlation.  
  • Gen 0 (99→0.85) > Gen 2 (126→0.81) > Gen 3 (132→0.79) > Gen 1 (120→0.69).  
- Gate count beyond ~300 offers diminishing returns.  
  • Best models cluster at 270–300 gates; increasing to 348 (Gen 3) did not improve accuracy.  
- Generalization gaps remain in the 0.04–0.10 range across architectures.  
  • Gen 0 gap 0.07, Gen 3 gap 0.04; low gap alone did not guarantee highest combined score.  
- Parameter efficiency stays uniformly low (<0.02), highlighting the need for leaner ansatz blocks.  
  • Gen 0 efficiency ≈ 0.01 outperforms Gen 4’s near-zero efficiency despite similar test accuracy.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Implement weight-sharing across repeated blocks. Modify the ANSATZ_SPEC and parameter indexing so that the two repeats per upload share the same set of RY–RZ parameters, cutting N_PARAMS_CIRCUIT in half. Since Gen 0’s expressivity arises from block structure rather than unique per-block parameters, tying weights may preserve accuracy while boosting parameter_efficiency.  

2. Augment the linear CZ chain with sparse next-nearest-neighbor couplings on alternating blocks. For example, after the standard CZ chain add CZ gates between qubit pairs (i, i+2) for even i on every second block. This preserves low depth/gate count yet introduces long-range entanglement that could capture higher-order feature interactions without the overhead of full circular topologies.  

3. Explore increasing N_UPLOADS from 3 to 4 while keeping N_REPEATS at 2. The current 3-upload pipeline achieves strong nonlinearity with minimal depth; an extra feature-reupload may further enrich the model’s capacity to discriminate complex patterns, at the cost of only ~50 additional gates. Track combined_score_raw vs. depth to identify the optimal upload/repeat tradeoff.  

4. Alternate the direction of the nearest-neighbor CZ chain between repeats. Define two entangler variants—one chaining qubits 0→1→…→7, the other 7→6→…→0—and apply them on odd vs. even block_index. This bidirectional mixing can improve information propagation across the line without adding gates beyond the simple CZ pattern.  

5. Collapse per-qubit pre- and post-RZ parameters into two global RZ layers. Replace the 16 individual RZ( rz_pre_i ) and RZ( rz_post_i ) with single shared parameters rz_pre_shared and rz_post_shared applied across all wires. By slashing 14 parameters per block, you test whether uniform phase shifts retain the expressivity seen in two-stage rotation blocks while dramatically raising parameter efficiency.
