# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parameter sharing across ansatz blocks  
  • The Parameter-Sharing RY-CRY-RZ Quantum Classifier (Gen 1, combined score 0.87) reused the same RY rotation parameters on two pairs of qubits and shared CRY gate parameters across uploads. This reduced N_params to 122 while achieving 0.90/0.90/0.85 (train/validation/test) and a small generalization gap (0.04).  
- Moderate circuit depth with interleaved feature re-uploads  
  • All top performers (Gen 0 score 0.85 at depth 99, Gen 1 score 0.87 at depth 87) used 3 feature uploads × 2 ansatz repeats. Maintaining depth ≲90 preserved expressivity while improving trainability and convergence (≈450 steps for Gen 1 vs ≈720 for deeper variants).  
- Fixed IsingZZ feature map with trainable gain/bias readout  
  • Both Gen 0 (0.85) and Gen 1 (0.87) used the fixed two-qubit IsingZZ encoding plus a trainable gain and bias on the mean-Z output. This calibration stabilized predictions and, together with early stopping on validation loss, limited overfitting.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Excessive depth and adaptive complexity  
  • The Adaptive CRY-CRX Quantum Classifier (Gen 4, score 0.57) and the Hybrid Star-Topology Classifier (Gen 6, score 0.57) both employed deeper circuits (depth 126 and 105) with hierarchical or star-topology entanglers yet failed to improve beyond random guessing. Their added complexity did not translate into increased expressivity for this dataset.  
- Under-expressive or imbalanced entanglement  
  • Gen 6’s use of a reduced-depth star-topology CZ layer with shared RZ rotations resulted in uniform 50% accuracy, indicating underfitting. Likewise, purely hierarchical or sparse entanglement without sufficient parameter diversity led to non-learnable models.  
- Over-parameterization without sharing  
  • The 8-Qubit RY-RZ CZ Layered Variational Classifier (Gen 5, score 0.85) used 68 parameters but still over-parameterized its dense CZ graph, yielding low parameter efficiency (0.01) and slower convergence (720 steps) compared to the shared-parameter Gen 1 design.  

## Implementation Insights

- Reusing parameter keys reduces parameter count without sacrificing performance  
  • In Gen 1’s ANSATZ_SPEC, RY and RZ parameters (“ry_0”…“ry_3”, “rz_0”…“rz_3”) are applied twice on symmetric wires. This simple naming trick cut N_params_per_block to 14 and total N_params to 122, yet maintained high accuracy.  
- Balancing single- and controlled rotations  
  • The successful Gen 1 ansatz interleaves per-qubit RY/RZ rotations with four CRY couplings per block. CRY delivers nontrivial two-qubit interaction while keeping gate count moderate (276), striking a balance between expressivity and noise susceptibility.  
- Early stopping and restoring best weights  
  • Both Gen 0 and Gen 1 used validation-based early stopping (patience 75, min_delta 1e-4) and restored the best-validation parameters at end of training. This consistently yielded small generalization gaps (0.07 in Gen 0, 0.04 in Gen 1).  
- Fixed feature map eases ansatz burden  
  • Leveraging the same IsingZZ encoding across all variants allowed the ansatz blocks to focus on variational expressivity rather than data encoding, simplifying the optimization landscape.  

## Performance Analysis

- Clear score hierarchy  
  • Gen 1 (Parameter-Sharing RY-CRY-RZ) leads with 0.87 combined score, followed by Gen 0 (0.85) and Gen 5 (0.85), with adaptive/hybrid designs trailing at 0.57.  
- Generalization gap correlates with parameter efficiency  
  • Gen 1 achieved the smallest gap (0.04) at moderate N_params (122), whereas Gen 0’s larger gap (0.07) accompanied its higher N_params (146). The adaptive models had trivial gaps (0.00) only because they never learned.  
- Convergence speed tied to depth and sharing  
  • Gen 1 converged in ~450 steps, ~40% faster than Gen 5’s 720 steps. Reduced depth and shared parameters likely smoothed the loss surface, accelerating training.  
- Gate count vs. performance trade-off  
  • Gen 1 used 276 gates to reach 0.87, while Gen 0 used 270 gates for 0.85. Going deeper to 330 gates in Gen 5 did not boost accuracy, highlighting diminishing returns beyond ~300 gates.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Share CRY parameters across both entangling sublayers  
   In the current ANSATZ_SPEC you have eight distinct CRY keys (“cry_01”, “cry_23”, … “cry_70”). Rename the second four (the [1,2], [3,4], [5,6], [7,0] layer) to reuse the first four keys. This cuts N_params_per_block by four while preserving the same two-qubit interactions, directly testing whether further parameter tying can reduce over-parameterization without harming accuracy.  

2. Introduce a single trainable scaling of the IsingZZ feature map  
   Modify `feature_map` to multiply `FEATURE_SCALE` by a shared scalar parameter (e.g. “fm_gain”) that is learned alongside gain/bias. Share that one extra scalar across all uploads to keep parameter efficiency high. This allows the model to adaptively calibrate the strength of data encoding—extending the fixed map approach that stabilized Gen 1.  

3. Rebalance uploads vs. repeats (2 uploads × 3 repeats)  
   Keep the total of 6 ansatz blocks but swap to `N_UPLOADS = 2` and `N_REPEATS = 3`. This preserves depth ≲90 while concentrating more variational steps between feature re-uploads. It’s a minimal change that explores whether fewer re-encodings but deeper within-upload ansatzes can further speed convergence or close the generalization gap.  

4. Mix CRY and CRX gates for richer two-qubit interactions  
   In ANSATZ_SPEC, alternate the controlled rotation axis: use CRY on half of the pairs and CRX on the other half within each sublayer, reusing parameter keys in the same sharing pattern. This keeps the gate count and parameter budget fixed but diversifies the entangling operations, potentially boosting expressivity in a noise-resilient way.  

5. Add a parameter-free CZ cross-block entanglement layer  
   Insert a CZ layer on non-adjacent qubit pairs (e.g. [0,4] and [1,5]) once per ansatz block using ALLOWED_TWO_QUBIT_GATES. Because CZ is parameter-free, you only incur a small gate-count increase (depth remains ≲90) while improving global connectivity. This tests if modest extra entanglement can strengthen representational power without over-complicating the optimizer’s landscape.
