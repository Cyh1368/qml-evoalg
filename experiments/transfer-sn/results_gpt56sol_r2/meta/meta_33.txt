- Shared rotation layers before and after entanglers (“ry_pre”/“ry_post”) across all qubits proved highly expressive per parameter.  
  • Evidenced in Gen 33/35/36/38—each block uses only 3–4 unique keys (pre-RY, phase-RZ, post-RY, optional CRZ) yet achieves state-of-the-art accuracy.  
- Three data re-uploads interleaved with exactly two ansatz repeats per upload strike the optimal expressivity–depth balance.  
  • Consistent layout across high scorers: N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2, depths in [135–141], gate counts around 396–402.  
- Readout calibration (trainable gain & bias on mean-Z) consistently accelerated convergence (~270–450 steps) and added ~0.01–0.02 to test accuracy.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Heavy SU2-style ansätze with many parametric two-qubit gates underperformed despite higher parameter counts.  
  • Gen 29 (152 params, extra CRZs) fell to 0.76 combined; Gen 31 (98 params) peaked at 0.85.  
- Over-wiring or ad-hoc entangler insertions without coherent topology hurt both convergence and generalization.  
  • Double CZs or random CRZ placements (Gen 29, Gen 34) led to plateaus and gaps > 0.05, versus structured skip-chain or complete-graph designs.  
- Depth reduction in isolation did not improve scores.  
  • Shallow (< 90) circuits never exceeded 0.84 combined; depth trimming must accompany topological revisions.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC coupled with a param-key → index map simplifies block reuse across uploads/repeats.  
  • The current best program’s 4-key spec (“ry_pre”, “rz_phase”, “crz_center”, “ry_post”) scales seamlessly to 6 blocks (24 params).  
- Leveraging Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue & QuantumScript for on-the-fly depth/gate_count extraction provides immediate feedback on resource trade-offs.  
- Strict separation of fixed IsingZZ feature maps (non-trainable) from variational layers focuses parameter budget on rotations and entanglers, boosting parameter efficiency to 0.04–0.05.  
- Small random initialization (±0.05) with gain=1, bias=0 and Adam backprop ensures reproducible convergence across seeds (~120–450 steps depending on param count).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined scores cluster around 0.94 for fully connected, shared-parameter designs at depth ≈ 135/gates ≈ 396–402.  
  • Gen 33/35/38: 0.94 combined, gap 0.01, 20 params. Gen 36: 0.94 combined, gap 0.02, 26 params.  
- Skip-chain entanglement (Gen 27) attained 0.87 combined at depth 93/gates 270 but required 80 params, showing lower parameter efficiency.  
- Parameter efficiency peaks at 0.05 when N_PARAMS=20 (Gen 33/35/38); adding a central CRZ (Gen 36) raises N_PARAMS to 26, reducing efficiency to 0.04 with no score gain.  
- Generalization gaps ≤ 0.02 correlate strongly with structured full-graph designs; gaps ≥ 0.05 appear in ad-hoc or SU2-heavy circuits.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Remove the single trainable CRZ and rely solely on the complete-graph CZ entanglers per block, dropping `crz_center` and reducing N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK from 4 to 3 (and total circuit parameters from 26 to 20). Gen33/35/38 hit 0.94 combined with 20 parameters and a generalization gap ≤0.01; reverting to pure CZs preserves full connectivity and boosts parameter efficiency to ≈0.05 without changing depth or gate count.  

2. Promote the fixed FEATURE_SCALE π/2 to a learnable scalar φ by adding φ to the parameter vector (initialized at π/2) and replacing `FEATURE_SCALE * records[..., k]` with `φ * records[..., k]` in the IsingZZ feature map. Prior experiments (Gen25, Gen27) show tuning embedding strength accelerates convergence by ~200 steps and adds ~0.01–0.02 test accuracy, all without extra gates.  

3. Tie ansatz parameters across the two repeats within each upload so that each upload has one shared block rather than two unique blocks—halving the unique circuit parameters per upload from 6 to 3 (after CRZ removal). Reinvest the freed parameter slots to insert a second CRZ on a high‐centrality edge (e.g., wires [2,5]) in each shared block, enriching targeted two-qubit expressivity while keeping depth and gate count constant.  

4. Upgrade readout calibration from a single global gain + bias to per-qubit affine corrections: introduce gain₀…gain₇ and bias₀…bias₇ applied to each qubit’s ⟨Z⟩ before mean pooling, increasing readout parameters from 2 to 16. Finer-grained calibration has been shown to tighten generalization gaps by capturing individual measurement biases without altering the core ansatz.  

5. Diversify entanglement schedules across uploads by cycling the CZ pattern: use the full complete graph in upload 1, a structured skip-chain in upload 2 (e.g., edges [0,2],[1,3],…, wrapping mod 8), and a cyclically shifted complete graph in upload 3. This “multi-tier” mixing preserves CZ density and depth (~140) while enriching qubit interactions over successive data re-uploads, leveraging the proven benefits of pattern variation.
