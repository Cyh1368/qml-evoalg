# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Fixed IsingZZ feature maps + three data re-uploads + exactly two ansatz repeats (as in Gen 25 “Re-uploading Quantum Classifier”) achieved the top combined score 0.86 (train/val/test = 0.94/0.89/0.88, gap 0.06) by balancing circuit depth (126) and expressivity.
- A uniform ring-of-CZ entanglement topology (Gen 25) matched the combined 0.86 of earlier linear-chain CZ designs but provided more symmetric qubit connectivity, showing ring layouts are a viable alternative to chains without loss in performance.
- Parameter-sharing in an Euler-angle ansatz (Gen 23) cut depth to 103 and reduced gate count to 294 while still attaining a solid combined 0.84 and a small generalization gap (0.07), highlighting that controlled reuse of single-qubit rotations curbs overfitting.
- Trainable gain/bias readout calibration (initialized to 1/0) consistently accelerated convergence (∼390 steps to 0.90 validation accuracy in Gen 25) and boosted test accuracy by ~0.01 across high-scoring variants.

## Ineffective Approaches

- SU2-style layered ansätze with heavy single-qubit rotations and sporadic CRZ insertions (Gen 22 & Gen 24) plateaued at combined 0.83 and exhibited large generalization gaps (0.13), indicating over-parameterization with no topological guidance hurts generalization.
- Adding an extra CRZ gate per block (Gen 28) raised the parameter count to 152 but dropped combined to 0.76—despite shallower depth (91), this ad hoc entangler degraded expressivity per parameter.
- Redundant or dense CZ connections (e.g., double CZ between qubits 7→0 in Gen 22/24) failed to improve scores over single-pass ring or chain patterns, suggesting marginal returns from over-wiring.
- Simply trimming depth without reorganizing entanglement (as in Gen 28’s 91-depth circuit) underperformed, showing that depth reduction must be paired with a coherent connectivity pattern.

## Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC lists paired with a key-to-position mapping (used in Gen 23 & Gen 25) streamline variant construction and ensure consistent parameter indexing across repeats and uploads.
- Leveraging Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript to extract depth and gate_count (as in the current best program) provides a reliable complexity fingerprint to directly link topological tweaks to performance.
- Separating fixed feature encoding (qml.IsingZZ on FEATURE_PAIRS) from variational layers preserves parameter budget for expressive rotations, a pattern common to all top performers.
- Initializing variational parameters in a small uniform range and setting gain=1, bias=0 (as in Gen 25) stabilizes early training dynamics and prevents output scaling drift.

## Performance Analysis

- Ring-CZ (Gen 25) at depth 126/gates 282 matched the combined 0.86 of linear CZ-chain designs at depth 99/gates 270, indicating that moderate depth increase is tolerable when entanglement is well structured.
- Circuits in the depth range [99, 126] and gate_count [270, 282] consistently hit combined scores ≥ 0.86; shallower (< 100) or deeper (> 130) architectures scored ≤ 0.83.
- Models with generalization gaps ≤ 0.07 (Gen 23 gap 0.07, Gen 25 gap 0.06) achieved combined ≥ 0.84, confirming that minimizing train-test discrepancy drives overall score.
- Parameter efficiency (~0.01) remained flat across high-scoring designs, underscoring that topology and re-upload strategy, rather than brute-force parameter count, underpin performance gains.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Revise the single‐qubit rotation layers into full Euler blocks: replace each qubit’s RY–RZ pre‐rotations and the post‐entangler RY with a single RZ(α)–RY(β)–RZ(γ) executed immediately before the CZ ring. This change keeps N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK at 24 and the gate depth unchanged, but grants full SU(2) expressivity around the entanglers—an approach that lifted performance in earlier Gen13 Euler‐based variants.

2. Implement cross‐block parameter sharing for the variational layers: use the same ANSATZ_SPEC parameters across both repeats (and optionally across all uploads), halving the unique circuit parameters from 144 to 72 (or 48) before readout. Reallocate the freed budget to insert a trainable CRZ gate on the central CZ link of each block—combining Gen23’s regularizing parameter reuse with adaptive two‐qubit coupling.

3. Make the feature‐map scale trainable by replacing the constant FEATURE_SCALE = π/2 with a learnable scalar φ (initialized to π/2) that multiplies all qml.IsingZZ rotations. Data‐adaptive embedding strength lets the model tune how aggressively inputs are encoded, focusing downstream variational layers on the most informative features without adding extra gates.

4. Alternate the CZ entanglement topology across the three uploads: keep the standard ring‐of‐CZ in upload 1, shift each CZ by one qubit (0–2, 1–3, …) in upload 2, and reverse the ring direction in upload 3, preserving eight CZ gates per upload. This “multi‐tier” entangler schedule augments qubit mixing and breaks symmetry in a structured way—leveraging the proven benefits of varied coupling patterns without raising depth or gate count.

5. Upgrade the readout calibration from a single global gain and bias to per‐qubit affine parameters (gain_i, bias_i for i=0…7), all initialized at (1, 0). Qubit‐level correction can compensate for individual measurement imbalances and has the potential to further reduce generalization gap, especially when combined with parameter savings from the ansatz pruning above.
