# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Skip-chain entanglement topology (Generation 27 “Skip-Chain 8-Qubit Binary Classifier”) with next-nearest-neighbor CZ links plus a 6–7 bridge delivered the top combined score of 0.87 (94% train, 90% val/test, 0.04 gap) and converged in ≈330 steps.  
- Fixed two-qubit IsingZZ feature map with three data re-uploads and exactly two ansatz repeats per upload was common to all high scorers (e.g., Gen 27 at depth 93/gates 270, Gen 26 at depth 99/gates 264) and balanced expressivity against depth.  
- Non-commuting local mixing: per-block layers of single-qubit RY before and after CZ entanglers (and interleaved RZ “pre” rotations) boosted expressivity without blowing up parameters, as evidenced by the 0.87 combined score at only 146 parameters.  
- Trainable gain and bias on the mean-Z readout (initialized 1/0) consistently accelerated convergence to 0.90 validation accuracy (compare Gen 25 and Gen 27) and added ~0.01 to test accuracy.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- SU2-style heavy rotation ansätze with sporadic CRZ insertions (Gen 29 “Layered RY-CZ” score 0.80; Gen 31 “Re-uploading Quantum Classifier” score 0.78) plateaued early and showed larger generalization gaps (0.08–0.13), despite similar depth or parameter counts.  
- Adding extra parametric two-qubit gates (e.g., an extra CRZ per block in Gen 29) raised parameters to 152 but dropped combined score from ~0.83 to 0.76 in earlier runs, indicating marginal returns on ad-hoc entanglers.  
- Over-wiring with dense or redundant CZ links (e.g., double CZ connections) failed to outperform more structured topologies—the simple skip-chain beat denser ring/chain designs at lower or equal depth.  
- Depth trimming alone without cohesive connectivity (Gen 29’s depth 87/gates 276) underperformed versus skip-chain at depth 93, showing depth reduction must accompany topological redesign.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC plus a key-to-position mapping (as in the current best program) simplifies block construction, ensures consistent parameter indexing across uploads/repeats, and makes rapid topology swaps trivial.  
- Leveraging Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue and QuantumScript to extract depth and gate_count yields a reliable “complexity fingerprint,” directly linking entanglement tweaks (e.g., skip-chain vs. ring) to performance.  
- Strict separation of the fixed IsingZZ feature map from variational layers reserves all trainable parameters for expressive rotations and entanglers. This pattern recurs in Gen 23, Gen 25, and the best Gen 27 design.  
- Small random initialization of all variational parameters (±0.05) combined with gain=1, bias=0 (to calibrate the readout) stabilized backpropagation and delivered reproducible convergence in ≈330 steps.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The top combined score (0.87) only appears at depth 93/gates 270 (Gen 27); shallower (< 90) or deeper (> 99) designs capped at ≤ 0.84 combined, confirming an optimal “sweet spot” in [90–99]/[270–282].  
- Generalization gap ≤ 0.06 correlated with combined ≥ 0.84; the skip-chain’s 0.04 gap was the smallest among all variants, directly boosting its combined metric.  
- Parameter efficiency remained flat at ~0.01 across all designs, reinforcing that topology and re-upload layout drive gains rather than raw parameter count.  
- Comparing similar 146-parameter architectures:  
  • Gen 26 (adaptive_central_crz) scored 0.83 (gap 0.13) vs. Gen 27’s 0.87 (gap 0.04) purely by switching from central-CRZ blocks to skip-chain CZ links and full RY mixing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the separate RY–RZ_pre–RY_post layers with a single SU(2) Euler block per qubit before the CZ entanglers: implement RZ(α)–RY(β)–RZ(γ) on each wire immediately before the skip-chain CZs, dropping the existing three single-rotation gates. This change keeps N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK at 24 and the overall depth/gate count identical, but grants full single-qubit expressivity as seen in earlier Euler-based variants (e.g., Gen13) that outperformed simpler RY-only mixes.  

2. Tie variational parameters across the two repeats in each upload (cross-block sharing) to halve the unique circuit parameters from 146 to 73, then reinvest the freed budget to insert a single trainable CRZ on the critical 6–7 bridge per block. This merges the regularizing benefits of Gen23’s parameter reuse with adaptive two-qubit coupling localized to the skip-chain’s central link, potentially boosting expressivity without exceeding the optimal [90–99]/[270–282] complexity window.  

3. Promote the fixed FEATURE_SCALE π/2 to a learnable scalar φ initialized at π/2, and multiply all qml.IsingZZ rotations by φ. Allowing the model to tune its embedding strength has repeatedly accelerated convergence (Gen25, Gen27) by focusing variational layers on the most informative features, all without adding gates or depth.  

4. Vary the skip-chain entanglement pattern across uploads to break symmetry: use the standard skip-chain in upload 1, cyclically shift every CZ edge by +1 qubit modulo 8 in upload 2 (e.g., replace [0,2]→[1,3], …), and reverse the direction of the original skip-chain in upload 3. This “multi-tier” coupling schedule preserves seven CZs per upload and maintains depth/gate count, yet enriches qubit mixing in a structured, proven-beneficial way.  

5. Upgrade readout calibration from a single global gain and bias to per-qubit affine parameters (gain₀…gain₇, bias₀…bias₇), all initialized at (1, 0). Per-qubit corrections can capture individual measurement biases—tightening the generalization gap further—and leverage the parameter efficiency budget freed by cross-block sharing or Euler-block compression.
