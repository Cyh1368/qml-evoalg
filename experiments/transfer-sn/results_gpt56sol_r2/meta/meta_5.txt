**Program Name: 8-Qubit RY/RZ-CZ Ansatz Classifier**  
- **Implementation**: Encodes 28 binary features with fixed two-qubit IsingZZ couplings, then alternates three feature uploads with two ansatz blocks each; every block applies single-qubit RY rotations, pre- and post-RZ rotations around a linear CZ entangling chain. Circuit parameters (144) plus a trainable gain and bias (2) are optimized via Adam on an L2 loss of mean Pauli-Z expectations with early stopping.  
- **Performance**: Achieves a combined score of 0.85 with train/validation/test accuracies ≈ 0.94/0.89/0.87, a generalization gap of 0.07, depth 99, gate count 270 and 146 total parameters.  
- **Feedback**: The repeated RY-RZ-CZ layering delivers high training accuracy but exhibits slight overfitting and low parameter efficiency; introducing parameterized two-qubit gates or deeper entanglement patterns may improve generalization.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Linear CZ-chain entanglement interleaved with RY/RZ rotations (as in the current best “8-Qubit Reuploading Variational Classifier,” combined score 0.86, depth 99, gate 270) matches or slightly exceeds the initial RY/RZ-CZ ansatz (Gen 0 score 0.85) while preserving a low generalization gap (0.06 vs. 0.07).  
- Three feature-map re-uploads paired with two ansatz repeats consistently deliver strong expressivity: both Gen 0 (score 0.85) and Gen 4 (score 0.86) use N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2.  
- Trainable output calibration (gain + bias) atop mean-Z readout appears crucial for stable convergence and high test accuracy (0.88 in the best program).  
- Restricting to fixed two-qubit CZ gates rather than denser or parameterized two-qubit layers maintains parameter efficiency and reduces overfitting, as evidenced by the current best outperforming the CRY-heavy butterfly ansatz (Gen 2).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Dense ring-CZ entanglement (Gen 1 “Ring-CZ layered,” score 0.78, gap 0.10, depth 120, gate 276) increased circuit depth and parameters without boosting accuracy.  
- Custom butterfly mixing with additional CNOT layers and shared CRY (Gen 2 “Reuploading Butterfly,” score 0.85 train/test 0.92/0.85) plateaued below the 0.90 validation target and yielded lower parameter efficiency (0.01).  
- Closed-ring CZ-RY layering (Gen 5, score 0.77, gap 0.11, depth 120) over-parameterized the ansatz, degrading generalization and test accuracy to 0.81.  
- Simply reducing parameter count (e.g., Gen 2’s 134 vs. 146) did not guarantee better performance; targeted structural simplification (linear vs. ring entanglers) was more effective.

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining the ansatz via a reusable ANSATZ_SPEC and indexing parameters per block (using `key_to_position` and block offsets) streamlines experimentation with different gate sequences.  
- The fixed feature map using qml.IsingZZ(FEATURE_SCALE·x, wires=[a,b]) across 28 feature pairs succinctly embeds binary inputs without adding trainable overhead.  
- Alternation of pre- and post-entangling RZ rotations around a linear CZ chain (as in the best program) yields deep expressivity with fewer two-qubit gates than ring or butterfly patterns.  
- Initializing gain to 1 and bias to 0, then optimizing alongside circuit parameters, consistently improves sign-based classification accuracy across multiple runs.

## Performance Analysis  
- Transition from the Gen 0 RY/RZ-CZ ansatz (score 0.85, test 0.87) to the Gen 4 reuploading variational ansatz (score 0.86, test 0.88) demonstrates a measurable gain (≈1 ppt in combined score) without increasing depth or gate count.  
- Programs with higher depth (> 120) uniformly underperform despite similar parameter counts (146), confirming a negative correlation between depth and generalization when entanglement is over-dense (Gen 1 & Gen 5).  
- The smallest generalization gap (0.06) and highest test accuracy (0.88) coincide in the current best program, underscoring the effectiveness of linear-chain CZ entanglement plus re-uploads over more complex mixers.  
- Parameter efficiency (test accuracy / n_params) remains around 0.01 across all evaluated 146-parameter ansätze; breaking this plateau appears to require structural, not just parametric, innovations.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace each single “RY” pre‐rotation in ANSATZ_SPEC with a full SU(2) Euler block (RZ–RY–RZ):  
   – In your EVOLVE‐BLOCK, wrap each `{"gate":"RY","wire":i}` in a trio  
     `{"gate":"RZ","param":"rz_pre2_i"}`, `{"gate":"RY","param":"ry_i"}`,  
     `{"gate":"RZ","param":"rz_pre3_i"}`.  
   – Remove the original single RZ pre‐layer (or drop an RY post‐layer on half the qubits) so total parameters stay ≈146.  
   Evidence: expanding local SU(2) expressivity around entanglers may boost performance without adding extra two‐qubit gates.  

2. Swap the upload/repeat schedule to N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3 (total blocks =6):  
   – In the program header set `N_UPLOADS=2; N_REPEATS=3` and leave ANSATZ_SPEC unchanged.  
   – This preserves depth and gate count but concentrates more ansatz depth per feature upload.  
   Evidence: three re‐uploads + two repeats gave 0.86; testing two re‐uploads with three repeats explores whether deeper variational mixing per upload further improves the combined score.  

3. Alternate the linear CZ chain direction between repeats:  
   – Programmatically modify ANSATZ_SPEC for blocks with even `block_index`: replace CZ wires `[(0,1),(1,2),…,(6,7)]` with the reversed chain `[(7,6),(6,5),…,(1,0)]`.  
   – Keep the same number of CZ gates and parameters.  
   Evidence: preserving linear‐chain sparsity while varying entanglement direction diversifies mixing patterns, potentially increasing expressivity without over‐parameterizing.  

4. Upgrade scalar output calibration to per‐qubit gains and biases:  
   – Replace the two readout parameters with `gain_i, bias_i` for each qubit (total +16 params).  
   – Compute `raw_i = gain_i*(⟨Z_i⟩ – bias_i)`, then average `mean_i(raw_i)` for prediction.  
   – Initialize all gains to 1 and biases to 0.  
   Evidence: trainable calibration was critical (gain+bias); a per‐qubit extension may further reduce bias and tighten the generalization gap.  

5. Introduce two sparse next‐nearest‐neighbor CZ links after the main chain:  
   – In ANSATZ_SPEC, append `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,2]}` and `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[5,7]}` immediately after the existing 7‐edge chain.  
   – This adds only 2 gates (depth +1–2) but captures long‐range correlations.  
   Evidence: maintaining CZ‐only entanglement was key; adding minimal non‐local links tests if a slight increase in connectivity can boost test accuracy without overfitting.
