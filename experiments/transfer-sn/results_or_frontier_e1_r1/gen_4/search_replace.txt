An excellent way to improve both the classification accuracy and the parameter efficiency of our variational circuit is to optimize the ansatz block architecture.

The original block uses a suboptimal sequence: an `RY` layer, an `RZ` layer, a linear `CZ` chain, and a final `RZ` layer. Because `CZ` commutes with `RZ` and the preceding feature map `IsingZZ` also acts in the Z-basis, some of these rotations are redundant or fail to generate optimal entanglement efficiently.

Instead, we can use a structurally stronger and more efficient entangling architecture:
1. **`RZ` layer**: Adjusts the local Z-phase, effectively adding a trainable bias to the Z-basis features uploaded by the `IsingZZ` feature map.
2. **`RY` layer**: Tilts the basis away from Z, ensuring that the feature information is rotated into superpositions and doesn't commute trivially with the entangling layer.
3. **Staggered `CNOT` ring**: Two parallel layers of `CNOT` gates (even pairs, then odd pairs with periodic boundaries) provide full all-to-all connectivity in a ring topology. This is significantly more expressive than a single non-periodic line of `CZ` gates.

This configuration cuts the block's parameters down from 24 to 16. Over 6 blocks, this drastically reduces the parameter count (146 -> 98 parameters overall), yielding a steep improvement in parameter efficiency while simultaneously expanding the expressivity and dynamic capability of the quantum model.
