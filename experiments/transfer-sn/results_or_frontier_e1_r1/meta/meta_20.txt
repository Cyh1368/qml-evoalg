# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Interleaved re-uploads with shared‐parameter ansatz repeats  
  • All top performers—including the current best “8-Qubit Perfect-Matching Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 18, combined_score_raw 0.94) and the “8-Qubit Evolved Ansatz Classifier” (Gen 17, 0.93)—use N_UPLOADS = 3 × N_REPEATS = 2 with one flat parameter block shared across uploads. This layout suffices for nonlinearity without inflating parameter count.  
- Perfect-matching CZ layers for full graph entanglement  
  • The seed program hard-codes 28 CZ pairs in two half-matchings per block, achieving complete K₈ connectivity in depth 123 and gate_count 444. It outperforms sparser entanglers: the Reuploading Cube Ansatz (Gen 15, raw 0.87, depth 93, gates 300) and CNOT rings (raw ≤ 0.66).  
- Two-axis collective decoding (RY + RX)  
  • Adding collective RX after the final RY layer boosts expressivity. The Perfect-Matching ansatz’s decode layers (“c_ry2” + “c_rx2”) lift combined score to 0.94, matching improvements seen in Gen 9’s Collective Graph Classifier (+0.01–0.02 raw for +6 depth/+48 gates).  
- Parameter collapsing via collective rotations  
  • Grouping eight qubit rotations under four scalar keys (c_ry1, c_rx1, c_ry2, c_rx2) yields just 26 variational angles (plus gain/bias) yet matches the 0.94 accuracy of denser 32-parameter designs (Gen 17), maintaining a strong parameter efficiency of 0.04.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Sparse entanglement topologies  
  • The 3D Cube ansatz (Gen 15) with only 12 CZ gates per block scores 0.87 and generalizes to 0.89 test accuracy—~0.05 lower than perfect-matching designs—demonstrating that minimal connectivity underfits the eight-qubit correlations.  
- Over-parameterized collective offsets  
  • The “Collective-phase Evolved” model (Gen 19) uses 68 parameters with full K₈ CZ in each block, yet achieves only 0.89 combined_score_raw and a 0.04 gap, yielding a low parameter efficiency of 0.01. Depth 135/gates 540 deliver diminishing returns.  
- Single-axis decoders underfit  
  • Omitting the RX decode (e.g., Gen 12’s Collective CRZ Entanglement Ansatz) yields raw 0.92—~0.02 below two-axis variants—indicating that RY-only decoding limits decision–boundary flexibility.  
- Extreme parameter tying or untying  
  • Excessive sharing (one angle for all rotations, Gen 10) drops raw to 0.79, while fully unshared, high-param circuits (>98 parameters) overfit (gaps ≥ 0.10) and converge slowly (>500 steps).  

## Implementation Insights

- Flat parameter indexing with key_to_position  
  • The seed program builds a map from param‐key to flat vector index (`offset = block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK; key_to_position = {…}`), allowing arbitrary N_UPLOADS×N_REPEATS without rewriting loops.  
- Hard-coded perfect-matching lists  
  • Encoding the two halves of the complete graph as fixed CZ‐wire lists in ANSATZ_SPEC avoids runtime graph searches and guarantees seven parallel CZ layers per half-matching, directly yielding full connectivity at minimal depth.  
- AnnotatedQueue for metadata-driven tuning  
  • Using `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` in `circuit_metadata()` extracts exact depth (123) and gate_count (444), informing trade-offs when considering additional decode axes or entangling layers.  
- Collective rotations via repeated spec entries  
  • Listing eight identical RY/RX entries keyed to “c_ry1”, “c_rx1”, etc., collapses eight potential parameters into one, slashing the optimizer’s dimension by >80% while preserving circuit behavior.  

## Performance Analysis

- Accuracy vs. connectivity trade-off  
  • As entanglement increases—Cube (12 CZ, raw 0.87) → Layered CZ-Rotation (full pairwise CZ but no two‐axis decode, raw 0.90) → Evolved (full CZ+ two‐axis decode, raw 0.93) → Perfect-Matching (full CZ+two‐axis+collective, raw 0.94)—test accuracy climbs in lock-step with graph density and decode richness.  
- Parameter count sweet spot around 20–26  
  • Circuits with ~20 params (CubeMixer) hit ≤ 0.87, while 26 params (Perfect-Matching) reach 0.94. Beyond ~26 parameters (32 in Evolved, 68 in Collective-phase) yields marginal or negative gains, highlighting a diminishing-returns region.  
- Depth/gates vs. raw score  
  • Increasing depth from 93 to 123 and gates from 300 to 444 buys +0.07 raw score; beyond that (depth 135/gates 540) raw dips to 0.89, showing optimal depth sits near 120 for this task.  
- Stable generalization with shared-parameter & early stopping  
  • Top models (Evolved, Perfect-Matching) converge in ~150–270 Adam steps, exhibit gaps ≤ 0.02, and maintain test accuracy below validation variance (std 0.00), confirming that shared-parameter layouts plus restore-best-weights effectively prevent overfitting.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce per-qubit offsets on the final RY decode. Extend your parameter vector by eight new keys `delta_ry_0…7`, and after the shared `c_ry2` entries in `ANSATZ_SPEC` insert individual RY gates on wires 0–7 keyed to `delta_ry_i`. This preserves the strength of collective decoding while giving each qubit light local tuning (only +8 parameters), building on the success of minimal gain/bias calibration.  

2. Inject a shared CRZ into the perfect-matching entangler. In your second half-matching block, replace every other CZ gate (e.g. gates at positions 18, 20, 22, 24, 26) with `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[…],"param":"shared_crz"}`, and add `"shared_crz"` to `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`. This adds one two-qubit phase parameter per block, leveraging the low-overhead expressivity gains of CRZ without deepening the circuit.  

3. Expand to three-axis collective decoding. After your existing RX decode layer in `ANSATZ_SPEC`, append eight RZ gates keyed to `"c_rz2"` and include `"c_rz2"` in your block keys. Given that adding a collective RX after RY lifted +0.01–0.02 accuracy, a third axis may similarly boost expressivity with just +8 gates and +1 parameter.  

4. Interleave rotations and CZ layers within each block. Rather than grouping all RY→CZ→RX→CZ→RY→RX, split each perfect-matching into two sub-matchings (14 CZs each) and apply them as: c_ry1 → first sub-matching → c_rx1 → second sub-matching → c_ry2 → c_rx2. Such alternating rotation–entangler patterns have shown better expressivity per depth in layered ansätze.  

5. Trade per-block entangler depth for more repeats. Redefine each ansatz block to include only one half-matching (14 CZs) and increase `N_REPEATS` from 2 to 3 (keeping `N_UPLOADS=3`). This spreads full K₈ connectivity across three blocks, testing whether multi-block connectivity can match the 0.94 score at a lower per-block depth (≈93) and similar gate count.
