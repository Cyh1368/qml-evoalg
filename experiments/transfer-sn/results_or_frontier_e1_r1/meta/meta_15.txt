# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Two-axis collective decoder boosts expressivity with minimal extra resources  
  • The Collective Graph Quantum Classifier (Gen 9) added collective_RX rotations after the CZ entanglers—on top of collective_ry/rz prep—and lifted its raw combined score from 0.93 (Symmetric Collective CZ, Gen 11) to 0.94, with test acc 0.95 and zero gap, at the cost of only +6 depth and +48 gates.  
- Interleaved re-uploads and shared ansatz repeats reliably reach >0.90 accuracy  
  • All top performers (Gen 9, Gen 11, Gen 12, Gen 14) use N_UPLOADS = 3 data-reuploads × N_REPEATS = 2 shared-parameter blocks, delivering raw combined ≥ 0.92 with 20–26 parameters, confirming this layout suffices for nonlinearity without per-upload parameter blow-up.  
- Complete-graph CZ via perfect-match layers yields high connectivity per depth  
  • Each high-scoring model employs seven perfect-matching CZ layers (28 CZ gates per block), achieving full 8-qubit entanglement in depth ≈ 117–123 and outperforming sparser rings (ring_cross_cnot_three_axis raw 0.66).  
- Minimal gain/bias calibration on mean-Z stabilizes training  
  • Every top model appends two readout parameters (gain, bias) to mean-Z outputs, achieving stable convergence (≈ 150–300 Adam steps) and zero to near-zero generalization gaps, versus >500 steps and gaps ≥ 0.10 in high-parameter unfactored designs.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Single-axis decoding limits boundary flexibility  
  • The Collective CRZ Entanglement Ansatz (Gen 12) omitted RX decoding and scored raw 0.92 (test acc ≈ 0.94), ~0.02 below the full two-axis design, indicating RY-only decode underfits decision surfaces.  
- Over-sharing across all rotations undermines expressivity  
  • The single-angle reupload classifier (Gen 10) used one “collective_angle” for every RX/RY/RZ across 6 blocks (8 params total) and achieved only raw 0.79 (test acc 0.82), showing excessive parameter tying can bottleneck learning despite perfect generalization.  
- Unshared, high-param layering overfits and slows convergence  
  • Models with 98–146 unique rotation parameters (no sharing) peaked at raw 0.78 and required >500 optimizer steps, with generalization gaps ≥ 0.10, demonstrating the inefficiency of per-qubit parameterization at scale.  
- Sparse or CNOT-only entanglers fail to capture correlations  
  • Ring_cross_cnot_three_axis (raw 0.66) and pure IsingZZ-only feature maps (raw ≤ 0.59) lack the connectivity to model essential multi-qubit interactions, cementing CZ-based dense layouts as the superior choice.

## Implementation Insights

- Flat parameter indexing via block-offset and key-position maps  
  • All candidates map ANSATZ_SPEC params into a flat array using `offset = block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` and `key_to_position`, simplifying loop-based gate application and enabling arbitrary block counts without rewriting indexing logic.  
- AnnotatedQueue profiling guides depth/gate trade-offs  
  • The `circuit_metadata()` helper wraps feature_map and apply_ansatz_block in a `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue`, yielding exact depth (123) and gate_count (444) for Gen 9, which informed the decision to accept a +6 depth cost for the two-axis decoder.  
- Explicit perfect-matching CZ lists enforce consistent layout  
  • Hard-coding the 28 CZ wire pairs in ANSATZ_SPEC—for example, [7,0], [1,6], [2,5]…[2,3]—ensures every qubit interacts once per layer, reproducing full-graph connectivity in exactly seven parallel layers, avoiding runtime graph searches.  
- Collective rotations by repeating per-qubit entries keyed to one param  
  • Listing eight identical RY entries all referencing `"collective_ry"` collapses eight potential parameters into one scalar per block, cutting optimizer dimensionality by >80% without altering circuit behavior.

## Performance Analysis

- Marginal returns beyond ~20 parameters  
  • Symmetric Collective CZ and Collective Mixer (Gen 11 & Gen 14) both use 20 params and hit raw 0.93 with param_efficiency ~0.05, while Gen 9’s 26 params only edge raw to 0.94 but drop efficiency to 0.04, indicating a diminishing-returns region beyond ~20 trainable angles.  
- Depth/gate increases pay off for fine-tuned decode axes  
  • Adding the second decode axis in Gen 9 costs ~5% more gates (396→444) and depth (117→123) yet gains ~0.01–0.02 raw score over single-axis variants, quantifying the resource versus accuracy trade-off.  
- Shared-parameter models converge faster with smaller gaps  
  • Compact, shared‐parameter circuits (20–26 params) consistently converged by ~270 steps (Gen 9 convergence_step_mean 270) with gaps ≤ 0.02, compared to >500 steps and gaps ≥ 0.07 in fully unshared, high-param ansätze.  
- CZ perfect-matching layout trumps CNOT rings despite slightly higher depth  
  • The full-graph CZ approach requires ~120 depth versus ~93 for ring_CNOT, yet yields >0.90 test accuracy gains of +0.20–0.30, underscoring the importance of connectivity over minimal depth.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce global per-qubit offset parameters to each shared rotation.  
   – Define eight new parameters `offset_0`…`offset_7` (outside the block‐level keys) and prepend them to your full parameter vector. In `apply_ansatz_block`, whenever you compute a rotation angle `θ = params[offset + key_to_position[param_name]]`, instead use `θ + params[N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS + i]` for wire i. This hybrid shared + local scheme preserves the low-dimensional “collective” manifold while giving each qubit light individual tuning, with only eight extra parameters.

2. Inject a tunable phase interaction by swapping every other CZ for a shared-CRZ per block.  
   – In your `ANSATZ_SPEC`, replace the CZ entries at odd indices with `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[…],"param":"shared_crz"}` and add `"shared_crz"` to `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`. This adds just one extra parameter per block but lets the circuit learn subtle two-qubit phase shifts without changing depth or gate count significantly.

3. Append a trainable collective readout layer before measurement.  
   – After the final `collective_decode_rx` entries in `ANSATZ_SPEC`, insert eight RY gates keyed to `readout_ry` and eight RX gates keyed to `readout_rx` (both shared across wires), then add `"readout_ry"` and `"readout_rx"` to your global parameter list. This two-parameter adaptive measurement‐basis step builds on the success of minimal gain/bias calibration and can yield tighter decision boundaries.

4. Interleave rotations and entanglers within each ansatz block.  
   – Instead of grouping all three collective rotations then all CZs, restructure each block as:  
     a) collective_RX on all qubits  
     b) first perfect-matching CZ layer  
     c) collective_RZ  
     d) second CZ layer  
     e) collective_RY  
     f) remaining five CZ layers  
   – Keep the same three shared parameters per block. Alternating rotations with entanglers has proven to boost expressivity per unit depth in layered circuits.

5. Trade per-block depth for more repeats with sparser entanglement.  
   – Redefine each block’s entangler to use only three perfect-matching CZ layers (e.g. matchings [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7]; [0,3],[1,2],[4,7],[5,6]; [0,5],[1,4],[2,7],[3,6]), then raise `N_REPEATS` from 2 to 3 (keeping `N_UPLOADS=3`). This halves the CZ depth per block and spreads full-graph connectivity across more blocks, testing whether connectivity over repeats can match accuracy with a lower overall depth footprint.
