# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three re-uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  • Present in the current best (“8-qubit Re-uploading Variational Classifier,” combined_score_raw 0.88) and earlier Gen 10/13 models (scores 0.87/0.86).  
  • Balances representation depth with controlled overfitting (generalization gaps 0.03–0.06).  
- Combined ring + cross-pair CZ entanglement  
  • The Gen 15 model adds both an 8-qubit CZ ring and “long-range” cross links (0–4, 1–5, 2–6, 3–7), pushing raw score to 0.88 (vs. 0.87 for simple ring in Gen 10/5).  
  • Enhances expressivity without dramatically increasing gate count beyond 300.  
- Paired single-qubit parameter sharing  
  • Grouping RY/RX/RZ on qubit pairs (01, 23, 45, 67) preserved high train/validation/test accuracy (94%/90%/90%) while limiting per-block parameters to 12.  
  • Consistent with parity-tied ring block (Gen 5) achieving 0.87 with only 38 parameters.  
- Two-parameter readout calibration  
  • Trainable gain and bias layers sharpen decision boundaries across all top performers (Gen 5, 10, 13, and 15).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Fully independent per-qubit rotations and entanglement (no sharing)  
  • Gen 14’s mirrored shared-parameter ansatz used 38 parameters but depth 126, gate count 300, yielding only combined_score_raw 0.80.  
  • Suffered convergence issues and offered no accuracy gain over shared-parameter designs.  
- Over-parameterized CRZ/CZ mixtures  
  • Models like Gen 7/8 employed 140+ parameters with depth ≥111 but test accuracies stagnated at ≤0.85 and generalization gaps of 0.10–0.12.  
  • Excessive two-qubit layers without structured sharing harmed both parameter efficiency and convergence speed.  
- Excessive group sharing → under-expressivity  
  • Gen 11’s four-group-shared ansatz halved parameters to 98 but raw score dropped to 0.85 (test 0.83), indicating too coarse sharing can degrade expressivity.  

## Implementation Insights  
- ANSATZ_SPEC + key-to-position lookup  
  • Defining each gate in a dict list and mapping “param” keys to indices makes it trivial to swap out entanglement patterns (used in Gen 15).  
- AnnotatedQueue for circuit metrics  
  • Extracts depth (126) and gate_count (300) systematically, enabling direct correlation between circuit complexity and accuracy (seen in all evaluations).  
- Modular feature_map + apply_ansatz_block  
  • Separates fixed IsingZZ uploads from trainable blocks; reusing feature_map(records) three times ensures consistent encoding.  
- Autograd interface with pnp arrays  
  • Using `pnp.array(..., requires_grad=True)` and `qml.AdamOptimizer` yields stable training and precise early stopping across varied ansatz specs.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Raw score progression  
  • Gen 5 (parity_tied_ring): 0.87, 38 params, depth 96  
  • Gen 10 (mirrored CZ reupload): 0.87, 74 params, depth 81  
  • Gen 15 (ring + cross CZ): 0.88, 74 params, depth 126  
- Trade-offs: depth vs. accuracy  
  • Increasing depth from 96→126 (Gen 5→15) yielded +0.01 raw score at the cost of lower parameter efficiency (0.023→0.012).  
- Generalization gap stability  
  • Best performers kept gaps ≤0.06; Gen 13 achieved the smallest (0.03) with only 38 params, while Gen 15 maintained 0.04 with richer entanglement.  
- Parameter efficiency vs. expressivity  
  • Highest efficiency in Gen 5 (0.023), but peak accuracy in Gen 15 (0.88); optimal designs balance sharing (38–74 params) with structured two-qubit layers.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a shared‐parameter CRZ layer on the ring edges.  
   After your fixed CZ ring in each ANSATZ_SPEC block, append eight CRZ gates on the same ring wires (0–1, 1–2, …, 7–0), all keyed by a single parameter `"crz_ring"`. Gen 2/3 showed that adding one shared parametric entangling layer yields a noticeable expressivity boost with just one extra parameter per block—and by keeping it shared you cap overfitting risk.

2. Add a shared‐parameter CRX layer on the long-range cross links.  
   Following your fixed CZ cross-pair couplings (0–4, 1–5, 2–6, 3–7), insert four CRX gates keyed by `"crx_cross"`. This mirrors the ring+cross CZ structure that edged Gen 15 to 0.88 raw score but makes the long-range links trainable—enhancing flexibility with only one more parameter per block, as parity-tied CRX improved convergence in Gen 4.

3. Share ansatz parameters across repeats within each upload.  
   Instead of unique `ry_*`, `rx_*`, `rz_*` for all 6 blocks, reuse block parameters between the first and third repeats, and between the second and fourth—for example, blocks 0 & 2 share `ry_01`, blocks 1 & 3 share `ry_23`, etc. Mirrored-block sharing in Gen 10 maintained 0.87 accuracy with half the unique params, suggesting this could regularize deeper 3×2 layouts without sacrificing performance.

4. Swap to 2 re-uploads × 3 ansatz repeats.  
   In your constants set `N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3` (leave ANSATZ_SPEC unchanged) to trade one data re-upload for an extra ansatz pass. Gen 5’s 2×3 configuration converged faster (≈270 steps) and retained a small generalization gap; increasing repeats may deepen expressivity under the same gate budget (≈300 gates).

5. Split readout calibration by parity group.  
   Replace the single `gain` with `gain_even` and `gain_odd` (one bias), then compute raw_even = mean〈Z〉 on wires 0,2,4,6 and raw_odd on 1,3,5,7, and output `gain_even*raw_even + gain_odd*raw_odd - bias`. Top models show two‐param readout sharpens boundaries; per-parity gains add just one extra param but can correct systematic shifts in even vs. odd qubit signals and shrink the generalization gap.
