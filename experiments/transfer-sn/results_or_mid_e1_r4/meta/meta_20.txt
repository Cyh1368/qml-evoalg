# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Even/Odd & Parity Parameter Sharing  
  • Shared single-qubit rotations on even/odd qubit pairs (ry_even/ry_odd, rx_even/rx_odd, rz_even/rz_odd) together with a single “crx_parity” across qubit‐pair CRX gates (Gen 18 “Parity-Enriched…”: combined_score_raw 0.92).  
  • Matches Gen 12’s interleaved CRZ shared-parameter approach (score 0.91) but with CRX adds expressivity for another +0.01.  
- Re-Uploading Feature Map with Two Ansatz Repeats  
  • Three re-uploads of the IsingZZ feature map, each followed by two ansatz blocks, stabilizes encoding depth and drove high accuracy in Gen 12, 16, 18 (all ≥0.89 test).  
  • Current best (Gen 18) converged in ~270 steps with a 0.03 generalization gap.  
- Combined Ring CZ + Controlled Rotations  
  • A full ring of CZ entanglers plus parity-tied CRX mixers (Gen 18) outperformed simple rings (Gen 10 score 0.87) and rings+cross links (Gen 15 score 0.88) without raising gate count beyond 300.  
- Readout Gain & Bias Calibration  
  • Two trainable readout parameters consistently sharpen boundaries—present in all top performers (Gen 5, 12, 15, 18)—and correlate with low generalization gaps (≤0.04).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Fully Independent Per-Qubit Parameters  
  • Gen 17’s symmetry-preserving block used 44 unique parameters but no sharing, yielding only 0.80 combined score with depth 132/gate 324 and stall in test accuracy (0.81).  
- Over-Parameterized Two-Qubit Layers  
  • Models with 140+ parameters and mixed CZ/CRZ/CRX (Gen 7/8) stagnated at ≤0.85 raw and suffered generalization gaps ≥0.10.  
- Coarse Group Sharing → Under-Expressivity  
  • Four-group shared ansatz (Gen 11) halved parameters to 98 but dropped raw to 0.85 (test 0.83), indicating too coarse sharing loses critical degrees of freedom.  
- Excessive Circuit Depth/Gate Count  
  • Gen 19’s 60-parameter, depth 132/gate 324 design achieved only 0.86 combined, showing that adding layers without targeted sharing or new entanglement patterns lacks payoff.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Modular Ansatz Specification  
  • ANSATZ_SPEC as a list of dicts + key-to-position mapping allows rapid swaps of entanglement (CZ ring vs. cross-links vs. parity CRX) as in Gen 15 vs. Gen 18.  
- Parameter-Sharing via Naming Conventions  
  • Defining “ry_even,” “ry_odd,” and “crx_parity” in ANSATZ_SPEC enforces tied weights automatically across multiple wires and blocks.  
- Separate feature_map & apply_ansatz_block Functions  
  • Clear separation of fixed IsingZZ encoding and trainable layers simplifies re-upload loops and supports consistent depth/gate count across candidates.  
- Automated Depth/Gate Metrics  
  • Using qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue with QuantumScript.from_queue yields programmatic circuit depth and gate_count—critical for correlating complexity with performance.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Top Raw Score vs. Parameter Count  
  • Gen 18 achieved the highest combined_score_raw 0.92 with 44 circuit parameters (0.02 efficiency), outperforming Gen 15’s 0.88 at 74 params.  
- Generalization Gap Consistency  
  • Best models (Gen 12, 15, 18) maintain gaps ≤0.04; Gen 18’s 0.03 gap ties for lowest while reaching peak test accuracy (0.93).  
- Depth/Gate Trade-off  
  • Increasing depth from 96 (Gen 5, raw 0.87) →126 (Gen 15, raw 0.88) →126 (Gen 18, raw 0.92) shows that targeted entanglement (parity CRX + ring CZ) yields better score per added layer than brute-force depth.  
- Convergence Speed  
  • Stable convergence in ~270 steps under Adam+early-stopping for all re-uploaded/shared-param designs, indicating reliable training dynamics when sharing and modularization are applied.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. In each ANSATZ_SPEC block, append a shared‐parameter CRZ layer immediately after the CZ ring: add eight entries  
   `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,i+1],"param":"crz_ring"}` for i∈{0–6} and `[7,0]`.  
   This complements the CZ entanglers with a single-phase parameter per block—Gen 2/3 showed that a shared CRZ on ring edges boosts expressivity by ≈+0.01 without overfitting.

2. Extend long-range connectivity by inserting parity-shared CRX cross-links: after the existing parity CRX mixers, add four entries  
   `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[0,4],"param":"crx_cross"}`, `[1,5]`, `[2,6]`, `[3,7]`.  
   Mirroring Gen 15’s ring+cross design but with one shared “crx_cross” weight controls overfitting while enriching global mixing.

3. Enforce parameter reuse across uploads by collapsing the upload_index offset in apply_ansatz_block:  
   compute block_offset = (repeat_index) × N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK, ignoring upload_index, so that the same two repeats share parameters in all three uploads.  
   This cuts total circuit parameters by ⅓, regularizes per-upload transformations, and retains the 3×2 depth/gate count.

4. Make the feature-map scale trainable per upload: replace the global FEATURE_SCALE with a vector of trainable keys  
   `["scale_0","scale_1","scale_2"]` and in feature_map use `qml.IsingZZ(scale_i*records[...,k], wires=[a,b])` for upload_i.  
   This endows the encoding stage with adaptive rotation magnitudes per re-upload—theoretically reducing embedding mismatch and tightening generalization.

5. Refine readout calibration with parity-split gains: replace single `gain` with `gain_even` and `gain_odd` (keeping one shared `bias`), compute  
   `raw_even = mean(Z on 0,2,4,6)`, `raw_odd = mean(Z on 1,3,5,7)`, and output `gain_even*raw_even + gain_odd*raw_odd - bias`.  
   Top models show that two‐parameter readout shrinks generalization gaps; splitting gains by parity adds just one extra weight and corrects systematic group shifts.
