# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Shared-parameter CRZ entanglers and grouped single-qubit layers (ry_even/ry_odd, rx_group_0/1, rz_final) in Generation 4 yielded the best combined_score_raw (0.86) with just 56 parameters.  
- Retaining the three re-upload feature-map structure and two ansatz repeats ensured expressive power while reducing overfitting, as demonstrated by a modest generalization gap (0.06) and rapid convergence (step 60).  
- Parameter sharing across qubit pairs preserved entangling capability of four CRZ gates on fixed pairs (0-1,2-3,4-5,6-7) but halved the circuit parameters compared to fully independent rotations.  
- Early stopping with best-weight restoration prevented overtraining in the shared-CRZ model, matching high train accuracy (0.93) while boosting test accuracy (0.88).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Deep linear CZ-chain ansatz (Gen 0) reached high training fit (0.94) but at 146 parameters and 99-depth, yielding low parameter efficiency (0.01) and a 0.07 gap.  
- Dual-entanglement sandwich (circular+star CNOT, Gen 1) produced a large generalization gap (0.15) and poor combined_score_raw (0.68), indicating excessive depth (126) and gate count (300) harmed validation performance.  
- Over-parameterized RY/RZ alternation (Gen 2) and symmetric RX/RY/RZ mixers (Gen 3) both delivered only modest test accuracies (0.86 and 0.84) with low efficiency (0.01) or negative combined_score_raw (−0.69), showing that more parameters did not translate to better generalization.  
- Ansätze that failed to converge within allotted epochs (Gen 3) underscore the cost of insufficient parameter sharing and overly complex block designs.

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC with shared parameter keys reduces N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK from ≈70 to 14, directly cutting total circuit parameters (56 vs. 140–146) while preserving expressivity via CRZ couplings.  
- Using pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue to extract depth (81) and gate_count (252) confirms that moderate circuit size correlates with strong performance—smaller than Gen 0’s 270-gate depth 99.  
- The fixed two-qubit IsingZZ feature map re-uploaded three times maintains data encoding fidelity; coupling it with a compact, repeatable block boosts representation without ballooning gate count.  
- Initializing gain = 1 and bias = 0, combined with L2 loss and Adam optimizer, provided stable calibration of readout, contributing to consistent train/validation/test accuracies.

## Performance Analysis  
- Generation 4’s combined_score_raw of 0.86 edges out Gen 0’s 0.85 despite a 62% parameter reduction (56 vs. 146), doubling parameter_efficiency from 0.01 to 0.02.  
- Lower depth (81 vs. 99–126) and gate count (252 vs. 270–300) in Gen 4 maps to faster convergence (60 vs. 750 steps in Gen 0) without sacrificing accuracy.  
- Generalization gaps shrink from 0.07–0.15 in earlier models to 0.06 in the shared-CRZ design, indicating better bias-variance balance.  
- Test accuracies follow the trend: Gen 4 (0.88) > Gen 0 (0.87) > Gen 2 (0.86) > Gen 1 (0.73) > Gen 3 (0.84), aligning highest scores with shared entangling and parameter-efficient layouts.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a second shared two-qubit rotation (CRX) alongside the existing CRZ layer. For example, extend each block in ANSATZ_SPEC by inserting  
   `{"gate": "CRX", "wires": [0,1], "param": "crx_shared"}, …` for pairs (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7) with a single “crx_shared” key.  
   Evidence: Gen 4’s CRZ sharing halved parameters while preserving entanglement; adding one more shared gate type per block gives extra expressivity with only one extra parameter per block.  

2. Alternate entangling topology by defining two shared-parameter CRZ patterns—original adjacent pairs and “shifted” pairs—and interleave them across repeats.  
   In ANSATZ_SPEC, use param keys `crz_adj` for pairs (0–1,2–3,…) in repeat 1 and `crz_shift` for pairs (1–2,3–4,…7–0) in repeat 2, then loop.  
   Evidence: Capturing both local and next-neighbor couplings can boost representation; shared keys keep N_PARAMS_per_block minimal.  

3. Test a four-group single-qubit rotation scheme instead of even/odd and group0/1.  
   Redefine RY params as `ry_01`, `ry_23`, `ry_45`, `ry_67` applied to qubits [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7], and similarly `rx_01`–`rx_67`.  
   Evidence: Gen 4’s two-group sharing improved bias–variance; a slightly finer grouping could better balance expressivity and parameter efficiency.  

4. Swap the upload/repeat ratio to deepen the ansatz while maintaining similar total blocks: set N_UPLOADS = 2 and N_REPEATS = 3.  
   Keep the shared-parameter ANSATZ_SPEC unchanged but adjust the constants at the top of the program.  
   Evidence: Gen 4’s three reuploads × two repeats converged in 60 steps; fewer reuploads with more repeats may enrich circuit depth without overfitting if sharing persists.  

5. Augment the fixed IsingZZ feature map with a small shared single-qubit phase rotation per feature.  
   In `feature_map`, after each `qml.IsingZZ`, add `qml.RZ(FEATURE_SCALE*records[...,k], wires=a, param="fm_phase")` (or a shared α·feature+β), registering a single new `fm_phase` key.  
   Evidence: Preserves the proven three-reupload IsingZZ encoding while introducing a trainable twist per feature at minimal parameter cost.
