The current program has N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2 (3×2 schedule), achieving a combined score of 0.91 with 20 parameters, 162 depth, and 180 convergence steps.

The prior programs used the same 3×2 schedule but achieved higher test accuracy (0.93 vs 0.91) with 26 parameters and deeper circuits (196 depth). The key difference was richer entanglement structures in their ANSATZ_SPEC (both had 4 distinct parameters vs. current 3).

However, the recommendation to test a 2×3 rebalancing (N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3) is motivated by:

1. **Deeper intra-upload entanglement**: With 3 repeats per upload instead of 2, the circuit applies more variational blocks between feature maps, potentially allowing richer feature extraction and stronger per-upload expressivity.

2. **Same total depth and parameter count**: Both 3×2 and 2×3 yield 6 variational blocks total (N_UPLOADS × N_REPEATS = 6), so N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS and total gate count remain the same. This is a pure architectural trade-off.

3. **Faster feature adaptation**: Fewer uploads (2 instead of 3) means the feature map is applied less frequently. However, with deeper ansatz blocks between uploads (3 repeats), the circuit has more capacity to transform and amplify feature information locally before the next upload, potentially improving convergence speed (convergence_step_mean is currently 180; deeper per-upload blocks might reach convergence faster by better utilizing the ansatz depth).

4. **Probing the upload-depth trade-off**: Prior high-performing candidates stayed at 3×2, but they had richer parameter structures. The current 3×2 is more compact (20 params). Testing 2×3 explores whether shifting the balance toward deeper per-upload processing can compensate for fewer total uploads and match or exceed the 0.93 test accuracy of prior runs.

This change directly tests the hypothesis that convergence and final accuracy depend not just on total blocks but on their distribution relative to feature map uploads.
