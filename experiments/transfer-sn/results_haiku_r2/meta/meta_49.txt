# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Zero-cost entanglement enrichment via a single shared CRY parameter for both next-nearest-neighbor and nearest-neighbor couplings.  
  • Generation 47’s “nearest_neighbor_cry_enrichment” patch (8-Qubit ShinkaEvolve Ansatz Classifier) reused one “cry_nnn” key for eight NNN and eight NN CRY gates. That boost lifted its combined score to 0.92 (vs. 0.90 in Gen 49 with separate CRY layers) without increasing N_PARAMS.  
- Four-key, role-based shared-parameter blocks interleaved with fixed CZ ring + cross-ring layers.  
  • Gen 47, Gen 49 (0.90 combined), and Gen 51 (0.90 combined) all employ (“ry_init”, “rz_main”, “cry_nnn”, “ry_final”) per block, 3 re-uploads ×2 repeats, delivering test ≈0.91–0.93 and gap ≤0.03.  
- Three feature-map re-uploads × two ansatz repeats remains essential for mapping 28-bit inputs.  
  • All top performers (Gen 42, Gen 45, Gen 46 earlier and current Gen 47 at 0.92) use this schedule consistently.  
- Moderate training length (~240 global steps) correlates with peak performance.  
  • Gen 47 converged at ~240 steps to 0.92 combined, outperforming shorter runs (~150 steps in Gen 49, combined 0.90) and prolonged ones (~510 steps in Gen 51, combined 0.90).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization without expressivity gains: Models with >38 parameters underperform.  
  • Gen 48 (56 params) scored 0.89 combined, Gen 50 (152 params) scored 0.79 combined and suffered a 0.07 generalization gap. Parameter efficiency fell to 0.02–0.01.  
- Circuit depth inflation without optimized sharing: Gen 50’s depth 162/gate_count 276 delivered only 0.79 combined, indicating raw depth/gate count isn’t sufficient.  
- Isolated entanglement patterns lacking zero-cost NN enrichment: Gen 49 parametrized only cross-ring CRZ, NNN CRY and omitted NN CRY reuse, capping combined at 0.90.  
- Convergence extremes degrade final accuracy: Both too-rapid (~150 steps, Gen 49) and drawn-out (~510 steps, Gen 51) training runs lag behind the moderate (~240 steps) optimum.

## Implementation Insights  
- Flat four-key block spec with validate_ansatz_spec → PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK → key_to_position map ensures error-free parameter slicing.  
- Role-based parameter names (“ry_init”, “rz_main”, “cry_nnn”, “ry_final”) cap N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK at four and simplify hyperparameter search.  
- Reusing a single “cry_nnn” key across two spec layers densifies entanglement without extra parameters—a coding pattern central to Gen 47’s top score.  
- circuit_metadata via AnnotatedQueue accurately captures depth/gate_count; Gen 47’s 198 depth/396 gates are justified by a +0.02 combined gain.

## Performance Analysis  
- Parameter count vs. combined score: 26-parameter designs peak (Gen 47: 0.92 combined, param_eff 0.04) over 32-param (Gen 49: 0.90) and 56-param (Gen 48: 0.89).  
- Depth “sweet spot” shifts marginally upward: While 150-depth/348-gate models reached combined ~0.90, Gen 47’s 198/396 design achieved 0.92, indicating extra entangling layers can pay off if parametrized efficiently.  
- Generalization gaps remain minimal (~0.02) in compact, shared-parameter designs; over-parameterized circuits balloon gaps to 0.07 (Gen 50).  
- Convergence correlation: Moderate training durations (~240 steps) yield superior test accuracy compared to both shorter and longer runs in sibling models.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the fixed cross-ring CZs in Layer 3 with a single shared-parameter CRZ gate (“crz_diag”) applied to each diagonal pair. This adds only one extra parameter per block but injects tunable long‐range entanglement exactly where fixed CZs currently sit, mirroring the zero-cost CRY enrichment that boosted Gen 47’s score by +0.02. You’ll keep the same gate count while testing whether learnable diagonal phases further improve expressivity.  

2. Insert an additional shared RX rotation layer (“rx_main”) immediately following the shared RZ layer in each block. Concretely, append eight RX(“rx_main”) gates on wires 0–7 after your existing RZ(“rz_main”) calls, raising N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK by one. Single-qubit rotation sandwiches around entanglers have proven to break symmetries and can lift barren-plateau barriers, so this modest expansion may yield a >0.92 combined score.  

3. Decouple nearest-neighbor and next-nearest-neighbor CRY entanglement by introducing two keys—“cry_nn” for the eight NN CRY gates and “cry_nnn” for the eight NNN CRY gates—instead of reusing a single key. This adds a single extra parameter per block but gives finer control over short- vs. long-range coupling strengths, letting the optimizer balance them rather than tying them together. Prior runs show that small increases in N_PARAMS (up to ~32 total) can still hit combined ≥0.92 when sharing is structured.  

4. Reschedule your CZ entanglers into two commuting sublayers to shave ~15–20% off circuit depth without altering N_PARAMS or gate count. For example: in one sublayer apply CZs on [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7], then in the next apply CZs on [1,2],[3,4],[5,6],[7,0] interleaved with the four diagonal CZs (or CRZs if you’ve parameterized them). This reordering preserves full connectivity and has already cut depth in sibling models, moving closer to the depth/gate sweet spot.  

5. Introduce per-upload amplitude scalars (“lambda_0”, “lambda_1”, “lambda_2”) that multiply all block parameters within each feature-map/upload cycle. Concretely, before calling apply_ansatz_block for upload i, scale the params slice by lambda_i (three new globals). This lightweight mechanism dynamically weights early vs. late variational blocks, potentially accelerating convergence and sharpening final accuracy as seen in role-based block scaling in prior algorithmic searches.
