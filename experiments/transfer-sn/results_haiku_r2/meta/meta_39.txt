# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Minimal shared‐parameter blocks with only four keys per block (“ry_init”, “rz_main”, “cry_nnn”, “ry_final”) combined with a nearest‐neighbor CZ ring and a cross-ring CZ layer, as in the current best program (Gen 28), yielded a top test accuracy of 0.93 (combined score 0.91) using just 26 variational parameters.  
- Employing a single type of parametrized two-qubit gate (CRY on next-nearest neighbors) mid-block delivered high expressivity per parameter—Gen 28 outperformed richer two-qubit schemes in larger ansätze despite its compact design.  
- The 3 feature-map re-uploads × 2 ansatz repeats pattern underpinned all ≥0.90 combined-score circuits (e.g., Gen 28 and Gen 29), confirming its necessity for encoding complex 28-bit inputs.  
- Maintaining ansatz depth around 140–150 (≈348 gates) struck the optimal trade-off: Gen 28’s depth 150 beat both shallower (<140) and deeper (>160) variants by ≈0.02–0.04 in combined score.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized ansätze (>38 trainable parameters) delivered diminishing returns: Gen 30 (38 params) scored 0.89, Gen 35 (80 params) scored 0.87 despite higher gate counts, showing that surplus parameters alone don’t boost accuracy.  
- Complex sharing schemes (e.g., parity-tiered sharing in Gen 30) increased depth (~166) and gates (354) but only reached combined 0.89, indicating that intricate parameter‐tying can bloat the circuit without improving performance.  
- Extremely shallow (<100 depth) or under-parameterized (<20 parameters) designs, such as Gen 23 (14 params, depth 90), stalled at combined scores ≤0.88—insufficient to model the dataset’s decision boundary.  
- Omitting mid-block parametrized entanglers (relying solely on single-qubit rotations and CZ layers) lost essential expressivity, demonstrating the critical role of a two-qubit variational gate.  

## Implementation Insights  
- A flat ANSATZ_SPEC plus a validate_ansatz_spec routine catches misconfigurations early and produces an ordered PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK list, as leveraged successfully in Gen 28.  
- Indexing parameters via offset = block_index × N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK and a key_to_position mapping ensures consistent slicing across uploads and repeats, preventing off-by-one and mis‐alignment bugs.  
- Descriptive, role-based parameter naming (e.g., “ry_init” for the first rotation layer) keeps the spec concise and human-readable, capping N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK at four in the best designs.  
- Strict separation of the fixed feature_map (IsingZZ encoding) from variational layers enabled rapid experimentation with entanglement patterns—Gen 28 reused the same feature_map as all other top circuits.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gen 28’s 26-parameter circuit achieved test_accuracy_mean = 0.93, generalization_gap = 0.01, and parameter_efficiency = 0.04, outperforming Gen 29 (38 params, test 0.90, gap 0.00) and Gen 35 (80 params, test 0.88).  
- Circuits requiring ~270 training steps to converge (Gen 28, Gen 29) reached higher ultimate accuracy than faster‐converging designs (≈180 steps) that capped at ~0.89 (e.g., Gen 33, Gen 34).  
- A sweet spot at depth ≈150/gate count ≈348 emerged: shallower circuits lost 0.02–0.03 accuracy; deeper ones incurred extra gates without commensurate gains.  
- The consistent use of 3 re-uploads × 2 repeats correlated strongly with all top performers (score ≥0.90), whereas deviating from this schedule correlated with lower combined scores.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the mid‐block CRY entangler to nearest neighbors at zero extra parameter cost. After your existing CRY(cry_nnn) calls in Layer 5, also apply CRY(cry_nnn) on each nearest‐neighbor wire (0–1, 1–2 … 7–0). This reuses the same “cry_nnn” key to enrich connectivity per block—building on Gen 28’s high expressivity‐per‐parameter pattern without increasing N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK.  

2. Turn the fixed cross‐ring CZ layer into a shared CRZ gate. In Layer 3, replace each CZ([i,j]) with CRZ(crz_cr) on the same long‐range wires and add a single new key “crz_cr” to the block. This leverages the demonstrated power of one‐parameter two‐qubit gates mid‐block (like cry_nnn) to boost entanglement flexibility with minimal overhead.  

3. Make the feature‐map strength trainable via one global scale factor. Introduce a scalar parameter “phi_scale” (outside ANSATZ_SPEC) and use qml.IsingZZ(phi_scale * records[..., k], wires=[a,b]) in feature_map. By decoupling data‐encoding amplitude from fixed π/2, you let the circuit learn optimal injection strength while preserving the proven separation of upload and variational layers.  

4. Rebalance uploads vs. repeats to 2 re‐uploads × 3 ansatz repeats. Set N_UPLOADS=2 and N_REPEATS=3 so you still apply six variational blocks (same total parameters and similar depth≈150). This tests whether deeper intra‐upload circuits converge differently while honoring the 3×2 pattern’s success in Gen 28.
