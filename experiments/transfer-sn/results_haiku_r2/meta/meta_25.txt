# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Lean shared‐parameter blocks drive top scores**  
  – Generation 22 (“8-Qubit Shared-Parameter Ansatz Classifier”) uses only four shared keys per block (ry_init, rz_main, cry_nnn, ry_final), for a total of 26 variational parameters, yet achieves the best combined score (0.91) with train/val/test accuracies of 0.94/0.92/0.93 and a generalization gap of 0.01.  
  – This contrasts with higher-param designs (e.g., Gen 18’s 38 params, Gen 25’s 50 params) that plateau at a 0.89 combined score.

- **Dense CZ plus targeted CRY entanglement**  
  – Gen 22 interleaves a nearest-neighbor CZ ring and cross-ring CZ layer with a single shared CRY on next-nearest neighbors, striking a balance between connectivity and parameter count.  
  – Circuits lacking either the cross-ring CZ (Gen 14: ladder only, 0.89) or mid-block CRY (e.g., symmetric Gen 25 missing CRY/CRZ/CRX parameters) never exceed 0.89 combined.

- **Three feature reuploads × two ansatz repeats**  
  – All top performers (Gen 14, Gen 18, Gen 22) adhere to N_UPLOADS=3 and N_REPEATS=2, confirming that the “3 uploads + 2 repeats” pattern consistently underlies stable convergence (Gen 22 converges in ~270 steps to ≥0.90 accuracy).

- **Selective two-qubit parametrization suffices**  
  – Limiting trainable two-qubit rotations to a single shared CRY key, instead of multiple CRX/CRZ layers, still outperforms more elaborate multi-gate schemes—highlighting that judicious placement of one mid-block entangler can maximize expressivity per parameter.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Over-compression below ~24 params**  
  – Generation 24 (“Minimal Shared-Parameter”) uses 20 params with depth 111 and reaches only a 0.88 combined score (0.89 test accuracy), showing that excessive parameter tying reduces circuit expressivity.

- **Parity-tiered sharing without uniform coverage**  
  – Gen 18’s parity-shared RY/RZ (38 params) and long-range CRY achieve 0.89 combined, but still underperform the uniformly shared layout of Gen 22, suggesting that tying parameters identically across all qubits is more effective than parity subsets.

- **Shallower circuits (<132 depth)**  
  – Gen 14 (depth 100) and Gen 24 (depth 111) cap out at 0.89 and 0.88 combined, respectively, reinforcing that depth around 140–150 is required to reach ≥0.90.

- **Missing mid-block parametrized entanglers**  
  – Designs that rely only on CZ chains and readout rotations (e.g., symmetric Gen 25 with shared RX/RY/RZ but no CRY/CRZ/CRX) stall at 0.89, indicating that mid-block two-qubit gates are essential for >0.89.

## Implementation Insights

- **Robust ansatz‐spec validation**  
  – All top programs (including Gen 22) report empty SPEC_ERRORS, and their accuracy std is 0.00, showing that early validation via `validate_ansatz_spec` is critical to avoid silent misconfigurations.

- **Offset‐plus‐key mapping for parameter slicing**  
  – Using `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` plus a `key_to_position` dict to compute `offset = block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` simplifies `apply_ansatz_block`, ensuring each shared parameter is applied consistently across the six blocks.

- **Decoupled feature_map vs. variational layers**  
  – Separating `feature_map` (fixed IsingZZ encodings) from `apply_ansatz_block` (rotations and entanglers) accelerates experimentation: Gen 22 swapped in cross-ring CZ and CRY without touching data-upload logic.

- **Minimal, descriptive param naming**  
  – Labels like “ry_init”, “rz_main”, “cry_nnn”, “ry_final” keep the spec concise (4 keys) while capturing functional roles, reducing cognitive load and controlling N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK.

## Performance Analysis

- **Best score at mid-20s parameter count**  
  – Gen 22’s 26 params yield the highest combined score (0.91). Circuits with ∼20 params hit 0.88, those with 38–50 params reach 0.89, and richer schemes (>60 params) show no further gains.

- **Depth ~150 correlates with top accuracy**  
  – The current best (depth 150) outperforms shallower variants (depth 100–132), confirming that depth in the 140–150 range is a sweet spot for this task.

- **Gate count ~348 balances expressivity and trainability**  
  – Gen 22’s 348 gates produce a generalization gap of only 0.01, whereas sparser circuits (≈300 gates) see gaps of 0.02–0.03.

- **Convergence vs. depth trade-off**  
  – Although Gen 22 requires ~270 steps to cross 0.90 accuracy—more than Gen 14’s ~120 steps—it ultimately achieves a higher asymptotic accuracy (0.93 test vs. 0.89 test), underscoring that depth and richer connectivity pay off with more training.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce coarse‐grained entanglement parameter tying  
   – Add a second shared two‐qubit rotation key “cry_nn” for nearest‐neighbor edges alongside the existing “cry_nnn” for next‐nearest neighbors.  
   – In your ANSATZ_SPEC’s Layer 5, apply CRY(cry_nn) to wires [(0,1),(1,2),…,(7,0)] and retain CRY(cry_nnn) on [(0,2),(2,4),…].  
   – This adds just one extra parameter per block (N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK→5) and tests whether distinguishing local vs. non‐local entanglers boosts expressivity per parameter, building on the success of selective two‐qubit parametrization.  

2. Enrich mid‐block nonlinearity with a shared IsingZZ layer  
   – Insert immediately after the cross‐ring CZ layer (Layer 3) a layer of qml.IsingZZ gates on each FEATURE_PAIRS[k], all sharing a new key “zz_mid.”  
   – Update feature_map or ANSATZ_SPEC to include these fixed wires with a trainable “zz_mid” rotation.  
   – This leverages the stable gradient flow of IsingZZ (as used in the feature map) to increase nonlinearity without significant depth or parameter‐count overhead.  

3. Alternate CZ‐ring connectivity across blocks  
   – In apply_ansatz_block, branch on block_index parity: for even blocks use the standard nearest‐neighbor CZ ring, for odd blocks shift every CZ by +1 mod N (i.e. apply CZ(i+1,i+2) mod 8).  
   – Keep the same total of eight CZ gates per block so gate_count and depth remain constant.  
   – This “dynamic rewiring” injects broader connectivity over repeats without extra gates, inspired by the rapid‐convergence of dense‐CZ designs.  

4. Add compact per‐wire variational readout rotations  
   – After all ansatz blocks and before measurement in circuit_z_expectations, apply qml.RY(params[…],wires=i) and qml.RZ(params[…],wires=i) on each qubit using two new shared keys “ry_read” and “rz_read.”  
   – These two keys supplement the gain/bias calibration and capture residual phase offsets per wire with negligible depth overhead.  
   – Similar readout corrections have lifted test accuracy in tight‐parameter regimes.  

5. Mix two‐qubit rotation axes across uploads  
   – Modify Layer 5 so that, based on upload_index = block_index // N_REPEATS, you use CRY(cry_nnn) in odd uploads and CRZ(cry_nnn) in even uploads on the same next‐nearest pairs.  
   – This alternation keeps N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK fixed while testing whether combining multiple rotation axes per entanglement scale enhances expressivity.  
   – It builds on the efficacy of single‐gate mid‐block entanglers by exploring cross‐axis richness without parameter explosion.
