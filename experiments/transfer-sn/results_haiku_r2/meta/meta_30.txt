# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Minimal shared-parameter blocks with four keys per block (“ry_init”, “rz_main”, “cry_nnn”, “ry_final”) yielded 26 total parameters and the top combined score of 0.91 (Gen 28 / seed program: test 0.93, gen­gap 0.01).  
- Entanglement layout of a nearest-neighbor CZ ring, a cross-ring CZ layer, plus a single shared CRY on next-nearest neighbors provided maximal expressivity per parameter (Gen 28 achieved depth 150, gate count 348).  
- The 3 feature-map reuploads × 2 ansatz repeats pattern consistently underpins strong performance—used by all ≥0.90-score circuits (Gen 22, Gen 28).  
- Restricting to one type of parametrized two-qubit gate (CRY) mid-block sufficed to surpass 0.90 test accuracy, echoing Gen 22’s success with a single shared CRY.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Parity-tiered sharing with richer two-qubit layers (Gen 26, 50 params) only reached a combined score of 0.88 and parameter efficiency 0.02, indicating over-parameterization without proportional gain.  
- Over-complex ansätze with >60 parameters (Gen 31) plateaued at a 0.89 combined score, incurred high depth/gate counts (depth 117, 396 gates), and exhibited a larger generalization gap (0.03).  
- Extremely shallow or under-parameterized designs (Gen 23: 14 params, depth 90) peaked at a combined 0.87, showing that depth <100 and param count <20 cannot capture the target complexity.  
- Omitting a mid-block parametrized entangler or compressing below ≈24 params (e.g., Gen 24’s minimal shared-param) stalled combined scores at ≤0.88, confirming the necessity of judicious two-qubit gate placement.  

## Implementation Insights  
- The flat ANSATZ_SPEC list plus `validate_ansatz_spec` ensures early detection of misconfigurations and produces an ordered, de-duplicated parameter key list, as demonstrated in the seed program.  
- Mapping `offset = block_index * N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK` with a `key_to_position` dict in `apply_ansatz_block` guarantees consistent slicing of shared parameters across all blocks, reducing indexing errors.  
- Descriptive, role-based parameter names (e.g., “ry_init”, “rz_main”) keep the spec concise and human-readable, directly tying each key to its functional layer and capping total parameters.  
- Strict separation of `feature_map` (fixed IsingZZ encoding) from variational layers enabled rapid experimentation—cross-ring CZ and CRY layers were swapped in without altering data-upload logic.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The current best (Gen 28) at depth 150/gate 348 outperforms slightly shallower variants (Gen 26: depth 144, gate 360) by ~0.05 in combined score, highlighting the depth sweet spot ~140–150.  
- A total parameter count around mid-20s (26 params) yields the highest combined score; increasing to 38–62 params yields diminishing returns (combined ≤0.89).  
- Although convergence to >0.90 validation accuracy takes ~270 steps for the top design, it attains a superior ultimate test accuracy (0.93) compared with faster but lower-depth circuits that cap at ~0.89.  
- Only high-depth, heavy-sharing circuits achieve a generalization gap near 0.01; other designs show gaps of 0.02–0.05, underscoring the role of deep, shared-param entanglement in robustness.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the shared CRY entangler to nearest‐neighbor pairs at zero parameter cost.  
   ­– In Layer 5, after your existing CRY(cry_nnn) calls, also apply CRY(cry_nnn) on each nearest‐neighbor wire (e.g. (0,1),(1,2)…,(7,0)).  
   ­– This reuses the same cry_nnn key to double mid‐block connectivity without increasing N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK, testing whether richer entanglement patterns boost expressivity.

   ­– Introduce a new scalar parameter “phi_scale” and replace FEATURE_SCALE with phi_scale in feature_map (i.e. qml.IsingZZ(phi_scale * records[..., k],…)).  
   ­– This adds one parameter that lets the circuit learn optimal encoding amplitudes, leveraging the separation of data‐upload and variational layers to tune feature injection.

   ­– Swap each CZ in Layer 3 for CRZ(crz_cr) (with one new key “crz_cr”) on the same long‐range wires.  
   ­– This turns deterministic entanglement into trainable rotations mid‐block, building on the power of single‐shared two‐qubit gates to elevate performance per parameter.
