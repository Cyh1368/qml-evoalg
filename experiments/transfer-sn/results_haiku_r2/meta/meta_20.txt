# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three IsingZZ feature‐map re-uploads combined with two ansatz repeats and a layered block structure (RY→RZ_pre→dense CZ chain→CRY→CRZ→RY_mid→CRX→RZ_post) consistently yields top performance (Gen 11/current best: train/val/test ≈0.92/0.92/0.90, combined 0.90, gap 0.02, converges in ~180 steps).  
- Aggressive parameter tying across wires into just seven keys per block (ry_0, rz_pre, cry_a, crz_b, ry_mid, crx_c, rz_post) delivers high expressivity with only 44 variational parameters, matching or exceeding higher-param designs (e.g., Gen 17’s 32 params at 0.89 combined, Gen 19’s 44 params at 0.88).  
- A dense CZ entanglement pattern—nearest-neighbor plus cross-block edges (0-1-…-7 chain and 0-2,1-3,…,5-7)—balances circuit depth (~144) and expressivity, accelerating convergence over simple chains.  
- Incorporating mid-block two-qubit rotations (CRY, CRZ, CRX) atop CZ layers boosts the combined score above 0.89; circuits lacking CRZ or CRX (e.g., Gen 17, Gen 19) capped at ≤0.89.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Circuit depth reduction below ~120 (Gen 16 depth 96, 20 params → combined 0.87) sacrifices accuracy despite excellent parameter efficiency (0.04).  
- Over-parameterized designs without sufficient tying (Gen 20: 80 params, depth 138 → combined 0.85, gap 0.06) suffer from low parameter efficiency (~0.01) and overfitting.  
- Fixed or overly sparse entanglement (Gen 21: nearest-neighbor CZ + CRY, depth 105, 50 params → 0.87 combined) underperforms compared to dense CZ+cross-block couplings.  
- Parity-sharing schemes without full mid-block layering (Gen 19: CRY+CRZ but no CRX, depth 132, 44 params → combined 0.88, gap 0.04) lag behind when missing one class of two-qubit rotation.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining a minimal ANSATZ_SPEC with exactly the needed shared parameter keys (7 keys) yields N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK=7 and drives both compactness and expressivity in the current best.  
- The validate_ansatz_spec routine prevents silent mis-specifications; an empty SPEC_ERRORS for the best program correlates with stable, high accuracy (std=0.00 across runs).  
- Offset-based parameter slicing plus a precomputed key-to-index map in apply_ansatz_block offers a clear, efficient mapping from the shared spec to the circuit_params vector.  
- Leveraging Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue and QuantumScript to extract depth and gate count ensures accurate logging (current best: depth 144, 402 gates) for direct performance comparisons.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Circuits with depth 132–144 and gate counts 330–402 cluster at the top combined scores (0.88–0.90); shallower designs (<110 depth) never exceed 0.89.  
- Optimal parameter budgets lie around 32–50 total variational parameters: Gen 17 (32 params → 0.89), Gen 19 (44 params → 0.88), current best (44 params → 0.90). Going above ~60 params yields diminishing returns.  
- Models achieving generalization gaps ≤0.02 (current best, Gen 17) hit combined ≥0.89, whereas gaps ≥0.04 (Gen 19, Gen 20) correspond to combined ≤0.88.  
- Rich entanglement plus mid-block two-qubit rotations accelerate training: the current best converges in ~180 steps versus >300 steps for simpler CZ-chain–only variants.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parity‐based RY sharing  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the single “ry_0” key with two keys—“ry_even” applied to wires [0,2,4,6] and “ry_odd” applied to [1,3,5,7]—for both the pre‐ and post‐CZ RY layers.  Generation 4 showed that an even/odd split preserves test accuracy while slightly boosting expressivity over uniform tying, and with only one extra parameter you remain within the 32–50‐parameter sweet spot.  

2. Hierarchical two-qubit rotation tying  
   Instead of one “cry_a”, one “crz_b” and one “crx_c” for all four pairs, introduce “cry_local”/“crz_local”/“crx_local” for nearest‐neighbor edges and “cry_cross”/“crz_cross”/“crx_cross” for cross‐block edges.  This coarse‐grained distinction tests whether differentiating local vs non‐local pathways preserves the small generalization gap (≤ 0.02) while giving the circuit more flexibility in capturing different entanglement scales.  

3. Mid-block IsingZZ enrichment  
   After the CZ chain and before your CRY/CRZ gates in each block, insert a layer of qml.IsingZZ(θ, wires=[a,b]) on the same FEATURE_PAIRS with a single new shared key “zz_mid”.  This leverages the stable gradient flow of IsingZZ (as in your feature‐map) to enrich nonlinearity without significantly increasing depth or parameter count, and may accelerate convergence toward or beyond ~180 steps.  

4. Alternating CZ-chain rewiring  
   Dynamically cycle your dense CZ pattern: in even-indexed ansatz blocks apply the standard nearest‐neighbor+cross-block CZ schedule, and in odd-indexed blocks shift each link by one qubit (e.g., replace CZ(i,i+1) with CZ(i+1,i+2) mod 8 and CZ(7,0)).  This injects broader connectivity over repeats without adding gates, building on the rapid convergence of your current dense‐CZ design.  

5. Compact variational readout  
   Immediately before measuring PauliZ on each wire, append RY(φ_read) and RZ(ψ_read) using two new shared keys “ry_read” and “rz_read”.  Since your gain–bias calibration is already stable, this lightweight per-wire correction can capture residual phase offsets and has been shown to lift test accuracy in tight‐parameter regimes with negligible depth overhead.
