# Second referee report: *Symmetry Spending Under a Quantum Shot Budget*

**Recommendation: Reject**

## Overall assessment

The response accepts and substantively addresses several defects identified in the first review. The Maurer–Pontil confidence radius now uses the contrast's correct range and menu correction. The authors have added an independent, small-budget enumeration, distinguished the input flip from a symmetry of the full physical experiment, and retracted the impossible distinct-circuit optimum. These changes improve the technical record. I do not regard the earlier implementation errors, by themselves, as grounds for another rejection once corrected.

The revised submission still does not establish its new central claim. The only reported positive matched-resource comparison uses a Studentised rule without the distribution-free certificate the paper promises, tests only synthetic controls, and compares symmetry policies with a deliberately narrow group-free policy class. The protocol's own A2.7 stop gate requires the advantage on **both physical mechanisms**; the new matched-resource experiment contains neither. Moreover, the manuscript still presents numerical results generated by the invalid old bound as if they were current results, and parts of the revised resource ledger are physically and arithmetically inconsistent. These are material problems with the present submission, not merely matters of wording.

## Major remaining concerns

### 1. The revised manuscript still publishes withdrawn certificate results

The response and [Addendum A2.0](docs/PROTOCOL.md) explicitly invalidate the original `control.json` coverage/frontier, `bound.json`, and variance-table bound split for any certificate claim. Nevertheless, the abstract of [`paper/main.tex`](paper/main.tex) still says that a “distribution-free empirical-Bernstein bound” gives power 0 up to $n=200$ and 1 at $n=400$. Section 4 still inputs [`tabs_frontier.tex`](paper/tabs_frontier.tex), whose caption calls the old $B=nS=6400$ experiment a certificate frontier. Section 4 also inputs the old [`tabs_bound.tex`](paper/tabs_bound.tex), and the text quotes its Studentised violation and power numbers. The old $B=1600$ allocation comparison also remains in the abstract. These results predate the corrected bound and full calibration ledger; the reader is not consistently told at the point of presentation that they are invalid for the claims being made.

The revised paper cannot mix an invalid distribution-free frontier with a new Studentised sweep and call both the study's evidence. Move obsolete results into a clearly marked historical appendix or remove them from the submitted argument, regenerate all current certificate tables from the corrected procedure, and make the abstract match those tables. The first term of Equation (1) also remains labeled `bias^2`, despite the response saying it was renamed “integrated squared discrepancy.” The access section still says “one labelled state preparation per calibration instance,” contradicting the revised ledger's intended distinction.

### 2. The reported positive selection rates are not certified at the advertised confidence level

The corrected `mp_bound()` is a legitimate simultaneous finite-sample starting point. The authors themselves find that it is too conservative to select a policy at their studied effect sizes and budgets. The new 108-point frontier in [`exp_ledger.py`](experiments/exp_ledger.py) therefore calls `certify(..., bound="normal")`. Its Studentised bound is a normal-quantile approximation, not a distribution-free upper confidence bound. Wilson intervals on simulated violation frequencies quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty **for the simulated regimes**; they do not turn the Studentised decision rule into a $1-\delta$ guarantee over an arbitrary future task distribution.

Thus the central phrase “certifies where a group-free menu cannot” is unsupported if “certifies” means the paper's stated finite-sample guarantee. The appropriate statement is that the Studentised selector *selected useful policies frequently in these controls*. The cost of the valid Maurer–Pontil rule is also only a bound-specific sufficient-radius calculation, not an information-theoretic lower bound on how many labeled instances or state copies any distribution-free procedure needs.

### 3. The new comparator does not isolate a method or measurement-structure advantage

The “symmetry” and “generic” arms in [`exp_ledger.py`](experiments/exp_ledger.py) use the **same generic paired certification procedure**. What differs is the candidate set: the first contains orbit averages and mixtures, whereas the second contains only shrinkage toward zero. Since the constructed `help` and `interior` regimes were designed so orbit averaging reduces bias, a group-free constant-shrinkage menu is expected to lack a sufficiently good action. That demonstrates that having a useful candidate matters; it does not show a better certification estimator, allocation strategy, or shot complexity from exploiting group structure. It does not compare against generic paired risk calibration applied to the *same symmetry menu*, the closest baseline requested in the original proposal.

The comparator's calibration cost is also avoidably inflated. Every `shrink` policy is a deterministic rescaling of the **same raw finite-shot estimate**. The implementation independently resamples a full raw batch for each shrinkage coefficient and charges those batches to the generic menu. One raw measurement record could evaluate all eight shrinkage choices, with the resulting dependence handled by a simultaneous bound. A fair measurement-resource comparison must permit this reuse; likewise, it must account for any physically available reuse among symmetry policies. A table that compares policies at different total costs without this optimization does not establish a matched-resource advantage.

The numerical claims are inconsistent even before that comparison is repaired. The response and Section 4.1 say that the symmetry menu reaches **power 1.000 at 20,800 shots** in `control_help` and **1.000 at 96,000 shots** in `control_interior`. The generated [`tabs_ledger.tex`](paper/tabs_ledger.tex) and [`results/ledger.json`](results/ledger.json) report **0.900** and **0.960**, respectively. The text says the generic menu “never certifies,” while its reported powers of 0.14 and 0.10 mean that it sometimes selects a useful policy under the paper's own definition. These statements need correction even for a descriptive simulation report.

### 4. The five-part resource ledger still miscounts preparations and compilations

The access model specifies scalar, single-copy shots. A destructive measurement shot consumes a newly prepared quantum state. [`calib_ledger()`](certify.py) instead sets `preparations_total = n_instances × number_of_policy_batches`; for the cited `n=50,S=8,m=8` point it reports **400 preparations** and **3,200 shots**. Under single-copy measurement, those 3,200 shots require approximately 3,200 physical state preparations, apart from any failed or calibration shots. A “shot-batch” cannot reuse one state across eight independent destructive measurements. The deployment ledger similarly adds only 1,000 preparations for 8,000 deployment shots.

The compilation count sums `p.circuits` over policies, reporting 12 for the two-element symmetry control. If compilation means *distinct parameterized circuits built once and reused*, the policies share at most the two orbit circuits, not 12 unique circuits. Charging compilations separately for each policy would be an explicit recompilation protocol, not the declared count of distinct circuits. The switch count is also summed over all candidate policies rather than generated from the actual measurement schedule of a calibration run or selected deployment policy. These defects do not change the raw shot totals, but they invalidate the claim that the full five-part physical resource ledger is now correct.

### 5. The study does not pass its own physical-task stop gate

Addendum [A2.7](docs/PROTOCOL.md) says that if the symmetry menu does not beat generic paired certification at matched total budget on **both physical mechanisms**, the paper will report a scoped negative and will not describe an advantage. The new ledger's 108 rows cover `control_help`, `control_hurt`, and `control_interior` only. They contain no Heisenberg or cluster comparison. The physical results file supplies policy risks and a frontier for the symmetry menu, but no group-free matched-cost frontier. The manuscript nonetheless claims a positive matched-cost advantage in its principal contribution section.

The cluster task is further acknowledged to have essentially identical even- and raw-target risks, so it does not test the stated odd-content sign reversal. The Heisenberg result now concerns a paired control transformation, not a symmetry of the full prediction experiment. Both facts are acceptable as limitations, but the scope of the evidence and the claimed operational contribution must agree. The A2.7 stop criterion has not been met.

### 6. The remaining theoretical contribution is too limited for this venue

The authors now rightly call the risk expression a conditional bias–variance decomposition and the calibration expression the law of total variance. The corrected Maurer–Pontil bound is an application of a known result to a fixed finite menu. The calculated minimum $n$ for its range term to fall below a small gap characterizes this particular conservative bound; it is neither a lower bound for the statistical problem nor a novel quantum measurement-complexity result. The per-switch optimum depends on a priced interleaved schedule even though the same predictor can group its shots and avoid most switches. No device evidence, new measurement primitive, general allocation rule, or independently replicated advantage compensates for that limited theory.

## Required work before a substantially new submission

1. Remove or unmistakably segregate every pre-A2 certificate result, and make the abstract, tables, text, response, and reproducibility section agree with the current code and JSON.
2. Use a rigorously valid selector for the claimed $1-\delta$ statement, or present the Studentised sweep solely as descriptive simulation evidence. Distinguish a bound-specific cost from a lower bound on the problem.
3. Compare against generic paired calibration on the **same candidate menu** and against resource-efficient group-free alternatives. Permit reuse of raw shot records across deterministic shrinkage policies and account for dependence correctly.
4. Count physical state copies per destructive shot and unique compiled circuits across policies; derive switches from the schedule actually executed.
5. Apply the frozen A2.7 criterion on both physical mechanisms at matched total resources. If it fails, report the promised scoped negative and identify what the controls do and do not establish.
6. For a top-tier claim, supply a nontrivial theorem or a reproducible, material resource advantage that remains under the strongest applicable equal-access baseline.

## Decision

**Reject.** The response has repaired important technical faults, and the independent enumeration is a meaningful improvement. The present manuscript's positive claim is still based on an uncertified Studentised selector, an underpowered comparator, and synthetic controls that do not meet its prespecified physical-task gate. Its current text also repeats results explicitly invalidated by the revision. These issues require a redesigned and coherently reported study, beyond what a rebuttal can establish.
