# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parameterized CRX “ring” + CRY “hub” entanglement  
  • Current best program (Combined 0.60; train 0.57, val 0.65, test 0.50, gap 0.07) uses CRX gates around the outer ring and CRY gates from center to edges.  
  • Compared to Generation 20’s CZ/CRZ-only entanglement (train ~0.32, val ~0.38, test ~0.33), this change boosts train accuracy by ~0.25 and validation by ~0.27.  
- Symmetry-aware parameter sharing across corners/edges/center  
  • Maintains a compact 66-parameter ansatz (depth 70, gate_count 261) while improving expressivity versus aggressive sharing alone.  
  • Yields a small generalization gap (0.07) versus 0.12 in Generation 22 with similar sharing but less diversified entanglement.  
- Three reuploads × two repeats layout  
  • Gives sufficient circuit depth (70) without exploding parameter count.  
  • Balances expressivity and trainability—evidence: convergence of validation accuracy to 0.65 under 100 epochs/30 steps.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Pure CZ/CRZ entanglement (Generation 20 & 24)  
  • Both used only ring CZ + hub CRZ and achieved ~32–38% accuracy, combined ~0.33–0.45.  
  • Lacks parametrized mixing angles, under-expresses spatial correlations in board configurations.  
- Excessive parameter sharing without entanglement diversity (Generation 22)  
  • Despite 66 parameters, train accuracy stalled at 0.37, validation 0.51, gap 0.12.  
  • Underfitting suggests that sharing RZ/RY alone cannot compensate for limited two-qubit gate variety.  
- Erroneous ansatz specifications (Generation 23)  
  • Misspecification of wires or gate types led to spec validation failures and zero accuracy across all splits.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit class-expectation mapping aligns qubit geometry with labels  
  • Averaging edge-qubit Z-expectations for “cross,” corner qubits for “circle,” and center qubit for “draw” leverages board symmetries to disentangle class outputs.  
- Rigorous ansatz‐spec validation prevents subtle adjacency or parameter‐key bugs  
  • The `validate_ansatz_spec` routine caught non-adjacent wires in Generation 23, ensuring only legal grid edges are entangled.  
- Use of Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue for circuit metadata  
  • Automatic extraction of depth and gate_count enables tight monitoring of complexity vs. performance trade-offs.  
- RX-based feature map with equidistant rotations  
  • Uniform scaling of input values across 3 reuploads provides stable gradients that supported convergence to 0.65 validation accuracy.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Entanglement gate selection is pivotal  
  • Switching from CZ/CRZ to parametrized CRX+CRY increased combined score from ~0.45 to 0.60 while holding depth and parameter count constant.  
- Generalization gap reduction  
  • Current best gap 0.07 vs. 0.12 in the closest predecessor (Generation 22), indicating better regularization via mixed two-qubit gates.  
- Validation accuracy as leading indicator  
  • Prior models peaked at ~0.51 validation; current best reached 0.65 under identical training settings, confirming substantial architectural impact.  
- Parameter efficiency remains low (~0.01)  
  • Even with improved accuracy, test accuracy (0.50) over 66 parameters yields efficiency 0.50/66≈0.0075, highlighting room for further parameter‐accuracy scaling.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the ring entanglement by appending a second, shared‐parameter CRZ layer around the outer eight qubits.  
   • In your `EVOLVE-BLOCK`, after all the existing CRX `"crx_ring"` entries, add eight `{"gate": "CRZ", "wires": [i, j], "param": "crz_ring"}` entries for the same perimeter edges.  
   • Evidence: combining CRX mixers with CRZ phase couplings will enrich two-qubit correlations as mixed entanglers (CRX+CRY) yielded the biggest jump in accuracy versus CZ/CRZ alone.

2. Split the single `"crx_ring"` angle into two shared parameters for horizontal versus vertical edges.  
   • Replace each `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[u,v],"param":"crx_ring"}` with `"crx_ring_horiz"` when `(u,v)` is horizontal (e.g. (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7)) and `"crx_ring_vert"` when vertical (e.g. (1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,0)).  
   • Evidence: modestly increasing ring entanglement parameters to capture directional correlations builds on the CRX ring’s proven boost to train/validation accuracy while keeping the overall ansatz compact.

3. Alternate the entangler type across repeats by using a shared‐parameter CRY ring on the second repeat.  
   • In `apply_ansatz_block`, detect `repeat_index==1` and apply `qml.CRY(params[offset+key["cry_ring"]], wires=[u,v])` for perimeter edges, with a new `"cry_ring"` key in `ANSATZ_SPEC`.  
   • Evidence: the current best program’s mixed two-qubit gates (CRX ring + CRY hub) reduced the generalization gap; extending mixing into the ring itself may further regularize and enrich expressivity.

4. Diversify the feature map axes by cycling through RX, RZ, and RY on successive uploads.  
   • Modify `feature_map` to do `qml.RX` when `upload_index==0`, `qml.RZ` when `upload_index==1`, and `qml.RY` when `upload_index==2`, each still scaled by `FEATURE_SCALE`.  
   • Evidence: the RX-only reuploads delivered stable gradients; introducing orthogonal encoding axes can expose different board patterns and potentially accelerate convergence.

5. Increase the ansatz depth by raising `N_REPEATS` from 2 to 3 while preserving symmetry sharing.  
   • Change `N_REPEATS = 3` (so each upload has three blocks) and keep your shared-parameter scheme intact across the larger block index space.  
   • Evidence: the 3×2 layout balanced depth and parameter count; a 3×3 layout will further expand expressivity under the same symmetry constraints without exploding unique parameter growth.
