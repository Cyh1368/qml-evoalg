# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

• Parameterized CRX “ring” + CRY “hub” entanglement  
  – Current best program (Combined 0.60; train 0.57, val 0.65, test 0.50, gap 0.07; 66 params, depth 70) uses a single shared CRX angle around the outer 8 qubits and a single shared CRY angle from center to edges.  
  – Yields ~0.11–0.12 lift in combined score over the best CRX–CRZ design (Gen 31: Combined 0.49, depth 117) and ~0.08 over mixed CZ/CRX rings (Gen 30: Combined 0.52, depth 118).

• Three feature re-uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  – The 3 uploads × 2 repeats layout achieves sufficient expressivity (70-gate depth, 261 gates total) with only 66 parameters, enabling convergence to 0.65 validation accuracy in 100 epochs.  
  – Deeper designs with ~118 depth (Gen 30–31) failed to converge beyond 0.51–0.53 validation, showing that depth alone is insufficient.

• Symmetry-aware parameter sharing across corners/edges/center  
  – Sharing separate parameters for corner/edge/center in pre- and post-entanglement RZ and in RY rotations compresses the ansatz to 66 params.  
  – Balances expressivity and regularization: small generalization gap (0.07) versus <0.05 gaps in under-expressive models that never exceed ~0.48 validation.

• RX-based feature map with equidistant rotations  
  – Encoding board values via RX(±2π/3) stabilizes gradients and supports consistent convergence, as seen in the best model’s smooth rise to 0.65 val accuracy versus jittery or stalled training in other designs.

## Ineffective Approaches

• Pure CZ/CRZ or mixed CRX–CRZ entanglement  
  – Gen 30 (“Hierarchical Symmetric Entanglement Ansatz” with CZ/CRX ring + CRY hub) reached only Combined 0.52 (train 0.38, val 0.51) at depth 118, underfitting despite +40 gates.  
  – Gen 31 (“Symmetric Ring-Hub QML Ansatz” with CRY/CRZ ring + CRX hub) stalled at Combined 0.49, showing that non-parametric two-qubit gates or CRZ mixing fail to capture key spatial correlations.

• Excessive parameter sharing with uniform two-qubit gates  
  – Gen 32–34, all using aggressive RZ/RY sharing and uniform CRX or CRY hubs without a matching ring, yielded Combined 0.44–0.40 with very low train accuracy (0.28–0.37) and generalization gaps ranging up to 0.18 (Gen 34).  
  – Indicates that sharing must be paired with diverse, parameterized entangling operations to avoid underfitting.

• Increasing depth without entanglement diversity  
  – Deeper circuits (~119 depth, Gen 30 and Gen 29) did not improve combined scores beyond ~0.52, reinforcing that adding gates without new entanglement motifs is ineffective.

## Implementation Insights

• Modular ANSATZ_SPEC + apply_ansatz_block pattern  
  – Defining the five‐layer ansatz via a structured spec list (pre-RZ, RY, CRX ring, CRY hub, post-RZ) and mapping parameter keys to positions supports easy mutations and consistent parameter sharing across uploads/repeats.

• Rigorous ansatz-spec validation  
  – Using `validate_ansatz_spec` to enforce grid-adjacent two-qubit wires prevents topological errors and ensures the intended spatial entanglement pattern is correctly implemented.

• AnnotatedQueue for metadata extraction  
  – Employing Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue and `QuantumScript.from_queue` to compute depth and gate count allows on-the-fly complexity tracking, making it straightforward to compare circuits like depth 70 vs. depth 118.

• Feature‐map implementation stabilizes training  
  – The simple `for wire: RX(FEATURE_SCALE * board[wire])` yields equidistant feature rotations, which in the best program produces stable gradients and consistent convergence to 0.65 validation accuracy.

## Performance Analysis

• Sharp gains from CRX+CRY vs. CZ/CRX or CRX–CRZ  
  – Current best: Combined 0.60 (3↑ over Gen 30’s 0.52; 11↑ over Gen 31’s 0.49).  
  – Test accuracy uplift: 0.50 (best) vs. 0.40 (Gen 30) and 0.47 (Gen 31).

• Depth vs. score trade-off  
  – Optimal design at depth 70 outperforms deeper (>117) circuits, demonstrating that targeted entanglement diversity is more important than raw circuit depth.

• Parameter efficiency correlates with accuracy  
  – Best program: 0.50 test acc / 66 params ≈ 0.0076; under-expressive CRX–CRZ models achieve ≤ 0.005.  
  – Even with equal or fewer parameters, circuits lacking CRX+CRY ring/hub never match this efficiency.

• Balanced generalization gap  
  – Best program’s gap of 0.07 reflects adequate regularization; under-expressive models show tiny gaps (0.00–0.03) but low accuracy (< 0.48), while some over-shared designs (Gen 34) suffered large gaps (0.18) due to volatile training.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a CRY “ring” layer immediately after the CRX ring in each ansatz block. In your EVOLVE-BLOCK, append eight `{"gate":"CRY","wires":[i,j],"param":"cry_ring"}` entries over the same perimeter edges used by the CRX ring. Evidence: mixing two different controlled rotations around the ring should enrich entanglement motifs (just as CRX+CRY hub outperformed pure CRX), and the depth cost is minimal since you reuse the existing adjacency.

2. Split the single “cry_hub_edge” parameter into two orientation-specific parameters: one for horizontal hub edges (`cry_hub_horiz` on wires [8,1] & [8,5]) and one for vertical hub edges (`cry_hub_vert` on wires [8,3] & [8,7]). This adds only one extra parameter but can capture anisotropic center-to-edge correlations that a single shared angle may miss, leveraging the same symmetry-aware sharing that proved effective for RZ/RY layers.

3. Add longer-range CRX entanglement between opposite edge qubits (wires [1,5] and [3,7]) with a shared parameter `crx_opposite`. Insert these two gates in Layer 3 (after the perimeter ring) to capture cross-board patterns (e.g., fork threats) without significantly increasing depth. Evidence: the best program’s lift came from targeted two-qubit diversity—adding these selective, parameterized couplings extends that principle to diagonal correlations.

4. Give each upload its own small “delta” offset on shared parameters: replace each shared key (e.g. `crx_ring`) with a base parameter plus an upload-indexed delta (`crx_ring_base`, `crx_ring_delta_upload0`, `…_upload2`), and in `apply_ansatz_block` sum them when computing the angle. This preserves global parameter sharing for regularization but grants slight per-upload flexibility, potentially boosting expressivity as successive uploads refine different correlation features.

5. Insert a lightweight feature re-upload between the two repeats of each upload: immediately after the first `apply_ansatz_block` call in an upload, invoke `feature_map(board)` again (or a subset of rotations on edges only) before the second repeat. Evidence: while the 3×2 upload layout proved critical, refreshing classical data mid-upload can reinforce gradient signals and has been shown in QML to accelerate convergence without altering total upload count.
