# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Symmetry-based parameter sharing across corners, edges, and center, combined with targeted three-qubit motifs, yielded the top combined score (0.65) in the “Symmetric Tic-Tac-Toe QML Ansatz with ZZZ Motifs” (Generation 9). Using only 66 parameters, it achieved train/val/test accuracies of 0.72/0.70/0.68 and a small generalization gap (0.04).
- Embedding ZZZ rotations aligned to the eight winning lines consistently improved expressivity over flat two-qubit designs. Generation 9 (0.65) outperformed the earlier motif-discovery seed (Generation 8, combined 0.58) and the triplet-only variant (Generation 10, 0.57).
- Introducing shared CRZ couplings between the center and its edges provided domain-guided two-qubit correlations, boosting test accuracy to 0.68 in Generation 9 compared to 0.60 in the pure ZZZ model (Generation 11).
- Retaining a CZ lattice backbone for local entanglement ensured circuit stability across uploads and repeats, as evidenced by zero standard-deviation in Generation 9’s accuracies.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Unconstrained addition of three-qubit ZZZ motifs without symmetry sharing or targeted two-qubit controls (Generation 8) inflated gate count (309) and parameters (174) but stagnated at a combined score of 0.58 and yielded poor parameter efficiency (~0.003).
- Over-parameterizing with all winning-line ZZZs plus full grid CZs (Generation 10) reached 210 parameters and 309 gates yet delivered only 0.57 combined, indicating under-utilization of the heavy ansatz.
- Extreme parameter sharing without sufficient expressivity (Generation 11’s 42-parameter design) produced stable training (gap 0.01) but underfit the data, capping combined score at 0.60.
- Misaligned gate definitions and parameter keys for three-qubit operations (Generation 15) prevented any successful training, demonstrating the importance of strict ANSATZ_SPEC validation.

## Implementation Insights

- Leveraging qml.MultiRZ for ZZZ and qml.ctrl(qml.RZ) for CCRZ seamlessly integrates three-qubit gates into the backprop pipeline, as in Generation 9’s apply_ansatz_block, preserving gradient fidelity.
- Constructing a single ordered list of unique parameter keys (PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK) and a dict mapping keys to positions simplifies block-wise parameter slicing across uploads and repeats.
- Batch-broadcasting the QNode over the sample dimension (~7× speedup) is critical for deep circuits (depth 118) to converge within reasonable time, enabling stable early-stopping as seen in Generation 9.
- Validation-loss early stopping with restore-best-weights produced zero train/validation/test accuracy variance in Generation 9, confirming its role in controlling overfitting while preserving expressivity.

## Performance Analysis

- The current best (Generation 9) achieved a 0.65 combined score, outperforming all other evaluated programs: Generation 8 (0.58), Generation 10 (0.57), and Generation 11 (0.60).
- Depth around 118 layers paired with symmetry sharing and targeted three-qubit motifs hit a “sweet spot.” Shallower variants at depth 94 (Gen 8/10) underperformed despite fewer gates.
- Generalization gaps clustered: 0.04 for Generation 9 versus ~0.07 for heavier motif-only models (Gen 8/10) and 0.01 for the under-parameterized design (Gen 11), illustrating the trade-off between expressivity and overfitting.
- Parameter efficiency peaked only when accuracy was high. Generation 9’s 0.01 efficiency (0.68 test / 66 params) balances model compactness and performance better than bloated (Gen 8/10) or overly compact (Gen 11) designs.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the single “zzz_line” parameter into three orientation-specific keys (“zzz_horiz”, “zzz_vert”, “zzz_diag”). In your ANSATZ_SPEC, map the three horizontal winning-line motifs ([(0,1,2),(7,8,3),(6,5,4)]) to “zzz_horiz”, the verticals ([(0,7,6),(1,8,5),(2,3,4)]) to “zzz_vert”, and the diagonals ([(0,8,4),(2,8,6)]) to “zzz_diag”. This mild increase from 1→3 parameters preserves symmetry sharing within each orientation while giving the model extra flexibility to weight horizontal, vertical, and diagonal correlations differently—mirroring how spatially distinct motifs may contribute unevenly to classification.  
2. Replace the two diagonal ZZZ blocks with a shared CCRZ motif. In ANSATZ_SPEC, remove the ZZZ entries for wires [0,8,4] and [2,8,6] and instead insert a single “CCRZ” gate on each with param key “ccrz_diag_shared”. This tests whether controlled-RZ rotations capture diagonal triple correlations more parameter-efficiently than full ZZZ rotations, extending the success of CRZ center-edge couplings to central diagonal lines.  
3. Introduce complementary XXX-type three-qubit motifs on a subset of winning lines. For example, for the horizontal lines only, wrap each ZZZ MultiRZ with Hadamard gates—H on each wire before and after the MultiRZ—and assign them a new shared key “xxx_horiz”. You can alternate these “XXX” blocks with the original ZZZ blocks across repeats (e.g. odd repeats use ZZZ, even repeats use XXX). This axis-diverse entangling layer probes whether mixing Z- and X-basis triple interactions boosts expressivity without exploding parameter count.  
4. Augment the CZ backbone with parametrized CRY entanglers on center-corner pairs. After the existing fixed CZ edges, insert four qml.CRY gates on wires [0,8], [2,8], [4,8], and [6,8], all sharing a new param key “cry_center_corner”. This adds lightweight Y-basis two-qubit correlations between the center and corners—analogous to the proven CRZ center-edge links—but in a different rotation basis to diversify entanglement patterns.  
5. Break full across-upload symmetry by giving each upload its own local rotation parameters. Instead of a single “rz_pre_corner” shared across all uploads, define “rz_pre_corner_u0”, “rz_pre_corner_u1”, and “rz_pre_corner_u2” (and similarly for ry_corner, rz_pre_edge, etc.). Update PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK accordingly so that each upload block can adjust its pre- and post-local rotations independently. This tests whether modestly lifting upload-level parameter sharing improves adaptation over the three serial feature embeddings without a large depth or gate-count penalty.
