# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- CCRZ-Based Winning-Line Motifs  
  • Generation 13 (“winning_line_ccrz_and_local_mixing”) replaced ZZZ gates on winning lines with doubly-controlled RZ (CCRZ), lifting the combined score to 0.72 (vs. 0.66 in Gen 18 and 0.68 in Gen 24) and driving test accuracy to 0.76 with a tiny 0.01 gap.  
- Four-Layer Symmetry-Grouped Ansätze  
  • The Gen 13 block—symmetry-shared RY, CCRZ line motifs, grid CRZ entanglement, then symmetry-shared RZ/RX—repeated over 3 uploads × 2 repeats, balanced expressivity and compactness. This structure outperformed both flat two-qubit designs (≈0.63 score) and over-deep motif variants.  
- Reduced Circuit Depth  
  • By pruning redundant three-qubit layers and leveraging CCRZ, Gen 13’s depth of 106 undercut the earlier “sweet spot” of ~118 layers (e.g., Gen 21 at depth 118 achieved 0.65), improving convergence speed without sacrificing accuracy.  
- Batch Broadcasting + Early Stopping  
  • All top performers, especially Gen 13, used single-call batch parameter broadcasting (∼7× speedup) with autograd backprop and Adam optimizer, plus validation-loss early stopping (patience = 75, Δ = 1e-4), yielding zero-variance accuracies (std 0.00) across splits.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- ZZZ-Only MultiRZ Motifs  
  • Replacing CCRZ with ZZZ on win-lines (Gen 18 and Gen 19) capped combined scores at 0.66 and 0.64 despite similar parameter counts, indicating that uncontrolled triple-Z rotations under-encode line correlations.  
- Over-Parameterization of Triples  
  • Gen 26’s 126-parameter, depth 123 circuit scored only 0.53 test accuracy (gap 0.10). Adding three-qubit gates indiscriminately inflated depth and gate count (333 gates in Gen 19) without proportional expressive benefit.  
- Excessive Depth without Symmetry Sharing  
  • Depths beyond ~130 (Gen 19: depth 130, score 0.64; Gen 20: depth 142, score 0.63) increased training overhead and overfitting risk, yet failed to improve test accuracy relative to shallower symmetric designs.  
- Omission of Controlled-Phase Structure  
  • Circuits that dropped CCRZ in favor of only ZZZ or single-layer motif insertions (e.g., Gen 16’s flat multi-ZZZ/CCRZ over-motif, combined 0.62) underperformed, confirming the importance of controlled two-in a-row conditioning.  

## Implementation Insights

- Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC & Key-to-Position Mapping  
  • Gen 13’s use of a formal `ANSATZ_SPEC` list and a `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`→index map simplifies slicing a 78-dim parameter vector per block and avoids indexing errors when adding new gates.  
- Leveraging qml.ctrl for CCRZ  
  • Implementing CCRZ via `qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[…])` retained backpropagation fidelity and cleanly encoded two-in-a-row control logic, directly aligning ansatz structure with game rules.  
- Symmetry-Grouped Rotations Reduce Parameters  
  • Grouping corner, edge, and center rotations (RY, RZ, RX) slashed per-block parameters from 84 to 13 unique keys, enforcing geometric priors and matching the winner-pattern motifs.  
- Single-Call Batch QNode with Autograd  
  • The pattern `circuit_z_expectations(boards, params)` broadcast over the batch dimension delivered identical gradients to per-sample loops but ∼7× faster. This was critical for training the depth-106 Gen 13 circuit within 100-epoch budgets.  

## Performance Analysis

- Gen 13 vs. Other Top Candidates  
  • Gen 13 achieved combined 0.72 (train/val/test = 0.76/0.79/0.76, gap 0.01) against the next best Gen 24 at 0.68 (0.79/0.75/0.74, gap 0.05), underscoring CCRZ’s impact.  
- Depth Correlation with Score  
  • Circuits at depths ≲110 (Gen 13 depth 106) outscored those at ~118 (Gen 21: 0.65) and ≳130 (Gen 19: 0.64), suggesting that targeted motif simplification beats uniform depth increases.  
- Parameter Budget vs. Efficiency  
  • While Gen 13 uses 78 parameters (vs. 66 in Gen 21), its higher test accuracy yields the best net parameter efficiency (~0.01), demonstrating that modest parameter increases can pay off when paired with expressive, rule-aligned gates.  
- Generalization Gap Trends  
  • Incorporating CCRZ motifs and symmetry sharing (Gen 13) drove the gap down to 0.01, compared with 0.04–0.06 in ZZZ-heavy or under-shared models, confirming that controlled three-qubit interactions reinforce stable generalization on tic-tac-toe patterns.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the “ccrz_line” key into orientation‐specific CCRZ parameters (“ccrz_horiz”, “ccrz_vert”, “ccrz_diag”).  
   – In your ANSATZ_SPEC, map the three horizontal edge‐to‐edge–corner triples to “ccrz_horiz”, the three vertical triples to “ccrz_vert”, and the two diagonal winning lines to “ccrz_diag.”  
   – This adds only two extra parameters but lets the circuit differentially weight horizontal, vertical and diagonal win‐line correlations, building directly on the CCRZ winning‐line motif that drove Gen 13’s outperformance.  

2. Split “ccrz_center” into two keys: “ccrz_edge_center” and “ccrz_diag_center.”  
   – Assign the two edge–edge–center control triples ([1,5,8], [3,7,8]) to “ccrz_edge_center” and the two corner–center–corner triples ([0,4,8], [2,6,8]) to “ccrz_diag_center.”  
   – This targeted parameterization tests whether center‐involving lines of different geometry benefit from distinct controlled‐phase weights, without bloating depth or gate count.  

3. Add a lightweight CRX entanglement layer mirroring the existing CRZ grid.  
   – Immediately after the CRZ lines in Layer 3, insert CRX gates on the same grid edges and center‐edge pairs, all sharing a new key “crx_grid.”  
   – By diversifying entanglement into the X basis with only one extra parameter, you probe whether cross‐axis two‐qubit correlations further boost expressivity while preserving the depth‐106 sweet spot.  

4. Parameterize the local RZ and RX rotations per upload instead of globally.  
   – Replace keys like “rz_corner” and “rx_corner” with per‐upload variants “rz_corner_u0”/“rz_corner_u1”/“rz_corner_u2” (and likewise for edge/center and RX).  
   – This change gives each of the three feature‐map uploads independent local phase flexibility, testing if mild relaxation of symmetry sharing across uploads improves convergence without exceeding Gen 13’s parameter efficiency.  

5. Alternate the three‐qubit motif basis across repeats by interleaving CCRZ and CCRX.  
   – In repeat 0 of each upload keep your existing CCRZ blocks (qml.ctrl(qml.RZ,…)), but in repeat 1 wrap the same two controls around an RX (qml.ctrl(qml.RX,…)) and share its angle under a new key “ccrx_line.”  
   – This axes-diverse controlled–phase alternation builds on the proven controlled-Z success while injecting complementary controlled-X interactions, potentially capturing orthogonal triple‐qubit correlations without adding extra repeats or depth.
