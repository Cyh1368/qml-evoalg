# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Geometric motif alignment: embedding eight ZZZ rotations on the exact tic-tac-toe winning lines plus CCRZ center/diagonal correlators drove the top performance. The current best ansatz (depth 118, 333 gates, 78 params) achieved a combined score of 0.74 (train 0.80, val 0.83, test 0.78, gap 0.02), mirroring the motif layout of Generations 72 and 77.  
- Five-stage ansatz block repeated over 3 uploads × 2 repeats:  
  1. Local symmetry-grouped RY  
  2. Shared CRZ ring & center couplings  
  3. ZZZ winning-line motifs  
  4. CCRZ high-order correlations  
  5. Compact per-orbit RZ+RX rotations  
  This structure yields the empirically optimal depth (~118) and gate count (~333).  
- Symmetry-grouped parameter sharing into three orbits (corners, edges, center) fixes the learnable parameter count at 78, balancing expressivity and over-/under-fitting. All top performers—including the best program—use exactly 78 params.  
- Single-call batch broadcasting of the QNode for full mini-batches yields ~7× speed-up with exact backprop gradients, enabling stable Adam optimization and validation-loss early stopping (zero std across seeds in best run).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- ZZZ-only motif insertions without matching CCRZ gates plateau performance below ~0.63. For example, Generation 90 (“zzz_winline_focus”) reached only a combined 0.61 (test 0.62), and Generation 80 (“none”) stalled at 0.62 (test 0.63).  
- Excessive circuit depth (>140) or gate count (>380) inflated complexity but degraded accuracy. Generation 84 (“post_motif_cz_refinement”) with depth 166/405 gates scored 0.64 combined, and Generation 92 (“zzz_ring_symmetry_crossover”) with 124/387 hit only test 0.71.  
- Unstructured triple-gate placements (swap_zzz_early in Gen 79) raised depth to 117/333 gates but only reached a combined 0.68 (test 0.72), underscoring that random or misaligned triples offer limited payoff.  
- Designs deviating from 78 parameters (previous over-parameterized ~96 params or under-parameterized 60 params) also fell off the performance cliff (combined ≤0.63), confirming the sweet spot at exactly 78 learnable angles.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Final‐rotation compression: tying each RZ+RX pair under a single per-orbit “rot_*” parameter (corners, edges, center) reduces parameter overhead without sacrificing local expressivity. This trick appears in the best program and the Gen 79 parameter‐compression patch.  
- Game-specific correlations via qml.MultiRZ (ZZZ) on each winning line and qml.ctrl(qml.RZ) (CCRZ) for center/diagonal triples are critical. The best program’s test accuracy of 0.78 hinges on these well-aligned higher-order gates.  
- Enforcement of circuit constraints is automated by Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript to extract depth and gate count, capping designs to ≤120 depth and ≤333 gates—a regime shown to maximize accuracy.  
- Clean parameter indexing across re-uploads/repeats (block_index × N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK + key-lookup) simplifies multi-block ansatz application and ensures consistent sharing across repeats.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The best ansatz (78 params, depth 118, 333 gates) leads by a full 0.06–0.13 Combined‐Score margin over the next-best variants (Gen 79 at 0.68, Gen 92 at ~0.70, Gen 84 at 0.64, Gen 90 at 0.61).  
- Circuit depth tightly correlates with score: staying within 112–120 layers yields peak performance; shifting outside this window—either too shallow (82 layers → 0.61) or too deep (166 layers → 0.64)—drops the combined score by ≥0.05.  
- Gate counts hovering around 333 gates maximize accuracy; both leaner (<201 gates) and heavier (>380 gates) designs underperform despite similar parameter counts.  
- Uniform parameter efficiency (~0.01 across all 78‐param runs) indicates that structured inductive biases (geometry + symmetry + motif alignment), not raw parameter count, drive the gains.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce per-upload, orbit-specific feature-map scales. Replace the single FEATURE_SCALE constant with three trainable parameters per upload—`feat_corner_u`, `feat_edge_u`, `feat_center_u` for upload u—and scale each RX in `feature_map` by its corresponding orbit and upload. This preserves the 78-parameter budget while giving the circuit the ability to learn how strongly to inject new game data into corners, edges, and the center at each re-upload step, potentially accelerating motif alignment in early blocks.

2. Diversify triple-interaction axes on the winning lines. Keep the eight ZZZ motifs on the rows under `zzz_rows`, but swap the four column triples for XXX rotations (`qml.MultiRX`) under a new `xxx_cols` parameter, and leave the two diagonals as ZZZ under `zzz_diags`. To maintain three triple parameters per block, merge `zzz_rows` and `zzz_diags` into a single `zzz_all` key, freeing one slot for `xxx_cols`. This tests whether alternating Z- and X-based entanglement boosts expressivity without changing depth or gate count.

3. Insert a symmetry-grouped controlled-RY layer mid-block. Immediately after the ring and radial CRZ gates in Layer 2, add four controlled-RYs from each edge into the center qubit—wires `[1,8],[3,8],[5,8],[7,8]`—all sharing a single `cry_center` parameter. To keep each block at 13 parameters, drop the final RX on one of the four edges (e.g. wire 7) in Layer 5. This Y-axis coupling may sharpen center–edge correlations that Z-only gates do not capture, building on the success of structured high-order correlations.

4. Alternate the order of high-order motifs between repeats. Modify `apply_ansatz_block` so that for even `block_index` you apply the four CCRZ gates before the eight ZZZ motifs (swapping Layers 3 and 4), while odd blocks retain the original ZZZ-then-CCRZ order. All parameter keys and counts remain identical. This tests whether interleaving high-order center/diagonal correlations earlier in some repeats accelerates hierarchical motif formation, leveraging the same geometric motifs that drove the best results.

5. Compress CRZ parameters to allocate a new diagonal CRX coupling. Tie `crz_ring` and `crz_center` into a single `crz_shared` parameter, freeing one slot per block. Use the freed parameter to add four controlled-X gates (`qml.CRX`) from the corner qubits into the center on diagonal pairs `[0,8]`, `[2,8]`, `[4,8]`, `[6,8]` under a shared `crx_diag` key. This maintains the depth (~118) and gate count (~333) while exploring whether X-axis two-body couplings along the diagonals complement the existing ZZZ and CCRZ motifs.
