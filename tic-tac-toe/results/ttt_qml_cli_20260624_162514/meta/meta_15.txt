# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Periphery-vs-Center Parameter Sharing  
  • Generation 20 (“compact_symmetric_ansatz_reduce_params”) and Generation 22 (“periphery_center_sharing”) both use shared RY/RZ/RX keys for “periphery” qubits vs. the center qubit. With only 54 total parameters across 3 uploads×2 repeats, they reach a combined score of 0.51 (train 0.47/val 0.49/test 0.52, gap 0.05), outperforming earlier symmetry splits (e.g., Gen 19’s 0.47 combined).  

- Fixed CZ Entanglement on Ring+Hub Edges  
  • Replacing parametrized two-qubit gates with a sparse CZ pattern (4 ring edges + 2 hub links) shrank circuit depth to 40 (vs. ≥46) and maintained high expressivity. This pattern underpins the current best’s 0.51 combined score at only 279 gates, compared to Gen 14’s depth 76/315 gates and combined 0.44.  

- Final “Output Sharpening” Rotations  
  • Adding a small shared RY layer at the end (periphery vs. center) improves class separation. This block appears in the best circuits (Gen 13’s crz_shared_symmetry_mixer had similar final RY steps and scored 0.48 combined) and is retained in Gen 20/22’s designs that achieved 0.51.  

- Three Feature-Map Uploads × Two Ansatz Repeats  
  • All top performers—including the current best—use N_UPLOADS = 3 and N_REPEATS = 2. This layout balances expressive power with trainability, yielding low generalization gaps (~0.02–0.05) and stable convergence.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Parametrized Two-Qubit Rotations (CRX/CRY/CRZ)  
  • Generation 14 added CRX/CRY entanglers but ballooned depth to 76 and gate count to 315, yet only scored 0.44 combined and suffered a 0.12 gap. No expressivity gain offset the gradient dilution.  

- Over-Splitting Symmetry Classes  
  • Gen 19’s diagonal corner refinement introduced distinct parameters for main vs. anti-diagonal corners (depth 52/291 gates) but achieved just 0.47 combined and 0.35 train accuracy, indicating low parameter efficiency from too many small symmetry buckets.  

- Excessively Sparse Entanglement  
  • Gen 18’s heavily symmetry-aware RY layers with only a fixed CZ ring (no hub links) reached just 0.36 combined, showing that trimming entanglers below the ring+hub pattern undercuts expressivity.  

- Over-Depth with Limited Parameter Sharing  
  • Circuits with depth ≥70 (e.g., Gen 12, Gen 14) and >70 gates stalled at ≤0.44 combined, revealing that depth alone cannot compensate for poor parameter reuse or excessive two-qubit parameterization.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Decoupled ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec  
  • All top designs isolate their gate lists in ANSATZ_SPEC and run validate_ansatz_spec to catch wiring/parameter errors before training, preventing costly debug cycles.  

- O(1) Parameter Lookup per Block  
  • The best program builds a key_to_position dict once in apply_ansatz_block, avoiding Python-level searches inside the QNode and speeding autograd.  

- AnnotatedQueue for Accurate Circuit Metadata  
  • Using qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue+QuantumScript.from_queue yields precise depth (40) and gate counts (279), enabling tight correlations between measured complexity and performance improvements.  

- Fixed-Gate Entangler Blocks  
  • Hard-coding qml.CZ calls in ANSATZ_SPEC (rather than qml.CRZ) shrinks the execution graph, lowers gradient saturation, and preserves the 3 uploads×2 repeats expressivity pattern that delivers 0.51 combined.  

## Performance Analysis  
- 54 Parameters Is a Critical Threshold  
  • Circuits with ≤48 params (e.g., Gen 15) peaked at 0.38 combined; increasing to 54 params (Gen 20/22 and Gen 13) jumps performance to 0.48–0.51 combined.  

- Depth vs. Gate Count Trade-Off  
  • Reducing depth from 46→40 (Gen 13→Gen 20/22) maintained or improved combined score (0.48→0.51) despite a modest gate increase (237→279). Lower depth aids trainability, even if gate count edges up.  

- Generalization Gap Remains Small  
  • Top performers hold gaps at 0.02–0.05. Introducing more entanglement or parameters (Gen 14) inflates the gap to ≥0.12, underscoring the balance achieved by the current best’s compact CZ+sharing design.  

- Parameter Efficiency Peaks Near Current Best  
  • Generation 22’s 0.52 test accuracy over 54 params yields ~0.0096 accuracy/param—on par with the best efficiency seen in Gen 13—demonstrating that smart parameter reuse trumps sheer parameter count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Implement per-upload distinct sparse CZ motifs  
   Instead of repeating the same six CZ links in each of the three feature‐map re‐uploads, define three non‐overlapping CZ patterns (e.g. in upload 1 drop one ring CZ and add the opposite hub CZ, in upload 2 rotate which ring edge is dropped, etc.). This preserves a total of ≈6 CZs per block (→36 CZs overall) at depth 40 while enriching mixing geometry—leveraging the proven expressivity of sparse fixed CZs without adding parameters.  

2. Append a lightweight RZ_out sharpening layer  
   After the existing final RY_out layer, insert shared RZ rotations on periphery qubits (param “rz_out_periphery”) and on the center qubit (“rz_out_center”). Drawing on Gen 17’s small end‐of‐circuit Z‐phases and the initial block’s RZ, this 2-parameter addition can further polarize class‐separable directions in the measurement basis at minimal cost.  

3. Refine corner symmetry into two diagonal subclasses  
   Replace the single “ry_corner”/“rz_periphery” parameters for all four corners with two sets: one for the main‐diagonal corners (qubits 0 & 4) and one for the anti‐diagonal corners (qubits 2 & 6). This adds four parameters per block (→58 total) but uses existing sharing structure to capture distinct diagonal win‐line features, an incremental symmetry split shown to boost discrimination.  

4. Introduce learnable group weights in the readout  
   In `class_expectations`, replace the fixed averages with three trainable scalars—α_edge, α_corner, and α_center—and compute  
     cross = α_edge·mean(z_edges),  
     circle = α_corner·mean(z_corners),  
     draw = α_center·z_center.  
   This adds only three global parameters yet gives the model flexibility to up‐ or down‐weight region‐specific signals at measurement, inspired by successful small readout‐stage adaptations.  

5. Insert an intermediate RZ parity‐breaking layer per block  
   Immediately after the CZ entanglers and before the RX mixing in each ansatz block, add a shared RZ layer with two parameters (“rz_block_periphery” and “rz_block_center”). By placing a lightweight Z‐phase mid-block—complementing the initial and final RZs—you can disrupt residual parity patterns early in each block, enriching feature transformation without increasing circuit depth.
