# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Fine-grained symmetry splits on single-qubit rotations:  
  • Current best (Gen 39, Combined 0.55) breaks RY/RZ/RX into three groups (corner/edge/center), boosting per-block params from 9→12 and raising combined score from 0.53 (Gen 36) to 0.55.  
- Fixed CZ-only entanglement on a reduced grid:  
  • Replacing parametric CRX/CRZ hubs with six CZ links cut depth from 52→40 and gate-count 291→279, improving generalization gap from ~0.15 to 0.02 (Gen 39).  
- Compact “output-sharpening” shared RY layer:  
  • Including a final grouped RY on corners, edges, center—present in Gen 36, Gen 39 (0.55), but missing or weakened in lower-scoring variants—consistently adds ~3–4 test-accuracy points.  
- Moderate re-upload × repeat structure (3 × 2):  
  • Top performers (Gen 32/36/39) all use 3 uploads and 2 ansatz repeats (6 blocks total), balancing expressivity against depth.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parametrized two-qubit gates:  
  • Gen 46 (78 params, depth 76, Combined 0.38) and Gen 48 (48 params, depth 70, Combined 0.41) both underfit (train ~0.32/0.27), showing that bloating with CRX/CRZ or extra CZ links dilutes gradient signals.  
- Excessive circuit depth without smarter patterns:  
  • Depth > 58 (Gen 46 depth 76, Gen 48 depth 70) correlates with combined scores ≤0.41, despite more parameters.  
- Heavy symmetry sharing with sparse entanglement:  
  • Gen 45 (54 params, Combined 0.46) and Gen 44 (60 params, Combined 0.51) used aggressive per-block sharing but limited entanglement, leading to underfitting and generalization gaps ~0.10.  
- Uniform or missing output mixing:  
  • Circuits lacking the grouped RX‐RY-RZ symmetry pattern or final RY sharpening fail to reach Combined ≥ 0.50.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Early ansatz validation and O(1) parameter lookup:  
  • Using `validate_ansatz_spec` to catch spec errors and building `key_to_position` once per block (Gen 39, current best) slims the autodiff graph and prevents wasted training.  
- Precise circuit metadata extraction:  
  • Leveraging `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` + `QuantumScript.from_queue` yields exact depth/gate_count, enabling tight performance–complexity correlations (Gen 39 reports depth 40, gate_count 279).  
- Direct use of primitive gates:  
  • Favoring `qml.RX/RY/RZ` and `qml.CZ` over parameterized two-qubit abstractions reduces QNode graph size and speeds backprop, as demonstrated by the current best.  
- Structured ANSATZ_SPEC layering:  
  • The best program’s spec sequences initial RY→RZ→CZ→RX→RY across three symmetry groups, repeated in 6 blocks, highlighting the power of a small, well-tuned building block.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Peak combined score at moderate params & low depth:  
  • Gen 39 (72 params, depth 40) hits 0.55, outperforming all Generation 44–48 variants (Combined 0.38–0.52) with higher depths or fewer params.  
- Sweet spot in parameter count:  
  • Combined scores rise from 0.41 (@48 params) →0.46 (@54) →0.51 (@60) →0.52 (@66) →0.55 (@72), then collapse to 0.38 (@78), pinpointing ~60–75 params as optimal.  
- Depth vs. score trade-off:  
  • Circuits ≤52 depth score ≥0.50, with the best at depth 40; depths >58 see Combined ≤0.41.  
- Generalization gap aligns with entanglement design:  
  • Gen 39’s minimal gap (0.02) follows its fixed CZ pattern; by contrast, richer entanglers in Gen 46/G48 yield gaps ≥0.15, despite similar test accuracies.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the shared RZ‐phase and RX mixing parameters into upload-specific keys. For each of the six “rz_corner/edge/center” and “rx_corner/edge/center” parameters, create three variants (e.g. `rz_corner_up0`, `rz_corner_up1`, `rz_corner_up2`, and likewise for edges and centers). This adds only 18 scalars, leaves depth and gate count unchanged, and mirrors the fine-grained symmetry splits that lifted Combined scores by ~0.02 in rotated-CZ seeds.  

2. Insert a lightweight grouped mid-block Z-phase layer immediately before each CZ entangler set. Add three new parameters—`rz_mid_corner`, `rz_mid_edge`, `rz_mid_center`—and apply RZ to corners, edges, and center once per block. This +1-depth, +3-param miniblock builds on the ~3–4-point accuracy lift seen with additional output RY layers and sharpens intra-block decision boundaries.  

3. Extend the final output-sharpening stage by appending a grouped RZ rotation alongside the existing `ry_out_…`. Introduce `rz_out_corner`, `rz_out_edge`, and `rz_out_center` applied immediately after the last RY. This +1-depth, +3-param tweak diversifies output-axis mixing in the same symmetry grouping that consistently boosted test accuracy in Gen 36/39.  

4. Enrich fixed entanglement with two sparse diagonal CZ links per block without adding parameters. In each block, include CZ(0, 3) and CZ(2, 7) in addition to the existing perimeter and hub CZs. These diagonal connections mimic earlier beneficial orthogonal mixing, add only +2 gates (keeping total depth ≤52), and maintain the minimal generalization gap of the CZ-only motif.  

5. Explore an alternative re-upload vs. repeat schedule: 2 uploads × 3 repeats (still 6 blocks total). By shifting from a 3×2 to a 2×3 structure, you concentrate expressivity into deeper consecutive ansatz repeats per feature upload, preserving overall depth and parameter count. This structural variant probes whether a different upload-repeat balance can further optimize the trade-off between feature re-injection and circuit depth.
