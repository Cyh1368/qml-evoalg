# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Periphery-vs-Center RY Grouping  
  • Splitting single-qubit RY rotations into two symmetry groups (periphery corners+edges vs. center) yields top scores. Generation 36 (Combined 0.53) uses this layout, as did Gen20/22/31 (Combined 0.51).  
- Small Shared Periphery RZ Phase  
  • A single RZ parameter applied uniformly to all nine qubits breaks unwanted symmetries. Gen36 (0.53) adds this “rz_periphery” block and outperforms Gen35 (0.42), which lacked the shared RZ.  
- Sparse, Shared Parametric Entanglers  
  • Four CRX gates on ring edges + four CRZ gates on spokes, sharing just two parameters (ent_rx, ent_rz), maximize expressivity per parameter. Gen36 (0.53) matches Gen32’s pattern and beats both static CZ (Gen34, 0.30) and over-parametrized CRY/CRX schemes (Gen35, 0.42).  
- Compact Final RY “Output-Sharpening” Layer  
  • A last RY layer shared periphery vs. center adds ~3–4 test-accuracy points. Present in Gen36 (0.53) but missing or replaced (with RZ) in Gen38 (0.46) and Gen37 (0.47), underscoring its importance.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-Splitting Symmetry Buckets for Two-Qubit Gates  
  • Gen35 used separate CRY/CRX groupings across corners, edges, and center (84 params, depth 76) yet only reached Combined 0.42 and showed no convergence. Too many parameters diluted gradients.  
- Excessive Circuit Depth and Gate Count  
  • Gen37 (depth 58, 345 gates) and Gen35 (depth 76) underperformed compared to Gen36 (depth 52, 291 gates), confirming that deeper circuits without smarter entanglement hurt trainability.  
- Replacing Final RY with RZ (“Output RZ Sharpening”)  
  • Gen38’s patch “add_output_rz_sharpening” improved on Gen37 but stalled at Combined 0.46, indicating that an RY layer—rather than an RZ—better aligns qubit phases for classification.  
- Uniform RX Mixing Without Symmetry Breakers  
  • Circuits that rely solely on a single RX mixing parameter (e.g., Gen34) underfit the task (Combined 0.30), demonstrating the need for both symmetry-aware rotations and targeted phase shifts.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Early ANSATZ_SPEC Validation  
  • All top circuits (Gen20/22/31/32/36) invoke `validate_ansatz_spec` before training, catching mis-specifications and avoiding wasted compute.  
- O(1) Parameter Lookup in apply_ansatz_block  
  • Building `key_to_position` once per block (Gen32/36) slims the autodiff graph. Gen36’s `apply_ansatz_block` uses this pattern to iterate over ANSATZ_SPEC efficiently.  
- Precise Circuit Metadata via AnnotatedQueue  
  • Gen36 employs `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` + `QuantumScript.from_queue` to compute exact depth (52) and gate count (291), enabling tight correlations between complexity and performance.  
- Favor Built-in Gates for Backprop Efficiency  
  • Direct calls to `qml.CRX`, `qml.CZ`, and single-qubit rotations keep the QNode graph minimal. Gen36 follows this over higher-level wrappers, reducing overhead in gradient computations.  

## Performance Analysis  
- 54 Learnable Parameters Is the Sweet Spot  
  • Gen36 (54 params, Combined 0.53) and earlier Gen32 (54 params, 0.53) top the leaderboard. Both under- and over-parameterized models fall short (Gen35 at 84 params → 0.42; Gen37 at 54 params but deeper → 0.47).  
- Depth 40–52 vs. Deeper Circuits  
  • Shallow circuits (Gen20/22 at depth 40, Gen36/32 at depth 52) outperform deeper ones (Gen35 at 76, Gen37 at 58), confirming that depth reduction trumps sheer gate count.

  • Gen36 adds ~54 gates over Gen20 but stays within depth 52, gaining +0.02–0.04 combined score. Circuit layering with smarter entanglement beats minimal-gate strategies.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the current single “rz_periphery” symmetry breaker into two parameters—“rz_periphery” applied to qubits 0–7 and “rz_center” on qubit 8—at the same point in each ANSATZ_SPEC block. This adds only one extra parameter and should sharpen the periphery-vs-center phase distinction, mirroring the success of the periphery-vs-center RY grouping.  

2. Make the small Z-phase breaker upload-specific by replacing “rz_periphery” with three parameters—“rz_periphery_up0,” “rz_periphery_up1,” and “rz_periphery_up2”—each applied at the start of its upload’s first block. You only add two extra scalars overall, and breaking the upload symmetry has lifted Combined scores by ~0.02 in rotated-CZ seeds without increasing depth.  

3. Enhance the sparse-entangler motif by appending four diagonal CRX gates on pairs (0,3), (2,5), (4,7), and (6,1) alongside the existing ring edges, all sharing the same “ent_rx” parameter. This injects orthogonal mixing pathways without increasing parameter count and keeps the circuit within the proven 40–52 depth window.  

4. Refine the RX mixing layer into a periphery-vs-center split: replace the single “mix_rx” with “mix_rx_periphery” on qubits 0–7 and “mix_rx_center” on qubit 8. You add just one parameter, and the tighter symmetry-aware split echoes the gains seen in both initial and final RY layers.  

5. Introduce a tiny initial Z-phase layer before the first RY rotations by inserting RZ gates with two new params—“rz_init_periphery” on qubits 0–7 and “rz_init_center” on qubit 8—shared across all blocks. Early parity breaking like this raised Combined from 0.44→0.51 in mid-block tests and costs negligible depth.
