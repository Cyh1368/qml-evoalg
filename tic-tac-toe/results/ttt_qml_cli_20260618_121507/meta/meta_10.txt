# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Structured CRY “line” entanglers on every adjacent grid edge driving two‐cell agreement features  
   – Generation 9 (“trainable_cry_line_features”) and the current best program both apply CRY gates between all adjacent qubit pairs to capture two-in-a-row correlations, lifting the combined score from ~0.39 to 0.53.  
- D₄‐orbit‐shared RY decoders on corners, edges, and center for sign extraction  
   – Sharing a single RY parameter across corner qubits, one across edges, and one on the center reduces to 72 total parameters (from 96+ in other designs) without loss of expressivity, enabling the top 0.53 combined score (Generations 9/10).  
- Center‐to‐edge broadcast via CRY entanglers for diagonal and center‐line evidence  
   – The best program’s “cry_center_to_edge” gates provide a low‐depth path for nonadjacent qubit correlations, matching Generation 9/10’s depth≈102 and gate_count≈285 with peak test accuracy 0.52.  
- Three feature‐map reuploads × two ansatz repeats balancing depth vs. expressivity  
   – All top‐scoring circuits (Gen 9, Gen 10, and the current best) use N_UPLOADS=3 and N_REPEATS=2, avoiding overfitting (generalization gaps ~0.02–0.08) while reaching validation_accuracy≈0.56.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Orbit‐shared decoders without explicit line‐segment entanglers underfitted (low test accuracy)  
   – Generations 14 (combined 0.45) and 15 (0.44) used only perimeter/center CRY edges but lacked full adjacent‐cell entanglers, yielding underfitting despite generalization gaps ≤0.08.  
- Over‐parameterized or poorly focused entanglement patterns failed to converge  
   – Generation 16 added diagonal corner‐to‐center CRY gates (84 parameters, combined 0.38) but still missed winning‐line features, resulting in high loss (~2.88) and no convergence.  
- Unstructured two‐qubit mixers (CNOT/CRX) collapsed performance  
   – Prior attempts swapping CRY for CNOT/CRX (Gen 11 “cnot_crx_line_mixer”) stagnated around 0.39 combined score and zero parameter efficiency, showing that symmetry‐aligned, trainable rotations are critical.  
- Shallow orbit‐split entanglers lacked expressivity  
   – Generation 18’s split‐orbit entangler design (combined 0.33) underperformed despite symmetry sharing and repeated reuploads, indicating that explicit two‐cell and center‐broadcast features are indispensable.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Maintain ANSATZ_SPEC as a declarative list of gate dictionaries with shared `param` keys  
   – The current best program’s use of validate_ansatz_spec → PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK ensures consistent auto‐indexed parameters across uploads/repeats, minimizing spec errors and boilerplate.  
- Precompute a `key_to_position` map once per block to avoid repeated lookups  
   – apply_ansatz_block builds this map in O(num_params) then iterates ANSATZ_SPEC, striking a balance between readability and runtime efficiency.  
- Fixed RX feature map encoding cell values into Pauli‐X rotations (±2π/3)  
   – Aligning the input encoding with RY decoders (“no wasted RX”) ensures that trainable decoders operate directly on the sign‐encoded quadrature, as in the best program and Generations 9/10.  
- Grouped Z‐expectations into three class logits without extra classical layers  
   – Summing edge, corner, and center measurements into [cross, circle, draw] directly leverages the circuit’s structure, simplifying the interface and focusing training on quantum features.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Peak combined score 0.53 vs. 0.44–0.45 for next‐best orbit‐shared variants  
   – Generation 9/10 and the current best both hit combined=0.53, while other orbit‐shared ansätze plateaued at 0.44–0.45 (Gens 14, 15, 16).  
- Parameter count 72 vs. 84–96 with similar or worse accuracy  
   – The best program’s 72 parameters yield test_accuracy=0.52, outperforming 96‐parameter designs (Gens 14/15: test≈0.37–0.44) and 84‐parameter Gen 16 (test≈0.25).  
- Depth≈102 and gate_count≈285 optimal for underfitting control  
   – Deeper circuits (depth≥138 in Gen 15/16) did not boost accuracy, reinforcing that targeted entanglement matters more than sheer depth.  
- Stable generalization gaps (~0.02–0.08) across top designs confirm underfitting as the primary limitation  
   – Even the best circuit leaves room for expressivity gains; however, its balanced depth and symmetry exploitation deliver the highest observed test performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the shared “cry_pair_to_edge” parameter into two orbit-specific keys—“cry_pair_horiz” for the four horizontal adjacencies and “cry_pair_vert” for the four vertical adjacencies. This adds only two extra parameters but lets the circuit weight row vs. column two-cell agreements independently, directly targeting the underfitting gap while preserving the proven full-adjacency CRY pattern.  

2. Likewise, refine the center-line aggregators by replacing “cry_pair_to_center” with separate “cry_center_to_row” (for middle-row pairs [7,8] & [3,8]) and “cry_center_to_col” (for middle-column pairs [1,8] & [5,8]). This small orbit split maintains symmetry-sharing but grants the network the flexibility to tune horizontal vs. vertical center aggregation strengths.  

3. Augment ANSATZ_SPEC with an explicit edge-to-corner CRY layer: add CRY gates on the eight edge-corner adjacent pairs (e.g. [1,0], [1,2], [3,2], …) under a new shared parameter “cry_edge_to_corner.” By encoding off-axis two-cell correlations in the same low-depth fashion as the existing line entanglers, you preserve depth≈102 and gate_count≈285 while enriching diagonal winning-line features.  

4. Introduce a parallel phase-based entangling layer by appending CRZ gates immediately after each existing CRY block, using orbit-shared parameters like “crz_pair_to_edge”, “crz_center_to_row/col”, and “crz_center_to_edge.” Dual-axis rotations (Y and Z) have boosted expressivity in QML and slot seamlessly into the declarative ANSATZ_SPEC without altering the upload/repeat layout.  

5. Make the fixed RX feature-map scale trainable per orbit by replacing the constant FEATURE_SCALE with three new parameters (“rx_corner_scale”, “rx_edge_scale”, “rx_center_scale”) and applying RX(FEATURE_SCALE_param × board_value) in feature_map. This lets the model learn the optimal input-encoding angles to align with the RY decoders, building on the insight that “no-wasted RX” improves signal extraction.
