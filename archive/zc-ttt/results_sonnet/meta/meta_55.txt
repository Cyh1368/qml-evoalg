# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Strict RY→CRZ→RZ “sandwich” layout  
  • Seed program (Combined 0.63, gap 0.09; 162 params, depth 41, 207 gates) and its close variant Gen 32 (Combined 0.62, gap 0.09; 162/41/207) both use exactly one encoding layer (RY), one entangling layer (CRZ), and one readout layer (RZ).  
  • This clear separation of roles yields the top observed score.  
- Hub-and-cluster CRZ entanglement with minimal symmetric sharing  
  • Seed’s hub qubit 0 connects to four leaves using only two unique parameters (“crz_2” reused twice), plus two small secondary clusters each sharing a single parameter across two edges, totaling 12 CRZ gates but only 12 unique params.  
  • This pattern achieves strong connectivity and expressivity without inflating the parameter count, matching the sweet spot of ~162 params.  
- Shallow, gate-efficient circuits  
  • Depth ≃41 and gate count ≃207 is consistently optimal: only circuits in this range (Seed, Gen 32) reach Combined ≥0.62.  
  • All deeper or heavier ansätze—despite more complex interactions—fail to exceed 0.61.  
- Explicit, unique parameter naming and full wire specification  
  • Seed’s use of distinct names (“ry_0…8”, “crz_0…11”, “rz_post_0…8”) and explicit wires lists (e.g. wires=[0,2]) avoids optimizer conflicts and ordering ambiguity, resulting in zero run-to-run variance.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mid-circuit RY “refresh”  
  • Shared-Parameter CRZ 9-Qubit Ansatz (Combined 0.60; depth 47, gates 231) and Symmetric CRZ Nine-Qubit Ansatz (Combined 0.61; depth 44, gates 231) both add a secondary RY layer mid-circuit.  
  • These refresh layers incur extra depth/gates with only marginal or negative impact on test accuracy, pushing depth above the optimal window.  
- Incorporation of unstructured three-qubit gates (ZZZ, CCRZ)  
  • Hub-Shared CRZ with 3-Qubit Interactions (Combined 0.57; depth 87, gates 309) and 9-Qubit RY-CRZ-CCRZ Ansatz (Combined 0.57; depth 59, gates 219) both underperform despite adding 3-body correlations.  
  • The added complexity neither improves convergence nor generalization, instead bloating gate count and reducing Combined scores by ≥0.06.  
- Excessive circuit depth/gate count  
  • Any ansatz deeper than ≃45 or with more than ≃210 gates (e.g., Shared-Parameter CRZ Hub-ZZZ Ansatz: depth 63, gates 219 → Combined 0.59) fails to match seed’s performance.  
- Over- or under-sharing of entangler parameters  
  • Aggressive reuse in Symmetric Hub CRZ Ansatz (150 params, depth 41 → Combined 0.59, gap 0.12) collapses expressivity.  
  • Conversely, over-parameterization (186 params in Gen 53/56) yields zero parameter efficiency without accuracy gains.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Keep all variant code confined to ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • The seed separates circuit definition from backend/training logic, ensuring experiments only explore gate patterns without side effects.  
- Use a single, consistent entangling block  
  • Seed issues all CRZ gates in one contiguous block, preserving clear parameter-to-gate mapping and reducing optimization complexity.  
- Enforce strict layer ordering for gradient stability  
  • Avoid interleaving additional rotations between entangler and readout layers (mid-circuit RY steps introduce gradient noise).  
- Leverage symmetric sharing sparingly but label each shared parameter uniquely  
  • As in seed, reuse a parameter name at most twice on symmetric edges (e.g., “crz_2” on [0,7] and [0,8]), rather than broad reuse across many wires.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Depth and gate count correlate inversely with Combined score  
  • Only depth ≤41 and gate count ≤207 achieve Combined ≥0.62; once depth exceeds 44 or gates surpass ~210, Combined never exceeds 0.61.  
- Parameter count sweet spot at ~162  
  • Both seed (162 params) and Symmetric CRZ Nine-Qubit Ansatz (162 params, Combined 0.61) hit the optimal parameter range; fewer params (150) or more (168–186) degrade Combined by ≥0.02.  
- Generalization gap remains stable around 0.09–0.10 for optimal designs  
  • Seed’s gap of 0.09 aligns with Gen 32 and Symmetric CRZ Nine-Qubit (0.10), whereas over-complex circuits show either larger gaps (0.11–0.15) or artificially low gaps at the expense of accuracy (e.g. Hub-Shared CRZ with 3-Qubit: gap 0.04 but Combined 0.57).  
- Combined score improvements trace directly to circuit simplicity and targeted sharing  
  • The +0.02 gain of the seed over the next best (0.61) arises solely from keeping depth/gate well in the optimal band and applying minimal, well-labeled parameter sharing.
