# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mixed-CRZ/CRY entanglement on ~12 high‐centrality edges  
  • Generation 8 (current best) interleaves six CRZ and six CRY gates on strategically chosen pairs, yielding a combined score of 0.60 (train 0.75/val 0.70/test 0.63).  
  • Outperforms CRZ‐only designs (Gen 14: 12 CRZ, score 0.57) and CRX‐inclusive variants (Gen 21: mixed CRZ/CRX/CRY, score 0.58).  
- RY → entangler → RZ “sandwich” structure  
  • Both Gen 8 and Gen 21 follow a single RY layer, the full entangling block, then a final RZ layer, achieving zero train/val std and smooth convergence.  
- Depth ≈ 41 and gate count ≈ 207 sweet spot  
  • Gen 8 (depth 41, 207 gates) and Gen 21 (41, 207) hit the highest combined (0.60/0.58) and test accuracies. Circuits deeper than ~47 or shallower than ~39 degrade performance.  
- Targeted parametric two-qubit gates without three-qubit interactions  
  • Limiting to ~12 CRZ/CRY gates (no ZZZ or CCRZ) sustains expressivity without overfitting, as seen in Gen 8’s top test accuracy.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- CRZ‐only or CZ‐heavy hub-and-spoke layouts  
  • Gen 14 (hub‐based CRZ only) scored 0.57; Gen 16 (hub-and-spoke with shared ZZZ term) scored 0.55. Both suffered zero parameter efficiency and moderate test accuracy.  
- Excessive depth (> 50) and gate count (> 225)  
  • Gen 18 & Gen 20 (depth ~59, gates 207–225) capped at 0.55–0.58 combined, with generalization gaps up to 0.14.  
- Shared-parameter multi-qubit correlations  
  • Gen 16’s shared ZZZ layer cut unique parameters but stagnated combined score (0.55) and offered no efficiency gain.  
- Overparameterization via dense controlled-rotation blocks  
  • Gen 21’s 13 mid-layer CRZ/CRX/CRY gates (180 params) delivered 0.58 combined, underperforming the balanced CRZ/CRY mix in Gen 8.

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-gate parameter naming  
  • Gen 8 labels each entangler (crz_0…crz_5, cry_0…cry_5) and post-RZ (rz_post_0…rz_post_8), avoiding collisions and yielding train/val std = 0.00.  
- Strict gate ordering: RY first, then all two-qubit entanglers, then RZ  
  • Simplifies gradient flow; shared by both Gen 8 and Gen 21, correlates with stable convergence.  
- Omit three-qubit gates  
  • Top performers (Gen 8/Gen 21) exclude ZZZ/CCRZ, while designs that included them (e.g., Gen 16) did not improve scores.  
- Balanced phase/amplitude entangling  
  • CRZ (phase) plus CRY (amplitude) in Gen 8 outperforms designs mixing in CRX or static CZ blocks.

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score hierarchy  
  • Gen 8 leads at 0.60; second-tier includes Gen 18 and Gen 21 at 0.58; hub-spoke variants cluster at 0.55–0.57.  
- Test accuracy vs. generalization gap  
  • Best test accuracy (0.63) in Gen 8 comes with a 0.12 gap. Gen 18 shrinks gap to 0.10 (test 0.59) but at a lower combined score.  
- Parameter count sweet spot  
  • 180 parameters (Gen 8/21) strike the best balance; trimming to ~150 (Gen 18) lowers combined by ~0.02 despite a smaller gap.  
- Depth/gate correlations  
  • Depth ~41 and ~207 gates are tightly correlated with top performance; deviations above 50 or below 39 in depth consistently underperform.

This preserves depth ≃41 and gate count ≃207, tests whether reallocating capacity to amplitude rotations in the “sandwich” structure further stabilizes convergence, and builds directly on the proven RY→entangler→RZ pattern.

By introducing controlled-X entanglement axes while holding total two-qubit gate count and depth constant, you probe whether a three-axis parametric block can boost expressivity beyond the balanced CRZ/CRY mix without overfitting.

This refocuses phase entangling power onto a more influential topology—consistent with high‐centrality deployment in Gen 8—while keeping n_params at 180 and depth at 41.
