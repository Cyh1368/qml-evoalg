# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
• RY → CRZ → RZ “sandwich” layout  
  – Seed (Combined 0.63, gap 0.09) and Gen 32 (0.62, gap 0.10) both use exactly this ordering.  
  – Ensures stable training (zero std across runs) and clear gradient flow.  

• Targeted high-centrality CRZ edges  
  – Seed selects 12 two-qubit CRZs on hub-and-spoke plus key pairwise edges (0–2, 0–3, …, 6–7).  
  – Yields 0.63 combined vs. generic sparse patterns in Gen 30/31/33/34, which plateau at 0.59.  

• Moderate parameter sharing  
  – Seed shares only two CRZ parameters (crz_2 over edges 0–7/0–8, crz_3 over 1–3/1–8), keeping the other 10 crz_i independent.  
  – Reduces params from 180→162, narrows gap to 0.09, but preserves expressivity—outperforming Gen 31’s aggressive sharing (7 reused params, 150 total, combined 0.59).  

• Sweet-spot depth and gate count  
  – Depth ≈ 41, gates ≈ 207 in Seed achieve best combined (0.63).  
  – Slight deviations: Gen 32 at depth 44/gates 231 reaches only 0.62; deeper/heavier circuits (>45 depth, >230 gates) drop below 0.60 elsewhere.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
• Overparameterization without targeted sharing  
  – Gen 32 uses 186 parameters and deeper CRZ layers, scoring 0.62 but with no efficiency gain (gap 0.10). Extra params slowed convergence.  

• Excessive CRZ parameter reuse  
  – Gen 31, 33, 34 reuse 7–12 CRZ parameters across many edges (150 total params), achieving just 0.59 combined and gap 0.12. Over-sharing limits circuit expressivity.  

• Inflated gate budgets  
  – Circuits exceeding ~210 gates (Gen 32’s 231 gates) or mixing in additional two-qubit gates (CRY/CRX in earlier generations) did not exceed 0.62 and often raised the generalization gap.  

• Increased depth without structural benefit  
  – Depth > 45 (e.g. prior Gen 27 with depth 45) yielded combined ≤ 0.58, confirming that deeper ≠ better beyond the 41±2 window.  

## Implementation Insights  
• Explicit, unique parameter naming  
  – Seed’s use of “ry_0…ry_8”, “crz_0…crz_11”, “rz_post_0…rz_post_8” avoids optimizer conflicts and produces consistent zero-std results.  

• Correct multi-qubit gate specification  
  – Using the “wires” key for CRZ gates (versus incorrect “wire”) prevents parsing errors, as evidenced by stable runs of the Seed.  

• Minimalist EVOLVE-BLOCK  
  – Restricting modifications to ANSATZ_SPEC keeps the interface clean and isolates performance changes to circuit structure alone.  

• Strict gate ordering  
  – Placing all single-qubit rotations first, then all entanglers, then all post-rotations—demonstrated by the Seed—yields stable training dynamics.  

## Performance Analysis  
• Seed vs. best runners-up  
  – Seed combined 0.63 outperforms Gen 32’s 0.62 and all others’ 0.59. Test accuracy 0.65 vs. 0.66 (Gen 32) but with a tighter gap (0.09 vs. 0.10).  

• Generalization gap correlation  
  – Seed’s moderate sharing (162 params) closes the gap to 0.09; excessive sharing (150 params) opens it to 0.12; over-parameterization (186 params) sits at 0.10.  

• Depth/gate count sweet spot  
  – All top-performers (Seed, Gen 17 previously) cluster at depth 41±2 and ~207 gates. Deviation in either dimension systematically lowers combined scores.  

• Parameter count trade-off  
  – Reducing from 180→162 parameters (Seed) improved combined score by +0.02 over full 180-param designs, but further reduction to 150 (over-sharing) lost −0.04. Balancing param count against expressivity is critical.
