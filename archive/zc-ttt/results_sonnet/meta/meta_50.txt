# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- RY→CRZ→RZ “sandwich” layout with clear separation of encoding, entangling, and readout layers  
  • Seed program (Combined 0.63, gap 0.09; 162 params, depth 41, gates 207) and Generation 32 (0.62, gap 0.10; 162/41/207) both use this strict ordering and achieve top scores with zero run‐to‐run variance.  
- Hub‐and‐cluster CRZ entanglement with moderate symmetric sharing  
  • Seed’s hub‐qubit-0 plus two secondary clusters share just 2–3 CRZ parameters per symmetric edge (reducing 180→162) and hits Combined 0.63.  
- Circuit depth ≃41 and gate count ≃207  
  • Every ansatz in the 39–43 depth and 200–210‐gate range (Seed, Gen 32) attains Combined ≥0.62, whereas deeper/heavier circuits never exceed 0.60.  
- Explicit, unique parameter naming and full “wire(s)” specification  
  • Seed’s distinct names (“ry_0…8”, “crz_0…11”, “rz_post_0…8”) eliminate optimizer conflicts, yielding stable (zero‐std) accuracy across train/val/test.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive circuit depth (>45) or gate count (>230)  
  • Layered RY-CRZ-ZZZ Ansatz (Gen 49): depth 57, gates 219 → Combined 0.55, gap 0.15 despite added 3-qubit ZZZ interactions.  
- Over-parameterization without targeted entangler structure  
  • Hub-Based RY-CRZ Ansatz (Gen 52): 186 params, depth 47 → Combined 0.60, gap 0.10; extra RY refresh and symmetric sharing add noise sensitivity without boosting test accuracy.  
- Aggressive CRZ parameter reuse (too few unique entangler parameters)  
  • Symmetric Hub CRZ Ansatz (Gen 44): 150 params, depth 41 → Combined 0.59, gap 0.12; excessive sharing collapses expressivity.  
- Incorporating unstructured three-qubit or mixed two-qubit gates  
  • Layered RY-CRZ-ZZZ (Gen 49) and CRY‐based hybrids raised gate count without performance gains, increasing the generalization gap.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Constrain all architectural tweaks to ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • Seed’s minimalist EVOLVE-BLOCK ensures no unintended side-effects in encoding or measurement modules.  
- Enforce strict RY→CRZ→RZ ordering for gradient stability  
  • Seed’s precise layer sequence underpins its zero‐variance training versus interleaved or post-entangling RY refresh in Gen 52.  
- Balance symmetric sharing with unique parametrization  
  • Seed shares only two CRZ parameters per cluster edge but labels each parameter distinctly (e.g. “crz_2” reused twice only on hub edges), hitting strong connectivity and expressivity.  
- Use explicit “wires” lists for multi-qubit gates  
  • All top‐performing circuits (Seed, Gen 32) specify wires=[i,j] to avoid implicit ordering errors seen in earlier drafts.
