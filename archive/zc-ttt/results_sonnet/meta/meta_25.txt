# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Hub-edge pruning with parameter sharing  
  • 9-Qubit RY-CRZ-RZ Ansatz (Gen 17) shares crz_2 over edges (0–7, 0–8) and crz_3 over (1–3, 1–8), cutting parameters from 180→162 while keeping depth = 41/gates = 207. Combined score rose to 0.63 (vs. 0.60 in Gen 8).  
- RY→entangler→RZ “sandwich” with pure CRZ  
  • Both Gen 17 (0.63) and prior Gen 8 (0.60) follow a single layer of RY on all qubits, then all CRZ gates, then RZ on all qubits—yielding zero std. dev. convergence and top test accuracies.  
- Depth ≈ 41 and gate count ≈ 207 sweet spot  
  • Best circuits (Gen 17 at 41/207, Gen 8/Gen 22 at 41/207) hit combined scores ≥ 0.60. Deviating below ~39 or above ~47 depth or above ~207 gates consistently degrades performance.  
- Targeted two-qubit CRZ on high-centrality edges  
  • Focusing on 12 informative edges (e.g., 0–2, 0–3, 2–4, 5–7, etc.) without three-qubit gates sustains expressivity and reduces overfitting.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Incorporating three-qubit gates (CCRZ/ZZZ)  
  • Symmetric CRZ-CCRZ (Gen 25) scored 0.56; CRZ/ZZZ hybrid (Gen 26) scored 0.58. Both increased depth to 51/57 and gate count to 225, yet failed to improve test accuracy or parameter efficiency.  
- Overparameterized multi-layer designs  
  • Layered 9-Qubit Ansatz (Gen 19; 204 params, depth 63) achieved only 0.56 combined with a 0.18 generalization gap. Large param counts and deep circuits drove overfitting without boosting test scores.  
- Mixed two-qubit gates with excess gate budget  
  • Gen 22’s prune_cry_add_mid_ry (mixed CRZ/CRY + extra RY, 198 params, 225 gates) plateaued at 0.60 while widening generalization gap to 0.12.  
- Gate counts > 207  
  • All circuits exceeding ~225 gates underperformed relative to those constrained to ~207.

## Implementation Insights  
- Unique per-gate parameter naming  
  • Explicit labels (ry_0…ry_8, crz_0…crz_11, rz_post_0…rz_post_8) in Gen 17 and Gen 8 yield zero train/val/test std. deviation, avoiding optimizer conflicts.  
- Strategic parameter sharing  
  • Sharing parameters on symmetric hub edges (crz_2, crz_3) reduces dimensionality without losing expressivity, directly lifting combined score to 0.63.  
- Strict gate ordering  
  • Consistently placing all RY rotations before any CRZ, then all RZ, simplifies gradient flow and stabilizes training.  
- Limiting to RY/RZ + two-qubit CRZ  
  • Omitting RX/CRX/CCRZ/ZZZ narrows the search space to phase–amplitude trade-offs that the optimizer exploits most effectively.

## Performance Analysis  
- Pruned CRZ-only design outperforms mixed-gate baselines  
  • Gen 17’s 0.63 combined tops Gen 8’s 0.60 (which mixed CRZ/CRY) by simplifying to pure CRZ on the same edge set.  
- Parameter reduction improves generalization  
  • Reducing params from 180→162 dropped the generalization gap from ~0.12 to 0.09.  
- Depth/gates vs. accuracy correlation  
  • Circuits at depth ≈ 41 and gates ≈ 207 consistently score ≥ 0.60; those deeper or with more gates cap out at ≤ 0.58.  
- Three-qubit interactions and gate inflation penalize test performance  
  • Designs injecting CCRZ/ZZZ or adding extra entangler layers uniformly register combined scores ≤ 0.58 with larger gaps, confirming the overhead outweighs any expressivity gains.

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend parameter sharing on leaf–leaf entanglers: merge the CRZ on edge [4,8] (currently `crz_9`) into the CRZ on [3,5] (`crz_5`), and merge the CRZ on [6,7] (`crz_11`) into [5,7] (`crz_10`). This mutation drops n_params from 162 to 160 while holding depth at 41 and gate_count at 207, testing whether further dimension reduction preserves the 0.63 combined score.  

2. Insert a lightweight mid-layer of RY rotations on qubits [1,3,5,7] between the CRZ block and the final RZ layer, with parameters `ry_mid_0…ry_mid_3`. This “RY→CRZ→RY→RZ” sandwich adds only 4 gates (gates≈211) and ≈2 depth units (depth≈43), probing whether a second amplitude refresh stabilizes convergence without inducing overfitting.  

3. Parallelize the existing CRZ entangler block into two maximal sublayers to shave depth:  
   • Sublayer A: CRZ on [0,2],[1,3],[2,4],[3,5],[5,7]  
   • Sublayer B: CRZ on [0,3],[0,7],[1,8],[2,6],[4,8],[6,7]  
   Executed back-to-back, this reorganization should cut ≈2 depth units (target depth≈39) at constant gate_count=207 and n_params=162.  

4. Prune and reallocate a low-impact CRZ: remove the CRZ on [0,7] (param `crz_2`) and reassign that slot to a new CRZ on the high-centrality edge [1,2], still named `crz_2`. This preserves 162 parameters and the 41/207 layout while concentrating entanglement on a more informative link, in line with centrality‐based pruning.  

5. Test extreme hub-edge sharing: collapse all CRZ gates incident on qubit 0—edges [0,2],[0,3],[0,7],[0,8]—into a single shared parameter `crz_hub0`. This cuts four two-qubit parameters down to one (n_params≈159) without changing depth (41) or gate_count (207), exploring whether stronger symmetry-driven pruning further boosts generalization.
