<DESCRIPTION>
Building on the best-performing prior variant (score 0.63, n_params=162) which used pairwise CRZ parameter sharing across symmetric qubit pairs, this version applies the recommended extreme hub-edge sharing: all four CRZ gates incident on qubit 0 ([0,2],[0,3],[0,7],[0,8]) now share a single parameter `crz_hub0` instead of three separate parameters. This reduces per-repetition parameter count from 27 to 25 (n_params drops from 162 to ~150), while keeping depth and gate_count unchanged. The hypothesis is that qubit 0 acts as a symmetric "broadcast hub" feature, so forcing all its entangling interactions to share one rotation angle acts as a strong inductive-bias regularizer, further narrowing the train-validation gap without sacrificing expressivity elsewhere in the circuit (where pairwise sharing on qubits 1,2 is retained). All other entangling pairs retain their previous sharing pattern (crz_3 shared on [1,3]/[1,8], crz_4 shared on [2,4]/[2,6]) and the surrounding RY/RZ single-qubit layers are unchanged.

Key parameter changes vs. baseline:
- crz_0, crz_1, crz_2 (3 params, 4 edges) -> crz_hub0 (1 param, same 4 edges)
- All other CRZ params unchanged (crz_3 shared, crz_4 shared, crz_5, crz_9, crz_10, crz_11 each single)
- Same RY (9) + RZ (9) single-qubit layers
- Net effect: per-rep param count 27 -> 25, depth/gate_count unchanged (41/207)

This directly tests whether stronger symmetry-driven pruning around the identified hub qubit improves generalization further than the previous best pairwise-sharing scheme.
