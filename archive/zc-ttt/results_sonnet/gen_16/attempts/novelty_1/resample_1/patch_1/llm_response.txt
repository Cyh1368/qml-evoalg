The key comparison in the trace is between a lean-parameter design (9 RY + 7 shared CRZ + 3 fixed CZ + 9 RZ = 25 unique params/rep, depth 41) that scored 0.60, versus a fully-parametrized entangler variant (30 unique params/rep) that scored only 0.57 with a much larger generalization gap (0.14 vs 0.09). This shows that adding more trainable two-qubit rotations increases train accuracy but hurts validation/generalization — the fixed (non-parametrized) CZ gates act as a useful regularizer, and parameter count should stay lean.

Building on this insight, I keep the lean hub-CRZ + fixed-CZ structure that produced the best score, but reorganize the fixed CZ gates into two qubit-disjoint parallel groups ({(1,8),(2,6)} then {(0,8),(6,7)}) to shave circuit depth without touching the parameter count, directly applying the depth-reduction recommendation. I also add a single three-qubit ZZZ interaction on qubits (0,4,8) with one shared parameter — this reuses the two "far" hub qubits (0 and 4/8 already connected via CZ(4,8) and CZ(0,8)) to inject a cheap, low-parameter higher-order correlation term that can improve expressivity for multi-qubit feature interactions while adding only 1 extra parameter per repetition (26 unique params/rep vs. 25 in the best prior variant, i.e. ~156 total vs. 150). This keeps the parameter-efficient regularized regime that generalized well, while giving the circuit slightly richer correlations to potentially close some of the train/val accuracy gap on the harder features that pure pairwise CRZ/CZ terms cannot capture.
