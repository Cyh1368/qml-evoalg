graph_symmetric_hybrid_ccrz

<DESCRIPTION>
Replaces the single-gate-type (CRZ-only) hub/spoke entangling backbone with a graph-symmetric design that exploits the actual connectivity graph induced by the 12 allowed qubit pairs. The graph decomposes naturally into a 4-cycle (0-2-4-8-0) and a 6-cycle (0-3-5-7-6-2-0) sharing the 0-2 edge, plus three chord/pendant edges (0-7, 1-3, 1-8) attached to node 1. Instead of applying only CRZ everywhere, the two cycles get CRZ, the chords get CRX (diversifying the rotation axis for richer coverage of Hilbert space), and two long-range CCRZ three-qubit gates ([0,4,7] and [1,5,6]) are inserted after the two-qubit layer to inject non-local, higher-order correlations that were previously state absent from the ansatz — this leverages the fact that three-qubit gates have no wire-adjacency restriction, giving essentially "free" extra connectivity at a very small parameter cost (+2 params only). Symmetric parameter sharing (e.g. the two CRX gates on node 1's spokes share one parameter, and the final RZ phase layer mirrors qubit pairs at the same cycle distance from the hub) keeps the per-block parameter count similar to the baseline (27 unique params/block) while removing the extra "mid-layer RY refresh" that empirically hurt validation accuracy in the previous iteration (score dropped 0.63->0.61). This design should improve expressivity per parameter, keep circuit depth/gate-count lower than the previous mid-refresh variant, and hopefully close the train/validation gap while recovering (or beating) the 0.63 baseline score.
