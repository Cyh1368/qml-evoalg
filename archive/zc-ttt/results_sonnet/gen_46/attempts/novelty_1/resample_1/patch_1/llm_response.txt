crz_seq_shared_midlayer

<DESCRIPTION>
This crossover combines the two best-performing seed structures. Analysis of
the metrics shows that the *sequential* (non-reordered) CRZ entangling layer
with symmetric-edge parameter sharing (27 distinct params per block) achieved
the best score (0.63), while re-ordering those same gates into "parallel
commuting sublayers" hurt performance (0.58) even though the resulting
unitary is logically equivalent — this suggests the backend's initialization
and optimization trajectory is sensitive to gate ordering, so we keep the
proven sequential order intact rather than reshuffling it.

The variant that added a full extra RY "mid-layer" (4 new independent
parameters) slightly *decreased* the score (0.62 vs 0.63) despite adding
expressivity, likely because the extra free parameters increased overfitting
risk / optimization difficulty without enough benefit. To capture the
potential benefit of an extra non-linearity between the entangling layer and
the final phase layer while minimizing added parameter count, this version
reintroduces a mid-circuit RY rotation on the "odd" qubits (1, 3, 5, 7) but
shares a *single* parameter across all four gates. This adds only 1 extra
distinct parameter (28 total vs 27) instead of 4, giving mild extra
expressivity/entanglement-breaking without materially increasing the risk of
overfitting or convergence difficulty seen in the 186-param variant.

Structure:
1. Initial RY layer on all 9 qubits (independent params).
2. CRZ entangling layer over all 12 allowed 2-qubit pairs, using the proven
   sequential ordering and parameter-sharing pattern from the best scoring
   seed (crz_0..crz_11, with symmetric pairs sharing 2, 3, 4).
3. A single shared-parameter RY "mid" rotation on qubits {1,3,5,7} to add a
   touch of extra expressivity for negligible parameter cost.
4. Final RZ phase layer on all 9 qubits (independent params).

This keeps parameter count essentially unchanged (28 vs 27 distinct params
per repetition) while blending the strongest structural traits of both
parent programs.
