# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Symmetry-tied single-qubit phase parameters  
  • Generation 19 (“add_zzz_correlation_probe,” combined 0.58) and Generation 16 (“Symmetry-Tied RY-RZ CZ Ansatz,” combined 0.58) both assign identical `param` names to RZ gates on automorphically equivalent wires (e.g. `"rz_14"` on wires 1 & 4). This preserves expressivity while reducing redundant degrees of freedom and keeps the generalization gap low (0.05 in Gen 19 vs. ≥0.09 in less-tied designs).

- Single-layer RY front end → single RZ layer → entanglement  
  • Both Gen 16 (depth 41, gates 207, val 0.58) and Gen 19 (depth 57, gates 213, val 0.60) use one RY layer followed by one RZ layer before inserting two- and three-qubit gates. This “shallow” variational backbone achieves consistent validation accuracy (~0.60) without stacking extra rotation blocks.

- Targeted three-qubit correlation probes  
  • Adding a parameterized ZZZ gate on wires [2,4,6] in Gen 19 boosted its combined score to 0.58—matching the best so far—while keeping overfitting in check (generalization gap 0.05). This shows that carefully placed higher-order gates can enhance expressivity.

- Full 12-edge CZ entanglement graph  
  • Retaining all allowed CZ interactions (as in Gen 16, Gen 19, and Gen 20) reliably yields combined scores ≥0.57. Full connectivity preserves path lengths essential for correlating qubits without resorting to deeper circuits or extra CRZ layers.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-constraining phase parameters  
  • Generation 20 (“partial_phase_symmetry,” score 0.57) and Generation 23 (“moderate_ry_pair_sharing,” score 0.56) tied or pruned too many single-qubit phases, leading to underfitting (test 0.51 and 0.54) despite small generalization gaps (0.05).

- Increased depth via post-entanglement mixers  
  • Gen 25 (“ccrz_567_triangle,” combined 0.57, gap 0.09) and Gen 26 (“tie_symmetric_ry_pairs,” combined 0.53, gap 0.09) added RX/CCRZ/ZZZ gates after or within the entangling block, pushing depth to 42–57. This deeper wiring either failed to improve test accuracy or exacerbated overfitting.

- Dense multi-layer RY/RZ stacks without symmetry tying  
  • Gen 23’s symmetric RY-RZ configuration (96 params, depth 41) still underperformed (0.56) compared to simpler, tied designs, indicating that mere layering of rotations does not substitute for targeted parameter sharing.

- Sparser entanglement without compensating expressivity  
  • Attempts to prune CZ links or replace with sparse CRZs (e.g. in earlier generations) yielded only marginal score gains (≤0.55) and risked under-entangling the system.

## Implementation Insights

- Explicit parameter labels for sharing  
  • Use identical `"param"` strings on multiple dict entries to tie gates at the code level, e.g.:  
    {"gate":"RZ","wire":1,"param":"rz_14"}  
    {"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"rz_14"}  

- Single-instance ZZZ declaration  
  • In Gen 19, the line  
    {"gate":"ZZZ","wires":[2,4,6],"param":"zzz_2_4_6"}  
  slots seamlessly into the front end without modifying the EVOLVE-BLOCK structure, enabling targeted higher-order correlations.

- Full CZ edge listing  
  • Explicitly enumerate all 12 CZ pairs in ANSATZ_SPEC to guarantee consistent gate_count (207 or 213 when including a 3-qubit gate), avoiding hidden backend defaults.

- Lean rotation layering  
  • Maintain exactly one RY per qubit followed by one RZ layer before entanglement. Avoid extra RX/RZ layers post-CZ, which inflate depth without boosting generalization.

## Performance Analysis

- Top combined scores (0.58) held by Gen 16 (depth 41, gap 0.03) and Gen 19 (depth 57, gap 0.05). Both use symmetry-tied phases and full CZ connectivity.

- Mid-range designs (0.57)  
  • Gen 20 (full graph, selective phase tying) and Gen 25 (added CCRZ + post-mixing) show that under- or over-tying phases and adding post-entanglement gates can each yield the same 0.57 but at the cost of either test accuracy or increased gap (0.09 in Gen 25).

- Decline in deeper circuits  
  • Designs with depth ≥47 (Gen 19 & Gen 26) do not outperform the 41-depth baseline: Gen 19 ties the best score only by adding a ZZZ gate, while Gen 26 drops to 0.53. This underscores that extra layers must be justified by expressivity gains.

- Generalization gap correlation  
  • The smallest gaps (0.03–0.05) occur in programs with disciplined symmetry tying and shallow structure. Gaps widen to ≥0.09 when adding extra entanglers/mixers without proportional parameter sharing (Gen 25, Gen 26).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace two peripheral CZs with parametrized CRZs on the longest‐range links. For example, swap  
   {"gate":"CZ","wires":[1,8]} and {"gate":"CZ","wires":[4,8]}  
   with  
   {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[1,8],"param":"crz_1_8"}  
   and  
   {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[4,8],"param":"crz_4_8"}.  
   This preserves the full 12‐edge graph and 57-depth footprint while improving gradient flow on weak long-range connections, as seen in successful mid-range designs.  

2. Tie RY rotations on automorphic qubit pairs to further reduce redundant single-qubit parameters. For instance, change  
   {"gate":"RY","wire":1,"param":"ry_1"} and {"gate":"RY","wire":7,"param":"ry_7"}  
   to both use "param":"ry_17", and similarly tie wires 2 & 6 under "ry_26".  
   This builds on the proven expressivity of symmetry-tied RZs in Gen 16/19 and tests if partial RY sharing can narrow the gap further without sacrificing validation accuracy.  

3. Swap the central hub CZ for a controlled-RY to probe axis-selective entanglement. Replace  
   {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,7]}  
   with  
   {"gate":"CRY","wires":[0,7],"param":"cry_0_7"}.  
   Retaining depth and gate count, the CRY at the busiest junction may tighten multi-qubit correlations, echoing the beneficial effects of trainable two-qubit gates on critical edges.  

4. Collapse two CZs into a single three-body CCRZ on a triangle motif. Remove  
   {"gate":"CZ","wires":[5,7]} and {"gate":"CZ","wires":[6,7]}  
   and add  
   {"gate":"CCRZ","wires":[5,6,7],"param":"ccrz_567"}.  
   This one-for-two swap maintains full connectivity and depth while testing richer three-qubit phase dynamics, following the success of Gen 25’s CCRZ probe but with disciplined parameter sharing.  

5. Introduce a second targeted ZZZ entangler on another symmetric triad. After the existing ZZZ[2,4,6], insert  
   {"gate":"ZZZ","wires":[1,3,8],"param":"zzz_1_3_8"}  
   immediately before the CZ block, keeping overall depth at 57 by forgoing one non-critical CZ (e.g., [3,5]).  
   This adds higher-order correlations on a second symmetry-related triple, extending Gen 19’s expressivity boost while controlling parameter count and generalization gap.
