# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Graph-aware RY sharing with independent RZ phases  
  • Generation 34 (Graph-Regularized RY-RZ CZ Ansatz) ties RY angles across automorphic vertex pairs (e.g. `"ry_23"` on qubits 2 & 3) while keeping each RZ distinct. This 96-parameter, depth-41 circuit achieves the top combined score of 0.59 and a small generalization gap (0.05).  

- Full CZ graph + single central CRY entangler  
  • Explicitly listing all 12 allowed CZ edges plus one trainable CRY at the graph’s hub (Gen 34’s `{"gate":"CRY","wires":[0,7],"param":"cry_0_7"}`) outperforms sparser entanglement or multiple multi-qubit gates.  

- Minimal rotation layering before entanglement  
  • A single layer of RY followed by one layer of RZ, then the entangling block (Gen 34), yields better validation accuracy (0.60) and lower depth (41) than deeper stacks (e.g. Gen 28’s depth 57 with ZZZ probe).  

- Independent RZ phases boost expressivity  
  • Allowing each qubit its own RZ parameter (Gen 34) improves combined score to 0.59 versus symmetry-tied RZ variants (Gen 28 only achieved 0.57 with ZZZ).

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-deep circuits with three-qubit probes  
  • Generation 28 inserted a ZZZ gate plus full CZ/CRY at depth 57 (213 gates) but only reached a combined 0.57 and gap 0.07—higher depth did not translate to better generalization.  

- Over-tying RZ phases  
  • Generation 29 tied RZ across symmetry groups (zero per-qubit phases), used one CZ/CRZ layer at depth 41, yet fell to a combined 0.52 with a large gap (0.13), indicating under-expressivity.  

- Redundant CRY-only entanglers without RZ independence  
  • Generations 32 and 33 both applied a single CRY at [0,7] plus full CZ but re-used symmetry-tied RZ. They stalled at a 0.55 combined score and a 0.09 gap, showing that CRY alone can’t compensate for over-constrained phases.  

- Excessive gate counts without targeted expressivity  
  • High-gate designs (depth > 48, >260 gates in Gen 22/30 from prior rounds) consistently underperformed (~0.55–0.57), underscoring that more gates ≠ better accuracy absent strategic sharing or probes.

## Implementation Insights

- RY parameter tying via identical `"param"` strings  
  • In ANSATZ_SPEC, assign the same name to symmetry-related RY gates:  
    {"gate":"RY","wire":2,"param":"ry_23"}  
    {"gate":"RY","wire":3,"param":"ry_23"}  

- Explicit full CZ listing  
  • Hard-code all 12 allowed CZ edges to ensure uniform connectivity rather than relying on implicit backends.  

- Single CRY hub insertion  
  • Place one CRY at the graph’s junction (e.g. qubits 0–7) with:  
    {"gate":"CRY","wires":[0,7],"param":"cry_0_7"}  

- Per-qubit RZ for local phase flexibility  
  • Use independent RZ parameters to capture fine-grained phase information:  
    {"gate":"RZ","wire":i,"param":f"rz_{i}"} for i in 0…8.

## Performance Analysis

- Gen 34 vs. Gen 28 (ZZZ + full CZ/CRY):  
  • Combined ↑0.02 (0.59 vs. 0.57), gap ↓0.02 (0.05 vs. 0.07), depth ↓16, gates ↓6.
