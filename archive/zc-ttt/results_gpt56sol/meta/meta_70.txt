# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-layer RY–RZ blocks bracketing four disjoint CZ matchings plus a final “hub” CZ edge consistently deliver peak performance.  
  • Seed program (180 params, 51 depth, 315 gates) and its derivatives Gen 49/Gen 52 all hit combined 0.62 with a tiny generalization gap of 0.02.  
- Graph-automorphism–informed parameter tying in both initial and output RY layers controls capacity without sacrificing accuracy.  
  • Seed’s use of “ry_14”/“ry_23” in the first layer mirrored by “ry2_14”/“ry2_23” in the readout keeps gen. gap at 0.02.  
- High total parameter count (~180) and gate count (~315) are critical when combined with symmetry-based tying.  
  • Circuits at ~315 gates but only 126–162 params peak at ≤0.59 (Gen 65: 0.59 combined), whereas upping to 180 params pushes to 0.62.  
- Strict single-qubit rotation + CZ two-qubit entanglement—avoiding mid-circuit RX mixers or parametric two-qubit gates—maximizes fidelity and expressivity per depth.  

Ineffective Approaches  
- Inserting mid-circuit RX mixing layers or higher-order three-qubit gates (ZZZ/CCRZ) did not improve scores.  
  • Gen 64 (“mid_rx_matching_mixer,” includes RX mixer & ZZZ) plateaued at 0.58 combined (gap 0.06).  
  • Gen 67 (CCRZ/ZZZ additions, 201 gates) fell to test 0.54.  
- Aggressive or broad parameter sharing across graph orbits constrains expressivity.  
  • Gen 65 (“split_graph_mixer,” 162 params) and Gen 63 (heavy tying, 96 params) both capped at 0.59 combined with larger gaps (~0.06–0.07).  
- Reducing the number of CZ matching layers below four lowers maximum achievable score to ≤ 0.60.  
  • Gen 41 (three CZ layers, 96 params) reached only 0.60 combined.  
- Shallow RY–CZ circuits without the two-layer RY–RZ bracketing deliver near-random accuracy.  
  • Gen 70 (“shared_ry_cz_basis,” depth 45, 261 gates) scored just 0.50 combined.  

Implementation Insights  
- ANSATZ_SPEC ordering is critical:  
  1) Initial graph-tied RY on each qubit  
  2) Per-qubit pre-RZ rotations (“rz_pre_*”)  
  3) Four disjoint CZ matchings (lists of 4, 4, 3 edges)  
  4) A single final CZ hub edge  
  5) Output RY then RZ layer with mirrored parameter tying (“ry2_*”, “rz2_*”)  
- Parameter names encode both symmetry orbit and layer position (e.g., “ry_23” vs. “ry2_23”), enabling concise spec and automatic sharing.  
- Restricting ALLOWED_SINGLE_QUBIT_GATES to {RY,RZ} and ALLOWED_TWO_QUBIT_GATES to {CZ} avoids noisy parametric two-qubit operations while retaining expressivity.  
- The current best program’s success hinges on balancing maximal—but non-overlapping—entanglement coverage with shallow two-layer single-qubit rotations and targeted symmetry tying.  

Performance Analysis  
- Only the full 51-depth/315-gate/180-param circuits with the two-layer RY–RZ + 4 CZ + hub pattern reach combined 0.62 (Seed, Gen 49, Gen 52).  
- Circuits matching depth/gate counts but lacking the exact two-layer structure (Gen 71: 180 params, 315 gates) score significantly lower (0.53 combined), underscoring the importance of the gate ordering and block design.  
- Stepping down to depth 45/261 gates drops combined performance to 0.56 (Gen 66) or lower—despite symmetry tying—confirming that four full CZ matchings plus hub are non-negotiable.  
- Generalization gap remains ∼0.02 for top performers; other deep but modified designs exhibit gaps of 0.06–0.08, linking the precise parameter-tying scheme to stable out-of-sample accuracy.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Duplicate the full 4-matching + hub entanglement block to deepen the ansatz. After the existing final CZ on [0, 8], insert a second sequence of the same four disjoint CZ matchings plus a hub CZ on [0, 8], and bracket it with a fresh RY–RZ layer using new tied parameters “ry_mid2_*”/“rz_mid2_*.” This maintains the proven bracketing pattern and graph-informed tying while roughly doubling depth (~102) and gate count (~630), testing whether extra entanglement layers can push combined score above 0.62 without hurting the generalization gap.  

2. Extend orbit-based tying to the RZ layers to further regularize capacity. Replace each independent “rz_pre_q” and “rz2_q” with orbit-tied parameters (e.g., “rz_pre_14” on qubits 1&4, “rz_pre_23” on 2&3, and similarly for “rz2_*”), mirroring the RY tying scheme that kept the seed’s gap at 0.02. By reducing the number of free RZ parameters by ~7, you retain expressivity where it matters most and may free headroom for subtle entanglement variations.  

3. Systematically permute the order of the four disjoint CZ matchings. Keep the same sets of edges but generate variants that start with different matchings (e.g., begin with [4, 8],[5, 7],[0, 2],[1, 3] instead of [0, 2],[1, 3],[4, 8],[5, 7]). Because long-range correlations appear crucial, testing all 24 permutations (or a representative sample) could reveal an ordering that accelerates learning or slightly improves fidelity while holding depth and gate count constant.  

4. Insert a mid-circuit shallow RY–RZ block between entanglement layers. After the second CZ matching and before the third, add RY then RZ rotations on all nine qubits using new tied parameters “ry_mid_*”/“rz_mid_*”. This injects additional single-qubit expressivity at the halfway point—analogous to “skip connections” in classical nets—without introducing new two-qubit gates, and can help disentangle local information before the remaining matchings.  

5. Augment the single hub edge into a “dual-hub” at the end of entanglement. Immediately after the existing CZ on [0, 8], append a second hub CZ drawn from the same symmetry class—e.g., CZ on [4, 5] or CZ on [2, 6]—to create two final star-like couplings. This tests whether reinforcing different central orbits in the hub position can further boost long-range entanglement coverage without violating the four-matching + hub paradigm.
