# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Removing redundant commuting RZ layers drove a measurable uplift. The “9-Qubit RY-RZ Ansatz with CZ Entanglement” (Gen 2, score 0.57) omits the post-entanglement RZ layer present in Gen 5/Gen 6, reducing depth to 41 and gate count to 207, and raising test accuracy to 0.55 (vs. 0.51).
- A single RY layer followed by one RZ pre-rotation layer sufficed for expressivity. Compared to Gen 9’s shallow real-only ansatz (score 0.51), the mixed RY+RZ pre-entanglement scheme achieved 0.60 validation accuracy.
- Fixed CZ entanglement across 12 carefully chosen qubit pairs consistently balanced expressivity and complexity. All top-scoring variants (Gen 2 at 0.57, Gen 5/Gen 6 at 0.56) shared this sparse-but-well-connected CZ graph.
- Explicit, unique parameter names for each single-qubit gate (“ry_i”, “rz_pre_i”) prevented schema errors and facilitated gradient updates, as seen in the current best program’s flawless validation.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Duplicate post-entanglement RZ layers inflated circuit depth from 41 to 47 and gate count from 207 to 261 without improving accuracy. Gen 5 and Gen 6 both scored 0.56, with higher validation losses (2.32 vs. 2.27) and lower test accuracy (0.51 vs. 0.55).
- Omitting RZ entirely (Gen 9 real-only RY-CZ ansatz) constrained expressivity, yielding the lowest combined score (0.51) and the largest generalization gap (0.07).
- Introducing unresolved merge conflicts or inconsistent key usage (Gen 7) led to execution failure (score 0.0), underscoring that syntax/schema violations directly translate to zero performance.
- Overparameterized RZ repetition produced zero parameter efficiency (all Gen 2–6 had 0.01–0.00), indicating diminishing returns from added single-qubit rotations.

## Implementation Insights
- Isolating the entire ansatz specification inside a dedicated EVOLVE-BLOCK ensures that accidental code changes don’t break schema validation, as demonstrated by the correct Gen 2 program.
- Pruning the post-entanglement RZ layer in Gen 2 cut depth by ~13% and gates by ~20%, correlating with a 0.04 absolute gain in test accuracy and a 0.05 reduction in validation loss compared to Gen 5/6.
- Consistent use of “wire” for single-qubit gates and “wires” for two-qubit gates, plus adherence to ALLOWED_*_GATES, prevented silent backend rejections and guaranteed all declared parameters were trainable.
- Naming conventions that distinguish “rz_pre_i” from any hypothetical “rz_post_i” improve readability and make future pruning of redundant rotations straightforward.

## Performance Analysis
- The best program (Gen 2) achieved 0.57 combined score—0.01 above the nearest rivals (Gen 5/Gen 6 at 0.56) and +0.06 above the real-only ansatz—demonstrating that minimal layering with no redundancy can outperform deeper but bloated designs.
- Depth vs. accuracy trade-off: reducing depth from 47→41 in Gen 2 improved test accuracy from 0.51→0.55 and lowered test loss from 2.43→2.35, while the generalization gap remained stable at 0.06.
- Gate count correlates inversely with validation loss: the 207-gate Gen 2 circuit had a 2.27 loss vs. 2.32–2.43 for 261-gate variants.
- All successful CZ-entangled ansatzes maintained low parameter efficiency (~0.01), indicating that beyond ~100 single-qubit parameters, expressivity saturates and redundancy impedes performance.
