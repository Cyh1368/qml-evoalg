# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Symmetry-tied single-qubit phase parameters  
  • Current best program (combined 0.58, gap 0.05) and Gen 19 both assign identical `"param"` strings to RZ gates on automorphically equivalent wires (e.g. `"rz_14"` on wires 1 & 4, `"rz_23"` on wires 2 & 3). This preserves expressivity while cutting the parameter count to 96 and keeps the generalization gap low.  

- Targeted three-qubit correlation probe  
  • Inserting a single ZZZ gate on wires [2,4,6] before pairwise entanglement (current best, Gen 19) boosts the combined score to 0.58 without widening the gap (0.05), showing that one well‐placed higher-order gate can yield expressivity gains.  

- Full CZ entanglement graph  
  • Retaining all 12 allowed CZ edges (current best, Gen 16, Gen 19) consistently achieves combined scores ≥ 0.57. Full connectivity correlates with better test accuracy than sparser or peripheral‐only entanglement (e.g. Gen 24’s restricted CRZ edges).  

- Lean rotation layering  
  • A single front-end RY layer per qubit followed by one RZ layer (current best, Gen 16) yields validation accuracy ~0.60 without extra depth. Avoiding additional RX/RZ blocks post-CZ prevents unnecessary circuit inflation.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Deep, multi-layer RY/RZ stacks without symmetry tying  
  • Gen 22 (depth 48, gates 261) and Gen 30 (depth 47, gates 261) used repeated local RY/RZ layers around a dense entanglement block but only achieved combined scores of 0.55 and 0.57, respectively, with low parameter efficiency (~0.01). High depth did not translate into better generalization.  

- Over-constraining or under-entangling phases  
  • Gen 27 tied RZ phases across symmetry‐related vertices but added a CRY(0,7) gate and boundary RX mixers, yielding a combined score of only 0.54 and a gap of 0.09. This shows that symmetry tying must be paired with sufficient—but not excessive—entanglement.  

- Sparse continuous entanglers without compensating expressivity  
  • Gen 24 limited trainable two-qubit gates to two peripheral CRZs and independent RZs, achieving a combined 0.58 (val 0.60) but with a higher gap (0.07) and still low parameter efficiency (0.01). Restricting entanglement to peripheral edges reduces the ansatz’s ability to correlate the full graph.  

- Unresolved syntax artifacts  
  • Gen 31 failed entirely (score 0.0) due to stray diff markers in the source. Ensuring clean gate definitions and no merge conflict remnants is critical for any performance gains.

## Implementation Insights

- Explicit parameter sharing via identical `param` fields  
  • Tying RZ gates is as simple as using the same string, e.g.:  
    {"gate":"RZ","wire":1,"param":"rz_14"}  
    {"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"rz_14"}  
  This pattern underlies the current best’s low overfitting and high efficiency.  

- Single ZZZ insertion  
  • A one-line addition in the ANSATZ_SPEC before CZ edges,  
    {"gate":"ZZZ","wires":[2,4,6],"param":"zzz_2_4_6"},  
  provides targeted three-qubit correlation without altering the EVOLVE-BLOCK structure or adding multiple multi-qubit gates.  

- Full enumeration of CZ edges  
  • Listing all 12 allowed pairs ensures consistent entanglement—avoid relying on backend defaults. The current best explicitly codes each CZ, matching Gen 16 and Gen 19’s 207–213 gate counts.  

- Minimal rotation layering  
  • Restrict the circuit to one layer of RY and one layer of RZ before entanglement. The current best program and Gen 16 both follow this template and achieve validation accuracies of ~0.60 with shallow depth relative to other designs.

## Performance Analysis

- Top combined 0.58 reached by two patterns  
  • Current best (96 params, depth 57, gap 0.05) and Gen 24 (102 params, depth 41, gap 0.07) both score 0.58. However, the symmetry-tied + ZZZ + full CZ design attains the same combined score with a smaller generalization gap.  

- Depth vs. performance plateau  
  • Circuits deeper than 47 layers (current best at 57, Gen 19 at 57) do not outperform the shallower 41-depth baseline (Gen 16) unless they include targeted enhancements like ZZZ gates and disciplined parameter sharing.  

- Generalization gap correlates with tying discipline  
  • The smallest gaps (0.03–0.05) occur in programs that balance full entanglement, single ZZZ probes, and strict symmetry tying (current best, Gen 16, Gen 19). Gaps widen to ≥ 0.07 when tying is partial or entanglement is restricted (Gen 24, Gen 27).  

- Inefficiencies penalize marginal gains  
  • High gate count (>260) and extra mixer layers in Gen 22/30 deliver only 0.55–0.57 combined scores, underscoring that extra depth or gates without targeted expressivity features do not yield proportional accuracy improvements.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Tie RY rotations on automorphic qubit pairs to shrink the single-qubit parameter pool.  
   • Change  
     {"gate":"RY","wire":1,"param":"ry_1"} and {"gate":"RY","wire":4,"param":"ry_4"}  
     to both use "param":"ry_14",  
     similarly tie wires 2&3 under "ry_23" and wires 5&6 under "ry_56".  
   This builds on the proven expressivity of symmetry-tied RZs and may further reduce overfitting without sacrificing validation accuracy.

2. Convert peripheral static CZs into trainable long-range CRZs for better gradient flow.  
   • Replace  
     {"gate":"CZ","wires":[1,8]} and {"gate":"CZ","wires":[4,8]}  
     with  
     {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[1,8],"param":"crz_1_8"} and  
     {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[4,8],"param":"crz_4_8"}.  
   Keeping the full 12-edge connectivity, these parametrized two-qubit gates can strengthen learning on the weakest links as seen in mid-range designs.

3. Probe axis-selective entanglement at the central hub by swapping a CZ for a CRY.  
   • Replace  
     {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,7]}  
     with  
     {"gate":"CRY","wires":[0,7],"param":"cry_0_7"}.  
   This mirrors the beneficial effects of trainable two-qubit gates on critical edges and preserves overall depth and gate count.

4. Introduce a second targeted ZZZ entangler on a symmetric triad while maintaining depth.  
   • Insert  
     {"gate":"ZZZ","wires":[1,3,8],"param":"zzz_1_3_8"}  
     immediately before the CZ block, and remove the non-critical  
     {"gate":"CZ","wires":[3,5]}.  
   Extending the single-ZZZ probe to another symmetry-related triple leverages the expressivity boost seen in Gen 19 without inflating the circuit.

5. Collapse two adjacent CZs into a single three-body CCRZ to test richer phase correlations.  
   • Remove  
     {"gate":"CZ","wires":[5,7]} and {"gate":"CZ","wires":[6,7]}  
     and add  
     {"gate":"CCRZ","wires":[5,6,7],"param":"ccrz_567"}.  
   This one-for-two swap maintains full connectivity and depth, following the success of three-qubit probes with disciplined parameter sharing.
