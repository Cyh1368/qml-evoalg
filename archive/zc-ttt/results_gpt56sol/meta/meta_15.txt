# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Symmetry-tied phase parameters: Generation 16’s Symmetry-Tied RY-RZ CZ Ansatz (score 0.58) groups RZ angles across automorphic vertices, cutting parameters from 108→90 and boosting combined score by +0.01 over the previous best.  
- Single pre-entanglement RZ layer: All top performers (Gen 2 and Gen 16) omit any post-entanglement RZ block, holding depth at 41 and gate count at 207, which correlates with higher test accuracy (0.55→0.54) versus 47-depth variants.  
- Full allowed CZ graph entanglement: Both the Gen 2 baseline and Gen 16 maintain the same 12-edge CZ subgraph, preserving expressivity while avoiding deeper or denser entanglers that add overhead with little accuracy gain.  
- Explicit, unique parameter naming: Across Gen 2, Gen 11 and Gen 16, labels like “ry_i” and “rz_14” eliminate schema ambiguity, ensuring every declared parameter trains and preventing silent backend drops.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Redundant RZ repetition (Gen 10 Nine-Qubit Two-Layer RY-RZ Ansatz): Adding a second RZ layer inflates depth to 47 and gate count to 261, yields zero parameter efficiency and only ties Gen 2’s combined score (0.57) without narrowing the generalization gap (0.10 vs. 0.03).  
- Over-shared hub-peripheral entanglers (Gen 12 Hub-Peripheral Sparse Ansatz): Collapsing three CRZ interactions into shared parameters limits expressivity, dropping the combined score to 0.54 and widening validation loss despite depth remaining 41.  
- Excessive symmetry mirroring (Gen 13 Symmetric RY-RZ CZ Ansatz): Tying RZ across symmetric pairs cuts parameters to 84 but pushes depth back to 47 and achieves only a 0.55 combined score, indicating over-tying can throttle representational capacity.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Modularity via ANSATZ_SPEC: Defining the entire variational pattern inside EVOLVE-BLOCK guarantees schema validation and isolates evolutionary changes, as seen in all correct programs.  
- Depth/gate reduction drives performance: The current best (Gen 16) and the earlier Gen 2 both hold depth=41, gate_count=207; this consistent footprint outperforms deeper (47) circuits by ~0.03–0.04 test-accuracy points.  
- Symmetry-aware parameter tying: In Gen 16, mapping wires {1,4}→“rz_14” and {2,3}→“rz_23” preserves crucial phase degrees of freedom while cutting 18 parameters, improving parameter efficiency without sacrificing generalization.  
- Single-layer RY+RZ front end: Consistently across Gen 2, Gen 11 and Gen 16, one RY layer followed by one RZ layer achieves sufficient expressivity; adding more single-qubit rotations leads to diminishing returns.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score range and best performers: Combined scores span 0.54 (Gen 12)→0.58 (Gen 16). Approaches that keep depth=41 with ~207 gates score ≥0.57, while deeper or over-parameterized designs score ≤0.56.  
- Parameter count sweet spot: Circuits with ~90–108 parameters (Gen 2:108, Gen 16:90) hit the top scores; blowing up to 132 (Gen 10) brings no gain, trimming below 84 (Gen 13) drops accuracy.  
- Generalization gap minimization: Gen 16 achieves a 0.03 gap versus 0.06–0.10 in other variants, suggesting symmetry-tied phases both bolster test accuracy (0.54) and tighten overfitting.  
- Gate-count vs. validation loss: The 207-gate circuits of Gen 2 and Gen 16 show validation_loss≈2.27–2.35, outperforming the 261-gate variants (loss up to 2.43), confirming leaner entanglement patterns yield steadier convergence.
