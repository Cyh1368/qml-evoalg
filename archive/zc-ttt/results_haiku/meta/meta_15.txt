# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mixed parametric CRZ and fixed CZ entanglers interleaved in pairs  
  • Seen in “9-Qubit Layered RY-RZ-CRZ-ZZZ Ansatz” (Generation 2, score 0.56, 120 params, depth 63, gap 0.10) and replicated in the current best program (score 0.56, 126 params, depth 63, gap 0.08).  
- Exactly two three-qubit ZZZ gates in a dedicated mid-layer  
  • Generation 2 and the current best both use 2 ZZZ blocks to boost expressivity without blowing up the generalization gap.  
- Split pre-entanglement RZ rotations across qubit halves  
  • The current best program’s use of “rz_pre_a” on qubits 0–4 and “rz_pre_b” on qubits 5–8 preserves a depth of 63 and gate count of 273 while matching the 0.56 score, refining phase control versus a single global pre-RZ.  
- Uniform final shared RZ layer (“rz_post”) on all qubits  
  • Employed in Generation 7 (score 0.54) and in the current best, this global phase shift tightens the generalization gap to ~0.08.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization beyond ~130 trainable parameters  
  • Generation 6 (186 params, depth 75) peaked at 0.55; Generation 8 (162 params, four ZZZ) fell to 0.50 with a gap of 0.19.  
- Excessive three-qubit interactions (>2 ZZZ or use of CCRZ)  
  • Generation 8’s four ZZZs and Generation 11’s CCRZ-heavy expansion increased training accuracy but severely overfit and depressed test performance.  
- Sole reliance on dense CRZ meshes without fixed CZs  
  • Generation 3 (138 params, CRZ-only) stalled at 0.54, underperforming mixed-entangler patterns.  
- Aggressive parameter sharing leading to failure  
  • Generation 12 and Generation 17 (extreme share of RY/RZ plus miswired entanglers) scored 0.0, failing all validation tests due to restricted expressivity and specification errors.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Consistent, systematic parameter naming  
  • The current best program’s naming (“ry_0…8,” “rz_pre_a/b,” “crz_0…6,” “zzz_0…1,” “rz_post”) aligns with Generation 10 and Generation 2, simplifying optimizer bookkeeping and convergence.  
- Two-segment pre-RZ split yields granularity without depth penalty  
  • Splitting one global pre-RZ into rz_pre_a and rz_pre_b maintained depth 63/gates 273 (vs. 81/435 in Generation 9) and held the 0.56 score.  
- Interleaving each parametric CRZ with a fixed CZ on strategically chosen edges  
  • Patterns such as CRZ[0,2]–CZ[0,3] and CRZ[1,3]–CZ[1,8] strike a balance between trainable expressivity and static entanglement stability.  
- Fixed footprint of ~63 depth and ~273 gates is optimal  
  • Both Generation 2 and the current best program hit top marks at this circuit size, suggesting a practical upper bound on expressivity before overfitting.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Peak combined score 0.56 at ~120–126 parameters  
  • Achieved by Generation 2 (120 params), Generation 10 (126 params), and the current best (126 params). Deviation above or below this range yields lower scores.  
- Depth ≈63 and gate count ≈273 correlate with best test accuracy  
  • Shallower (<50) circuits lacked expressivity; deeper (>75) circuits overfit without improving combined score.  
- Current best improves test accuracy to 0.56 (from 0.54 in Generation 2)  
  • By adopting split pre-RZ and preserving core entanglement, test performance rose while validation held at 0.60 and the gen-gap shrank to 0.08.  
- Consistent validation accuracy of 0.60 across top designs  
  • Indicates that balanced two-layer RZ framing and mixed entangler patterns reliably generalize, with final shared RZ further reducing overfitting.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a global “rz_mid” layer on all qubits immediately before the two ZZZ blocks.  Concretely, add  
    {"gate": "RZ", "wire": i, "param": "rz_mid"} for i = 0–8 between the last CZ/CRZ pair and the first ZZZ.  
   This adds one parameter (n_params→127) and one depth level (depth→64), leveraging the proven power of symmetric pre/post RZ framing to fine-tune phase relationships right before three-qubit interactions without blowing up the generalization gap.  

2. Diversify your entangling axes by swapping one CRZ+CZ pair for a CRX+CZ pair on a long-range link.  For example, replace  
    {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[0,8],"param":"crz_2"}  
    {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,3]}  
   with  
    {"gate":"CRX","wires":[0,8],"param":"crx_08"}  
    {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,3]}.  
   This preserves the mixed‐entangler pattern but tests whether a Y-axis control rotation better captures high-order correlations.  

3. Share parameters between symmetrically placed CRZ gates to free capacity for new static entanglement.  Tie  
    crz_0 ([0,2]) and crz_6 ([5,7]) → single parameter “crz_shared_a,”  
   then repurpose the freed slot to insert an extra CZ on wires [2,5].  
   You keep n_params = 126 and depth = 63/273 gates, maintain balanced expressivity, and explore whether bolstering static connectivity on [2,5] further improves generalization.  

4. Probe alternative CZ topology by shifting one fixed CZ from a saturated region to a new coupling.  Replace the existing  
    {"gate":"CZ","wires":[6,7]}  
   with  
    {"gate":"CZ","wires":[4,7]}.  
   This minor rewiring keeps your CZ count and overall circuit size constant but tests whether redirecting entanglement toward qubit 4 more effectively bridges subcircuit correlations.  

5. Explore different three-qubit correlations by moving the second ZZZ block onto a new triplet.  Keep exactly two ZZZs, but change  
    {"gate":"ZZZ","wires":[1,5,6],"param":"zzz_1"}
