# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Combining parameterized two-qubit CRZ gates with fixed CZ entanglers and a pair of three-qubit ZZZ interactions (Generation 2 “9-Qubit Layered RY-RZ-CRZ-ZZZ Ansatz”) lifted the combined score from 0.52 → 0.56, improved validation accuracy from 0.55/0.53 → 0.60, and test accuracy from 0.49/0.48 → 0.54.  
- Surrounding the entangling block with global pre- and post- RZ layers provided additional phase flexibility, contributing to the current best generalization gap of 0.10 (vs. 0.07–0.12 in other designs).  
- A dedicated per-qubit RY input layer proved consistently beneficial: all higher-scoring circuits (Scores ≥ 0.54) start with individual RY rotations on each of the 9 qubits.  
- Mid-range parameter budgets (~120 parameters) and moderate circuit depth (~60 layers, ~270 gates) struck the best trade-off between expressivity and trainability, as exemplified by the current best program.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Heavy parameter sharing in RY/RZ layers (Generation 5 “Layered Shared-Parameter RY-RZ CZ Ansatz,” 72 parameters) produced high depth (89) and gate count (423) but only a 0.54 score, indicating under-expressive shared rotations plus too-deep circuits hurt train/test performance.  
- Substituting CRX/CRY for CRZ entanglers (Generation 1 “Layered 9-Qubit RY-CRX/CRY-ZZZ Ansatz”) maintained a shallow depth (52) and lower parameter count (84) but delivered only a 0.52 score and higher generalization gap (0.11), showing that CRX/CRY alone were insufficient to capture problem structure.  
- Omitting fixed CZ layers in favor of CRZ-only entanglement (Generation 4 “9-Qubit RY-CRZ-ZZZ Ansatz,” 162 parameters) penalized combined score (0.54) despite higher train/test accuracy, suggesting that a blend of static and parameterized two-qubit gates is critical.  
- Excessive circuit depth without commensurate expressivity gains led to parameter inefficiency: deeper designs (> 80 layers) did not surpass or even match the performance of the current best mid-depth ansatz.

## Implementation Insights  
- The current best program’s ANSATZ_SPEC directly encodes  
  • Per-qubit RY input rotations (“ry_0”…“ry_8”);  
  • A shared “rz_pre” layer across all qubits;  
  • Six parameterized CRZ pairwise gates interleaved with six static CZs;  
  • Two three-qubit ZZZ interactions on strategically chosen triples;  
  • A final shared “rz_post” layer.  
- Balanced gate mix: 120 trainable parameters, depth = 63, gate_count = 273. This mid-sized ansatz yields stronger generalization than both smaller (< 50 layers) and larger (> 80 layers) designs.  
- Parameter naming conventions (e.g., “crz_0…crz_6,” “zzz_0…zzz_1”) and uniform application of pre/post RZ layers simplify parameter grouping, likely aiding optimizer convergence.  
- Explicitly alternating parameterized and fixed entanglers fosters circuit expressivity while containing overfitting, as seen by a controlled generalization gap of 0.10.

## Performance Analysis  
- Score progression: 0.52 (Gen 0 & Gen 1) → 0.56 (Gen 2 best) → 0.54 (Gen 4 & Gen 5). Only the balanced CRZ+CZ+ZZZ design achieved the 0.56 peak.  
- Parameter count vs. score: too few parameters (84) or too many (162) both underdeliver; 120 parameters delivered the highest combined score.  
- Depth vs. score: circuits around 50–65 layers hit top performance; depths below 50 limited expressivity (0.52 score) and depths above 80 incurred overhead without accuracy gain (0.54 score).  
- Generalization gap remains around 0.10–0.12 for higher-scoring circuits, indicating the dominant challenge is model bias (ansatz expressivity) rather than variance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a “rz_mid” shared rotation layer between your CZ/CRZ alternation and the ZZZ block.  Specifically, after the last CZ gate add an RZ on all nine qubits with a single new parameter “rz_mid,” then proceed to the two ZZZs as before.  This mirrors the proven benefit of global pre/post-RZ flexibility and only increases depth by one layer, keeping total parameters ~121 and depth ~64.

For example, remove the CRZ on wires [0,8] and its neighboring CZ, and insert a CCRZ on wires [0,4,8] with param “ccrz_0.”  This swaps two gates for one parameterized three-qubit interaction, testing whether CCRZ can outperform ZZZ in capturing higher-order correlations.

For instance, append ZZZ on wires [2,6,7] with param “zzz_2” and remove the CRZ on [1,3] (“crz_3”).  This keeps depth ~63 and gate count ~273 while probing whether an extra three-qubit block boosts expressivity.

This slight asymmetry injects more phase flexibility where needed, leveraging the success of shared RZs while controlling total parameter count and depth.

For example, change “crz_2” from [0,8] to [3,6] and “crz_5” from [3,5] to [4,7], keeping parameter names and counts identical.
