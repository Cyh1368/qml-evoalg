# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Balanced two-qubit and three-qubit entanglement: The “9-Qubit Layered RY-RZ-CRZ-ZZZ Ansatz” (Generation 2) and the current best seed program both combine parameterized CRZ gates on six pairs with interleaved fixed CZs and exactly two ZZZ interactions. This pattern lifted the combined score from 0.52 → 0.56 and achieved the best validation (0.60) and test (0.54) accuracies with a generalization gap of 0.10.  
- Pre- and post-entanglement global RZ layers: Surrounding the entangling block with a shared “rz_pre” and “rz_post” layer consistently tightened the generalization gap (0.10 in the best program vs. 0.12–0.15 in alternatives like Gen 3 and Gen 6).  
- Full per-qubit RY initialization: Every top-performing circuit (scores ≥ 0.54) begins with individual RY rotations on all nine qubits (e.g., “ry_0” … “ry_8”), providing a consistently strong input embedding.  
- Mid-range parameter budget and depth: Circuits with ~120 trainable parameters, depth ~63, and gate count ~270 (as in the best program) hit the optimal expressivity–trainability trade-off, outperforming both smaller (< 100) and larger (> 150) parameter designs.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization and high gate count: Generation 6’s “9-Qubit Layered Variational Ansatz” (186 params, depth 75) reached only 0.55, and Generation 3’s dense CRZ mesh (138 params, depth 47) stalled at 0.54, indicating diminishing returns beyond ~120 parameters.  
- Excessive three-qubit interactions: The Generation 8 “Layered CRZ-Ansatz” (162 params, four ZZZ gates, depth 69) dropped to a 0.50 combined score with a 0.19 gen-gap, showing that too many multi-qubit gates boost training accuracy but harm generalization.  
- Sole reliance on parameterized entanglers: Gen 3’s CRZ-only network (no fixed CZ) delivered modest gains (0.54) but underperformed the mixed CRZ+CZ pattern, confirming that a blend of static and parametric two-qubit gates is critical.  
- Inconsistent parameter specification: Generation 13 failed validation entirely due to mismatched “wire” vs. “wires” fields in ANSATZ_SPEC, underscoring the need for strict formatting consistency.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Consistent naming and grouping: The best program uses systematic param names (“ry_0…8,” “rz_pre,” “crz_0…6,” “zzz_0…1,” “rz_post”), simplifying optimizer state and likely aiding convergence.  
- Alternation of fixed and controlled rotations: Placing each CRZ next to a CZ on neighboring or nonadjacent pairs (e.g., [0,2] CRZ–[0,3] CZ, [1,3] CRZ–[1,8] CZ) balances expressivity with parameter efficiency.  
- Shared-parameter layers for global phases: Single “rz_pre” and “rz_post” parameters applied across all qubits add flexible phase shifts without inflating parameter count.  
- Moderate circuit footprint: A depth of ~63 and ~273 total gates (including 120 trainable) strikes a sweet spot—deep enough for expressivity but shallow enough to avoid overfitting.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score evolution:  
  • 0.52 – 0.54 range: early designs with pure CRZ or mixed entanglers (Gen 3 & Gen 7 at 0.54)  
  • 0.55: added RX+CCRZ in Gen 6 (186 params, depth 75)  
  • 0.56: optimal CRZ+CZ+ZZZ pattern in Gen 2 and replicated in the current best (120 params, depth 63)  
- Parameter count vs. score: peak performance at ~120 params; modest drop-offs at 126 params (Gen 7 → 0.54) and 138 params (Gen 3 → 0.54), and sharper declines beyond 160 params.  
- Depth vs. score: ideal layer count ~60–65; shallower (< 50 in Gen 3) limited expressivity, deeper (> 75 in Gen 6) incurred overhead without surpassing 0.56.  
- Generalization gap trends: best designs hold ∼0.10 gap, whereas over-parameterized or deep circuits see gaps grow to 0.12–0.19, reflecting overfitting risk.
