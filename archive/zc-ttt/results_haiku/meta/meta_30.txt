# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mid-layer three-qubit ZZZ interactions boosted expressivity without overfitting. Generation 26 (“Layered RY-RZ Entangled Ansatz with Shared Phases”) added a third ZZZ gate (on [2,6,7]) and reached the top combined score of 0.60, up from 0.59 in Generation 18’s three-ZZZ design.  
- Parameter sharing on symmetric CRZ edges cut overhead while preserving fidelity. Both Generation 18 and Generation 26 reused “crz_shared_0” for edges [0,2] and [2,4], achieving 114 total parameters and pushing scores above 0.58.  
- Fixed five-layer structure with global pre- and post-entanglement RZ phases stabilized training. Generations 18, 20, and 26 all used a single shared “rz_pre” and “rz_post,” consolidating phase shifts into two parameters and maintaining generalization gaps ≤ 0.09.  
- Tuning to ~114 variational parameters hit the sweet spot. Generation 26’s 114 parameters outperformed both higher-param (120 in Generation 27, score 0.58) and lower-param (108 in Generation 28, score 0.57) architectures.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Overparameterization above ~120 increased depth but yielded diminishing returns. Generation 25 (144 params, depth 69) scored only 0.55, and Generation 27 (120 params, depth 75) reached just 0.58 despite extra CCRZ interactions.  
- Pruning below ~110 parameters undercut expressivity. Generation 28’s reduction to 108 params (depth 69) fell to 0.57, showing that cutting too many parameters hurts performance.  
- Heavy use of three-qubit CCRZ gates without balanced sharing led to overcomplexity. Generation 25 combined ZZZ and CCRZ interactions and settled at 0.55, while Generation 27’s added CCRZ yielded only 0.58.  
- Excessively deep circuits (>75 layers/gates) failed to improve accuracy. Circuits like Generation 19 with 90 params and 339 gates got stuck at 0.57, underscoring a practical gate budget near 270–280.

## Implementation Insights  
- Maintain a clear, systematic ANSATZ_SPEC naming scheme. The winning Generation 26 lists “ry_0…ry_8,” “rz_pre,” “crz_shared_0…crz_5,” “zzz_0…zzz_2,” and “rz_post,” simplifying parameter management and optimizer convergence.  
- Reuse a single shared CRZ parameter on geometrically equivalent edges. Employing “crz_shared_0” on [0,2] and [2,4] cut parameters by ~10% with no measurable drop in expressivity.  
- Distribute three-qubit ZZZ gates over non-overlapping triplets. Placing ZZZ on qubit sets [0,4,8], [2,5,8], and [2,6,7] provides broad mid-circuit correlations that lift the combined score by ~0.01.  
- Centralize phase shifts with two global RZ parameters. Sharing “rz_pre” and “rz_post” across all nine qubits reduces parameter count by 16 without harming model flexibility.

## Performance Analysis  
- Generation 26’s combined score of 0.60 edges out Generation 18 (0.59) by adding one more ZZZ gate while holding parameters at 114.  
- Generalization gap rose from 0.06 (Generation 18) to 0.09 (Generation 26), indicating a trade-off between slightly higher expressivity and overfitting.  
- Gate counts clustered around 270–280 correlated with top scores; moving above (291 in Generation 25) or below (267 in Generation 28) saw performance drop.  
- Parameter-to-score mapping shows a clear peak at 114 parameters. Architectures below (~108) or above (~120–144) uniformly underperform the 114-param baseline.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Add a shared mid-layer RZ gate (“rz_mid”) on all nine qubits immediately before the ZZZ block. This +1-param/+1-depth tweak mirrors past successes with global phase injections, tightening control over collective three-qubit correlations right before your most expressive interactions.  

2. Swap the CRZ on wires [0,7] for a CRX with a new parameter “crx_1.” By keeping the rest of the entangling layer identical, you preserve gate count and depth while probing complementary controlled-X rotations—a mixed-entangler strategy that has lifted expressivity in prior trials.  

3. Introduce a second shared CRZ parameter “crz_shared_1” for edges [3,5] and [5,7], merging the existing “crz_4” and “crz_5” into one. Reinvest the freed parameter slot to add a CZ between qubits [1,6], boosting connectivity on a new axis without increasing your total parameter count (~114) or depth (~75).  

4. Replace the ZZZ on [2,6,7] with a CCRZ gate using the same “zzz_2” parameter. This isolates the effect of a lighter three-qubit coupling, helping you balance expressivity against the CCRZ-heavy complexity that dampened performance in overparameterized designs.  

5. Split the global pre-entanglement RZ into two zone-specific parameters—“rz_pre_a” on qubits 0–4 and “rz_pre_b” on qubits 5–8. This +1-param refinement offers finer phase control across subgraphs, emulating successful phase-splitting patterns from earlier generations while retaining your five-layer structure and near-optimal parameter budget.
