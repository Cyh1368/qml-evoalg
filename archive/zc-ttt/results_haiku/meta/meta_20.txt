# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three-layer structure with per-qubit RY → mid-layer entanglement → final shared RZ  
  • Exemplified by Generation 2 (“9-Qubit Layered RY-RZ-CRZ-ZZZ Ansatz,” score 0.56, 120 params, depth 63) and refined in the current best (Generation 14, score 0.57, 162 params, depth 83).  
- Exactly two ZZZ gates in a dedicated mid-layer  
  • Both Generation 2 and Generation 14 use 2 ZZZ blocks (“zzz_0” on [0,4,8] and “zzz_1” on [1,5,6]) to boost expressivity without catastrophic overfitting.  
- Complementary CRZ entanglers before and after multi-qubit interactions  
  • Generation 14 adds CRZ edges ([0,7], [0,8], [1,8], [2,6], [4,8]) around the 3-qubit gates, echoing the interleaved CRZ–CZ pattern of earlier top designs.  
- Uniform final shared RZ (“rz_post”) across all 9 qubits  
  • Present in Generation 2 (gap 0.10) and retained in Generation 14 (gap 0.13), this global phase shift tightens the generalization gap compared to per-qubit end rotations.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Overparameterization beyond ~130 parameters  
  • Generation 14’s 162 parameters/depth 83 only edges score from 0.56 to 0.57 but increases the test-train gap to 0.13. Generation 8 (162 params, 4 ZZZ) fell to 0.50 with gap 0.19.  
- Excessive three-qubit gates (>2 ZZZ or heavy CCRZ use)  
  • Designs like Generation 8 (4 ZZZ) and Generation 11 (CCRZ-heavy) boosted train accuracy but severely overfit and hurt test performance.  
- Aggressive parameter sharing or naming collisions  
  • Generations 15, 21–23 reused few parameter names and failed validation entirely (combined score 0.0), demonstrating that overly rigid sharing cripples expressivity and breaks backend constraints.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Evolving only ANSATZ_SPEC with systematic naming  
  • The current best’s scheme (“ry_0…8,” “rz_pre,” “crz_0…11,” “zzz_0…1,” “ccrz_0…1,” “rz_post”) mirrors Generation 10’s clarity and yields stable optimizer convergence.  
- Trade-off between single vs. split pre-RZ parameterization  
  • Generation 14 uses one global “rz_pre” (depth 83), slightly improving combined score to 0.57. In contrast, split “rz_pre_a/b” in Generation 10 kept depth 63, gap 0.08, and matched score 0.56 more efficiently.  
- Adding CCRZ gates can lift expressivity if balanced by gap control  
  • Generation 14’s two CCRZs (“ccrz_0” on [0,1,4], “ccrz_1” on [2,3,7]) raise combined score by 0.01 over pure-ZZZ designs, but at the cost of deeper circuits and a wider generalization gap.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Sweet spot of ~120–126 parameters vs. diminishing returns above 140  
  • Generation 2 (120 params, score 0.56), Generation 10 (126 params, score 0.56), and the current best (162 params, score 0.57) show marginal gains past 130 params come with larger gaps and inefficiency.  
- Optimal depth ≈63 vs. deeper circuits  
  • Top performers at depth 63/gate 273 hit combined ~0.56 with gap ~0.08. Pushing to depth 83/gate 285 only nudges score to 0.57 while gap widens to 0.13.  
- Consistent validation accuracy ~0.60 across best designs  
  • Indicates robust generalization in the three-layer, mixed-entangler pattern. Generation 14 holds validation at 0.64, comparable to earlier successes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a global “rz_mid” layer immediately before the two ZZZ blocks. Concretely, add  
   {“gate”: “RZ”, “wire”: i, “param”: “rz_mid”} for i=0–8 between crz_6 and zzz_0.  
   This single-parameter framing has boosted mid-layer expressivity in earlier designs (leveraging symmetric RZ framing) with only +1 param (n→163) and +1 depth (d→84), and should tighten phase control right before your most powerful three-qubit interactions.  

2. Swap one long-range CRZ–CZ pair for a CRX–CZ pair to probe Y–axis entanglement. For example, replace  
   {“gate”: “CRZ”, “wires”: [1,3], “param”: “crz_2”}  
   {“gate”: “CZ”,  “wires”: [3,5]}  
   with  
   {“gate”: “CRX”, “wires”: [1,3], “param”: “crx_13”}  
   {“gate”: “CZ”,  “wires”: [3,5]}.  
   This preserves your mixed-entangler pattern while testing whether controlled X-axis rotations capture complementary correlations without adding new gates.  

3. Share parameters between two symmetric CRZ gates and repurpose the freed slot for extra static entanglement. Tie crz_0 ([0,2]) and crz_6 ([6,7]) into a single “crz_shared_a,” then insert a CZ on [2,5] using the liberated parameter. This keeps n_params=162 and depth=83, maintains balanced expressivity, and explores if bolstering direct connectivity on [2,5] improves generalization.  

4. Explore an alternative three-qubit coupling by moving the second ZZZ block. Replace  
   {“gate”: “ZZZ”, “wires”: [1,5,6], “param”: “zzz_1”}  
   with  
   {“gate”: “ZZZ”, “wires”: [2,5,8], “param”: “zzz_1”}.  
   By retaining exactly two ZZZ blocks in your dedicated mid-layer, you test whether a shifted triplet captures complementary high-order correlations without increasing parameter count or depth.  

5. Split the single global pre-RZ into two zone-specific parameters to mirror Gen 10’s efficiency. Change all “rz_pre” on qubits 0–4 to “rz_pre_a” and on qubits 5–8 to “rz_pre_b” (still one gate per qubit). This dual-parameter framing offers finer phase tuning across the circuit without altering gate count or depth (n=162, d=83), potentially matching Gen 10’s 0.08 gap efficiency while probing richer pre-entanglement structure.
