# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
• Five-layer functional template (input RY → single global RZ → mixed two-qubit entanglement → balanced three-qubit block → output RY) consistently achieved the top score (current best: Combined 0.60; train/val/test 0.69/0.64/0.65; depth 63; gates 279).  
• Using one shared “rz_phase” across all nine qubits (instead of regional or per-qubit RZ) boosted validation accuracy by ~0.02–0.03 (current best val 0.64 vs. Gen 36/Gen 43 at ~0.62).  
• Non-overlapping triplet interactions covering every qubit exactly once (ZZZ on [0,4,8] & [2,5,7], CCRZ on [1,3,6]) minimized the generalization gap to 0.04 (current best) versus 0.06–0.09 in other high-depth designs.  
• A carefully chosen mixed two-qubit entanglement pattern (12 gates: CRX, CRY, CRZ, CZ on nonadjacent pairs such as [0,2], [0,7], [1,8], [5,7], [6,7], [4,8]) balanced expressivity and gate count, stabilizing val/test accuracies around 0.64–0.65.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
• Excessive circuit depth (>75) and gate count (>327) with high parameter counts (Gen 40: depth 81, 393 gates, 144 params; Combined 0.57) led to only moderate accuracy gains and larger gaps (0.09).  
• Over-parameterized three-qubit blocks without balanced CCRZ usage (multiple ZZZ layers in Gen 40/Gen 42) plateaued at Combined 0.56–0.57 and yielded zero parameter efficiency.  
• Regional or multi-zone shared-RZ strategies (Gen 36-style) did not reduce parameter redundancy effectively and correlated with lower val/test (0.62/0.60) and higher gap (≥0.09).  
• Gate-dictionary inconsistencies and incorrect parameter naming (Gen 49 & Gen 51) caused outright validation failures (Combined 0.0), underscoring the need for strict spec compliance.  

## Implementation Insights  
• Define ANSATZ_SPEC as an explicit, ordered list with uniform parameter names (e.g., ry_0…ry_8, rz_phase, crx_i, zzz_j, ry_out_k) to prevent parsing errors and align with optimizer expectations.  
• Collapse nine independent RZs into one shared “rz_phase” entry to reduce parameter bloat without sacrificing expressivity, directly improving validation performance.  
• Arrange three-qubit gates to cover disjoint triplets—current best’s [0,4,8], [2,5,7], [1,3,6] mapping ensures full qubit coverage and minimal overlap, driving down the generalization gap.  
• Implement a fixed mixed two-qubit entanglement topology (CRX/CRY/CRZ + CZ) on strategically chosen nonadjacent wire pairs to capture diverse correlations with a controlled gate budget (~12 gates).  

## Performance Analysis  
• The current best program’s Combined 0.60 outperforms Gen 40/Gen 42’s 0.57 and Gen 45’s 0.58, yielding +0.02–0.03 in combined score and +0.01–0.05 in test accuracy.  
• Optimal trade-off found at depth 63 and gate count 279; increasing depth to 81 (Gen 40/Gen 45) provided negligible accuracy improvements but worsened generalization.  
• Generalization gap dropped to 0.04 in the best ansatz versus 0.06–0.13 in other five-layer circuits, directly tied to balanced three-qubit layering and global phase sharing.  
• A mid-range parameter count (174) achieved the highest overall performance; both over-parameterization (222 in Gen 42) and under-parameterization (126 in Gen 45) led to either overfitting or underfitting.
