# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-stage echo block with a single shared parameter (“collective_mode”) applied to RY→CZ→RZ twice (Seed program): combined score 0.80, perfect accuracies, zero generalization gap, convergence in ~30 steps with only 8 parameters.  
- Single shared RY rotation + disjoint CZ pairs (“Collective RY with Disjoint CZ Entanglement,” Gen 62): combined 0.80, perfect generalization, depth 12, 72 gates, converges in ~60 steps—proving minimal entangling suffices.  
- Echo layers interleaved with ring-shifted CZ (“Collective-Mode RY-CZ Ring Ansatz,” Gen 59): combined 0.80, depth 24, 144 gates, 8 parameters, converges in ~30 steps—matching the seed’s speed at lower depth.  
- Single global RY parameter + cyclic CZ entanglement (“Global-RY CZ 8-Qubit Ansatz,” Gen 58): combined 0.80, perfect accuracy, but depth 72 and 60-step convergence highlight expressivity without extra parameters.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization of echo stages (“Collective-Mode Echo Ansatz,” Gen 56): 14 parameters, depth 36, gate count 240 but only combined 0.72—extra angles did not boost generalization.  
- Excessive circuit depth without echo duplication (“Global-RY CZ 8-Qubit Ansatz,” Gen 58): despite perfect accuracy, 72-depth circuits converged in ~60 steps, showing depth overhead slows training.  
- Single rotation stage + CZ only (earlier Gen 53 summary in prior insights): score 0.00—confirms need for at least two rotation stages or multi-axis sharing to maintain expressivity.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-wire listing of the same `"param": "collective_mode"` for RY/RZ gates ensures backend compatibility and concise sharing (used in the Seed program and Gen 62).  
- Reusing one angle across two echo stages doubles effective circuit capacity without adding parameters, accelerating convergence to ~30 steps (Seed program vs. single-stage designs).  
- Interleaving collective rotations with sparse CZ entanglement patterns (disjoint pairs or ring shifts) balances expressivity and hardware efficiency—e.g., Gen 59’s ring-shift pattern achieves the same 0.80 score at half the depth of the seed.  
- A single shared RY parameter suffices for full-register mixing when paired with two rotation layers or multi-axis sharing (RY/RZ), as seen in both the Seed program and Gen 63.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Parameter-count sweet spot: all designs with 8 trainable parameters (Seed, Gen 59, 62, 63) reached combined 0.80; increasing to 14 parameters (Gen 56) reduced the score to 0.72.  
- Depth vs. convergence trade-off:  
  • Depth 12 (Gen 62) → ~60 steps to converge.  
  • Depth 24–36 (Seed, Gen 59, 63) → ~30 steps.  
  • Depth 72 (Gen 58) → ~60 steps.  
- Gate-count efficiency: the lowest gate count (72 gates, Gen 62) delivered perfect generalization but slower convergence, while mid-range gate counts (144–240 gates) hit both fast convergence and high accuracy.  
- Loss trends: shallower echo-ring designs (Gen 59 loss 0.03) slightly outperform the seed’s loss 0.04, indicating that compact entangling topologies can improve loss while maintaining perfect accuracy.
