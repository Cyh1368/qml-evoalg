# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Single‐layer parallel CZ matching + global shared RY rotations (8 parameters) yields top combined score (0.80):  
  • “Collective RY and CZ Entangling Ansatz” (Gen 19) uses eight RY(“collective_mode”) gates (one param repeated on wires 0–7) + one layer of CZ on pairs [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7]; depth 12, 72 gates, converges in 60 steps, zero generalization gap.  
  • Aligns with earlier Gen 10 “single_matching_entangler” (also 8 params, depth 12, score 0.80), confirming this minimal entangler pattern.  
- Moderate entanglement depth accelerates training without sacrificing accuracy:  
  • Reducing from chain/ring topologies (depth 18–23, 90–210 steps) to one parallel CZ layer halves convergence time (60 steps) while preserving 100% train/validation/test accuracy.  
- Eight logical parameters (one per repeated RY) hit the expressivity sweet spot:  
  • Parameter efficiency ≈0.12 (8 params/72 gates) consistently correlates with combined score 0.80.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Heavy parameter sharing + fixed ring CZ yields zero scoring and fails validation:  
  • “Shared-RY Ring Entanglement Ansatz” (Gen 15) uses one shared RY + two CZ ring matchings → score 0.0, incorrect behavior due to under-expressivity.  
- Spec‐format errors + minimal rotations break validation:  
  • “8-Qubit Collective RY with Parallel CZ” (Gen 17) mixes `"wire"` vs `"wires"` keys and uses a single shared RY → score 0.0, fails all tests.  
- Pure rotation layers without entanglement underfit:  
  • “Collective RY Ansatz on 8 Qubits” (Gen 21) applies one shared RY layer only, depth 6 → combined score 0.45, test accuracy 0.52, indicating entanglement is essential.  
- Over-parameterization adds cost without benefit:  
  • “Global Rotations with Parallel CZ Entanglement” (Gen 18) uses two collective parameters (RX+RY) → 14 params, depth 18, score 0.72; perfect accuracy but lower parameter efficiency (0.07) than 8-param designs.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Repeat identical RY gate entries to define a collective mode:  
  • Eight dictionary entries `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"collective_mode"}` create uniform coverage with one parameter, as in Gen 19.  
- Use fixed parallel CZ entanglers for minimal depth:  
  • A single CZ layer on disjoint adjacent pairs (`"wires":[0,1]`, etc.) yields depth 12 total and fast convergence.  
- Maintain correct spec keys to pass validation:  
  • Use `"wire"` for single‐qubit gates and `"wires"` for two‐qubit gates consistently; misnaming causes spec parsing failures (Gen 17).  
- Avoid extra rotation or RZ layers beyond collective RY:  
  • Additional RZ or RX layers increase depth/gate count without score gains (see Gen 5 “shared_parity_layer” with depth 29, score 0.68).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score vs. parameter count:  
  • 8-param ansätze → 0.80 combined; 14-param → 0.72; minimal/no entangler → 0.45; over-shared ring/no spec alignment → 0.0.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with final score:  
  • Efficiency ≈0.12 (8/72) → 0.80; efficiency ≈0.07 (14/120) → 0.72; efficiency ≤0.06 → ≤0.45.  
- Depth vs. convergence steps:  
  • Depth 12 designs consistently converge in ~60 steps; depth 18–23 designs require 90–210 steps for similar accuracy.  
- Entanglement topology impact:  
  • One parallel CZ layer suffices for zero generalization gap, matching multi‐layer chain or ring entanglers at lower circuit cost.
