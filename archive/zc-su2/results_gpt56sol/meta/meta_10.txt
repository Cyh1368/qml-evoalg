# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Global-shared single-qubit rotations plus a single parallel CZ matching attains top score  
  – Program “single_matching_entangler” (Gen 10) achieved combined 0.80, perfect accuracies, depth 12, 72 gates, and converged in 60 steps using one shared RY parameter on all qubits and one depth-1 CZ layer.  
- Edge-colored CZ chains with collective RY also hit top performance  
  – “collective_ry_colored_chain” (Gen 7) and “globally_shared_ry_chain” (Gen 9) each scored 0.80 with eight parameters, zero generalization gap, but deeper circuits (depth 18/23, 90 gates) and ~120 steps to converge.  
- Two-parameter parity-shared RY plus linear CZ chain delivers strong baseline  
  – Earlier designs (Gen 3 & Gen 6) using “ry_even”/“ry_odd” achieved combined 0.72, perfect test accuracy, zero gap, 14 params, depth 23, 90 gates, and converged in ~90 steps.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-sharing rotations and adding global RZ layers inflates depth without benefit  
  – “shared_parity_layer” (Gen 5) introduced a fully shared RZ atop parity-shared RY, yielding only 0.68 combined score, low parameter efficiency (0.05), depth 29, despite perfect accuracies.  
- Partial coverage of rotation parameters limits expressivity  
  – The 4-qubit shared RY scheme in Gen 12 converged to ~94% accuracy and combined 0.77, showing that rotating only a subset of wires can induce a small generalization gap (~0.01) and lower overall score.  
- Ring entanglement doubles training time versus chains  
  – Previous ring-CZ designs matched accuracy but required ~180 steps to converge compared to ~90 for linear CZ chains, highlighting over-entanglement overhead.  
- Excessive parameterization fails to pay off on this task  
  – The 146-parameter RY–RZ sandwich ansatz (Gen 0) scored just 0.61, with parameter efficiency of 0.01, despite perfect train/test accuracy, due to unnecessary depth/gates.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-wire entries for identical params enable collective rotations  
  – In ANSATZ_SPEC, listing eight `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"collective_ry"}` lines creates a true global rotation mode with minimal code changes.  
- One-layer parallel CZ matchings minimize depth  
  – Specifying CZ gates on disjoint pairs `{"wires":[0,1]}`, `[2,3]`, etc., yields a depth-1 entangling block, halving depth relative to nearest-neighbor chains.  
- Linear CZ chains trade depth for connectivity  
  – A sequence of CZs on `[i,i+1]` preserves expressivity but pushes depth to ~18–23 and gate count to 90, useful when device connectivity is limited.  
- Avoid unnecessary RZ/extra rotation layers  
  – Adding shared RZ (Gen 5) or sandwiching rotations around CZs (earlier high-param designs) increases gate count/depth with no accuracy gain on this dataset.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score correlates with parameter efficiency up to ~0.12  
  – Ansätze with 8 params (efficiency 0.12) reached 0.80 combined, whereas parity-shared designs (efficiency 0.07) peaked at 0.72, and over-parametrized circuits (0.01) fell to 0.61.  
- Depth reduction accelerates convergence without losing accuracy  
  – Moving from 23–18 depth (chain CZ) to 12 depth (parallel CZ) halved convergence steps from ~120 to 60 while maintaining perfect test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
- Minimal entanglement suffices for this classification task  
  – A single parallel CZ layer (Gen 10) matches the performance of multi-layer chains (Gen 7/9) at lower hardware cost, highlighting that expressivity requirements are modest.  
- Partial or uneven qubit coverage impairs results  
  – Designs that skip rotating some qubits (Gen 12) or over-entangle via ring topologies introduce small accuracy drops or longer training, confirming that uniform single-qubit coverage plus sparse entanglement is optimal here.
