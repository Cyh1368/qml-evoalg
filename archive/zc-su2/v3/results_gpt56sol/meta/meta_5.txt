# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity-based parameter sharing  
  • Generation 3 (“Ring Entangled RY-RZ Quantum Ansatz”) uses just two RY parameters (`ry_even`, `ry_odd`) alternating on even/odd wires.  
  • This pattern achieved the top combined score (0.38) with train/val/test = 0.75/0.60/0.57, outperforming both full-param (Gen 0: 0.29) and over-shared (Gen 1/5: 0.36) designs.  
- Shared pre- and post-entanglement RZ layers  
  • Using a single `rz_pre` on all qubits before CZ and `rz_post` after reduces distinct parameters to 4 without harming performance.  
- Cyclic CZ entanglement ring  
  • Full 8-qubit ring connectivity (CZ between 0–1,…,7–0) consistently beat linear chain entanglers (Gen 5 test 0.46 vs Gen 3 test 0.57).  
- Moderate parameter count + high depth/gate count  
  • Retaining depth = 33 and gate_count = 96 while reducing distinct parameters to 4 hit the sweet spot—high capacity but controlled overfitting.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterization (Gen 0: 24 distinct params)  
  • Yields very high train accuracy (0.88) but large generalization gap (0.31) and test accuracy only 0.56, combined score 0.29.  
- Aggressive parameter tying (3 distinct params in Gen 1 & Gen 5)  
  • Both designs stalled at combined 0.36, with underfitting visible in low train (0.69) and test (≈0.47) accuracies.  
- Simplified linear CZ chains  
  • Shared-param CZ-chain Ansatz (Gen 5) matched the depth/gate count of the ring (20 depth/93 gates) yet fell short (test 0.46) of ring-entangled versions.  
- Excessive depth-to-parameter ratio without structured sharing  
  • Gen 2 had depth = 33 and 5 distinct params but no alternating pattern; it achieved combined 0.37 but lagged behind Gen 3’s 0.38.

## Implementation Insights

- Define RY layers with just two named parameters (`ry_even`, `ry_odd`) and assign them alternately across wires to boost expressivity per parameter.  
- Use global `rz_pre` and `rz_post` parameters on all qubits instead of distinct RZs per wire—simplifies tuning and limits overfitting.  
- Encode the CZ entanglement as a true ring (including the wrap-around gate between qubit 7 and qubit 0) for maximal connectivity.  
- Maintaining a consistent high depth (33) and gate count (96) is acceptable when paired with strategic parameter sharing, as seen in the best-performing spec.

## Performance Analysis

- Combined score progression:  
  • Gen 0: 0.29 → Gen 1/5: 0.36 → Gen 2: 0.37 → Gen 3: 0.38  
  • Each generational tweak to parameter sharing and entanglement yielded measurable gains.  
- Test accuracy vs. generalization gap trade-off:  
  • Gen 2 minimized gap (0.12) but had lower test (0.50); Gen 3 balanced both with test 0.57 and gap 0.18.  
- Parameter efficiency (n_params/depth) rose modestly from Gen 2 (0.07) to Gen 3 (0.09), correlating with the top combined score.  
- The best program leverages structured sharing and full ring entanglement to outperform both heavily shared under-parametrized models and fully unshared over-parametrized ones.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce parity‐based pre‐entanglement RZ sharing. Replace the single “rz_pre” with two parameters, “rz_pre_even” and “rz_pre_odd”, applied to wires by parity. This preserves the parity‐sharing principle that boosted RY expressivity in Gen 3 while giving the model a bit more flexibility to fit phase shifts without exploding the parameter count.

2. Swap each static CZ in the ring for a shared‐parameter controlled‐RZ gate. Define a single “crz_entangle” parameter and replace all eight CZ gates with CRZ(wires=[i,i+1], param="crz_entangle"). You keep full ring connectivity (proven superior to chains) but now the entanglers themselves become tunable, potentially capturing correlations more richly with only one extra parameter.

3. Double the ansatz depth by stacking two identical parity‐RY → parity‐RZ pre → ring‐CZ → RZ post blocks back-to-back. This raises depth to ~66 and gate count to ~192 but retains 4–6 distinct parameters. Given that high depth paired with structured sharing yielded Gen 3’s best result, this tests whether repeated feature‐extraction stages further boost generalization.

4. Enrich the entanglement graph with long-range CZ edges (e.g. CZ on wires [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7]) in addition to the 8-qubit ring. This adds only four extra gates yet densifies connectivity, which may reduce the generalization gap further by enabling more direct correlations across distant qubits—leveraging the success of full-ring entanglement.

5. Evaluate a mod-4 RY‐sharing scheme for finer granularity. Instead of two parity parameters, introduce four RY parameters (“ry_0”–“ry_3”) assigned by wire_index mod 4. This modest increase from 2→4 distinct RY parameters preserves structured sharing while probing whether slightly richer angular diversity can edge test accuracy above Gen 3’s 0.57.
