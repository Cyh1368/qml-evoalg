# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Single-parameter alternating-axis mixer plus multi-scale CZ entanglement delivers top scores.  
  - The current best “Shared-parameter ring-structured quantum ansatz” (Gen 24, patch shared_axis_brickwork) uses one shared “shared_mix” on alternating RY/RX and achieves combined score 0.51 (train/val/test = 0.94/0.78/0.78, gap 0.16, depth 12, 60 gates, param eff 0.26).  
- Two-scale CZ layering (nearest-neighbor brickwork, staggered links, antipodal pairs) maximizes spread of correlations without extra parameters.  
  - Gen 24 and the Parity-Based RY and CZ Entanglement Ansatz (Gen 21, parity_cz_global_mix) share this pattern and score 0.50 with only three distinct parameters, depth 15, gate 84.  
- Depth-limited circuits around 12–15 layers consistently outperform deeper designs.  
  - Depth 12 (Gen 24) and depth 15 (Gen 21) beat depth 24–33 circuits, reducing overfitting and generalization gaps.  
- Omitting RZ rotations preserves expressivity while cutting depth and gate count.  
  - Top performers (Gen 24, Gen 21) exclude any RZ gates, whereas Gen 14 (with global RZ) attained only 0.44 and a larger gap (0.21).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Extreme over-tying of single-qubit parameters leads to underfitting.  
  - Shared Even-Odd RY Ansatz with CZ Brickwork (Gen 22, shared_depth_doubling) used only two parameters over 24 layers and scored 0.39 (train/test ≈ 0.56/0.46, gap 0.10).  
- Dense two-qubit entanglement with few parameters constrains margins.  
  - Parity-Shared RY-CZ Ansatz (Gen 19, stacked_parity_cz) used six parameters and a dense CZ layout but scored 0.39 (gap 0.13) with worst-group margin 0.02.  
- Adding global RZ rotations increases depth without commensurate gains.  
  - Even-Odd Shared-Parameter Ring CRZ-RY (Gen 14, tunable_shared_ring) used a global RZ layer and 96 gates, scoring 0.44 but suffering a 0.21 gap and low parameter efficiency (0.08).  
- Simply deepening circuits without targeted connectivity worsens generalization.  
  - Alternating RY + global RZ + CZ mesh designs at depth 33–36 did not surpass 0.44 and exhibited gaps up to 0.28.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Encode the mixer as a single shared parameter on alternating axes to maximize regularization and parity distinction.  
  - In Gen 24 ANSATZ_SPEC: alternate “RY” on even wires and “RX” on odd, all using param “shared_mix.”  
- Append CZ gates in three blocks: nearest-neighbor brickwork, staggered brickwork, and antipodal links.  
  - Example snippet:  
    for (i,j) in [(0,1),(2,3)…]: ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,j]})  
    then staggered pairs and finally antipodal [(0,4),(1,5)…].  
- Omit single-qubit RZ gates unless they close a demonstrable expressivity gap; none of the top circuits include RZ.  
- Maintain shallow depth (≤15) and keep gate count around 60–84 to balance expressivity and generalization.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The best combined score rose from 0.48 (earlier top designs) to 0.51 in Gen 24 by trimming depth to 12 and collapsing to one distinct parameter.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates positively with score:  
  - Gen 24: 0.26 efficiency → 0.51 score; Gen 21: 0.15 → 0.50; mid-range designs (eff 0.08–0.12) only reached 0.39–0.44.  
- Generalization gaps shrink as depth decreases: top circuits (depth 12–15) show gaps 0.16–0.19, whereas depth 24–33 designs see gaps up to 0.28.  
- Gate count around 60 yields peak performance; exceeding ~84 gates leads to overfitting without score improvements.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parameterize the antipodal CZ links as shared CRZs  
   Replace each static antipodal CZ in ANSATZ_SPEC (wires [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7]) with  
       {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"crz_long"}  
   This adds a single “crz_long” parameter to let the optimizer tune the strength of long-range correlations, building on the proven benefit of multi-scale entanglement without inflating parameter count.  

2. Inject a mid-range (distance-2) CZ layer with one shared parameter  
   After the staggered CZ block and before the antipodal links, append:  
       for (i,j) in [(0,2),(1,3),(4,6),(5,7)]:  
           {"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"cz_mid"}  
   This introduces a third entanglement scale via a single “cz_mid” parameter, tightening the correlation spread while keeping depth ≤15 and param_eff high.  

3. Split the single “shared_mix” into two parity-tied mixers  
   Change the alternating RY/RX entries to use distinct shared parameters “mix_even” on even wires (RY) and “mix_odd” on odd wires (RX):  
       param = "mix_even" if wire%2==0 else "mix_odd"  
   This moves from 1→2 distinct parameters, allowing finer control over each parity’s rotation without losing the regularization benefits of structured tying.  

4. Add a nearest-neighbor XX entangling block with one shared angle  
   Immediately after the single-qubit mixer layer, insert for each even i:  
       {"gate":"XX","wires":[i,i+1],"param":"xx_nbr"}  
   This tests an alternative Ising-type interaction (XX) with a single “xx_nbr” parameter, potentially capturing correlations that CZ alone may miss while preserving shallow depth.  

5. Reorder CZ blocks to apply antipodal links first  
   Move the four antipodal CZ (or CRZ, if #1 applied) entries to directly follow the single-qubit mixer, then execute the local brickwork and staggered CZ layers as before.  
   By promoting global entanglement earlier, this mutation explores whether front-loading long-range correlations improves training dynamics and generalization, all without changing depth or parameter count.
