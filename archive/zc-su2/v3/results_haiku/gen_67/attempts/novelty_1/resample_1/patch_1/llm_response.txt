# Analysis

Looking at the prior performance data:

1. **Seed 1** (score 0.58): 12 Ising gates (XX, YY, ZZ) with 12 distinct params, worst_group_margin 0.34
2. **Seed 2** (score 0.59): Mixed approach with CRX/CRY + ZZ, 10 distinct params, worst_group_margin 0.31
3. **Current** (score 0.50): Added CRZ diagonal gates (shared param "crz_diag"), 11 distinct params, **worst_group_margin 0.30**, but validation accuracy dropped to 0.88 and convergence slowed to 510 steps

**Key observations:**
- The current program's performance degraded despite lower parameter count
- The shared CRZ gates with parameter "crz_diag" appear to hurt worst-group margin and generalization
- Seed 1 achieved the best worst_group_margin (0.34) with all unique Ising gates
- The recommendation emphasizes pure Ising gates (XX, YY, ZZ) with unique parameters

**Strategy:**
Return to the Seed 1 architecture (which scored 0.59 nominally but had better worst_group_margin structure) but:
1. Replace the alternating RY pattern with a cleaner single RY layer
2. Use all unique Ising gate parameters (no sharing on two-qubit gates)
3. Add selective RZ gates for rotation flexibility
4. Keep total parameter count economical (aim for ~10 distinct params)
5. Simplify the structure to reduce convergence time

The issue with current is that the CRZ shared parameters ("crz_diag") don't provide good signal for separating the hardest samples. Returning to pure Ising with unique parameters should restore worst-group margin.
