# Analysis

Looking at the performance metrics:

1. **Seed program (0.44 score)**: Good baseline with 12 distinct params, decent validation margin (0.44) but weak worst_group_margin (0.18).

2. **Second program (0.58 score)**: Best performer with 10 distinct params, excellent validation accuracy (0.97), strong worst_group_margin (0.28), and good generalization (gap 0.04). Uses diverse gate types (XX, YY, ZZ) on different qubit pairs.

3. **Current program (0.42 score)**: Over-regularized with only 3 distinct params. This extreme parameter sharing has collapsed performance:
   - Train accuracy drops to 0.62
   - Validation margin nearly zero (0.02)
   - Worst group margin is zero (0.00)
   - The circuit lacks expressivity despite minimal parameters

The problem is clear: the current program took parameter sharing too far. Sharing RY across all qubits, XX across all even/odd pairs, and RZ globally removes critical flexibility needed for classification, especially for the hardest samples (worst_group_margin).

The recommendation to test "modest additional parameter sharing among entanglers" is the right direction. The second program (0.58) demonstrates that:
- Alternating RY parameters (ry_0, ry_1) work better than full sharing
- Diverse two-qubit gates (XX, YY, ZZ) with modest sharing significantly improves worst_group_margin
- 10 distinct parameters balances expressivity and regularization

I propose to implement the recommendation: reduce the entangler sharing by introducing YY and ZZ gates with selective parameter sharing, moving closer to the high-performing second program structure.
