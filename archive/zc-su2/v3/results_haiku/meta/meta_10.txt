# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating two RY parameters on even/odd qubits plus shared pre/post RZ layers drove the top combined score of 0.58 (Best Program). This pattern preserves single-qubit rotation diversity with only 2 RY variables (ry_0/ry_1) and tightens phase flexibility via rz_pre/rz_post.  
- Enriching the entangling block with the full set of Ising gates (XX, YY, ZZ) on both nearest neighbors and strategically chosen cross pairs raised combined score from 0.46 (8-Qubit Parity RY-RZ Ising Ansatz) to 0.58. Diversification across [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7] plus [1,4],[3,6] proved especially effective.  
- Capping the number of distinct parameters to ~10 (n_distinct_params = 10, n_params = 12) balanced expressivity and regularization: Best Program converged in ~150 steps, achieved test acc 0.96 with a small gap (0.04), outperforming both over- and under-parameterized designs.  
- Maintaining circuit depth around 15 and total gate count near 90 hit the “sweet spot” for expressivity without overfitting—mirroring the Best Program’s configuration and corroborating earlier findings from Generation 4.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive parameter tying (3–5 distinct parameters) crippled expressivity: e.g., Shared-Parameter Variational Ansatz (Combined 0.38, gap 0.33, param eff 0.09) and Symmetric 8-Qubit Ising-Layer Ansatz (Combined 0.37, gap 0.07, param eff 0.05) both suffered low test accuracy.  
- Restricting two-qubit gates to nearest neighbors only (no cross-pair entanglement) yielded only moderate gains—8-Qubit Shared-Param Ising Ansatz reached Combined 0.44 (test 0.87, gap 0.13) but fell well short of the Best Program’s performance.  
- Over-symmetric entangling patterns with uniform parameter assignments led to overfitting or underfitting: e.g., Shared-parameter Ising entanglement ansatz (Combined 0.34, gap 0.29) showed that simply mixing XX/YY/ZZ without alternating single-qubit rotations and global phases is insufficient.

## Implementation Insights  
- In the Best Program’s ANSATZ_SPEC, RY gates use “param”: “ry_0” on qubits [0,2,4,6] and “ry_1” on [1,3,5,7]—a concise alternation that maximizes per-qubit heterogeneity with just two parameters.  
- The shared RZ blocks (“rz_pre” before and “rz_post” after entanglement) are applied uniformly across all 8 wires, yielding improved validation margins (0.57) and reducing the generalization gap to 0.04.  
- Entangling operations are coded as explicit dict entries—e.g.,  
  {"gate":"XX","wires":[0,1],"param":"xx_01"}—allowing easy extension to YY/ZZ gates on both nearest and cross pairs. This explicit wiring enabled the Best Program’s high expressivity.  
- Structuring the SPEC for 12 total gate parameters but limiting to 10 distinct names ensures sufficient flexibility without inviting overfitting, as evidenced by the Best Program’s stable convergence at ~150 training steps.

## Performance Analysis  
- The Best Program’s combined score of 0.58 surpasses the next best (0.46 from the 8-Qubit Parity RY-RZ Ising Ansatz) by 0.12, with test accuracy jumping from 0.77 to 0.96 and gap shrinking from 0.17 to 0.04.  
- Programs featuring only nearest-neighbor XX couplings or heavy parameter sharing peaked at combined scores ≤0.44, confirming that cross-pair XX/YY/ZZ entanglement is critical for capturing complex correlations.  
- A generalization gap ≤0.07 was observed only when both alternating RY and shared RZ blocks flanked a diversified entangling layer (Best Program and earlier Gen 4), whereas other designs had gaps ≥0.13.  
- Parameter efficiency around 0.08 (Best Program) aligns with optimal accuracy; designs with efficiency >0.10 (e.g., Parity RY-RZ Ansatz, 0.10) or <0.06 (e.g., Shared-Param Ising Ansatz, 0.06) underperformed.
