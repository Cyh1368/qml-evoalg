# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parity-based RY layering with two shared parameters and a shallow four-stage block (RY parity → global RZ_pre → diverse entangler block → global RZ_post) drives top performance.  
  • Seed (“Seed program”) achieves combined 0.59, depth 15, 12 params, worst-group margin 0.31.  
  • Generation 54 (“Alternating RY-Ising Layered Ansatz”) mirrors this pattern and scores 0.58 with 97%/95% val/test accuracy and a 0.05 gap.  
- Explicit per-link entangler parametrization (xx_01, yy_23, zz_45, xx_67, yy_14, zz_36) boosts robustness.  
  • Seed’s unique two-qubit params yield the highest worst-group margin (0.31) compared to shared-entangler variants (e.g., Gen 53’s margin 0.28).  
- Moderate distinct parameter counts (~10) with high parameter efficiency (~0.08) correlates with top scores.  
  • Seed: 10 distinct params, efficiency 0.08 → combined 0.59.  
  • Gen 52/53: 13/10 distinct, efficiency 0.06–0.07 → combined 0.51.  
- Shallow circuits converge stably in fewer steps.  
  • Seed and Gen 54 converge in exactly 90 steps with zero stddev; deeper/shared designs take longer or vary across seeds.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-sharing single-qubit rotations constrains expressivity.  
  • Gen 51 (“8-Qubit Layered Ising Variational Circuit”) uses 15 params (13 distinct) with heavy sharing, scoring only 0.42 and exhibiting a large generalization gap (0.22).  
- Sparse or narrowly targeted entanglement without diversity plateaus performance.  
  • Gen 52’s sparse six-gate layout yields 0.51 combined but still falls short of parity-block designs.  
- Excessive depth/gate count without new entanglers yields diminishing returns.  
  • Gen 51 (depth 24, 144 gates) underperforms vs seed (depth 15, 90 gates).  
  • Gen 57 (“Parity-Split Ising Entangling Ansatz”) adds CRX/CRY/CRZ to parity split but only scores 0.45 with a 0.13 gap.  
- Shared two-qubit parameters limit worst-group margins.  
  • Gen 53 shares long-range CRZ links and reaches only 0.28 worst-group margin vs seed’s 0.31.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Use a simple loop for parity-based RY:  
  for i in range(N_QUBITS):  
  param = f"ry_{i%2}"  
  append({"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":param})  
  • Reduces code duplication and enforces the ry_0/ry_1 pattern of the seed.  
- Broadcast global RZ layers via a wire loop for “rz_pre” and “rz_post”:  
  • Adds uniform phase flexibility at negligible code cost; used in seed, Gen 52, 53, 54.  
- Specify explicit entangler params per link:  
  • {"gate":"XX","wires":[0,1],"param":"xx_01"} …  
  • Enables independent tuning, raising worst-group margins from ~0.14 (under-connected) to ~0.31.  
- Encapsulate the four-stage block in an EVOLVE-BLOCK to ensure reproducible convergence.

- Convergence stability: parity-block designs converge exactly at step 90 with zero stddev; over-shared and deeper circuits show higher variance and slower convergence.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parity-split global RZ layers: replace the single-param “rz_pre” and “rz_post” with even/odd versions—“rz_pre_even”, “rz_pre_odd”, “rz_post_even”, “rz_post_odd”—by looping over wires [0,2,4,6] vs. [1,3,5,7]. This four-parameter extension enforces the same parity symmetry that drove the RY layers’ success and should tighten the generalization gap with negligible depth increase.  
2. Convert the CRX/CRY entanglers into explicit Ising gates: change {"gate":"CRX","wires":[6,7],"param":"xx_67"}→{"gate":"XX","wires":[6,7],"param":"xx_67"} and {"gate":"CRY","wires":[1,4],"param":"yy_14"}→{"gate":"YY","wires":[1,4],"param":"yy_14"}. Unifying all six two-qubit links as XX/YY/ZZ with unique parameters aligns with the seed’s proven pattern and is expected to preserve or boost the worst-group margin.  
3. Enrich connectivity by appending two long-range Ising links—{"gate":"ZZ","wires":[0,3],"param":"zz_03"} and {"gate":"XX","wires":[2,7],"param":"xx_27"}—to the existing entangler block. Prior variants with eight distinct Ising links at depth≈15 saw measurable margin gains, so this adds entanglement diversity without breaking the shallow-circuit regime.  
4. Insert a parity-based mid-circuit mini-block before the final RZ_post stage: for each qubit i apply RY with “ry_mid_{i%2}” then broadcast RZ with a new “rz_mid” parameter. This compact, three-parameter motif mirrors the front-end parity block, adds only ~10 gates, and taps into the sweet spot of 10–12 distinct parameters to boost expressivity.  
5. Introduce a lightweight RX parity layer immediately after the entangler block: apply RX on wire i with “rx_{i%2}” (two shared params for even/odd qubits). Mixing in an orthogonal rotation axis via the same parity-sharing logic enhances local expressivity at minimal depth cost (~8 gates) and may accelerate convergence.
