# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-parameter RY alternation on even/odd qubits + global RZ pre/post layers  
  • Current Best Program (Combined 0.58, train 1.00/val 0.97/test 0.96, gap 0.04) uses “ry_0” on qubits [0,2,4,6], “ry_1” on [1,3,5,7] plus shared “rz_pre”/“rz_post.”  
  • Similarly, Generation 16 (“Shared-Parameter 8-Qubit Ansatz,” Combined 0.55) and Generation 13 (“Shared-Parameter 8-Qubit Ising Ansatz,” Combined 0.55) exploited this pattern to hit train 1.00 and val ≈0.92–0.97.  
- Diversified Ising entanglers with distinct parameters on nearest-neighbor and cross-block pairs  
  • Best Program defines six unique XX/YY/ZZ parameters on wires [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7], [1,4], [3,6], boosting combined from 0.54 (Generation 18) to 0.58.  
  • Generation 13’s improvement to Combined 0.55 over earlier designs coincided with adding cross-block links [1,4] and [3,6] with distinct entangler parameters.  
- Balanced total vs. distinct parameters (n_params≈12, n_distinct≈10) at depth≈15, gate_count≈90  
  • Best Program’s param_efficiency 0.08 and convergence in ~150 steps reflect the sweet spot identified previously.  
  • Generation 18 (n_params 10, n_distinct 8, Combined 0.54) and Generation 16 (n_params 8, n_distinct 8, Combined 0.55) confirm that ~10 distinct parameters at depth 15 avoid both under- and over-fitting.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Heavy entangler parameter sharing across multiple XX/YY/ZZ gates  
  • Generation 15 (“double_ry_alternation_mid_circuit,” Combined 0.33, gap 0.29) and Generation 17 (“deepened_ising_block_reuse,” Combined 0.37, gap 0.25) reused a single XX/YY/ZZ parameter many times, leading to poor validation/test accuracy and low margins.  
- Restricting to nearest-neighbor entanglers or omitting ZZ/YY links  
  • Generation 14 (“Layered RY-RZ-XX Ansatz,” Combined 0.43) only used XX on [i,i+1], capping test accuracy at 0.67.  
  • Earlier “RY-RZ-XX” variants and sparse two-gate patterns (Combined ≤ 0.44) underperformed compared to designs that included full XX/YY/ZZ sets plus cross-links.  
- Increasing depth or gate count without proportionally more distinct parameters  
  • Generation 10’s 18-depth/114-gate circuit with only 11 distinct parameters achieved Combined 0.39, worse than the 15-depth/90-gate Best Program.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-gate parameter naming enables fine-grained expressivity  
  • Best Program’s ANSATZ_SPEC lists each entangler as  
     {"gate":"XX","wires":[0,1],"param":"xx_01"}  
    …  
    {"gate":"ZZ","wires":[3,6],"param":"zz_36"}  
  yielding six distinct entanglers.  
- Two-line RY parity assignment  
  • “for w in range(8): {‘gate’:’RY’,’wire’:w,’param’:f‘ry_{w%2}’}” enforces even/odd alternation with only two parameters.  
- Uniform RZ blocks via shared “rz_pre” and “rz_post”  
  • Eight repeated entries pre- and post-entanglement maintain phase coherence without adding distinct parameters.  
- Parameter balance drives generalization  
  • Best Program’s n_params 12 vs. n_distinct 10 matches its high parameter efficiency (0.08) and low gap (0.04), illustrating the importance of reusing just enough parameters for regularization.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score hierarchy  
  • Best Program: 0.58 > Generation 16 & 13: 0.55 > Generation 18: 0.54 > Generation 7: 0.47 > Generation 14: 0.43 > Generation 10: 0.39 > Generation 17 & 19: ~0.37 > Generation 15: 0.33.  
- Generalization gap vs. expressivity  
  • Best Program gap 0.04 contrasts with gaps 0.12–0.25 in Generations 13,16,17,18—underscoring that diversified entanglers plus parity-RY/RZ sharing minimize overfitting.  
- Parameter efficiency sweet spot  
  • Optimal performance clusters around param_efficiency≈0.08 (Best Program) and declines when efficiency is too low (<0.06 in Gen 17/19) or too high (>0.10 in Gen 7).  
- Depth/gate count sweet spot  
  • Both Best Program and Generation 13 (depth 15, gate_count 90) outperform deeper (>18) or shallower (<15) circuits, confirming ~15 layers/90 gates as ideal for this task.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a mid-circuit RY+RZ “heterogeneity” layer between the existing XX/YY/ZZ block and the final rz_post. Specifically, apply RY(ry_0) on qubits [0,2,4,6] and RY(ry_1) on [1,3,5,7], then a shared RZ(rz_mid) on all eight qubits. Evidence: doubling the proven two-parameter RY alternation mid-circuit adds expressivity with only one extra distinct parameter, mirroring the best program’s low-gap pattern.  

2. Add a set of long-range CRZ entanglers using a single shared parameter. Insert CRZ(crz_diag) on diagonal pairs [0,5], [1,6], [2,7], and [3,4] immediately after the XX/YY/ZZ block. This one-parameter addition tests global phase correlations over maximal qubit distance, leveraging the efficiency of few high-value cross-block couplings.  

3. Expand the Ising entangler graph with two new cross-block ZZ links. Append ZZ gates on wires [0,3] and [4,7] with distinct parameters zz_03 and zz_47. Prior generations (e.g. Gen 13→Best) showed that adding well-chosen cross-block Ising links with unique params boosts combined score, and keeping n_distinct ≤ 12 stays within the sweet spot.  

4. Experiment with moderate two-qubit parameter tying to probe regularization effects. Replace xx_01 and xx_67 with a shared xx_nn parameter, and tie yy_23 and yy_14 to yy_nn, while preserving distinct zz_45 and zz_36. This reduces n_distinct from 10 to 8 and tests whether stronger entangler tying further shrinks the generalization gap without sacrificing expressivity.  

5. Enhance pre-entanglement phase heterogeneity by splitting rz_pre into two parameters. Use rz_pre_even on qubits [0,2,4,6] and rz_pre_odd on [1,3,5,7] instead of a single rz_pre. This mirrors the successful two-parameter RY parity assignment in the phase domain, adding just one new distinct rotation and staying within the empirically optimal parameter-count range.
