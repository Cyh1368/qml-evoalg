<DESCRIPTION>
The current program's full 8-cycle CRZ entangling layer (8 gates sharing "ent1") plus an extra long-range CNOT layer adds substantial depth (63) without improving generalization — indeed it shows a much larger train-test gap (0.16) than the simpler disjoint-pair design (0.03) that scored higher (0.41) in a prior seed. Full-ring parametrized entanglement over-couples all qubits with a single shared angle, which likely amplifies overfitting on the tiny training set, while the extra long-range CNOT layer only adds circuit depth with no trainable benefit.

I propose reducing entangling depth and coupling strength while keeping the same parameter count (still using "ent1"): replace the full 8-edge ring CRZ layer with a sparser 4-edge disjoint-pair CRZ layer (matching the better-performing seed's design), and drop the redundant long-range CNOT layer. This keeps the parameter-free backbone connectivity (from the CNOT ring) for information flow, but reduces the number of parametrized entangling gates and total depth, which should shrink the train/validation gap and improve worst-group margin without changing the parameter count (still 5 distinct params).
</DESCRIPTION>
