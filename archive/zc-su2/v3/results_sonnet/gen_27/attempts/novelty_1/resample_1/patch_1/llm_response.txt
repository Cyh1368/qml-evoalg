<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz (ry_a/ry_b → ring1 → rz_a/rz_b → ring2 → ry_c) achieves reasonable train/val accuracy but the generalization gap is high (0.20) and score is lower than the simpler seed circuits. Following the recommendation, we duplicate the entire gate sequence back-to-back while reusing the exact same five parameter names (ry_a, ry_b, rz_a, rz_b, ry_c). This doubles the effective circuit depth (adding more entangling structure and expressive re-parametrized rotations) without increasing the trainable parameter count at all, since parameters are shared across the repeated block. Deeper circuits with fixed parameter budgets often improve the expressivity/generalization trade-off by allowing richer feature-map re-use (similar to data re-uploading / parameter-tying strategies), which can help reduce the train-test gap while maintaining separation on harder validation groups. Because n_distinct_params stays at 5, parameter economy is preserved, and since scoring rewards fewer distinct params strongly, this is a low-risk way to test whether extra depth (without extra parameters) improves the worst-group margin and reduces the generalization gap.
