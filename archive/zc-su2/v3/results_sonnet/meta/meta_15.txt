# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Global shared rotations + cheap parametrized entanglers drove score gains  
  • The current best ansatz (Combined 0.41, gap 0.03) uses five distinct shared parameters—RX(a), RY(b), RZ(c), CRZ(d) on disjoint pairs, and a final RY(e).  
  • Introducing CRZ(d) on four non‐overlapping qubit pairs added expressive two-qubit phase without raising depth excessively, outperforming “Shared-Parameter Ring Entangler” (Gen 9, 0.39, gap 0.17).  
- Dual CNOT rings with an offset second ring improved mixing  
  • Splitting 12 CNOTs into an 8-gate full ring plus a 4-gate offset ring (as in the best program) reduced the generalization gap to 0.03, compared to single-ring designs (e.g., “Alternating RY-CNOT-RZ”, 0.38, gap 0.20).  
- Depth tuned under parameter constraints  
  • The best circuit’s depth of 42 strikes an optimal balance—deeper than many weaker designs (e.g., Gen 11 depth 45, score 0.34; Gen 13 depth 54, score 0.37) yet limited to five distinct parameters to prevent overfitting.  
- Final shared rotation layer enhances decision boundary  
  • Adding a concluding RY(e) layer (absent in earlier high-scoring circuits) boosted validation accuracy to 0.58 versus ~0.54 in Gen 12 (“Skip-2 Entangling Ansatz”, 0.38, gap 0.23).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-deep ansätze without new parameters  
  • “Deep Shared-Parameter Qubit Ansatz” (Gen 10) reached depth 47 and 228 gates but only 4 distinct parameters, yielding score 0.36 and gap 0.25—extra layers improved training fit (0.81) but failed to close test gap.  
- Heavy global sharing with insufficient entanglement variety  
  • Both Gen 11 (“Shared-Rotation Entangling Ansatz”, score 0.34, gap 0.23) and Gen 14 (“Shared-Param Layered Ring Ansatz”, 0.35, gap 0.26) used 3–6 parameters and fixed CNOT rings only, leading to near-zero validation margins (≈0.01) and robustness issues.  
- Skip-2 or long-range CNOTs alone do not substitute for parametrized gates  
  • Gen 12’s skip-2 backbone (depth 38, 117 gates) reached 0.38 score but kept gap 0.23—better connectivity did not offset lack of cheap two-qubit parameterization.  
- Pairwise ZZ entanglers without global rotations fail to generalize  
  • The “Pairwise ZZ and Layered Rotation Ansatz” (Gen 13) injected ZZ interactions but achieved only 0.37 and gap 0.12, showing that disjoint CRZ plus global rotations outperform static ZZ pairings.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-wire shared rotation declarations enforce correct parameter broadcasting  
  • Listing RX(a), RY(b), RZ(c), RY(e) on each qubit in ANSATZ_SPEC ensures truly global rotations—critical for uniform feature encoding.  
- Manual CNOT-ring definitions maximize qubit mixing at controlled depth  
  • Hard-coding the two CNOT rings ([0–1–…–7–0] and offset [1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0]) yields full connectivity across 12 gates and depth 42, directly correlating with the best validation accuracy (0.58).  
- Disjoint CRZ placements add low-overhead non-Clifford entanglement  
  • Four CRZ(d) gates on pairs [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7] inject entanglement while keeping both parameter count (one extra) and circuit depth minimal—key to closing the generalization gap.  
- Final shared RY(e) layer refines output manifold  
  • Appending a concluding RY(e) across qubits sharpens class decision boundaries, as seen by superior test accuracy (0.47) relative to earlier variants lacking this layer.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score progression: 0.34 → 0.38 → 0.39 → 0.41  
  • Parity-Shared Ring Entangler (0.34) → Skip-2 Entangler (0.38) → Shared-Param Ring (0.39) → Best program (0.41), each step adding richer entanglement or low-cost parametrization.  
- Dramatic gap reduction with CRZ + offset ring  
  • Generalization gap fell from ~0.20–0.27 in Gen 10–14 to 0.03 in the best program, directly tied to disjoint CRZ(d) plus two CNOT rings.  
- Parameter efficiency remains ~0.07 despite varied gate counts  
  • Best program’s 5 distinct parameters over 144 gates yields efficiency 0.07, matching prior designs but delivering superior accuracy through smarter parameter placement.  
- Validation accuracy peaks at moderate depth  
  • The top script’s 0.58 validation accuracy at depth 42 outperforms both shallower (depth 38, Gen 12, 0.62 train/0.62 val) and deeper (depth 54, Gen 13, 0.54 val) variants, confirming an optimal depth window when combined with global sharing and disjoint CRZ.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Duplicate the entire RX(a)→RY(b)→CNOT-ring→RZ(c)→CRZ(d)→offset-CNOT→RY(e) block to double circuit depth while reusing the same five parameters {a,b,c,d,e}. This tests whether further layering under a fixed parameter budget can boost validation accuracy without inflating the generalization gap, building directly on the best program’s depth-vs-parameter sweet spot.  

2. After the existing CRZ(d) layer, insert a new CRX(f) gate on each disjoint pair [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7] sharing a fresh parameter f. Cheap two-qubit rotation entanglers like CRX have shown high expressivity per parameter in closing the generalization gap, complementing the phase entanglement introduced by CRZ.  

3. Prepend a global ZZ(g) layer before the first RX(a) block, applying ZZ(g) on opposite-qubit pairs [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] with a single shared parameter g. Disjoint, long-range parameterized entanglement can seed global correlations early, leveraging the success of CRZ and offset rings in improving train/validation balance.  

4. Refine the CRZ(d) entangler by splitting it into two shared parameters d1 and d2: use CRZ(d1) on pairs [0,1] & [4,5], and CRZ(d2) on [2,3] & [6,7]. This adds only one extra distinct parameter but allows more nuanced phase entanglement, testing whether finer-grained sharing outperforms a single global CRZ.  

5. Replace the second fixed offset CNOT ring with a skip-2 CNOT pattern: apply CNOTs on [0→2], [2→4], [4→6], [6→0] and [1→3], [3→5], [5→7], [7→1]. Richer non-nearest-neighbor mixing topologies have driven successive score improvements and may further reduce the generalization gap without adding parameters.
