# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Dual CNOT rings + disjoint CRZ block (Gen 20 best and current seed):  
  • Current best program (Combined 0.41, gap 0.03, depth 42) and earlier Gen 20 both achieve top scores by interleaving  
    – Ring 1: full-cycle CNOTs [0–1–…–7–0]  
    – Disjoint CRZ(d) on (0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7)  
    – Ring 2: offset CNOTs on [1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0]  
- Moderate depth (~36–42) with 5 distinct parameters:  
  • Current best uses depth 42, n_distinct_params = 5 → Combined 0.41, gap 0.03  
  • Gen 29 at depth 36 with only 3 distinct params reached 0.40 but had lower validation (0.53→0.58)  
- Final global RY(e) layer sharpens decision boundaries:  
  • Present in current best (validation 0.58) and Gen 20 (validation 0.58) vs. ~0.54–0.56 in similar-depth circuits lacking it  
- Balanced parameter sharing (a,b,c,d,e) across all qubits:  
  • Avoids underfitting of over-shared 3–4-param circuits (Gen 25/26) while controlling total parameter count  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive depth with heavy parameter sharing:  
  • Gen 25 (depth 90, 4 distinct params) → Combined 0.36, gap 0.27  
  • Gen 27 (depth 102, 5 total uses of 5 distinct) → Combined 0.39 but very low train (0.56) and underfit  
- Single-ring or sole global entanglement without dual ring:  
  • Gen 26 (one long-range CRZ block + single CNOT ring) → Combined 0.35, gap 0.31  
- Repeating uniform RY/RZ/CNOT blocks without non-Clifford two-qubit phases:  
  • Gen 27’s layered RY–CNOT–RZ repeat → modest 0.39 score, small gap but low margins (∼0.01)  
- Over-sharing parameters below 4 distinct values:  
  • Gen 29 (3 distinct) hit 0.40 but validation margin collapsed (0.01), suggesting expressivity limits  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-wire shared gates ensure true global rotations:  
  • Current best lists RX(a), RY(b), RZ(c), RY(e) on wires 0–7 rather than loop constructs  
- Disjoint CRZ(d) across qubit pairs injects non-Clifford entanglement at low depth cost:  
  • Four parallel CRZs add expressivity for only one extra parameter and ~1 depth layer  
- Two offset CNOT rings maximize mixing with minimal gate overhead:  
  • 8‐gate full ring + 4‐gate offset ring achieves near‐all‐to‐all connectivity in 12 gates  
- Final RY(e) layer on all qubits focuses the decision boundary after entanglement stages:  
  • Increases validation accuracy by ~2–4% compared to ansätze stopping at RZ or CRZ  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score progression by depth and param count:  
  • Deep, under-parameterized circuits (Gen 25/26, depth >90, 4 distinct) scored 0.35–0.36, large gaps (0.27–0.31)  
  • Ultra-deep repeat designs (Gen 27, depth 102) improved to 0.39 but underfit (train 0.56)  
  • Mid-depth (36–57) designs rose to 0.40 (Gen 28/29) with gaps 0.07–0.09  
  • Optimal at depth ~42 with 5 distinct parameters (current best): 0.41, gap 0.03  
- Distinct-parameter count correlates with expressivity:  
  • Increasing from 3 (Gen 29) → 5 (seed) distinct params boosted validation from 0.53→0.58  
- Generalization gap minimized by combining dual-ring+CRZ pattern at moderate depth:  
  • Gap fell from ~0.15–0.31 in earlier generations to 0.03 in the current best  
- Final RY layer consistently correlates with higher validation accuracy:  
  • Circuits with concluding RY(e) achieved 0.58–0.59 vs. ~0.54–0.56 in those lacking it

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a parallel CRX(f) block immediately after the existing CRZ(d) gates, using the same disjoint pairs [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7] and a new shared parameter f. Evidence: cheap two-qubit rotations like CRX provide high expressivity per depth cost and, when combined with CRZ phases, can further close the generalization gap without appreciable depth increase. This directly builds on the CRZ(d) block’s success by enriching non-Clifford entanglement.  

2. Refine the non-Clifford block by splitting CRZ(d) into two parameters: apply CRZ(d₁) on pairs [0,1] & [4,5] and CRZ(d₂) on [2,3] & [6,7]. Prior global insights indicate that adding one extra phase parameter yields more nuanced control of opposite-qubit correlations and can help the ansatz adapt its entanglement profile to diverse data features. This simple split only adds one extra scalar but can boost expressivity where the current single-d block may be too coarse.  

3. Replace the second 4-gate CNOT ring with a skip-2 “long-hop” entangler: CNOTs on [0→2], [2→4], [4→6], [6→0] and [1→3], [3→5], [5→7], [7→1]. This preserves the same four-gate depth while increasing connectivity diversity, leveraging the depth-sweet-spot (~42) established by the best program’s dual-ring pattern. By mixing local and longer-range mixing steps, you may capture correlations that the standard offset ring misses.  

4. Prepend a global ZZ(g) layer before the RX(a) rotations, acting on opposite-qubit pairs [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] with a new shared parameter g. Seeding the circuit with long-range Ising interactions has been shown to amplify the efficacy of subsequent local rotations and rings, potentially boosting expressivity early and sharpening the decision boundary downstream.
