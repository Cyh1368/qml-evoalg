# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
• Dual CNOT rings plus disjoint CRZ entanglement drove the top score (0.41, gap 0.03) in the current best program, improving over the single-ring Skip-2 Entangler (Gen 12, 0.38, gap 0.23) and the Shared-Parameter Ring Entangler (Gen 16, 0.40, gap 0.18).  
• Injecting four cheap, shared-parameter CRZ(d) gates on non-overlapping qubit pairs added non-Clifford entanglement without raising depth excessively—this pattern first boosted the shared-param ring from 0.39 (Gen 9) to 0.41 when combined with dual rings.  
• Final global RY(e) layer sharpened decision boundaries: the best program’s concluding RY across all qubits lifted validation accuracy to 0.58 from ~0.54 in comparable circuits lacking that layer (e.g., Gen 12).  
• Maintaining a moderate depth (~42) with only five distinct parameters balanced expressivity and generalization, outperforming deeper (depth 54) or shallower (depth 20) ansätze with similar parameter counts (Gen 13, score 0.37; Gen 18, score 0.37).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
• Over-deep circuits without adding distinct parameters underfit test data: Gen 15 (depth 39, 4 parameters) and Gen 19 (depth 54, 4 distinct parameters) both scored 0.37 with gaps ~0.15–0.17, mirroring past failures of Gen 10 (depth 47, gap 0.25).  
• Heavy global sharing of rotations with only fixed CNOT rings led to near-zero validation margins and poor test accuracy (e.g., Gen 17 scored 0.34, gap 0.26; Gen 14 in prior runs scored 0.35, gap 0.26).  
• Relying solely on ZZ or long-range CNOT patterns without parameterized two-qubit gates failed to close generalization gaps: Gen 15’s shared ZZ layer plus CNOT ladders scored 0.37 with low parameter efficiency (0.09), and Gen 19’s disjoint ZZ-RY-RZ design likewise stagnated at 0.37.  

## Implementation Insights  
• Explicit per-qubit specification of shared rotations (e.g., RX(a) on wires 0–7) guarantees true global application and uniform feature encoding, as codified in the best program’s ANSATZ_SPEC.  
• Manually coding two interleaved CNOT rings ([0–1–…–7–0] and offset [1–2,3–4,5–6,7–0]) achieves full qubit mixing at controlled depth (42) and was central to the jump from 0.39 (single ring) to 0.41.  
• Placing a block of four CRZ(d) gates on disjoint pairs injects non-Clifford entanglement with minimal depth and parameter overhead, markedly reducing the generalization gap compared to static ZZ implementations.  
• Appending a final RY(e) rotation layer across all qubits refines the output manifold and was responsible for the superior validation accuracy (0.58 vs. ~0.54) seen only in the best program.  

## Performance Analysis  
• Score progression among recent programs: Gen 17 (0.34) → Gen 15/18/19 (0.37) → Gen 16 (0.40) → best program (0.41), underscoring successive gains from richer entanglement and targeted parameterization.  
• Generalization gap shrank from ~0.15–0.26 in Generations 15–19 to 0.03 in the best program, directly tied to the combination of disjoint CRZ and dual CNOT rings.  
• Depth-to-accuracy correlation peaks around depth 42: shallower designs (depth 20–39) underperform (<0.38), and deeper designs (>45) fail to generalize without new parameters.  
• Parameter efficiency (~0.07 distinct parameters per gate) remains constant across top designs, but strategic placement of those parameters (non-overlapping CRZ, final RY) differentiates the best program’s performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Duplicate the entire RX(a)→RY(b)→ring-1→RZ(c)→CRZ(d)→ring-2→RY(e) block back-to-back, reusing the same five parameters {a,b,c,d,e}. This will test whether deeper stacking under a fixed parameter budget (depth ~84) continues to improve validation accuracy without inflating the generalization gap, building directly on the best program’s depth-vs-parameter sweet spot.  

2. Immediately after the four disjoint CRZ(d) gates, insert a parallel block of four CRX(f) gates on the same qubit pairs [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7] sharing a new parameter f. Cheap two-qubit rotations like CRX have shown high expressivity per parameter and should complement the phase entanglement of CRZ, potentially shrinking the gap further.  

3. Refine the CRZ(d) layer by splitting it into two shared parameters, d1 and d2: apply CRZ(d1) on pairs [0,1] & [4,5] and CRZ(d2) on [2,3] & [6,7]. This adds only one extra parameter but gives more nuanced phase control, letting the ansatz adapt non-Clifford entanglement more flexibly across the circuit.  

4. Replace the second fixed CNOT ring with a skip-2 CNOT pattern—apply CNOTs on [0→2], [2→4], [4→6], [6→0] and [1→3], [3→5], [5→7], [7→1]. This richer non-nearest-neighbor mixing topology may further reduce the generalization gap without adding parameters, leveraging the same depth (~42) that optimized dual-ring mixing.  

5. Prepend a single shared CRZ(g) (or ZZ(g)) layer on opposite-qubit pairs [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] before the RX(a) block. Seeding long-range correlations early can amplify the effectiveness of subsequent rings and cheap entanglers, following the pattern where disjoint nonlocal gates drove the best generalization improvements.
