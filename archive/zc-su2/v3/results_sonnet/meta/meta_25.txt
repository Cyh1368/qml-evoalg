# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
• Dual CNOT rings interleaved with a disjoint-CRZ block drove the top performance.  
  – Best program (Combined 0.41, gap 0.03) uses an 8-gate CNOT ring, four disjoint CRZ(d) gates, then a second 4-gate CNOT ring.  
  – This pattern outperforms single-ring designs such as Gen 24 (Shared-Parameter Skip-2 Entangler, Combined 0.39, gap 0.13).  
• Cheap non-Clifford entanglement via disjoint CRZ on pairs [0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7] boosted expressivity with minimal depth overhead.  
  – Injecting four CRZ(d) gates reduced generalization gap from ~0.15 in Gen 21 (Combined 0.38, gap 0.15) to 0.03 in the best program.  
• Final global RY(e) layer sharpens decision boundaries.  
  – Best program’s concluding RY(e) on all qubits raised validation accuracy to 0.58, compared to ~0.54 in Gen 22 (Shared-Parameter Global Entanglement, Combined 0.37).  
• Moderate circuit depth (~42) with just five distinct parameters balanced expressivity and generalization.  
  – Echoes prior finding that depth ~42 is optimal—shallower (<38) underfit, deeper (>54) stagnated unless new parameters were added.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
• Over-shared single-ring ansätze limited expressivity and underfit.  
  – Gen 21 (Shared-Parameter 8-Qubit CRZ Ansatz) used one CNOT ring + heavy sharing (4 distinct params), scored 0.38 with gap 0.15.  
• Deep but under-parameterized circuits failed to improve.  
  – Gen 23 (Multi-Stage Shared-Parameter, depth 66, 4 distinct params) scored 0.37, validation 0.50, showing that extra depth alone doesn’t close gaps.  
• Sole reliance on fixed CNOT structures without interleaved second ring or skip connections under-mixed qubits.  
  – Gen 20 (RY-RZ-CRZ Ring, depth 36) achieved 0.41 but plateaued at ~56% accuracy; lacked the second entangling ring that drove down the gap in the best program.  
• Pure global ZZ or RZ layers without targeted two-qubit parametrization led to near-random validation margins.  
  – Gen 22’s shared antipodal ZZ + CNOT chain (Combined 0.37, gap 0.21) underperformed despite long-range connectivity.  

## Implementation Insights  
• Per-qubit shared rotations guarantee true global gates.  
  – Best program explicitly lists RX(a), RY(b), RZ(c) and RY(e) for wires 0–7, ensuring uniform feature encoding and avoiding implicit loops.  
• Interleaved CNOT rings achieve full mixing at controlled depth.  
  – First ring connects [0–1–…–7–0], second offset ring connects [1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–0], delivering high connectivity in 12 gates.  
• Disjoint CRZ block injects non-Clifford phase efficiently.  
  – Four parallel CRZ(d) gates add expressivity with only a 1-parameter overhead and minimal depth.  
• Strategic placement of RZ(c) between entangling layers amplifies state flexibility.  
  – RZ(c) after the first CNOT ring allows the circuit to steer amplitudes before and after two-qubit coupling, outperforming circuits that group all rotations at one end.  

## Performance Analysis  
• Combined score climbed from 0.34 (e.g. Gen 17) → 0.37 (Gen 15/18/19) → 0.39 (Gen 24) → 0.41 (Gen 20 and current best), tracing gains from richer entanglement and block-parameterization.  
• Generalization gap shrank from ~0.15–0.26 in mid-generations to 0.03 in the best program, directly tied to the dual-ring + disjoint CRZ pattern.  
• Validation accuracy peaked when a concluding RY layer was present: 0.58 in the best program vs. ~0.54–0.56 in similar-depth circuits lacking that layer.  
• Depth-accuracy relationship confirms an optimum around 42: both shallower (<38) and deeper (>54) ansätze underperform without new parameters or entanglement structures.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a parallel CRX(f) block immediately after the four CRZ(d) gates, applying CRX(f) on the same disjoint pairs [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7] with a new shared parameter f. Cheap two-qubit rotations like CRX have demonstrated high expressivity per parameter and, when combined with the existing CRZ phase entanglement, may further close the generalization gap without appreciable depth overhead.  

2. Refine the non-Clifford block by splitting CRZ(d) into two parameters: use CRZ(d1) on pairs [0,1] & [4,5] and CRZ(d2) on [2,3] & [6,7]. Prior experiments showed that adding just one extra phase parameter yields more nuanced control of opposite-qubit correlations, which can help the ansatz adapt its entanglement profile to diverse data patterns.  

3. Replace the second fixed CNOT ring with a skip-2 “long-hop” entangler: apply CNOTs on [0→2], [2→4], [4→6], [6→0] and on [1→3], [3→5], [5→7], [7→1]. This non-nearest-neighbor topology preserves the 4-gate depth but increases connectivity diversity, leveraging the same depth sweet-spot (~42) that underpinned the best program’s low gap.  

4. Prepend a global shared ZZ(g) layer before RX(a), acting on opposite-qubit pairs [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] with a new parameter g. Seeding the circuit with long-range Ising interactions has been shown to amplify the efficacy of subsequent local rotations and rings, potentially boosting expressivity early and sharpening decision boundaries downstream.  

5. Stack a second RX–RY–ring1–RZ–CRZ–ring2–RY block directly after the existing one, but equip it with its own parameter set {a₂,b₂,c₂,d₂,e₂} instead of sharing. This tests whether adding fresh parameters in a deeper (~84-gate) circuit can push beyond the current depth/parameter trade-off, exploring if a two-layer residual-style ansatz can capture more complex decision surfaces without overfitting.
