# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parity-split single-qubit rotations with shared parameters  
  • Seed (combined 0.42) applies RY(a) on even qubits and RY(b) on odds, then similarly for RZ(a)/RZ(b), yielding high validation (0.75) and modest generalization gap (0.19).  
- Bidirectional CNOT cascade to concentrate entanglement  
  • Seed’s forward (0→7) and backward (7→0) ladders prepare qubits for centralized two-qubit gates, supporting strong class separation (validation_margin 0.20).  
- Disjoint-pair CRZ entanglers sharing a single “ent” parameter  
  • Four CRZ gates on neighbors (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7) in the Seed deliver non-Clifford expressivity with low parameter count (param_eff 0.09) and boost val 0.75/test 0.68.  
- Concluding global RY readout layer  
  • Seed’s final RY(c) on all eight qubits sharpens decision margins; absent in lower-scoring variants (e.g., Gen 52 combined 0.31, gap 0.41).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-aggressive parameter sharing with deeper circuits  
  • Gen 52 “Shared-Parameter Ring” (combined 0.31, gap 0.41) and Gen 54 “Global Shared-Parameter” (0.34, gap 0.26) tied almost all gates to 5–6 distinct parameters, undercutting expressivity despite depth ≥45.  
- Excessive depth without final uniform rotation  
  • Gen 51 “Parity-Split Dual-Generator” (depth 57, combined 0.39) and Gen 50 “Layered Ansatz” (depth 54, combined 0.38) both overfit (gaps ≥0.27) even with parity splitting, lacking a concluding global RY.  
- Heavy ZZ-chain or entangler chaining up front  
  • Gen 53 “8-Qubit Tunable ZZ” (combined 0.41) achieved perfect train (1.00) but overfit (gap 0.31); front-loaded ZZ layers without balanced readout impede generalization.

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit per-wire parameter specification  
  • Seed spells out each RY/RZ on wires [0,2,4,6] vs. [1,3,5,7] rather than looping, ensuring correct parity grouping.  
- Two-phase CNOT ladder implementation  
  • Writing forward then backward CNOT sequences in ANSATZ_SPEC concentrates entangling power and sets up CRZ gates effectively.  
- Minimal distinct parameters for two-qubit gates  
  • Single shared “ent” for all disjoint CRZs maximizes expressive non-Clifford rotations per parameter.  
- Final layer of uniform single-qubit rotations  
  • A concluding RY(c) on each qubit directly correlates with improved margins and reduced overfitting compared to variants lacking it.

## Performance Analysis  
- Validation accuracy is the key driver of combined score  
  • Only the Seed (val 0.75) exceeds the 0.70 threshold; all other designs plateau at 0.48–0.78.  
- Optimal depth/parameter balance around 54/6 distinct parameters  
  • Seed (depth 54, n_distinct 6) attains the best trade-off (combined 0.42, gap 0.19). Deviations to depth 57 (overfit) or depth 39 (underfit) degrade performance.  
- Parameter efficiency alone does not guarantee success  
  • Gen 48 matched the Seed’s eff 0.09 but underfits (val 0.51); balanced architecture matters more than efficiency metric.  
- Generalization gap correlates inversely with margin gains  
  • Seed’s worst_group_margin 0.13 and validation_margin 0.20 accompany its lowest gap (0.19), whereas heavy-sharing circuits show margin→0 and gap ≥0.26.
