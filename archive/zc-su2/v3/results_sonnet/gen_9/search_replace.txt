<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses 11 distinct parameters (one per qubit for the initial RY layer) leading to strong overfitting (train 0.94 vs val 0.72, gap 0.24) and a low combined score despite decent margins. Prior experiments show that fewer distinct parameters correlate strongly with higher combined score (the 5-parameter seed scored 0.41 vs the 4-parameter seed's 0.39 and the 11-parameter current version's 0.37), confirming that parameter economy dominates the score and that per-qubit individualized parameters mainly drive overfitting rather than generalizable structure.

I replace the per-qubit RY layer and the two separate RZ layers (rz_pre/rz_post) with a compact, heavily shared-parameter design: a global RX layer, a global RY layer, a full entangling ring (structural CNOTs, free), a shared-angle CRZ layer along the same ring, a fixed long-range entangling layer (opposite-qubit CNOTs, free, to boost connectivity/expressivity without adding parameters), a shared RZ layer, and a final shared RY layer. This yields only 5 distinct trainable parameters (down from 11) while increasing entangling connectivity (full ring + long-range links) to help the model separate harder validation groups without needing many trainable rotations, aiming to shrink the train/validation gap and increase worst-group margin as seen in the better-performing 5-parameter seed.
</DESCRIPTION>
