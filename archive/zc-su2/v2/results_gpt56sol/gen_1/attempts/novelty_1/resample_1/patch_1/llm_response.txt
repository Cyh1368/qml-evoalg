<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses 24 distinct trainable angles, including 16 independently parameterized RZ gates surrounding an open CZ chain. Because RZ and CZ are diagonal in the same basis, much of that structure is redundant, while the large parameter count encourages overfitting and reduces parameter efficiency.

The new ansatz uses only three distinct parameters: separate RY angles for even and odd wires and one globally shared RX mixing angle. A closed, alternating CZ ring gives every qubit equal connectivity and avoids edge-specific behavior that can weaken the worst-group margin. The RX layer does not commute with the CZ entanglers, so it provides useful basis mixing without per-wire parameters. Compared with the original block, this configuration reduces the distinct parameter count from 24 to 3, lowers gate count and expected depth, and imposes a strong translational/parity-based inductive bias intended to improve few-sample generalization, convergence, and robust separation. Learning rates and iteration counts remain controlled by the fixed external training loop.
</DESCRIPTION>
