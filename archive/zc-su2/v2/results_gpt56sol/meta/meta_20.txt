# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Global single-parameter RY mixing + shared RX “tilt” output mixer  
  • Generation 20 (global mix/global tilt) achieves the top combined score 0.73, parameter efficiency 0.25, and converges in ~60 steps.  
  • Generation 21 with parity-tied RY and the same global RX mixer scores 0.72, perfect generalization, and worst-group margin 0.38.  
- Three-layer multi-scale CZ “butterfly” entanglement (distances 1, 2, 4)  
  • Present in Gen 20/21/18; supports local and long-range correlations, driving validation_accuracy ≥0.98.  
  • Outperforms linear CZ chains (Gens 11–13 combined ≈0.60–0.61) with substantially fewer gates and depth.  
- Extreme parameter tying (only 2 distinct parameters)  
  • Gen 20 uses “global_mix” and “global_tilt,” yielding n_params=4 but n_distinct=2; delivers high accuracy with minimal overfitting.  
  • Parity-Butterfly (Gen 18) ties 3 distinct parameters and still achieves combined 0.71, showing diminishing returns beyond 2–3 shared parameters.  
- Shallow yet expressive depth (~15)  
  • Gen 20’s depth 15 beats deeper designs (Gen 18 depth 30 yields 0.71; Gen 16 depth 15 with more params yields only 0.68)  
  • Confirms that a lean, focused ansatz balances trainability and expressivity.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Adding trainable mid-link two-qubit rotations (CRZ/CRY)  
  • Gen 16 (“butterfly_trainable_midlinks”): 7 parameters, depth 15, combined 0.68; more parameters but lower score and slower convergence (~90 steps).  
- Repeating entire entangling/mixing blocks for depth  
  • Gen 18 (“tied_double_butterfly”): depth 30, gate 168, combined 0.71; deeper circuit but marginal score gain over shallower Gen 20.  
- CZ-only entanglement with no output mixer or inconsistent parameter naming  
  • Gen 19 (“regularized_butterfly_bridge”): rigid CZ-only pattern, heavy sharing, naming conflicts; failed validation (score 0.0).  
- Over-tying or omitting non-commuting mixers  
  • Designs lacking a final RX “tilt” (e.g., shared-RX only) plateau at combined ≈0.60 (Gens 12–13).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Consistent schema use (“wire” for single-qubit, “wires” for two-qubit) avoids backend errors  
  • All top performers (Gens 20/21/18) strictly adhere to schema; Gen 19’s schema slip-ups led to outright validation failures.  
- Explicit list-of-dicts ansatz block simplifies adding multi-scale CZ layers and shared mixers  
  • Facilitates rapid iteration on entanglement patterns without nested loops or merge artifacts.  
- Minimal distinct parameter naming for maximal regularization  
  • The shift from two distinct RY params (“mix_even”/“mix_odd”) plus RX to just “global_mix” yielded +0.03 combined score and faster convergence.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Best combined score rises as distinct parameters drop to 2  
  • Gen 20 (n_distinct=2) → 0.73; Gen 21 (n_distinct=3) → 0.72; Gen 16 (n_distinct=5) → 0.68.  
- Depth ≈15 is optimal for butterfly topologies  
  • Depth 15 (Gen 20) outperforms depth 18 (Gen 21) and depth 30 (Gen 18) in combined score per gate.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with convergence speed  
  • Higher efficiency (0.25 in Gen 20) converges in 60 steps vs. 90 steps for Gen 16 (eff 0.14).  
- Worst-group margins retained despite aggressive tying  
  • Gen 20 margin 0.35 matches the best margins of earlier designs (e.g., Gen 12) while lifting combined score to 0.73.
