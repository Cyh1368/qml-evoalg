# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Global single-parameter RY mixer + shared RX “tilt”  
  • Generation 30 (global_butterfly_mixer) & Generation 23 both collapse all RY rotations to “global_mix” and all RX to “global_tilt,” yielding the top combined score of 0.73, perfect train (1.00) and 0.98 val/test accuracy, 0.02 gen-gap, and parameter efficiency 0.25.  
- Three-layer multiscale CZ “butterfly” entanglement (distances 1, 2, 4)  
  • Present in Gen 23, Gen 26 (0.71), Gen 28 (0.73) and Gen 30, this pattern consistently achieves ≥0.98 validation/test accuracy by capturing both local and long-range correlations with exactly 12 CZ gates.  
- Extreme parameter tying to 2 distinct angles  
  • Reducing n_distinct_params from 3 in Gen 26 (0.71 combined) and 5 in Gen 27 (0.70) to 2 in Gen 30/23 drives the best regularization and highest efficiency, without sacrificing worst-group margin (~0.35).  
- Shallow depth (~15) & moderate gate count (84)  
  • Gen 30 converges in ~60 steps—matching deeper repeats (Gen 28, depth 30) but halving gate count—demonstrating a sweet spot for expressivity vs. trainability.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Inserting trainable two-qubit rotations mid-link  
  • Gen 16 (“butterfly_trainable_midlinks”) added CRX/CRY gates (n_params=7) but scored only 0.68 and required ~90 steps, showing extra 2Q parameters hinder efficiency without accuracy gains.  
- Over-tying mirror structures without noncommuting mixers  
  • Gen 24 & Gen 25 failed validation entirely (score 0.0), as strict mirror pairing and missing a second mixer rendered the ansatz under-expressive or syntactically invalid.  
- Shared parameters with insufficient expressivity  
  • Gen 29 (Symmetric Butterfly Ansatz Classifier) used 3 distinct parameters but only achieved a 0.50 combined score and a 0.16 generalization gap, indicating subgroup vulnerabilities from over-constrained parameterization.  
- Depth doubling without param reduction  
  • Gen 28 doubled the butterfly block (depth 30, 168 gates) and matched 0.73 score but offered no accuracy or convergence speed advantage over the shallower Gen 30, while incurring higher hardware overhead.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Consistent “wire” vs “wires” schema  
  • All top performers (Gen 23, Gen 30) use `"wire"` for single-qubit gates and `"wires"` for two-qubit gates; failures in Gen 24/25 often coincided with schema slips.  
- Flat list-of-dicts ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • Explicitly enumerating eight identical RY entries, three CZ butterfly layers, and eight identical RX entries (as in Gen 30) simplifies both human review and automatic validation.  
- Manual specification of butterfly distances  
  • Hard-coding CZ pairs at distances 1, 2, 4 yields exactly 12 entanglers and depth 15, which matches the optimal expressivity–trainability trade-off observed in Gen 30.  
- Minimal, descriptive parameter naming  
  • Collapsing all mixing rotations into `"global_mix"` and all tilts into `"global_tilt"` enforces a strong symmetry prior; this naming convention directly correlates with the highest parameter efficiency (0.25) in the current best program.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Distinct-parameter count vs combined score  
  • 2 distinct → 0.73 combined (Gen 23, Gen 30)  
  • 3 distinct → 0.71 (Gen 26)  
  • 5 distinct → 0.70 (Gen 27) / 0.50 (Gen 29)  
- Circuit depth & gate count vs convergence  
  • Depth 15, 84 gates → 0.73 & 60 steps (Gen 30)  
  • Depth 30, 168 gates → 0.73 & 60 steps (Gen 28)  
  → Shallow designs achieve identical accuracy and speed with half the hardware.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with convergence speed  
  • Efficiency 0.25 → 60 steps (Gen 30)  
  • 0.20 → 120 steps (Gen 27)  
  • 0.14–0.17 → ~90 steps (Gen 16, Gen 29)  
- Robustness under extreme tying  
  • Worst-group margin remains ~0.35 in both Gen 23 and Gen 30 despite reducing distinct parameters to 2, indicating no expressivity loss in subgroup performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a global RZ “phase-alignment” layer between the RY mixer and the CZ blocks, with a single new shared parameter `"global_phase"`.  
   Implementation: after the eighth RY entry, append for i in 0…7  
     `{"gate":"RZ","wire":i,"param":"global_phase"}`  
   Rationale: uniform phase shifts immediately before entanglement can align relative phases and have been shown to improve worst‐group margins in symmetry-tied ansätze, at the cost of only one extra distinct parameter.

2. Prepend a global RX “seed” layer on all qubits using the existing `"global_tilt"` parameter.  
   Implementation: before the first RY, insert eight entries  
     `{"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"global_tilt"}` for i in 0…7.  
   Rationale: breaking initial symmetry with an orthogonal mixer accelerates convergence (as seen in prior RX-prep experiments) without increasing the distinct-parameter count.

3. Replace the distance-2 CZ entanglers with controlled-phase rotations (CRZ) tied to the new `"global_phase"` parameter.  
   Implementation: for each distance-2 pair [a,b], swap  
     `{"gate":"CZ","wires":[a,b]}`  
   with  
     `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[a,b],"param":"global_phase"}`.  
   Rationale: augmenting medium-range coupling via phase rotations can boost expressivity while preserving n_distinct ≤ 3 and capitalizing on the same `"global_phase"` added in recommendation 1.

4. Swap two of the distance-4 CZ gates for parameterized XX interactions with a shared `"global_xx"` angle.  
   Implementation: replace CZ(0,4) and CZ(1,5) with  
     `{"gate":"XX","wires":[0,4],"param":"global_xx"}` and  
     `{"gate":"XX","wires":[1,5],"param":"global_xx"}`.  
   Rationale: introducing alternate Ising couplings on the longest links may capture complementary long-range correlations; one extra parameter keeps parameter efficiency high.

5. Experiment with a reversed-order butterfly entanglement block—distance-4, then 2, then 1—while keeping the same 12 CZ gates and global mixers.  
   Implementation: reorder the CZ entries to list the four distance-4 pairs first, then distance-2, then distance-1, before the RX tilt.  
   Rationale: front-loading long-range entanglers could better seed nonlocal correlations early in the circuit without changing depth, gate count, or parameter tying.
