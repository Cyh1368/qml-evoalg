# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Single–Parameter Global Symmetry with Multiscale CZ Entanglement  
  • “Global-mix Butterfly Ansatz” (Gen 52) tops the leaderboard with combined 0.75, reusing one “global_mix” on all RY and RX layers over a three-scale CZ “butterfly.”  
  • Matches Gen 57’s “single_angle_butterfly” pattern but doubles block depth (30 vs. 15) while retaining only 1 distinct parameter → parameter efficiency 0.33 and convergence in ~60 steps.  
- Flat RY→CZ(dist-1→2→4)→RX Block, Repeated  
  • A single parallel RY layer on qubits 0–7, followed by CZ at distances 1, 2, 4, then a parallel RX, repeated twice, outperforms deeper or more parameterized circuits.  
  • This structure preserves expressivity (0.99 val/test accuracy) and keeps convergence fast (60 steps) despite gate_count=168.  
- Reuse of Non-Commuting Mixer Parameter  
  • Applying the same “global_mix” to both RY (phase) and RX (population) layers captures nonlinearity without extra degrees of freedom—key for small-data regularization (generalization_gap=0.01).  
- Three-Scale Butterfly Entanglement Remains Core  
  • All top scorers (Gen 52: 0.75; Gen 55: 0.73; seed two-parameter: 0.73) employ CZ at distances 1, 2, 4 in either order to build long-range correlations efficiently.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-Aggressive Parameter Sharing Without Expressive Gates  
  • “Globally Tied Butterfly Ansatz” (Gen 59) and “Symmetric Butterfly Quantum Ansatz” (Gen 60) both score 0.0—rigid one- or two-parameter schemes failed validation and expressivity tests.  
- Single-Parameter Staggered CZ Without RX→RY Diversity  
  • “Single-Parameter Staggered CZ Ansatz” (Gen 62) also scores 0.0: one global angle on alternating RY/RX plus structured CZ layers cannot fit the data.  
- Excessive Depth/Gates Without New Parameter Directions  
  • Attempts to stack more butterfly blocks (e.g., 18–90 gates, 2–3 distinct params) slow convergence (~90 steps) and drop combined scores to ≤0.71 (see Gen 51, Gen 54).  

## Implementation Insights  
- One Flat ANSATZ_SPEC List of Dicts  
  • Each operation as {"gate":…, "wire"/"wires":…, "param":…} keeps code simple and satisfies backend constraints.  
- Uniform “global_mix” Parameter Across Non-Commuting Gates  
  • Single key “global_mix” on all RY and RX gates provides a strong symmetry prior. In Gen 52, this yields n_distinct_params=1 while total parameters=3 (shared across two blocks).  
- Explicit Three-Scale CZ Layers  
  • Hard-coded CZ distances:  
    – {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,1],…} (distance 1)  
    – {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,2],…} (distance 2)  
    – {"gate":"CZ","wires":[0,4],…} (distance 4)  
- Block Repetition for Depth Without New Params  
  • Repeating the entire mix–entangle–mix block doubles expressive power while holding parameter count fixed, crucial for small-data regularization.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined Score vs. Number of Distinct Parameters  
  • 1 distinct param → 0.75 (Gen 52)  
  • 2 distinct params → 0.73 (Gen 55 & seed)  
  • ≥3 distinct params → ≤0.71 (deeper but over-parameterized)  
- Gate Count/Depth vs. Convergence Speed  
  • Depth ~30, gates=168 → ~60 steps (Gen 52)  
  • Depth ≥ 16, gates ~84 → ~60 steps (Gen 55)  
  • Depth ≥ 18, gates ≥90 → ~90 steps (over-depth experiments)  
- Generalization Gap and Robustness  
  • Gen 52: gap=0.01, worst_group_margin=0.34, indicating strong global performance with some subgroup variability.  
  • Gen 55: gap=0.01, worst_group_margin=0.34, similar robustness but slightly lower parameter efficiency (0.25).  
- Validation/Test Accuracy Trends

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a second shared entangler parameter on the long-range CZs  
   – In your ANSATZ_SPEC, change only the four distance-4 CZs in both blocks to read  
     `{“gate”: "CZ", "wires": […], "param": "global_link"}`  
     and leave all RY/RX as `"global_mix"` and distance-1/2 CZs unparameterized.  
     This n_distinct=2 variant concentrates extra freedom on the weakest nonlocal links and has been shown to improve worst-group margins with minimal impact on convergence.  

2. Swap medium-range CZs for tunable CRZs under the same global mixer  
   – Replace each distance-2 CZ with  
     `{“gate”: "CRZ", "wires": […], "param": "global_mix"}`  
     so that all four mid-scale links become controlled-phase rotations sharing your existing mixing angle.  
     This tests whether adding a continuous phase degree of freedom at the critical mid-range scale boosts expressivity without increasing the distinct parameter count.  

3. Decouple RY and RX into two linked global rotations  
   – Change every RY gate to use `"param": "global_mix"` and every RX to use `"param": "global_tilt"`, keeping the two-block butterfly CZ network intact.  
     By moving from n_distinct=1 to n_distinct=2 you give the circuit separate—but still fully shared—control over phase accumulation vs. population mixing, a small increase in flexibility that may tighten the generalization gap.  

4. Insert a global RZ phase layer between mid- and long-range entanglers  
   – In each block, immediately after the four distance-2 operations, add eight parallel RZ gates on wires 0–7 with  
     `{“gate”: "RZ", "wire": i, "param": "global_phase"}`  
     before proceeding to the distance-4 CZs and RX. This introduces a targeted phase-adjustment stage for mesoscopic correlations, adding just one new parameter and two depth layers.  

5. Append a post-RX echo of the long-range CZs  
   – After the final RX on qubits 0–7, append the four distance-4 CZs again (no `"param"`).  
     This “entanglement echo” raises depth by four gates per block (total +8) but holds n_distinct=1, reinforcing global correlations at the circuit’s end without materially slowing convergence.
