# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Even/odd–tied local mixers plus a single global mixer  
  • Current best program (“Tied-Mixer CZ Butterfly Ansatz”) uses two RY parameters (“mix_even”/“mix_odd”) across sites and one shared RX “global_tilt.”  
  • Achieves combined score 0.70, parameter_efficiency 0.20, depth 15, gate_count 84, convergence in 60 steps.  
- Multi-scale CZ “butterfly” entanglement  
  • Three CZ layers at distances 1, 2, and 4 (12 gates total) deliver both local and long-range correlations.  
  • Yields validation_accuracy 0.98 and validation_margin 0.73—outperforming linear CZ chains in Gens 11–13 (combined ≈0.60–0.61).  
- Shallow but expressive depth  
  • Depth 15 (best) beats deeper per-qubit designs: Gen 12 (depth 20, score 0.61), Gen 11 (depth 35, score 0.60).  
  • Strikes a balance between expressivity and trainability, minimizing overparameterization.  
- Post-entanglement noncommuting mixer  
  • A final RX “global_tilt” across all qubits converts phase entanglement into measurable populations.  
  • Correlates with highest worst_group_margin 0.35 among non-zero programs.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Full per-qubit RY–RZ–CZ stacks with repeated layers  
  • Gen 11’s double-layer RY-RZ-CZ (24 params, depth 35) hits train/val/test ≈1.00 but combined score 0.60 and parameter_efficiency 0.04.  
  • High gate count/depth yields diminished returns and lower robustness (worst-group margin 0.33).  
- Shared-RX mixing without multi-scale entanglement  
  • Gen 13’s shared RX + linear CZ chain (25 params, depth 23) also scores 0.60 with efficiency 0.04.  
  • Lacks the expressivity of butterfly topology, manifests same worst-group margin 0.33.  
- Over-tying or omission of parameterized two-qubit gates  
  • Gen 15/17: symmetric CZ backbone or butterfly with merge-conflict markers → combined 0.0, validation failures.  
  • Either too few parameters (no CRX/CRY/CRZ) or syntax/schema errors completely block optimization.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Rigid adherence to gate-spec schema  
  • All high-scoring circuits use `"wire"` for single-qubit and `"wires"` for two-qubit gates, and only allowed gate names.  
  • Invalid programs fail due to inconsistent `"wire"`/`"wires"` usage or leftover merge markers (Gen 17).  
- Parametric tying strategy  
  • Two distinct RY mixer names plus one RX param yields only 3 distinct parameters yet full 8-qubit coverage.  
  • This tying yields parameter_efficiency 0.20 and maintains near-perfect accuracy.  
- List-of-dicts ansatz specification  
  • Explicit gate lists (rather than nested loops) simplify readability and minimize schema mis-annotations.  
  • Facilitates easy insertion of multi-scale CZ layers and shared mixers.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Best vs. next best  
  • Current best: combined 0.70 (depth 15, eff 0.20). Parity-tied butterfly (Gen 10) had 0.66 (depth 28, eff 0.14).  
- Depth/performance trade-off  
  • Depth 15 (best) > depth 20–35 designs in score, showing that leaner multi-scale circuits outperform deeper stacks.  
- Parameter efficiency vs. convergence  
  • High-efficiency (0.20) circuit converges in 60 steps; low-efficiency designs (0.04) may converge faster (Gen 12 in 30 steps) but plateau at lower combined scores (~0.60).  
- Robustness margins  
  • Best worst_group_margin 0.35 ties Gen 12 but with higher combined score (0.61 → 0.70), indicating the butterfly+tied mixer pattern also supports subgroup robustness.
