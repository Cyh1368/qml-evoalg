# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Layered single-qubit rotations before and after entanglement (RY → RZ_pre → CZ chain → RZ_post)  
  • Seen in the 8-Qubit RY-RZ Ansatz with CZ Chain (Generation 0, score 0.60), yielding 100% train/test accuracy and 99% validation accuracy.  
- Fully parameterized per-qubit RY and RZ gates  
  • Using distinct parameters ry_0…ry_7 and rz_pre_0…rz_pre_7/rz_post_0…rz_post_7 maximized expressivity, driving rapid convergence (≈60 steps).  
- Linear (“chain”) CZ entangling topology  
  • A single nearest-neighbor CZ chain sufficed for full model capacity—depth 20, 93 gates—while avoiding over-complexity.  
- Strict adherence to allowed gate sets and spec conventions  
  • Consistent use of `"wire"` for single-qubit and `"wires"` for two-qubit gates prevented validation errors seen in other patches.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Even-odd parameter sharing (Generation 2, 5, 6 “parity_shared_ring” and “bipartite_parameter_sharing”)  
  • Drastically reduced parameter count to 2–4 shared values, scored 0.0, and failed validation due to insufficient expressivity and naming conflicts (`"wire"` vs `"wires"`).  
- Overly static, low-parameter ansatz (Generation 4 “shared_mobius_ising”)  
  • Only four parameters (field, local_corr, global_corr, mixer) led to no training signal and 0.0 score.  
- Shallow single-layer designs without parameterized two-qubit gates  
  • Uniform layering (one RY/RZ layer + fixed CZ ring) lacked depth; achieved 0.0 and did not converge.  
- Inconsistent spec keys and missing param-two-qubit gates  
  • Mixing `"wire"`/`"wires"` and omitting CRX/CRY/CRZ prevented correct parsing or execution.

## Implementation Insights
- Unique per-qubit parameters boost expressivity at the cost of efficiency  
  • The best ansatz uses 24 distinct parameters (n_params = 26), achieving top accuracy despite a low parameter-efficiency of 0.04.  
- Two-stage single-qubit rotation framing around entanglement  
  • RZ_pre and RZ_post phases allow local phase adjustments that improve optimization landscapes, as evidenced by rapid convergence (~60 steps).  
- Minimal, structured entanglement suffices  
  • A linear CZ chain captures essential correlations without extra gates, balancing depth (20) and gate count (93).  
- Meticulous spec formatting  
  • Conformance to the backend’s gate naming and JSON schema (e.g., `"wire"` vs `"wires"`) is critical to pass validation.

## Performance Analysis
- Only the fully parameterized RY–RZ with CZ chain achieved nonzero performance  
  • Generation 0 attained combined score 0.60; all other variants (Generations 2–6) scored 0.0.  
- Parameter sharing and static ansätze uniformly failed  
  • Despite reducing parameter count (down to 2–4), these approaches lost trainability entirely.  
- Depth and gate count correlate with expressivity  
  • The successful depth 20/93-gate design outperformed all shallower (<10-gate/layer) attempts.  
- Zero generalization gap and high margins  
  • Generation 0’s worst-group margin (0.35) and validation margin (0.73) indicate robustness benefits from per-qubit parameterization and two-stage rotations.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replicate the full RY–RZ_pre–CZ_chain–RZ_post block in multiple layers. For example, stack two or three identical blocks back-to-back to increase circuit depth (e.g. depth 40–60) while preserving per-qubit parameterization. The extra layers build on the proven two-stage rotation framing and minimal linear entanglement to further boost expressivity without introducing ad hoc gates.

2. Introduce parameterized two-qubit rotations by replacing every other CZ in the chain with a CRZ or CRY gate. Retain the linear nearest-neighbor topology but swap, say, CZ(1,2) and CZ(4,5) for CRZ(1,2,param) and CRY(4,5,param), giving two new entanglement parameters. This leverages the allowed ALLOWED_PARAM_TWO_QUBIT_GATES set to enrich the entangling landscape while conforming to spec.

3. Augment the linear CZ chain with a small number of long-range links to capture non-local correlations. After the nearest-neighbor CZs, add one or two extra CZ gates connecting qubit pairs like (0,7) or (2,5). Empirical patterns show that minimal, structured entanglement suffices—so a couple of extra CZs can increase capacity without exploding gate count.

4. Add an interleaved RX rotation layer before the existing RZ_pre stage. Prepend each RY_i with an RX_i rotation (new parameters rx_0…rx_7) to form an RX–RY–RZ_pre sequence per qubit. Mixing two orthogonal rotation axes has been shown in other variational contexts to improve trainability and landscape conditioning, and it remains within the allowed single-qubit gate set.

5. Implement symmetric parameter sharing for the RZ layers while keeping distinct RY parameters. Group qubits in mirror pairs—(0,7),(1,6),(2,5),(3,4)—and share their rz_pre and rz_post parameters, reducing those from 16 to 8 unique values. This partial sharing tests a middle ground between full per-qubit expressivity and parameter efficiency, grounded in the observation that too aggressive sharing destroyed performance but some structured reduction may preserve accuracy.
