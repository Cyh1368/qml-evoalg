param_efficient_entangling_ring

<DESCRIPTION>
Redesign the ansatz to drastically cut the number of distinct trainable parameters (from 24 down to 10) while still providing strong, parametrized entangling power that should help separate the hardest validation groups.

Rationale:
- The scoring rule states that reducing distinct-parameter count *always* helps, with no diminishing returns, so aggressive parameter sharing is directly rewarded.
- The previous design used per-qubit RZ pre/post rotations (16 params) around a *fixed*, non-parametrized CZ entangling ladder — the entangling structure itself carried no trainable freedom, so those 16 RZ parameters were essentially just phase adjustments with high redundancy.
- Replacing the fixed CZ chain with a parametrized CRZ ring (controlled-RZ around the full 8-qubit ring, single shared parameter) gives the entangling operation itself trainable strength, which is more useful for correlating qubits (and thus for separating hard/robust groups) than many redundant single-qubit phase knobs.
- Keeping the initial per-qubit RY layer (8 distinct parameters) preserves enough single-qubit expressivity to adapt to each wire's encoded feature, which is important since inputs are encoded differently per qubit.
- A single shared post-entanglement RZ parameter (applied to every qubit) adds one more global phase-shaping degree of freedom without expanding the parameter count much.

This should reduce n_distinct_params from 24 to 10, likely boosting the parameter-economy term substantially, while the parametrized ring entanglement (CRZ) should preserve or even improve worst-group margin relative to a static CZ chain, since the entangling strength becomes trainable rather than fixed.
