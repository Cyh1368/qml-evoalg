<DESCRIPTION>
Building on the observed trend that reducing the number of distinct trainable
parameters (via aggressive but structured sharing) monotonically increased the
combined score in previous iterations (24→16→13 distinct params raised the
score from 0.60→0.61→0.62), this version pushes parameter sharing further
while preserving the exact same gate topology, gate count (31 gates per
block, giving depth_mean 20 / gate_count 93 after the fixed 3x repetition)
and entangling connectivity (a 7-edge nearest-neighbour ring of ZZ
interactions). 

Changes from the current program:
- The 8-qubit RY pre-rotation layer is now split into just two symmetric
  groups (qubits 0-3 share `ry_a`, qubits 4-7 share `ry_b`) instead of four
  pairwise groups.
- The RZ layers before and after the entangling ring now reuse the *same*
  two parameters (`rz_a` for qubits 0-3, `rz_b` for qubits 4-7) instead of
  having separate pre/post parameters per group, removing redundant
  parameters that mostly encode the same effective rotation given the shared
  RY layer.
- The ZZ ring keeps a single shared parameter `zz_ring`, exactly as before,
  since collapsing all entangling gates to one angle already worked well.

This reduces the total number of distinct trainable parameters from 13 down
to 5 (`ry_a, ry_b, rz_a, rz_b, zz_ring`), a very aggressive but structured
reduction that keeps the circuit's expressive backbone (local single-qubit
rotations + a fully connected ring of entanglers) intact. Given that all
prior variants achieved train/val/test accuracy ~0.99-1.00 regardless of
this level of sharing, the underlying decision boundary appears to be
easily captured with very few free parameters, so this should continue the
observed trend of improved parameter efficiency and robustness (worst-group
margin) without sacrificing accuracy, while also plausibly reducing the
train/validation gap and convergence steps due to a smaller, better
conditioned parameter landscape.
