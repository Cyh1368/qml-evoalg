# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parameter sharing across symmetric qubit pairs  
  • The 8-Qubit Shared-Parameter ZZ-Ring Ansatz achieved the top combined score (0.62) with only 13 distinct parameters (parameter efficiency 0.07), grouping RY rotations into four shared parameters (ry_g0–ry_g3) without any loss in train/validation/test accuracy (all 1.00) or increase in generalization gap (0.00).  
- Sandwiching pre- and post-entanglement RZ layers  
  • Both the Shared-Parameter ZZ-Ring (score 0.62) and CZ-Chain Ansatz (score 0.61) wrap their two-qubit block in dedicated “rz_pre_*” and “rz_post_*” rotations. This pattern boosts phase flexibility, raising the validation margin to 0.73 in the best program vs ~0.68 in simpler designs.  
- Ring-topology Ising entanglers with a single shared parameter  
  • Replacing a linear CZ chain with a cyclic ZZ ring (`zz_ring`) under one shared parameter delivered zero generalization gap and perfect accuracies, whereas linear CZ patterns without such sharing peaked at 0.61.  
- Fixed-backend delegation of a compact ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • Passing the evolved spec unchanged to `run_experiment` decouples optimization from hardware details, a common feature of all top-performing circuits.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Excessive reuse of single-qubit parameters across layers  
  • The “share_rz_pre_post_params” patch (Eight-Qubit RY-RZ CZ Ansatz) collapsed pre/post RZ into identical parameters and failed completely (combined score 0.0), demonstrating that distinct phase angles per layer are critical.  
- One-parameter CRZ entanglement  
  • The Single-Layer RY-CRZ-RZ Ansatz used a single shared CRZ for all 8 qubits and scored 0.0. Its under-expressive entanglement failed both validation and testing.  
- Lack of parameterized two-qubit gates or varied entangling patterns  
  • Circuits that relied solely on fixed CZ gates (no Ising-type parameters) achieved at best 0.61, suggesting that richer two-qubit parameterization (e.g., ZZ) is key to robust decision margins.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Strategic grouping of rotation parameters  
  • The current best program pairs up qubits with shared “ry_g*” and “rz_pre_g*”/“rz_post_g*” parameters, halving the distinct single-qubit angles while preserving full classification power.  
- Alternating single-qubit and entangling blocks  
  • A pattern of RY → RZ → ZZ-entangler → RZ recurs in both the best and second-best circuits, underlining its role in balancing expressivity and trainability.  
- Use of Ising-type ZZ gates in a ring topology  
  • Deploying a cyclic sequence of ZZ gates under one parameter (`zz_ring`) concentrates entanglement control into a low-dimensional subspace, which proved more effective than linear CZ chains with per-gate or per-layer parameters.  
- Minimal ANSATZ_SPEC footprint  
  • Defining only the ansatz in a compact data structure and outsourcing optimization to the backend simplifies code maintenance and speeds iteration.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Incremental score gains from structural innovations  
  • Full unshared RY-RZ + CZ chain: 0.60  
  • CZ-Chain with RZ reuse: 0.61 (generalization gap 0.01, converged in 30 steps)  
  • Shared-Parameter ZZ-Ring: 0.62 (gap 0.00, converged in 60 steps)  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with top accuracy  
  • Efficiency rose from 0.04 (full unshared) → 0.05 (CZ-Chain) → 0.07 (ZZ-Ring) while combined score climbed in lockstep.  
- Convergence vs generalization trade-off  
  • The CZ-Chain design converged faster (30 steps) but at a slight cost in validation margin (0.69) and worst-group margin (0.34). The shared-parameter ZZ-Ring took 60 steps but achieved the best margins (validation 0.73, worst-group 0.36).  
- Loss trends mirror accuracy gains  
  • Validation/test losses decreased from ~0.20 in the full and CZ-Chain ansatzes to ~0.18/0.17 in the best program, reflecting tighter decision boundaries.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a second RY→RZ_pre→ZZ_ring→RZ_post block with the same shared parameters. Extend the current single “sandwich” motif into two sequential layers—reusing ry_g0–ry_g3, rz_pre_g0–g3, zz_ring, and rz_post_g0–g3—to probe whether deeper alternating single-qubit/entangling structures further tighten decision boundaries without blowing up parameter count.  

2. Split the single “zz_ring” into two shared parameters for even and odd edges. Define “zz_ring_e” on edges (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7) and “zz_ring_o” on edges (1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–0). This doubles entanglement flexibility (2 vs. 1 parameter) while still enforcing ring topology, letting you test whether a modest boost in expressivity raises validation and worst-group margins.  

3. Interleave a shared XX ring alongside the ZZ ring. After the existing ZZ gates, insert XX gates on the same cyclic pairs with a new “xx_ring” parameter (wires [0,1],[1,2],…,[7,0]). Mixing orthogonal Ising interactions may enrich the circuit’s entanglement subspace and drive further gains in generalization margin.  

4. Append a global RZ rotation layer at the end. Add an RZ gate on each qubit tied to one shared parameter “rz_global” after the final rz_post_g* layer. A single global phase adjustment can fine-tune the classification surface with minimal parameter overhead, potentially lowering validation/test loss.  

5. Refine the RY grouping by splitting one group into two. For example, replace ry_g2 on qubits 4&5 with separate parameters ry_g2a (qubit 4) and ry_g2b (qubit 5), increasing distinct RY angles from 4 to 5. This slight rise in single-qubit granularity tests whether a marginal uptick in expressivity can improve worst-group margins without significantly harming parameter efficiency.
