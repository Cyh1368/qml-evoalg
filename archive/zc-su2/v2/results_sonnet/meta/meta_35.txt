# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Two-group shared RY → global shared RZ (pre/post) → parametrized ZZ-ring + long-range cross-links → forward/backward CNOT ladders  
  • Exemplified by the current best program (Gen 24): combined score 0.71, perfect train/val/test accuracies, param efficiency 0.17, convergence in 30 steps, validation margin 0.71, worst-group margin 0.36.  
  • Also mirrored in Gen 33 (Shared-Parameter Ring-Entanglement Ansatz): score 0.71, depth 63, gates 150, 30-step convergence, worst-group margin 0.35.

- Alternating ZZ sublayers with two-group RY and reused RZ  
  • Gen 28 (“Shared RY/RZ Layers with Alternating-ZZ Ring”): 15-depth, 96 gates, param efficiency 0.17, combined score 0.70, perfect accuracies, convergence ~30 steps.  
  • Shows that partitioned ring scheduling can trade depth/gate count for near-peak score.

- Sandwich architecture with repeated blocks of (shared RY_a/b → shared RZ → ZZ-ring + diagonals → CNOT ladders)  
  • Gen 31 (4-Param Shared ZZ-Ring Ansatz): two blocks, depth 126, gates 300, param efficiency 0.16, score 0.69, 0.98 val/test accuracy, 0.02 generalization gap.  
  • Confirms that repetition of the same shared-param structure boosts expressivity at the cost of depth/gate count.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-constraining to only three distinct parameters collapses expressivity  
  • Gen 30 (QCNN-style 8-qubit hierarchical ansatz) and Gen 36 (Shared-Param Entangling Ansatz) both scored 0.0 and failed all validation.  
  • Feedback: three trainable names couldn’t capture necessary correlations.

- Omitting long-range cross-links reduces worst-group robustness  
  • Pure ring designs (e.g., Gen 21) hovered around score 0.69 with lower worst-group margins versus Gen 24’s 0.71/0.36.  
  • Indicates cross-links are critical to shore up the hardest subgroups.

- Excessive circuit depth/gate count without new parameters limits hardware scalability  
  • Gen 31’s 126-depth/300-gate ansatz achieved 0.69 but at twice the depth and gates of Gen 24.  
  • Diminishing returns on score vs. resource cost when simply stacking more layers.

- Collapsing dual RZ layers into a single application (no reuse) degrades phase flexibility and margins  
  • Earlier experiments (before Gen 24) saw lower validation margins when omitting the post-entangler RZ(rz_p) reuse.

## Implementation Insights

- Parameter naming & reuse maps directly to performance  
  • “ry_a”/“ry_b” for two RY groups, “rz_p” for all RZ gates (pre and post), “zz_ring” for both ring and cross-links.  
  • The current best program and Gen 33 strictly adhere to this scheme, yielding top scores.

- Zero-cost entangling via CNOT ladders accelerates convergence  
  • Forward ladder (wires 0→1→…→7) and backward ladder (7→…→0) introduce deep mixing without extra trainable names.  
  • Both Gen 24 and Gen 27 converged in ~30 steps versus ~60 for pure-ring setups.

- Hypercube-like connectivity under a single ZZ parameter

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a mid-circuit mini-sandwich block: immediately after the forward CNOT ladder, add a two-group RY layer (reuse “ry_a”/“ry_b”) followed by a global RZ on all wires with a new shared param “rz_mid”. This leverages the successful sandwich architecture (Gen 31) to boost expressivity in the mixing region without dramatically increasing depth or gate count.  

2. Enrich entanglement with an XX-ring sublayer: between the ZZ ring (including long-range links) and the backward CNOT ladder, apply XX gates on each nearest-neighbor pair ([0,1],[1,2],…,[7,0]) sharing a new param “xx_ring”. Orthogonal two-qubit couplings like XX have been shown to complement ZZ-only designs and can improve worst-group margins with minimal added complexity.  

3. Differentiate diagonal couplings via two shared params: split the long-range hypercube ZZs into primary diagonals ([0,4],[1,5],[2,6],[3,7]) with param “zz_lr1” and secondary diagonals ([0,5],[1,6],[2,7],[3,4]) with param “zz_lr2”. This targeted parameterization gives the optimizer finer control over distinct cross-link patterns, likely boosting robustness on hard subgroups.  

4. Hybridize CNOT ladders with CZ insertions: in both forward and backward ladders, replace every third CNOT (e.g. on wires [2,3] and [5,6]) with a CZ gate. Prior ladder-mixing insights indicate that alternating entanglers accelerates convergence and deepens mixing at zero additional parameter cost.  

5. Split pre- vs. post-entangler phases into two params: rename the existing RZ before the entangler to “rz_pre” and assign all RZs after the backward ladder to a new param “rz_post.” Evidence shows that dual RZ layers with independent phase angles enhance phase flexibility and can raise validation and worst-group margins with only one extra distinct parameter.
