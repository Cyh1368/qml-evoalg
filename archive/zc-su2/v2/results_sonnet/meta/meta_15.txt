# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Two-group single-qubit rotation sharing around an Ising ring  
  • In the current best “Shared-Parameter RY–RZ ZZ Ring Ansatz” (Gen 12, score 0.69), defining ry_a on wires 0–3 and ry_b on wires 4–7 yields perfect train/val/test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
- Sandwiching a fully shared entangling ring with pre- and post-RZ layers  
  • Gen 12 wraps a single zz_ring parameter between rz_a/​rz_b layers (and repeats this “sandwich” twice), boosting phase flexibility (validation margin 0.73) while reusing only four rotation parameters.  
- Fully shared ZZ-ring entanglers for concentrated expressivity  
  • Using one shared “zz_ring” across all ring edges (no CZ/CRZ) consistently achieved top combined scores (0.62 → 0.69) and eliminated overfitting in Generations 2, 8, 9, and 12.  
- Minimal distinct parameters with block repetition accelerates convergence  
  • Repeating the 5-parameter block twice in Gen 12 led to convergence in 30 steps—half the iteration count of earlier high-scoring designs (~60 steps).

## Ineffective Approaches
- Over-collapsing RZ layers into a single global parameter  
  • Gen 15’s single rz parameter for both pre- and post-layers collapsed phase degrees of freedom, failed validation, and scored 0.0.  
- Unparameterized or linear CZ chains  
  • The Shared-Parameter RY-RZ CZ Chain Ansatz (Gen 7) used fixed CZ edges, scored only 0.64, had low parameter efficiency (0.10) and a worst-group margin of 0.34.  
- Single-parameter controlled-phase entanglers  
  • Gen 11’s ring of identically parameterized CRZ gates lacked expressivity, did not pass validation, and scored 0.0.  
- Aggressive three-parameter global sharing without wrap-around entanglement  
  • Omitting the ZZ edge between qubits 7 and 0 and using only {RY, RZ, ZZ} globally prevented meeting ansatz formatting rules and collapsed performance.

## Implementation Insights
- Explicit parameter naming enforces symmetry and limits trainables  
  • The Gen 12 ANSATZ_SPEC uses exactly five distinct names—ry_a, ry_b, rz_a, rz_b, zz_ring—yet realizes seven total parameters across two layers.  
- Reuse of pre-entangle RZ parameters post-entangling minimizes distinct angles  
  • RZ gates before and after the ZZ ring both reference rz_a/rz_b, preserving worst-group margin (0.36) without extra trainables.  
- Static ANSATZ_SPEC delegation isolates circuit design  
  • Passing a self-contained list of gate specs to `_run` separates encoding and optimization logic, enabling rapid patch iterations and halving convergence steps.  
- Block repetition balances depth and parameter economy  
  • Duplicating the RY–RZ–ZZ–RZ “sandwich” achieves sufficient entangling depth (35 layers) while keeping distinct parameters to five.

## Performance Analysis
- Combined score uplift from 0.64 to 0.69 by switching CZ chains → fully shared ZZ rings  
  • Gen 7 (CZ chain) vs Gen 12 (ZZ ring) saw a +0.05 score gain, parameter efficiency jump 0.10→0.14, and margin improvement 0.34→0.36.  
- Zero generalization gap maintained across fully shared ZZ-ring designs  
  • Generations 2, 8, 9, and 12 all report perfect train/val/test accuracy and generalization_gap_mean = 0.00.  
- Validation margin correlates with parameter grouping granularity  
  • Designs with two-group RY/RZ sharing (Gen 8/12) reach validation_margin_mean ≈ 0.73, versus ≈ 0.36 in single-group-only schemes.  
- Convergence speed linked to block repetition and gate variety  
  • Gen 12’s two-block ZZ-ring circuit converges in 30 steps, outperforming Gen 13’s added XX-ring variant (60 steps) despite identical combined scores (0.69).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the sandwich motif to three repetitions. After the second RZ_post layer, duplicate the entire RY→RZ_pre→ZZ_ring→RZ_post block once more—still reusing ry_a/ry_b, rz_a/rz_b, and zz_ring. This deeper 3-block circuit preserves only five distinct parameters while increasing decision‐boundary complexity, which may tighten margins without slowing convergence dramatically.  

2. Close the ring by adding the missing wrap-around entangler [7,0]. Insert a final {"gate":"ZZ","wires":[7,0],"param":"zz_ring"} after each set of sequential ZZ gates. Completing the cyclic connectivity leverages full eight-qubit expressivity at no extra parameter cost, potentially boosting both validation margins and worst‐group performance.  

3. Split the single zz_ring into two alternating parameters (zz_even, zz_odd). Assign zz_even to edges (0-1,2-3,4-5,6-7) and zz_odd to edges (1-2,3-4,5-6,7-0) in each entangling layer. This 1→2 parameter increase doubles entangling flexibility in the ring topology and has improved margins in prior generations.  

4. Interleave a shared XX ring immediately after each ZZ ring. For each neighbor pair [i,i+1 mod 8], insert {"gate":"XX","wires":[i,i+1],"param":"xx_ring"} following the ZZs in both sandwich blocks. Mixing orthogonal Ising interactions has empirically lifted generalization margins and parameter efficiency in related ansätze.  

5. Decouple pre- and post-entangling RZ layers into four parameters. Replace rz_a/b with rz_pre_a, rz_pre_b for the pre-ZZ layer and rz_post_a, rz_post_b for the post-ZZ layer. Independent phase shifts around the entanglers raise expressivity and have shown to improve worst-group margins without increasing entangler or RY count.
