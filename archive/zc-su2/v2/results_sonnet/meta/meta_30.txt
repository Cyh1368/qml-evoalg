# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Long-range ZZ cross-couplings under the same “zz_ring” parameter (Gen 24 – add_long_range_zz_couplings) lifted the combined score from 0.70 → 0.71 over the pure ring design (Gen 21), while retaining perfect accuracies and halving convergence to 30 steps.  
- Forward/backward CNOT ladders surrounding the parametrized entangler (Gen 24 & Gen 27) deepen mixing at zero parameter cost, accelerating training (convergence in ~30 steps vs 60 in Gen 21) and supporting a high validation margin (0.71).  
- Two-group shared RY rotations (wires 0–3 vs 4–7) combined with a single global RZ reused pre- and post-entangler (“rz_p”) appear in both Gen 24 (0.71) and Gen 27 (0.69), delivering perfect accuracies, zero generalization gap, and strong class asymmetry encoding.  
- Alternating even/odd ZZ sublayers with fully shared rotations (Gen 28 – Shared RY/RZ Layers with Alternating-ZZ Ring) achieved a combined score of 0.70 at only 15-depth and 96 gates (param efficiency 0.17), demonstrating minimal-depth expressivity through entangler scheduling.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-constraining parameter sharing (Gen 29 – 8-Qubit Shared-Parameter ZZ-Ring Ansatz) collapsed rotations and entanglers into just 3 distinct parameters, yielding low combined score (0.39) and only modest accuracies (validation 0.49, test 0.52).  
- Schema violations (inconsistent use of “wire” vs “wires” in Gen 32) caused outright validation failures and a 0.0 score, underscoring the need for strict adherence to the ansatz specification format.  
- Omitting long-range cross-links (Gen 27 vs Gen 24) cost ~0.02 in combined score (0.69 vs 0.71) and reduced parameter efficiency (0.14 vs 0.17), indicating ring-only couplings limit worst-group robustness.  
- Collapsing dual RZ layers into a single application without reuse (seen in earlier failed patches) removes critical phase flexibility and degrades both accuracy and margin, as evidenced by lower validation margins in those designs.  

## Implementation Insights

- Consistent parametrization scheme: define “ry_a”/“ry_b” for two shared RY groups, “rz_p” for all RZ gates (pre/post), and “zz_ring” for every ZZ coupling (including cross-links). Gen 24’s clear ANSATZ_SPEC structure directly maps to its top performance.  
- Circuit template:  
  • pre-rotation RY → forward CNOT ladder → RZ(rz_p) → ZZ-ring + cross-links → backward CNOT ladder → RZ(rz_p)  
 Captures symmetry, maximizes entanglement, and reuses parameters without increasing distinct names.  
- Parameter reuse amplifies expressive power: Gen 24 packs six parameterized gates into only four distinct names, yielding the peak parameter efficiency (0.17) and perfect generalization.  
- Entangler scheduling: alternating ZZ sublayers (Gen 28) compress depth to 15 while maintaining score 0.70, suggesting that ring partitioning can trade gate count for depth without hurting margin.  

## Performance Analysis

- Combined Score Trend: Gen 24 (0.71) > Gen 28 (0.70) > Gen 27 (0.69) > Gen 29 (0.39) > Gen 32 (0.00). The top two designs both employ shared parameters plus additional connectivity (cross-links or alternating sublayers).  
- Parameter Efficiency vs Depth:  
  • Gen 24: 150 gates, depth 63, eff 0.17, score 0.71  
  • Gen 28: 96 gates, depth 15, eff 0.17, score 0.70  
  • Gen 27: 162 gates, depth 84, eff 0.14, score 0.69  
  → Sharing parameters smartly (Gen 24 & 28) can preserve eff even at different depth/gate regimes.  
- Convergence: high-connectivity designs with CNOT ladders (Gen 24/27) converge in 30 steps; pure-ring circuits without ladders needed ~60 steps (Gen 21).  
- Margins: validation margins peak at ~0.72 (Gen 27) and ~0.71 (Gen 24), while worst-group margins stabilize at 0.36 across top designs, indicating that cross-links and ladder mixing uniformly bolster the hardest-to-classify group.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Partition the ZZ ring into two alternating sublayers to compress depth. Instead of applying all eight nearest-neighbor ZZs in one block, first apply ZZ on “even” edges ([0,1],[2,3],[4,5],[6,7]) with param “zz_ring”, then a second sublayer on the “odd” edges ([1,2],[3,4],[5,6],[7,0]) with the same param. This schedules entanglement like Gen 28’s alternating sublayers—halving depth toward ~30 while preserving the 0.17 efficiency and high margins.  

2. Interleave both sets of long-range diagonals in two micro-sublayers. Rather than grouping all cross-links together, apply ZZ(0,4), ZZ(1,5), ZZ(2,6), ZZ(3,7) with “zz_ring” immediately after the even-ring sublayer, then ZZ(0,5), ZZ(1,6), ZZ(2,7), ZZ(3,4) after the odd-ring sublayer. This single-block diagonal interleaving retains n_distinct_params=4, deepens hypercube connectivity, and may boost worst-group margins without extra depth.  

3. Differentiate ring vs. cross-link couplings with two shared parameters. Rename the eight nearest-neighbor ZZs to param “zz_nn” and the eight long-range ZZs to “zz_lr”, keeping both fully shared across their respective sets. By giving the optimizer targeted control over adjacency vs. diagonal interactions, you exploit the proven value of cross-links while adding minimal expressivity, potentially lifting the combined score above 0.71.  

4. Hybridize the CNOT ladders with interleaved CZ gates at zero param cost. In both forward and backward ladders, replace every second CNOT (for example on wires [1,2], [3,4], [5,6]) with CZ. This injects an orthogonal entangling interaction—as recommended by prior ladder-mixing insights—accelerating convergence and enriching two-qubit mixing without increasing the parameter count.  

5. Split the single global RZ into pre- and post-entangler phase parameters. Change all RZ gates before the ZZ block to param “rz_pre” and those after to “rz_post”, instead of reusing “rz_p”. This adds one distinct trainable name but restores full phase flexibility between entangling stages—addressing the earlier finding that dual RZ layers are critical for high validation margins.
