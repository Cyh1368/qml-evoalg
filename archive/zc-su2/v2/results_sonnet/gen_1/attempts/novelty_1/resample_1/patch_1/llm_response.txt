<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses 24 distinct trainable parameters (one RY + two RZ per qubit, i.e., 3 per-qubit parameters × 8 qubits), which the scoring function penalizes heavily since parameter economy is rewarded at every count. To cut parameter count while preserving expressivity for separating the hardest validation groups, I group qubits into pairs (0-1, 2-3, 4-5, 6-7) and share single-qubit rotation parameters within each pair. This reduces the RY layer from 8 params to 4, and each RZ layer from 8 params to 4, for 12 total single-qubit params instead of 24. I also replace the fixed CZ entangling ring with parametrized ZZ (Ising) gates sharing a single parameter across the whole ring, which adds tunable entanglement strength (helpful for correlating qubit pairs relevant to hidden difficulty groups) while adding only 1 new parameter. Net distinct parameter count: 4 (RY groups) + 4 (RZ pre groups) + 4 (RZ post groups) + 1 (shared ZZ) = 13, roughly half of the original 24, which should substantially boost parameter_efficiency and reduce train/val gap risk, while keeping enough per-pair granularity to maintain the strong margin structure already observed (worst_group_margin 0.35).
</DESCRIPTION>
