<DESCRIPTION>
Crossover of the two top-scoring (0.71) seed variants, taken a step further along the "fewer distinct parameters always wins" guidance that the task description states explicitly. Both 0.71 seeds already reduced the trainable-parameter set to {ry_a, ry_b, rz_p, zz_ring} (4 distinct names) by sharing an RY parameter across each half of the register, a single global RZ phase before/after the entangling ring, and a single shared ZZ-ring coupling (including long-range "hypercube" cross-couplings) that costs nothing extra in parameter count. The current mutant that added a second rz_a/rz_b split (5 distinct params, more gates, no CNOT mixing) scored worse (0.69), confirming that adding parameters or dropping the zero-cost CNOT mixing layers hurts the metric even when accuracy stays perfect.

This crossover keeps every zero-cost structural idea from the winning seeds (forward+backward CNOT ladders for mixing, global RZ phase reused twice, ZZ ring with long-range "opposite-qubit" couplings all sharing one coupling parameter) but merges the two RY groups (`ry_a`, `ry_b`) into a single shared `ry_a`, cutting the distinct-parameter count from 4 down to 3 (ry_a, rz_p, zz_ring). To compensate for the reduced expressivity from merging the RY groups and to help the worst-group margin (the dominant score term), an additional shared-parameter "skip-2" ZZ shell (0-2,1-3,2-4,3-5,4-6,5-7,6-0,7-1) is added — still reusing the single `zz_ring` parameter, so no new trainable names are introduced, but it enriches connectivity beyond the nearest-neighbor + opposite-qubit couplings of the seeds, aiming to better separate hard validation groups while remaining as parameter-economical as possible.

This design targets: fewer distinct parameters (3 vs 4) for a higher parameter-economy score, at least as much or richer entangling structure to preserve/raise worst_group_margin, and no duplicate motif repetition (which was shown to hurt convergence/param count in the current program).
