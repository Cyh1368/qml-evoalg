The current approach achieves high accuracy but struggles with the worst_group_margin (0.35), indicating that hard samples within specific difficulty groups are not being separated robustly. The prior programs also show this bottleneck: validation_margin ~0.73 but worst_group_margin only ~0.36.

This new approach takes a fundamentally different strategy: instead of a fixed layered architecture, we design the circuit to progressively refine decision boundaries through adaptive feature extraction. The key innovations are:

1. **Multi-scale entanglement pyramid**: Rather than uniform nearest-neighbor and offset patterns, we use a pyramid structure where entanglement scope progressively increases (nearest → skip-1 → skip-2), allowing the circuit to simultaneously operate at multiple resolution scales.

2. **Quadrature rotations for phase refinement**: After entangling layers, we apply RX (not just RY/RZ) on strategically chosen qubits to add phase information orthogonal to the current rotation plane. This breaks symmetries that might make hard samples indistinguishable.

3. **Reduced parameter sharing in critical zones**: Instead of globally sharing ry_mid across [1,3,5,7], we use a mix of shared and distinct parameters, concentrating sharing only on truly redundant operations to maximize discriminative capacity where it matters most.

4. **Targeted CZ barriers before final rotation**: CZ gates (not just CRZ) create structured correlations that help separate hard samples by introducing controlled phase flips that depend on multi-qubit entanglement.

This design should improve worst_group_margin by providing more expressive decision boundaries for challenging samples while maintaining parameter economy through selective sharing.
