# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Narrow-CRX enrichment within the primary entangling block  
   • Layered Eight-Qubit Shared-Parameter Ansatz (Gen 25, combined 0.67) and the Current Best program (Gen 20, combined 0.67) both insert three CRX gates (sharing the “ent_nn” key) immediately after the XX layer. This boosts validation margin from ~0.73→0.74, holds worst-group margin at ~0.36, and retains 30-step convergence.  
- Three-block entanglement: XX → YY → CRZ  
   • All top performers (Gen 11, Gen 20, Gen 23, Gen 25) use nearest-neighbor XX (“ent_nn”), offset YY (“ent_off”), and long-range CRZ (“ent_lr”) in sequence. They achieve zero generalization gap, perfect train/val/test accuracy, and combined scores ≥ 0.66.  
- Parity-based RY sharing plus an intermediate “ry_mid”  
   • Splitting initial RY into ry_even/ry_odd and adding a mid-layer ry_mid on qubits [1,3,5,7] concentrates expressivity. This pattern underlies Gen 11 and the Current Best, driving convergence to ~30 steps and keeping validation/test losses at ~0.18/0.17.  
- Final global shared RZ refinement  
   • Applying a single “rz_final” to all eight qubits (present in Gens 11, 20, 25) reliably lifts validation margin by ~0.01 at minimal depth/gate cost.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- High-depth, high-gate-count “ring” or multi-scale ansätze without targeted sublayers  
   • Parameter-Sharing Entangled Ring Ansatz (Gen 27; depth 49, 153 gates) plateaus at combined ~0.66 and requires ~60 steps, showing that more layers alone don’t translate to better margins.  
- Skip-4 CZ margin-booster  
   • Shared-Parameter Multi-Block Entangling Ansatz (Gen 29) adds a skip-4 CZ sublayer but remains at combined 0.66 and worst-group margin 0.35—no better than simpler designs.  
- Over-parameterization without new entangling motifs  
   • Designs with 8–9 distinct parameters (e.g., Gen 19) slow convergence to ~60 steps and drop combined score to ~0.64, confirming that extra parameters must be paired with fresh entanglement patterns.  
- Isolated gate-type substitutions  
   • Simply swapping long-range ZZ for CRZ (Gen 28) or adding extra Ising layers (Gen 17) holds combined score at ~0.65–0.66, indicating that gate-type changes need to be embedded in an optimized block structure.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Parameter-sharing “ent_nn” across both XX and CRX gates  
   • By using the same parameter key for nearest-neighbor XX and the narrow CRX sublayer, the model focuses gradient updates on essential correlations without increasing parameter count.  
- Declarative block naming accelerates convergence  
   • Naming parameters semantically (ry_even/ry_odd/ry_mid, ent_nn/ent_off/ent_lr, rz_final) halves convergence steps versus unlabeled θ₁…θₙ, as seen in Gen 11 and Gen 20.  
- Lightweight sublayers yield high margin leverage  
   • Inserting only three CRX gates (+1 depth, +3 gates) right after the XX block produces a +0.01 boost in validation margin and achieves combined 0.67.  
- Dropping unlabeled CZ avoids complexity without margin loss  
   • The Current Best program removes the skip-3 CZ entirely yet retains worst-group margin 0.36 and improves validation margin to 0.74, simplifying the circuit.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score gains track entanglement enrichment  
   • Baseline tri-block designs (Gen 23/28/29) sit at combined 0.66. Adding CRX sublayer (Gen 25/20) lifts to 0.67.  
- Convergence tied to distinct-parameter count  
   • Circuits with exactly seven distinct parameters (Gens 11/20/25) converge in ~30 steps; those with eight or more need ~60 steps or stagnate.  
- Validation margin correlates with score  
   • Validation margin moves from ~0.72 (7-param baseline) → 0.73 (with skip-3 CZ) → 0.74 (with CRX enrichment), mirroring combined score increases from 0.66→0.67.  
- Gate-count trade-offs are minimal for margin gains  
   • The +3-gate CRX sublayer yields +0.01 validation margin without extending convergence or adding distinct parameters, demonstrating a high margin-per-gate payoff.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the long-range CRZ sublayer by appending two more CRZ gates on wires [5,0] and [6,1], all sharing the existing “ent_lr” parameter. This deepens diagonal entanglement across the circuit’s opposite corners without increasing the distinct parameter count, leveraging the same gradient signal that gave previous +0.01 validation-margin gains.  

2. Enforce cyclic boundary conditions on your nearest-neighbor blocks by adding an XX gate on [7,0] with “ent_nn” and a matching YY gate on [7,0] with “ent_off.” Ring-closure has boosted connectivity in past gens and uses only already-shared parameters, so you keep parameter parsimony while strengthening local correlations.  

3. Immediately after the current CRZ block, replicate the three-block entanglement motif (XX → CRX → YY → CRZ) a second time, reusing “ent_nn,” “ent_off” and “ent_lr.” Doubling this proven seven-parameter substructure enriches expressivity further but preserves the ~30-step convergence and zero-gap generalization of the best program.  

4. Insert a lightweight RZ bracketing layer on the ry_mid wires: after your RY gates on qubits [1,3,5,7], add RZ rotations using the existing “rz_final” key. This non-commuting RY→RZ pair exploits the same global refinement that lifted validation margins by ~0.01, yet costs no extra parameters.  

5. Carve out a distinct parameter “ent_crx” exclusively for the three-gate CRX sublayer instead of sharing “ent_nn.” This tests whether decoupling narrow-angle CRX rotations can unlock fresh entanglement patterns and potentially raise margins further, trading one extra distinct parameter for new expressivity.
