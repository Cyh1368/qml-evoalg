# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Layered entangling blocks with shared parameter strength  
  • Generation 4 (current best “Layered 8-Qubit Variational Ansatz”, score 0.65) and Generation 6 (“Shared Parameter RY-CRZ Ansatz”, score 0.65) both use three distinct entangling interactions (nearest-neighbor, offset, long-range) each controlled by a single shared parameter. This yields perfect accuracy, zero generalization gap, and fast convergence.  
- Alternating rotation–entanglement–rotation structure  
  • In Gen 4, interleaving an initial shared-RY layer (even/odd), a mid-layer of RZ/RY, and a final shared-RZ around each entangling block gives rich non-commuting rotations. This pattern achieves validation/test loss ≈ 0.18/0.17 and margins ≥ 0.73.  
- Minimal distinct parameters for expressivity  
  • Both Gen 4 and Gen 6 use only 8 distinct parameters (10 instances) yet reach top performance. Previous Gen 5 (score 0.64) with 9 distinct parameters also reinforces that ~8–9 distinct parameters strike an optimal balance between expressivity and overfitting.  
- Sparse long-range connectivity atop local chains  
  • Adding a long-range ZZ block in Gen 4 (and Gen 7’s similar block) improves worst-group margin to ~0.35 without blowing up circuit depth, boosting robustness on hardest data subsets.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-sharing across heterogeneous gates  
  • Generation 10’s globally shared RX/RY plus static CZs collapsed expressivity and failed validation (score 0.0). Excessive parameter reuse prevented the circuit from fitting even trivial patterns.  
- Single or shallow entanglement layers  
  • Generation 2’s lone ZZ layer under-entangled the ansatz, scoring 0.0. A single two-qubit interaction is insufficient for this dataset.  
- Typographical/inconsistent gate specifications  
  • Gen 10’s mix of “wire” vs. “wires” fields and omission of parametrized two-qubit gates caused execution errors, underscoring the need for a correct, consistent JSON-style spec.  
- Adding depth without new parametric diversity  
  • Gen 7 and Gen 8 increased depth to 27 with modest additions of distinct parameters (11–12) yet scored only 0.62 and converged in ~90 steps. Depth alone did not recover the top score.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC enables rapid iteration  
  • The current best program’s explicit list of dicts for each gate/param makes it trivial to tweak sharing patterns or insert new blocks without modifying training code.  
- Block-level parameter naming drives clarity  
  • Using names like “ent_nn”, “ent_off”, “ent_lr” cleanly separates entangling strengths and speeds up optimizer convergence (60 vs. ~90 steps in other top scorers).  
- Even/odd qubit sharing balances expressivity  
  • Sharing RY across all even qubits and another across all odd qubits (8 wires → 2 params) preserves local asymmetry while slashing parameter count.  
- Dual-phase rotations bracket entangling blocks  
  • Mid-layer RZ on a subset of wires before YY and ZZ blocks, plus a final global RZ, systematically introduce additional non-commuting rotations, lowering both validation and test losses.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Convergence speed vs. depth trade-off  
  • Although Gen 6 has shallower depth (15) and fewer gates (81), Gen 4’s deeper (24) 108-gate circuit converges in 60 steps (vs. 90), suggesting richer entanglement yields stronger gradients.  
- Distinct parameter sweet spot  
  • Programs with 8–9 distinct parameters (Gen 4, Gen 6, Gen 5) consistently hit combined scores ≥ 0.64. Exceeding ~11 distinct parameters (Gen 7/8) yields diminishing returns.  
- Entanglement variety boosts worst-group margin  
  • The three-block structure in Gen 4 pushes worst-group margin to 0.35 vs. ~0.34 in Gen 7’s mixed CZ/Ising or 0.34 in Gen 5’s sparse CZ pattern.  
- Zero generalization gap as hallmark of balanced sharing  
  • Gen 4, Gen 6, and Gen 5 all record a 0.00 generalization gap—evidence that balanced parameter sharing with sufficient entanglement prevents both under- and overfitting.
