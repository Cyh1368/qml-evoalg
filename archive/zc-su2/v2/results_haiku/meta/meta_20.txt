# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Multi-scale entanglement with skip-3 CZ boosts margins without new parameters  
  • The Parity-Shared Mixed Entanglement Ansatz (Gen 20, score 0.67) layers nearest-neighbor XX, offset YY, long-range CRZ, then adds a skip-3 CZ block. This raises the validation margin to 0.73 (from 0.72 in Gen 11) and worst-group margin to 0.36, all while keeping n_distinct_params at 7 and convergence at ~30 steps.  
- Parity-based RY sharing plus mid-layer rotations drives fast convergence  
  • Across top performers (Gen 11 score 0.66, Gen 20 score 0.67), splitting the initial RY layer into “ry_even” vs. “ry_odd” and inserting a shared “ry_mid” on every other qubit yields rich non-commutativity and cuts convergence to ~30 steps.  
- Final global RZ refines class separation cheaply  
  • The final shared “rz_final” on all eight qubits recurred in Gen 11 (0.66) and Gen 20 (0.67) to boost validation margins without increasing depth or distinct parameters.  
- Balanced entanglement variety with minimal parameters  
  • Best-in-class circuits (Gen 11, 7 params; Gen 20, 7 params) mix XX, YY, CRZ, CZ gates in three–four blocks. This variety drives expressivity, yields perfect train/test accuracy, zero generalization gap, and high combined scores (≥0.66).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterization without margin gains  
  • Layered Shared-Parameter Qubit Ansatz (Gen 19, score 0.64) expanded to 9 distinct parameters, depth 27, gate_count 117, but worst‐group margin fell to 0.34 and convergence slowed to ~60 steps—an indication that extra parameters and tangled entanglement reduced robustness.  
- Replacing parameterized ZZ with CRZ alone underwhelms  
  • Generation 16’s swap of long-range ZZ→CRZ (score 0.65) maintained perfect train/test but only 0.99 validation and a worst‐group margin of 0.35, suggesting that simply changing gate type without new entanglement structure offers limited benefit.  
- Deep circuits without fresh entanglement fail to improve  
  • Generation 19 and the Layered Shared-Parameter Ansatz (depth 27+) added gates but no new entangling motifs beyond the three core blocks, yet saw combined scores drop by ≥0.02 relative to Gen 11 and Gen 20.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative gate lists with clear param keys  
  • All top circuits (Gen 11 & 20) define ANSATZ_SPEC as a list-of-dicts, labeling blocks “ent_nn,” “ent_off,” “ent_lr,” etc. This consistent naming sharpens gradient signals and halves convergence steps vs. generic θ₁…θₙ.  
- Skip-3 CZ as a non-parametric “margin booster”  
  • Gen 20 appends CZ gates on qubit pairs spaced by three, with no new parameters (“wires”: [0,3], [1,4], …). Embedding these unparameterized entanglers raises worst-group margins at zero param cost.  
- Even/odd and mid-layer sharing balance expressivity and parsimony  
  • The current best program replicates the Gen 11 pattern of RY on even/odd qubits plus a shared mid-layer “ry_mid” on wires 1,3,5,7—retaining asymmetry with only three rotation parameters.  
- Global RZ last layer for fine-tuning  
  • Applying a single “rz_final” parameter across all qubits at the circuit’s end, as in Gen 11 and Gen 20, consistently lifts validation margins by ~0.01–0.02.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score vs. entanglement breadth  
  • Combined score climbs from 0.65 (Gen 16: XX→YY→CRZ) → 0.66 (Gen 11: add global RZ & block names) → 0.67 (Gen 20: + skip-3 CZ). Each new entangling pattern incrementally raises margins and the combined score.  
- Parameter count, convergence, and generalization gap  
  • Ansätze with 7 distinct parameters (Gen 11, Gen 20) hit zero generalization gap and converge in ~30 steps. Gen 19’s 9-parameter variant needs ~60 steps and shows a 0.00 gap but lower margins—underscoring that more parameters don’t always speed learning or boost robustness.  
- Depth/gate trade-offs  
  • Gen 11 (depth 21, gates 93) → Gen 20 (depth 26, gates 108) sees +5 depth/+15 gates yield +0.01 combined score and +0.01 worst-group margin, with no convergence penalty. Moderate depth increases pay off when matched with fresh entangling motifs.  
- Validation accuracy plateau  
  • All top designs stabilize at validation accuracy ≈0.99 and test accuracy 1.00. Further gains hinge on margins rather than raw accuracy, highlighting the importance of worst-group and validation margins as differentiation metrics.
