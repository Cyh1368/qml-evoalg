# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Merging XX and YY entanglers into a single `ent_nn` parameter  
  • Seen in Generation 52 (merge_ent_off_into_ent_nn) and the current seed program, raising combined score from 0.66 → 0.68 and halving convergence steps (~60 → 30).  
- Three-block entanglement backbone with minimal parameters  
  • Nearest-neighbor XX+YY (both using `ent_nn`), followed by long-range CRZ (`ent_lr`), achieved perfect accuracies and zero generalization gap in Gen 54, Gen 57, Gen 58 and the seed program.  
- Parity-based rotation sharing plus targeted mid-layer rotations  
  • Initial RY_even/RY_odd layers bracketing an RY_mid on odd wires, then a shared RZ_final (seed program), consistently pushes worst-group margin to 0.36 at combined score 0.68.  
- Aggressive parameter sharing (6 distinct parameters)  
  • All 0.68-scoring ansätze—including the seed program (n_params 8, n_distinct 6, param_efficiency 0.12)—converged in 30 steps with perfect train/val/test accuracy and zero gap.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-restrictive single-parameter sharing across blocks  
  • The Multi-block Parameter-Shared Ansatz (Gen 62) collapsed to combined score 0.0, failing validation due to underfitting.  
- Maintaining a separate offset entangler parameter  
  • Earlier designs with distinct `ent_off` (e.g., Gen 49/51) plateaued at 0.66, converging in ~60 steps and yielding worst-group margins ≤ 0.35.  
- Adding gates/layers without parameter consolidation  
  • Deeper circuits with extra CZ or CRX layers (not in the current top evaluations) increased depth and gate count without improving score or convergence speed.  
- Over-parameterization beyond six distinct parameters  
  • Designs using seven or more distinct parameters saw no accuracy gain and slower optimization.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Entanglement blocks specified contiguously in ANSATZ_SPEC enable parallel scheduling and minimal depth  
  • The seed program packs four XX gates then three YY gates in two back-to-back blocks, keeping depth at 20 and gate count at 93 (lowest among 0.68-scoring circuits).  
- Parity-based parameter naming (`ry_even`/`ry_odd`) simplifies weight sharing  
  • Across Gen 54, Gen 57, Gen 58 and the seed, this pattern maintained expressivity while reducing unique parameters to two for the initial layer.  
- Targeted mid-circuit rotations on odd wires sharpen decision boundaries  
  • The `ry_mid` parameter applied only to qubits 1, 3, 5, 7 consistently raised worst-group margin to 0.36 (seed program) versus 0.35 in less tuned designs.  
- Single shared CRZ parameter (`ent_lr`) ensures global connectivity without extra parameters  
  • Adopted in Gen 54, Gen 57, Gen 58, and the seed, this yielded zero generalization gap and low validation loss (~0.17).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Combined score jump from 0.66 → 0.68 aligns exactly with merging offset YY into `ent_nn` (Gen 52 onward).  
- Convergence steps uniformly drop to 30 in all 0.68-scoring programs, compared to ~60 in 0.66-scoring variants.  
- Worst-group margin peaks at 0.36 only in the seed program and Gen 54; other 0.68-scoring runs (Gen 56–58) stagnate at 0.35.  
- Gate count and depth reductions correlate with implementation refinements:  
  • Gen 54: 102 gates/depth 23 → seed: 93 gates/depth 20, same 0.68 score and margins, demonstrating tighter gate scheduling.
