The current program uses a fixed architecture with separate RY gates on all qubits followed by a chain of CZ gates and post-processing RZ gates. While it achieves high accuracy (1.00 train, 0.99 validation), the worst_group_margin (0.35) indicates poor separation on hard samples—the core optimization target.

Key insights from studying the seed program:
1. Parameter economy is critical: the current 24 distinct parameters could be reduced through careful sharing
2. The linear CZ chain may not capture complex entanglement patterns needed for hard samples
3. Separate pre/post RZ gates are redundant—they can be consolidated
4. The worst-group margin suggests the model lacks expressiveness for difficult boundary cases

Novel approach:
- Use **parameter sharing across spatial structure** to reduce parameter count while maintaining capacity
- Employ **cross-entangling gates** (non-linear connectivity) beyond the simple chain to better explore the solution space
- Apply **dynamic gate placement** with parametrized two-qubit gates (XX, YY, ZZ) that directly encode rotations, reducing overhead
- Reduce from 24 to ~12-14 distinct parameters through strategic sharing
- Use multiple short entangling blocks with different qubit pairings to improve separation on hard samples

This reduces parameter count for better generalization, introduces richer entanglement patterns for robust separation of difficult samples, and leverages two-qubit parametrized gates for more efficient quantum circuits.
