efficient_shared_entangler

<DESCRIPTION>
The seed ansatz used 24 independent parameters per repetition (8 RY + 8 RZ‑pre + 8 RZ‑post), giving 146 total trainable parameters for a circuit that already reaches perfect train/val/test accuracy — meaning most of that parameter budget is wasted and heavily penalizes the parameter‑economy score.

This version keeps the essential per‑qubit expressivity (8 independent RY angles, since these directly shape the per‑qubit response to the fixed data encoding) but replaces the two full layers of 8 independent RZ angles with a **single shared RZ parameter** applied to all wires (a global phase‑style correction) and adds a **cross‑ring CRX entangling layer** using **one shared parameter** across 4 long-range pairs (0-4,1-5,2-6,3-7) to mix information between the two halves of the register without adding extra trainable degrees of freedom. The nearest‑neighbor CZ ring (no parameters) still provides local entanglement.

This cuts the parameter count per repetition from 24 down to 10 (8 RY + 1 shared RZ + 1 shared CRX), roughly a 58% reduction, while preserving both local (CZ ring) and long‑range (CRX cross links) entanglement structure needed for expressivity. Fewer trainable parameters should shrink train/validation gap risk, speed convergence, and substantially boost the parameter‑efficiency term in the score while retaining near‑identical circuit depth/gate count and thus similar representational power.
