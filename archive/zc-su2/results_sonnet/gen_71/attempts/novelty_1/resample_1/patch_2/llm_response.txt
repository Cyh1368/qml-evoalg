The double-block stacking (calling `_add_theta_block` twice) kept the same combined score (0.80) as the single-block version but roughly doubled depth/gate-count and quadrupled the convergence steps (120 vs 30), which is strictly worse for parameter/step economy. Revert to a single full `_add_theta_block` call (matching the best-converging prior variant) and instead add a *lightweight, localized* fractional duplication restricted to only 4 of the 8 qubits (wires 0-3), reusing the same shared "theta" parameter. This targeted local RX→RY→(XX/CZ ring)→RZ sub-pass sharpens correlations among a subset of qubits at a fraction of the gate-count cost of a full second block, aiming to preserve the fast (~30-step) convergence and low depth of the single-block design while potentially improving accuracy/generalization via the extra localized non-linearity, with zero additional trainable parameters.

Argumentation: Since parameter count is already minimal (1 shared parameter, 8 total due to per-block instantiation) and the score is dominated by parameter economy and few-shot generalization, the marginal cost of the fractional block is small (16 extra gates on 4 qubits vs 240 extra gates for a full second block), while it still deepens the circuit non-uniformly, which can help expressivity without hurting convergence speed as much as full duplication did.
