# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- **Extreme single-parameter sharing with dense entanglement**  
  • Gen 25 (“Single-Parameter RY-RZ CZ Entangled Ansatz”) uses one trainable θ reused for all pre- and post- rotations interleaved with a closed CZ ring plus antipodal CZ edges. Achieves combined score 0.80, perfect accuracy, zero generalization gap, n_params = 1, depth = 66, gates = 168, converging in 120 steps.  
  • Mirrors Gen 23 which also hits 0.80 with p_shared and a closed-ring CZ, underscoring that a single shared parameter plus a dense graph suffices for full expressivity.  
- **Axis-switch parameter reuse**  
  • Reusing “θ” for RY and RZ rotations (pre- and post-entanglement) doubles effective degrees of freedom at zero parameter cost—critical to reaching 100% accuracy with minimal parameters.  
- **Dense, parameter-free entangler compensates for minimal parameters**  
  • The combination of nearest-neighbor wrap-around CZ edges and antipodal CZ links delivers degree-3 connectivity without trainable two-qubit gates, preserving expressivity under extreme parameter economy.  
- **Valid spec formatting avoids silent failures**  
  • Consistent use of `"wire": int` for single-qubit and `"wires": [i,j]` for two-qubit specifications (as in Gen 25) prevents validation errors seen in earlier collapsed-param designs.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- **Over-deep, repeated shared-block designs**  
  • Gen 16 (“Double-Stacked Shared-Parameter Ansatz”) doubled the shared-block depth to 97 (gates = 228) but only achieved 0.65 score, showing diminishing returns from unmodified repetition.  
- **Over-parameterization without targeted topology**  
  • Gen 17 (“Global-Shared Ring Entangler Ansatz”) employed 20 unique parameters in a ring entangler but scored 0.68, underperforming the 2-param chain (Gen 14, score 0.72). Simply adding parameters doesn’t guarantee better expressivity.  
- **Under-parametrized designs without dense graph**  
  • Gen 22 (“Single-Parameter RY-CZ-RZ Ansatz”) collapsed to one global θ but relied on a basic ring; it scored 0.0 due to insufficient degrees of freedom and spec errors. Dense entanglement is necessary to rescue extreme sharing.  
- **Mis-specified gates blocking validation**  
  • Gen 21 (“Shared-Param CRZ Linear Ansatz”) and Gen 22 suffered from inconsistent use of `"wire"` vs. `"wires"` and invalid CRZ specifications, leading to zero validation passes despite intended parameter sharing.  

## Implementation Insights  
- **Loop-driven ANSATZ_SPEC for concise sharing**  
  • `for wire in range(N_QUBITS): ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"RY","wire":wire,"param":"theta"})` plus a similar loop for RZ reuses the same parameter name across all qubits and axes.  
- **Two-step entangler construction**  
  • Building nearest-neighbor wrap-around CZ edges in one loop and antipodal CZ links in a second loop gives each qubit degree 3 connectivity with zero extra parameters:  
    ```python
    for i in range(N): ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,(i+1)%N]})
    for i in range(N//2): ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,i+N//2]})
    ```  
- **Consistent gate-spec keys**  
  • Always use `"wire"` for single-qubit gates and `"wires"` for two-qubit gates. This consistency prevented the silent failures that plagued Gen 21/22.  
- **Axis-switch reuse amplifies expressivity**  
  • Assigning the same parameter to both RY and RZ doubles the non-commuting rotation effect without increasing parameter count, a pattern validated by both Gen 23 and Gen 25.  

## Performance Analysis  
- **Top score with minimal parameters**  
  • Gen 25 and Gen 23 both reach the maximum combined score of 0.80 using just one unique parameter (parameter_efficiency = 0.12). This outperforms all multi-parameter designs.  
- **Convergence trade-offs**  
  • Gen 25 halves the convergence steps (120 vs. 240) relative to Gen 23 by augmenting the entangler with antipodal edges (gates = 168 vs. 144), demonstrating that modest increases in gate count can accelerate training.  
- **Parameter count vs. topology**  
  • The 2-parameter chain (Gen 14) scores only 0.72 despite converging fastest (30 steps, gates = 138). In contrast, single-parameter dense designs score higher but require deeper circuits and more gates.  
- **Diminishing returns from depth alone**  
  • Beyond a depth of ~60–70, score improvements stall unless accompanied by topology enhancements (e.g., antipodal links). Deepening the shared-block (Gen 16) without new connectivity yielded lower scores.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Prepend a global RX layer reusing “theta.”  
   In the EVOLVE-BLOCK, insert before your RY loop:  
     for wire in range(N_QUBITS):  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"RX","wire":wire,"param":"theta"})  
   This implements a third‐axis rotation with zero extra parameters, echoing the RY→RZ axis-switch trick that doubled expressivity in Gen 23/25.  

2. Append a “skip-1” CZ ring for non-nearest‐neighbor connectivity.  
   After your existing CZ loops, add:  
     for i in range(N_QUBITS):  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,(i+2)%N_QUBITS]})  
   This twisted length-2 loop strengthens long-range entanglement at no parameter cost—mirroring how antipodal links accelerated convergence in Gen 25.  

3. Insert a shared-θ CRZ layer on antipodal pairs.  
   Right after your CZ blocks, add:  
     for i in range(N_QUBITS//2):  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i, i+N_QUBITS//2],"param":"theta"})  
   This injects a non-commuting two-qubit rotation with the same global parameter, probing whether parametric entanglers further boost expressivity without increasing n_params.  

4. Split “theta” into two block-shared parameters.  
   Replace your single-param loops with:  
     for wire in range(N_QUBITS):  
         block = wire//(N_QUBITS//2)  
         p = f"theta{block}"  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"RY","wire":wire,"param":p})  
     … (CZ layers) …  
     for wire in range(N_QUBITS):  
         block = wire//(N_QUBITS//2)  
         p = f"theta{block}"  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"RZ","wire":wire,"param":p})  
   This raises n_params from 1 to 2—still extreme sharing—testing whether a tiny extra degree of freedom captures any residual asymmetries.  

5. Alternate CZ and CNOT in the nearest-neighbor ring.  
   Change your first CZ loop to:  
     for i in range(N_QUBITS):  
         gate = "CNOT" if i%2==0 else "CZ"  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":gate, "wires":[i,(i+1)%N_QUBITS]})  
   By mixing gate types without new parameters, you perturb the entanglement pattern and may unlock faster convergence or higher robustness, building on the dense-graph principle.
