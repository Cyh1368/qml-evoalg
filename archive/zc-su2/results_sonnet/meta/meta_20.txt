# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Coarse-grained 2-parameter RY–CZ–RZ stack (Gen 14 “Coarse-Grained 2-Parameter RY-RZ Ansatz with CZ Chain”):  
  • Combined score 0.72 (highest), perfect train/val/test accuracy and zero generalization gap.  
  • Only two unique parameters (one per half of the register) reused across both RY and RZ layers.  
  • Fixed nearest-neighbor CZ chain (no wrap-around) yields rapid convergence (30 steps) with minimal depth (29) and gate count (138).

- Axis-switch parameter reuse:  
  • Sharing the same group parameters for both pre- and post-entanglement rotations preserves non-trivial expressivity without extra variables.  
  • Mirrors the Gen 7 insight that separate “ry_global” vs. “rz_global” drives entanglement under extreme sharing.

- Group-based parameter assignment:  
  • Partitioning qubits into equal-sized blocks (wire//4 → p_g0 or p_g1) balances expressivity and minimalism.  
  • Ensures each shared parameter controls multiple rotations, amplifying its effect across the circuit.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-collapsed single-parameter ansatz (Gen 22 “Single-Parameter RY-CZ-RZ Ansatz”):  
  • Score 0.0—complete failure.  
  • One global θ lacks sufficient degrees of freedom; inconsistent use of `"wire"` vs. `"wires"` in ANSATZ_SPEC triggered validation errors.

- Rigid single-layer shared-CRZ design (Gen 21 “Shared-Param CRZ Linear Ansatz”):  
  • Score 0.0—insufficient expressivity and mis-specified wires prevented any validation pass.

- Deep repetition of shared block (Gen 16 “Double-Stacked Shared-Parameter Ansatz”):  
  • Combined score 0.65 despite perfect accuracy.  
  • Doubling depth (97) and gate count (228) with no new unique parameters yields diminishing returns.

- Excessive unique parameters with ring entangler (Gen 17 “Global-Shared Ring Entangler Ansatz”):  
  • Combined score 0.68 with 20 parameters—outperformed by the 2-parameter chain design.  
  • Indicates that simply adding parameters (or switching to a ring) does not guarantee higher score.

## Implementation Insights

- Loop-driven ANSATZ_SPEC generation:  
  • `for wire in range(N_QUBITS): group = wire//4; ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"RY","wire":wire,"param":f"p_g{group}"})`  
  • Reuse of `f"p_g{group}"` across RY and RZ layers enforces global sharing with minimal code.

- Explicit chain-entangler enumeration avoids wrap-around:  
  • `for i in range(N_QUBITS-1): ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i, i+1]})`  
  • Simpler than coding a ring and trains faster (30 vs. 60 convergence steps).

- Consistent gate-spec keys:  
  • Always use `"wire": int` for single-qubit and `"wires": [i,j]` for two-qubit gates to pass validation.

- Parameter reuse across axes:  
  • Using identical parameter names for pre-entanglement RY and post-entanglement RZ doubles circuit expressivity at zero extra parameter cost.

## Performance Analysis

- Best vs. competitors:  
  • Gen 14 (2 unique params) → 0.72 combined score, conv = 30, depth = 29, gates = 138.  
  • Gen 17 (20 params, ring) → 0.68 score, conv = 60, depth = 60, gates = 144.  
  • Gen 16 (≈5 params, repeated block) → 0.65 score, conv ≈ 30, depth = 97, gates = 228.

- Parameter efficiency correlates with score:  
  • Gen 14: 0.07 > Gen 17: 0.05 > Gen 16: 0.03.  
  • Optimal unique-parameter window is extremely small (2), but >1 to avoid expressivity collapse.

- Depth/gate trade-off:  
  • Shallow, low-gate CZ chain supports faster convergence and higher score than deeper shared-block or ring designs.

- Convergence speed:  
  • Chain-based 2-param ansatz converges in half the steps of the ring-entangler design, underscoring the benefits of minimal, well-targeted topology under extreme parameter sharing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Increase group granularity from 2 halves to 4 blocks of 2 qubits each.  
   Replace `group = wire // 4` with `group = wire // 2` so that you now have p_g0…p_g3 controlling RY and RZ on each 2-qubit block. This follows the proven group-based assignment pattern, slightly boosting expressivity (4 params) while still sharing heavily across the circuit.  

2. Prepend a global RX layer reusing the existing p_g0 and p_g1 parameters.  
   Insert before your RY layer:  
     for wire in range(N_QUBITS): group = wire // 4  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"RX","wire":wire,"param":f"p_g{group}"})  
   This mirrors the RY→RZ sharing trick on a third axis at zero extra-param cost, testing whether tri-axis rotations further lower loss.  

3. Deepen entanglement by appending a reverse CZ chain.  
   After your forward nearest-neighbor CZs, add:  
     for i in reversed(range(1, N_QUBITS)):  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i, i-1]})  
   This “backward” CZ chain doubles the entangling layers with minimal depth penalty and no new parameters, probing if richer connectivity speeds convergence further.  

4. Introduce a single shared-param CRZ layer on disjoint pairs.  
   Following the CZ chain, add:  
     for i in range(0, N_QUBITS, 2):  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i, i+1],"param":"p_crz0"})  
   This adds just one extra parameter p_crz0 to tune two-qubit interaction strengths, leveraging ALLOWED_PARAM_TWO_QUBIT_GATES to inject controlled entanglement without blowing up the parameter count.  

5. Enrich non-local connectivity with a “skip-1” CZ ring.  
   Immediately after all existing CZ layers, append:  
     for i in range(0, N_QUBITS, 2):  
         ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ","wires":[i, (i+2)%N_QUBITS]})  
   With zero new parameters, this twisted ring of length-2 hops tests whether shallow, non-nearest-neighbor loops can boost expressivity and combined score beyond the simple chain.
