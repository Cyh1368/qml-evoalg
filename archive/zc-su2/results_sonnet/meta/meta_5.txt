# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parameter sharing on qubit pairs with a simple CZ chain (Paired RY-CZ-RZ Chain Ansatz, Gen 1) raised the combined score from 0.61→0.63 while cutting parameters from 146→50 and depth from 35→29.  
- Maintaining pre- and post-entanglement single-qubit rotations (shared RY before, shared RZ after CZ) preserved full (1.00) accuracy and zero generalization gap with minimal parameters.  
- A pure CZ entangling chain—without additional CRX/CRY gates—proved sufficient for expressivity: Gen 1 (CZ only) matched the 0.63 score of more complex Ring/CRX designs (Gen 2/4) but at lower depth and gate count.  
- Converging in 30 steps consistently across Gen 1–5 indicates that balanced parameter sharing and entanglement depth, rather than sheer parameter count, drive rapid optimization.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Full per-qubit parameterization (8-qubit layered RY-RZ CZ Ansatz, Gen 0) delivered 1.00 accuracy but scored only 0.61 and had the lowest parameter efficiency (0.01), evidencing overparameterization.  
- Shared-parameter Ring Ansatz (Gen 2) and CRX-augmented chain (Gen 4) reached the same 0.63 score but incurred high depths (66, 35) and gate counts (168, 186), degrading resource efficiency.  
- Removing pre-entanglement rotations without adjusting overall depth (Chain-CZ RY-RZ Ansatz, Gen 5) did not improve score beyond 0.62 and kept parameter efficiency low (0.01).  
- Any addition of multi-qubit parametric gates (CRX/CRY) or global shared corrections failed to boost the combined score beyond 0.63, despite increasing circuit complexity.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC with paired parameters (e.g., `"param": "ry_01"` applied on wires 0 and 1) directly halved the trainable variables while retaining full fidelity.  
- A linear CZ ladder (`{"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,i+1]}` for i=0…6) is a minimal entangling structure that supports perfect generalization when flanked by single-qubit rotations.  
- Structuring RY→CZ→RZ in one block ensures expressivity: the current best program’s EVOLVE-BLOCK implements exactly eight shared RY, seven CZ, and eight shared RZ gates per layer.  
- Passing a static ANSATZ_SPEC to the backend (`run_experiment(ANSATZ_SPEC,…)`) isolates circuit design from training logic, simplifying iterative ansatz tweaks.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Reducing parameters from 146 (Gen 0) to ~50 (Gen 1) raised the combined score from 0.61→0.63, showing strong correlation between leaner circuits and overall performance.  
- Depth cuts from 35 to 29 (Gen 0→Gen 1) maintained convergence at 30 steps and zero gap, indicating that shallow CZ chains suffice for 8-qubit tasks.  
- Gate counts below ~140 (Gen 1: 138 gates) proved optimal: deeper designs (Gen 2: 168 gates, Gen 4: 186 gates) matched but did not exceed 0.63, penalizing parameter efficiency.  
- All high-scoring programs (0.62–0.63) achieved 1.00 accuracy and zero gap, but only Gen 1 balanced these metrics with low resource usage, highlighting the efficiency-score plateau at 0.63.
