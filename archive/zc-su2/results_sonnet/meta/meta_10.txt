# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Pre- and post-entanglement single-qubit rotations with **shared parameters across qubit pairs** deliver perfect accuracy and zero generalization gap, as seen in  
  • Gen 1 (Pairwise RY-CZ-RZ Chain, 50 params → score 0.63, depth 29)  
  • Gen 7 (Seed program, 26 params → score 0.66, depth 29)  
- **Reusing the same parameter** for RY before and RZ after the CZ chain (axis-switching reuse) **halves the parameter count** and boosts the combined score from 0.63 (Gen 1) to 0.66 (Gen 7).  
- A **linear nearest-neighbor CZ chain**, sandwiched between shared RY and RZ layers, provides sufficient entangling power for 8 qubits, enabling convergence in ≈30 steps (Gen 1, Gen 7).  
- Keeping total trainable parameters **below ~30** via pairwise grouping and axis reuse correlates with highest parameter efficiency (0.04) and combined score (0.66 in Gen 7).

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Full per-qubit parameterization** (Gen 0: 146 params) delivered only a 0.61 score and poor parameter efficiency (0.01), demonstrating that overparameterization degrades performance.  
- Adding **multi-qubit parametric gates** or expanding to a ring entangler (Gen 8: 38 params, depth 66, score 0.64; Gen 9: 32 params, depth 49, score 0.65) increased circuit complexity without surpassing the 0.66 benchmark.  
- High-depth designs like the Shared-Parameter Ring Ansatz (Gen 2: depth 108) or CRX-augmented chains (Gen 4: depth 35, gate 186) plateaued at scores ≤0.63 while incurring large gate counts and depths.  
- Simply **removing pre-entanglement rotations** without reducing overall depth (Gen 5) stalled the score at 0.62, indicating layer removal alone fails unless coupled with structural simplification.

## Implementation Insights

- Defining a static ANSATZ_SPEC list of dictionaries (e.g. `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"p_g{k}"}`) makes **parameter grouping** and **axis-switch reuse** explicit and easy to audit.  
- Naming each pair’s shared parameter (`p_g0`…`p_g3`) and applying it to both wires—and to both RY and RZ—**cuts the parameter count in half** (26 vs. 52 distinct angles).  
- Explicitly listing the CZ chain (`{"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,i+1]}` for i=0…6) provides a **minimal entangling backbone** that supports full expressivity when flanked by rotations.  
- Packaging the pattern **RY→CZ→RZ** in one EVOLVE-BLOCK yields a **compact circuit** (depth 29, 138 gates) while preserving rapid convergence and top accuracy.

## Performance Analysis

- The combined score improved in stages: 0.61 (Gen 0) → 0.63 (Gen 1) upon pairwise sharing → 0.66 (Gen 7) with axis-switch reuse, illustrating that **progressive parameter economization** drives performance.  
- The highest score (0.66) coincides with the **lowest depth (29) and gate count (138)** among perfect-accuracy circuits, confirming that **shallowness plus sharing** trumps added complexity.  
- Ansätze with **26–50 parameters** achieved the best performance; those with >50 or heavy multi-qubit gates stagnated at ≤0.64, defining a clear **optimal parameter window**.  
- **Convergence in ≈30 steps** consistently across GEN 1 and GEN 7 indicates that once minimal entanglement structure and sharing are in place, **optimization speed** is largely unaffected by further parameter reductions.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Adopt coarser pre/post‐entanglement grouping by dividing the 8 qubits into three shared‐parameter blocks—e.g. wires [0,1,2], [3,4,5], [6,7]—each driven by its own “p_blk0…p_blk2” for RY and reused for RZ. This reduces independent parameters from 4 to 3 while preserving the proven RY→CZ→RZ structure, testing whether slightly larger group sizes maintain perfect accuracy and zero gap.  

2. Stack a second copy of the existing RY–CZ–RZ block immediately after the first but reuse all four “p_g0…p_g3” parameters across both layers. By deepening the circuit without increasing parameter count, you can probe whether added variational depth can accelerate convergence below ≈30 steps or even nudge the combined score above 0.66.  

3. Enhance the entangler with light parametric freedom by replacing every second CZ (e.g. links [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7]) with an XX rotation “XX” gate, all sharing a single “xx_ent” parameter. This injects tunable two‐qubit interaction strength at minimal extra cost (one new parameter) while keeping the backbone and overall depth nearly unchanged.  

4. Introduce two global single‐qubit rotations—one “RX” on all wires before the RY layer (param “rx_global”) and one “RZ” on all wires after the RZ layer (param “rz_global”). These two extra parameters model collective biases across qubits and may capture residual global phase patterns unaddressed by pairwise sharing, still keeping total parameters ≲6.  

5. Swap the linear CZ chain for a brickwork pattern: first apply CZ on disjoint pairs [(0,1),(2,3),(4,5),(6,7)], then CZ on the staggered neighbors [(1,2),(3,4),(5,6)]. Retain the four p_g parameters and the RY→RZ sandwich. This alternative connectivity explores a proven shallow topology that may boost expressivity without increasing depth beyond ~30.
