# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Tri-axis parameter reuse with a single “theta”: stacking RX→RY→RZ layers (as in the current best “Single-Parameter Dense-CZ Ansatz,” combined score 0.80) triples the effective rotation space per parameter, driving perfect accuracies and boosting expressivity over bi-axis reuse (Gen 25’s 2-parameter RY/RZ ansatz scored 0.72).  
- Degree-3 CZ connectivity via a two-step entangler: first a nearest-neighbor CZ ring, then antipodal (“diameter”) CZ links. Generation 28 (depth 72, 216 gates) converged in 60 steps to score 0.80, outperforming Gen 25’s ring-only (depth 66, 168 gates, 120 steps) and Gen 27’s dual-ring (depth 84–90, 216 gates, ~450 steps).  
- Extreme parameter economy paired with dense, parameter-free entanglers: reusing one parameter across all single-qubit rotations yields parameter_efficiency 0.12, demonstrating that minimal n_params combined with high-degree connectivity attains top performance.  
- Moderate depth/gate-count increase for accelerated convergence: adding the RZ layer and diameter CZ edges (+48 gates, +6 depth) more than halves convergence steps (60 vs. 120 in Gen 25), showing modest circuit expansion can drastically speed training.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Overaggressive parameter collapse without sufficient topology: Generations 31/32 (single-parameter ring ansatz) both scored 0.0, failing expressivity and validation due to sparse entanglers and spec errors (“wire” vs. “wires”).  
- Under-parametrized XX entanglers: Gen 19’s shared RY+XX scheme (20 params) only reached 0.68, indicating tunable XX alone can’t substitute for dense CZ connectivity when rotation parameters are limited.  
- Depth increase without connectivity enhancement: Gen 16’s double-stacked shared-θ blocks (depth 97) scored 0.65, and Gen 17’s 20-parameter ring entangler scored 0.68, confirming that simply deepening or adding parameters without richer entangling graphs yields diminishing returns.  
- Coarse two-parameter groupings (Gen 26) failed entirely (0.0), underscoring that too few distinct rotation angles cripples learning even if gate counts are moderate.

## Implementation Insights  
- Loop-driven tri-axis rotations:  
  ```python
  for wire in range(N_QUBITS):
      ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate": axis, "wire": wire, "param": "theta"})
  ```  
  repeated for RX, RY, RZ condenses three rotation axes into one parameter, maximizing expressivity per param.  
- Two-step CZ entangler assembly:  
  ```python
  for i in range(N):
      ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ", "wires":[i,(i+1)%N]})
  for i in range(N//2):
      ANSATZ_SPEC.append({"gate":"CZ", "wires":[i,i+N//2]})
  ```  
  yields degree-3 connectivity with zero extra parameters, a pattern central to the best‐performing Gen 28.  
- Consistent gate-spec keys: always use `"wire": int` for single-qubit and `"wires": [i,j]` for two-qubit gates to avoid silent validation failures (critical after Gen 31/32 errors).  
- Axis-switch across three non-commuting gates: extending shared-θ reuse beyond RY/RZ to RX→RY→RZ (in the seed program) unlocked the single-parameter performance ceiling at 0.80.

## Performance Analysis  
- Highest single-parameter ceiling (0.80) achieved only when combining tri-axis reuse with degree-3 CZ graphs (Gen 28). Any deviation—fewer axes or sparser entanglers—drops the score by ≥0.12.  
- Convergence speed correlates with modest gate additions: Gen 28 (216 gates) converges in 60 steps vs. Gen 25’s 168 gates/120 steps, showing a ~2× speedup for +48 gates.  
- Depth trade-offs: Gen 28’s depth 72 hits the sweet spot between Gen 25 (depth 66) and Gen 27/33 (depth 84–90); slightly deeper circuits with richer connectivity converge far faster.  
- Parameter count vs. topology: two-parameter designs (Gen 25) top out at 0.72, twenty-parameter or tunable-XX designs (Gen 19) at 0.68—only the minimal-param + max-connectivity pattern reaches 0.80 with zero generalization gap.
