# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Single‐Parameter, Tri‐Axis Rotation + Entangler (Current Best, Combined 0.80, 30 steps)  
  • Reuse one “theta” for RX→RY pre-entanglement and RZ post-entanglement layers on all 8 qubits.  
  • Insert alternating XX(θ)/CZ on the nearest-neighbor ring (no new params).  
  → Yields the 0.80 score ceiling with only 1 trainable parameter per block, 96 depth, 240 gates, and fastest convergence.  

- Alternating Fixed CZ & Parametrized XX Ring (Seed vs. Gen 62)  
  • Seed (Current Best): alternates XX(θ)/CZ → 30 steps.  
  • Gen 62 (“dual_stacked_theta_block_with_skip2”): same pattern but two stacked blocks → 120 steps.  
  → Single block of alternating XX/CZ with shared θ maximizes expressivity per parameter and minimizes training iterations.  

- Lightweight Skip-2 Sub-Ring + Antipodal CZ Shortcuts (Gen 59 & Seed)  
  • Sparse skip-2 CZ links on every other qubit (0-2,2-4,4-6,6-0) plus fixed antipodal CZs (i,i+4).  
  • Provides long-range connectivity with only 8 extra gates → accelerates mixing versus full skip-2 or parametrized CRY/CRZ variants.  

- Depth≈96 & Gate Count≈240 “Sweet Spot”  
  • Current Best: 96 depth/240 gates → 30 steps.  
  • Gen 59 (depth 72/216 gates) → 120 steps despite same score.  
  → Circuits deeper than ~114 gates or shallower than ~216 gates see diminishing returns in convergence speed.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Dual-Shared-Parameter Schemes (Gen 60 “split_theta_rotation_entangler”, Gen 63 “add_skip2_cry_theta_e”)  
  • Each used two unique θ’s (rotation vs. entangler) → combined score stagnated at 0.72 and convergence in 60–90 steps.  
  • Extra parameters increased circuit complexity without boosting accuracy or reducing steps.  

- Over-Stacked Blocks (Gen 62 “dual_stacked_theta_block_with_skip2”)  
  • Doubling block depth to 192 (480 gates) maintained perfect accuracy (1.00) but slowed convergence to 120 steps.  
  • Depth increase alone does not improve expressivity beyond the single-block sweet spot.  

- Parametrized Long-Range Entanglers (Gen 63’s CRY skip-2, Gen 65’s stacked CRX/CRZ)  
  • Introducing θ_e for skip-2 CRY or antipodal CRZ gates adds parameter overhead and depth (168–192) → only moderate convergence (~60–120 steps) vs. 30 steps.  
  • Fixed CZ shortcuts proved more effective per resource cost.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Loop-Driven, Single-Param Tri-Axis Rotations  
  for axis in [“RX”,”RY”,”RZ”]:  
    for wire in range(8):  
      ANSATZ_SPEC.append({“gate”:axis, “wire”:wire, “param”:“theta”})  
  • Maximizes expressivity with one parameter name; underpins all 0.80 scorers.  

- Alternating XX(θ)/CZ Nearest-Neighbor Pattern  
  for i in range(8):  
    if i%2==0: {“gate”:“XX”, “wires”:[i,(i+1)%8], “param”:“theta”}  
    else:    {“gate”:“CZ”, “wires”:[i,(i+1)%8]}  
  • Injects non-commuting entanglement without new parameter names—crucial to 30-step convergence.  

- Sparse Skip-2 & Antipodal CZ Connections  
  for i in range(0,8,2): ANSATZ_SPEC.append({“gate”:“CZ”,“wires”:[i,(i+2)%8]})  
  for i in range(4):     ANSATZ_SPEC.append({“gate”:“CZ”,“wires”:[i,i+4]})  
  • Provides global mixing in 8 gates vs. full skip-2’s 8+ antipodal links—key to shallow depth.  

- Consistent “wire” vs. “wires” Keys  
  • Single-qubit: “wire”; two-qubit: “wires”. Prevents silent spec validation failures (lesson from Gen 53).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score Ceiling at 0.80 Tied to One-Theta Designs  
  • Only Current Best (seed), Gen 62, Gen 65 reached 0.80; all others plateaued at 0.72 despite extra params.  

- Convergence Steps vs. Depth/Gate Count  
  • Current Best: 96 depth/240 gates → 30 steps.  
  • Gen 65: 168/456 → ~60 steps.  
  • Gen 62: 192/480 → 120 steps.  
  → Depth/gate bloat directly slows training iterations.  

- Parameter Efficiency Alone Is Insufficient  
  • Gen 62 & Gen 65 both have efficiency 0.12 but require 60–120 steps.  
  • Optimal designs balance efficiency with minimal depth (~96) to achieve fastest convergence.  

- Zero Generalization Gap Across Top Scorers

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace the fixed antipodal CZ shortcuts (block 2c) with parametrized CRZ gates that reuse the single “theta” parameter.  
   • In 2c, swap  
      {"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,i+4]}  
     for  
      {"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,i+4],"param":"theta"}  
   • This injects non-commuting long-range entanglement without adding new parameter names, leveraging the efficiency of the one-θ design while boosting expressivity per gate.

2. Convert the nearest-neighbor entangler layer (block 2a) to alternate between XX(θ) and YY(θ) instead of XX(θ)/CZ.  
   • For even i: {"gate":"XX","wires":[i,(i+1)%8],"param":"theta"}; for odd i: {"gate":"YY","wires":[i,(i+1)%8],"param":"theta"}.  
   • By using two non-commuting Ising interactions under the same parameter, you enrich mixing without increasing parameter count—extending the successful alternating-entangler motif.

3. Insert an intra-block RY(θ) rotation layer between the skip-2 CZ sub-ring (2b) and antipodal shortcuts (2c).  
   • After block 2b, append for wire in 0…7: {"gate":"RY","wire":wire,"param":"theta"}.  
   • Interleaving an extra rotation axis mid-entanglement heightens non-commutativity and has improved convergence in richer ansätze—all while preserving the one-θ economy.

4. Add a lightweight skip-3 CZ sub-ring alongside existing skip-2 links.  
   • After block 2b, for i in [0,2,4,6]: {"gate":"CZ","wires":[i,(i+3)%8]}.  
   • This zero-parameter, four-gate ring extends global connectivity in the same spirit as skip-2 and antipodal shortcuts, potentially accelerating mixing with minimal depth impact.

5. Apply a fractional block duplication on half the qubits to sharpen local correlations.  
   • Immediately after the post-entanglement RZ layer, reapply on wires 0–3: RX(θ)→RY(θ)→alternating XX(θ)/CZ (nearest neighbors among 0–3)→RZ(θ).  
   • Inspired by full-block stacking’s convergence gains—but trimmed to four qubits—this localized second pass targets under-mixed regions while keeping depth near the 96-gate sweet spot.
