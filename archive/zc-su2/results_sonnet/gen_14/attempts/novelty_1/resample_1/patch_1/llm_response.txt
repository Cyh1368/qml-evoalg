<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses 4 RY groups + 4 RZ groups + 1 shared CRZ ring = 9 unique parameters per repetition (56 total, since the backend applies the block 6 times plus 2 fixed extra params: 9*6+2=56). The earlier simpler design (RY groups reused as RZ groups via shared param names, nearest-neighbor CZ chain, no CRZ ring) used only 4 unique parameters per repetition and scored higher (0.66 vs 0.63), showing that trimming parameters and gates (dropping the wrap-around CZ and the CRZ ring) helps.

Building on that insight, I cut parameter count further: instead of 4 rotation groups, use only 2 groups (halves of the register), and reuse the same parameter name for both the pre- and post-entanglement rotation layers (RY then RZ), exactly as in the higher-scoring seed but with half as many groups. This yields only 2 unique parameters per repetition (n_params = 6*2+2 = 14, vs 26 or 56 previously), while keeping a full nearest-neighbor CZ entangling chain for expressivity. Since all prior variants already reach perfect train/val/test accuracy, further reducing parameter count while keeping entanglement structure intact should directly boost the parameter-economy-dominated combined score, and the shallower/simpler circuit (fewer unique params, same gate depth as the CZ chain) should also help convergence speed and generalization gap.
