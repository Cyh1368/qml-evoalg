Building on the successful trend of aggressive parameter sharing (146→98→50 params while keeping perfect train/val/test accuracy and increasing the combined score from 0.61→0.63), this version pushes parameter economy further by sharing a single parameter across BOTH the pre-entanglement RY rotation and the post-entanglement RZ rotation for each qubit-pair group. Instead of using 8 distinct parameters per block (4 for RY groups + 4 for RZ groups), we now use only 4 distinct parameters per block, with each parameter driving both the RY and RZ gates for its group. This halves the parameter count again relative to the current best-performing candidate while preserving the same circuit depth, gate count, and entangling structure (nearest-neighbor CZ chain), which are the primary drivers of expressivity and accuracy in this regime. Because the previous experiments show that this task is very easy to fit (accuracy 1.0 in all cases) and that the score is dominated by parameter economy, reducing to 4 params/block should further reduce n_params and improve parameter_efficiency and the combined score, while retaining enough structure (four independent groups, alternating RY/RZ and entangling gates) to keep the train/val/test accuracy at 1.0 and the train-test gap at 0.

Argumentation: the earlier ablation (from 8 to 4 unique params per block, going from 98 to 50 total params) already showed no accuracy loss and a score improvement. Extending the same sharing idea across gate types (RY and RZ using the identical parameter) is a natural and even more economical next step, since RY and RZ acting on the same wires before/after a fixed entangler still provide meaningfully different rotations (different axes) even when driven by the same scalar parameter, thus retaining nontrivial expressivity without doubling parameter count. This should further reduce n_params (roughly half of 50, so ~25) while depth and gate count remain identical to the current best candidate, which should improve the parameter_efficiency term and thus the combined score, provided the accuracy remains perfect (very likely, given the easy separability apparent from previous experiments with even simpler ansätze).
