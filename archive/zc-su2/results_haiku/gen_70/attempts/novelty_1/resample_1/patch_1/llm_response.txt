Looking at the performance metrics, I notice:

   - Two-block version: Same accuracy, 14 params, but doubled depth (125 vs 35) and much faster convergence (30 vs 90 steps)

   - Pure shared parameters across all qubits limits expressivity
   - No per-qubit or per-layer tuning capability
   - Convergence is bottlenecked despite having enough capacity

<DESCRIPTION>
This approach replaces global parameter sharing with a strategically sparse per-qubit/per-layer parameterization. Instead of one shared RY and one shared RZ across all 8 qubits (2 params), I'll use:
- Two layers: Layer 1 uses different RY/RZ for qubits [0,2,4,6] and another set for [1,3,5,7] (4 params total for RY and RZ)
- This allows the optimizer to differentiate between even/odd qubits without exploding parameter count
- The fixed CZ entanglement chain remains unchanged
- Layer structure: RY(qubit-specific) → RZ(qubit-specific) → CZ-chain → RZ(qubit-specific)

Why this should work better:
1. **Faster convergence**: Enables per-qubit gradient signals instead of averaging across all 8 qubits
2. **Better expressivity**: Even/odd qubit separation leverages the chain structure naturally
3. **Parameter efficiency**: Only 4 unique parameters (not 14), better than current
4. **Same accuracy**: The problem structure is still solvable with this richer but sparse parameterization
5. **Optimization landscape**: More informative gradients for the optimizer to follow

The key is that by separating even/odd qubits, we reduce the effective averaging that was slowing convergence, while still maintaining extreme parameter economy.
