# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Block replication with shared rotations  
  • The **Seed (Generation 48: triple_cz_sandwich_stack)** triples the same RY→RZ→CZ-chain→RZ block using only two parameters (`ry_shared`, `rz_shared`), yielding a combined score of **0.72**, perfect accuracies, and convergence in ~30 steps.  
- High gate-to-parameter ratio  
  • Seed’s **558 gates / 14 params ≈ 39.9 gates/param** correlates with the top score. In contrast, 20-param variants (Gen 51/54/57) plateau at 0.68 with ≈ 9–10 gates/param.  
- Linear CZ-chain entanglement  
  • Every top design (Seed 0.72; Gen 44–47 at 0.68) uses a nearest-neighbor CZ chain. Substituting parameterized entanglers (CRZ, CRX) or more complex topologies never exceeded 0.68.  
- Aggressive parameter tying  
  • Collapsing 48 single-qubit rotations into two shared parameters drives parameter efficiency to **0.07** (Seed) and perfect generalization. Designs with 20–26 parameters only achieve PE ≈ 0.04–0.05.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Single-block CZ variants plateau  
  • **Gen 44 (Shared-Param RX/RZ Chain)** and **Gen 47 (Global RX-CZ Chain)** each score **0.68** with only one CZ-sandwich block, demonstrating that without block repetition, expressivity caps out.  
- Over-under-parameterization  
  • The four-parameter two-layer CZ-chain (Gen 46) completely failed validation (score 0.00). Too few degrees of freedom cripples trainability.  
- Parameterized two-qubit gates alone  
  • **Hardware-Efficient RY-CRZ Ansatz (Gen 35)**, with 50 parameters and depth 42, only reached **0.63**, showing that replacing CZ with CRZ entanglers reduces expressivity at equal or greater parameter counts.  
- Excessive new parameters  
  • **Gen 53 (26 params)** achieves perfect accuracy but scores **0.66**, underperforming the 20-param and 14-param designs due to a lower gate-to-param ratio (≈ 11.8 gates/param).  

## Implementation Insights  
- Manual block copying enforces exact parameter reuse  
  • In the Seed’s `ANSATZ_SPEC`, the three identical blocks are copy-pasted with the same `"param": "ry_shared"` and `"param": "rz_shared"`, preventing accidental new-parameter introduction and doubling/tripling depth without cost.  
- Consistent schema keys avoid validation errors  
  • Uniform use of `"gate": "CZ"` with `"wires": [i, i+1]` and identical `"param"` names across every single-qubit rotation entry prevented the schema mismatches seen in failed/underperforming variants.  
- Balancing depth with noisy-hardware constraints  
  • The top Seed circuit has depth 95 and 558 gates; while deeper than 20-param designs (depth 35–65), it converges faster (30 vs. 60 steps) and scores higher—highlighting that depth trade-offs are justified when maximizing gates/parameter.  
- Delegating non-ansatz logic to fixed backends  
  • All high-performing programs (Seed, Gen 51/54/57) factor out encoding, measurement, and training into `_backend.run_experiment`, focusing solely on optimizing `ANSATZ_SPEC`.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score vs. gates/parameter correlation  
  • Seed (0.72) at **558/14 ≈ 39.9** gates/param vs. Gen 51/54/57 (0.68) at **186–372/20 ≈ 9–19** gates/param; Gen 53 (0.66) at **306/26 ≈ 11.8**. Higher gates/param directly yields higher combined score.  
- Convergence speed trade-off  
  • Despite its greater depth, the Seed converges in **30 steps**, half the ~60 steps of the 20-param designs, indicating that deeper, higher-expressivity circuits can train more quickly given sufficient gates/param.  
- Parameter efficiency vs. total params  
  • Best designs achieve PE ≈ 0.07 (Seed), whereas 20-param and 26-param circuits linger at PE ≈ 0.04–0.05, reaffirming that fewer shared parameters with more repeated structure maximize efficiency.  
- Generalization gap uniformly zero  
  • All successful designs (scores ≥ 0.66) report **0.00** generalization gap, confirming that parameter sharing and CZ-chain entanglement robustly prevent overfitting across architectures.
