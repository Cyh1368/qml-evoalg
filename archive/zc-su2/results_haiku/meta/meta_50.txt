# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Block replication with aggressive parameter sharing  
   • The top design (Shared-Parameter CZ-Chain Ansatz, Gen 49, score 0.72) triples an identical RY→RZ→CZ-chain→RZ block using only two shared parameters, yielding perfect accuracies and zero generalization gap in 30 steps.  
- High gate-to-parameter ratio  
   • The best circuit uses 558 gates over 14 parameters (~39.9 gates/param), outperforming all 20-param variants (372 gates, ~18.6 gates/param, score 0.68).  
- Nearest-neighbor CZ chains in every block  
   • Fixed CZ entanglers in Gen 49 outscore hybrids that introduce CRZ (e.g., Gen 55/56/58/59 all plateau at 0.68).  
- Minimal distinct parameters  
   • Collapsing all single-qubit rotations into two shared parameters (`ry_shared`, `rz_shared`) maximizes parameter efficiency (0.07) and drives rapid convergence.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Parameterized two-qubit gates instead of CZ  
   • Gen 55, Gen 56, Gen 59 introduce CRZ chains but only reach a combined score of 0.68 despite 20 parameters. CRZ entanglers did not boost expressivity beyond fixed CZ.  
- Increasing unique parameters without boosting gates  
   • All 20-parameter designs (Gen 55/56/58/59) achieve perfect accuracy yet plateau at score 0.68, indicating lower parameter efficiency (0.05) limits performance gains.  
- Single-block ansätze  
   • Architectures with only one CZ-sandwich block (e.g., earlier Gen 44/47) also cap at 0.68, showing block repetition is critical.  
- Over-parameterization  
   • Designs like Gen 53 (26 params) hit perfect accuracy but score only 0.66 (≈11.8 gates/param), confirming that more parameters without proportionally more gates degrades combined score.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Manual block copy-paste with shared param names  
   • The seed’s `ANSATZ_SPEC` repeats three identical blocks, each referencing `"param": "ry_shared"` and `"param": "rz_shared"`, preventing accidental parameter proliferation.  
- Consistent schema keys and wire indexing  
   • Uniform use of `"gate": "CZ"`, `"wires": [i, i+1]`, and identical param identifiers across all blocks avoids validation issues and simplifies backend parsing.  
- Focusing on ansatz only  
   • Delegating encoding, measurement, and optimization to `_backend.run_experiment` centralizes logic and isolates circuit design as the sole optimization target.  
- Depth-for-expressivity trade-off  
   • Embracing a deeper circuit (depth 95) with more gates per parameter yields faster convergence (30 steps) and higher combined score than shallower 20-param designs (depth 65, 60 steps).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gates/parameter strongly correlates with score  
   • Gen 49 at ~39.9 gates/param → score 0.72 vs. Gen 55/56/59 at ~18.6 gates/param → score 0.68.  
- Convergence speed vs. depth  
   • Despite greater depth (95 vs. 65), the three-block design converges in half the steps (30 vs. ~60), highlighting the benefits of higher expressivity per parameter.  
- Parameter efficiency drives generalization robustness  
   • Highest PE (0.07) in Gen 49 aligns with perfect generalization (0.00 gap) and top score, whereas lower PE (0.05) in 20-param variants plateaus performance.  
- Fixed CZ entanglers outperform hybrids  
   • All high-scoring programs use nearest-neighbor CZ chains; substituting any block’s CZ with CRZ did not increase combined score beyond 0.68.
