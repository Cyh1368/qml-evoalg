# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-block CZ-sandwich with global shared rotations  
  • Seed program (“two-block RY→RZ→CZ-chain→RZ”, 14 params, depth 65, 372 gates) achieves the top combined score of 0.72, zero generalization gap, and convergence in ~90 steps by repeating the same entangling block twice while reusing only “ry_shared” and “rz_shared.”  
- RX–RZ variant confirms CZ-chain primacy  
  • The Shared RX-RZ Ansatz (Generation 36) swaps RY for RX but retains the CZ-chain and two shared rotations, matching 0.72 score with only 138 gates (depth 29) and converging in ~120 steps. This shows that the CZ-chain topology plus block repetition, not the specific rotation axis, drives expressivity.  
- Aggressive global parameter sharing  
  • Collapsing all single-qubit rotations into two parameters (“ry_shared”/“rz_shared” or “rx_shared”/“rz_shared”) boosts parameter efficiency to ~0.07 and enforces tight regularization, yielding perfect accuracy and zero gap.  
- Depth-per-parameter drives expressivity  
  • Doubling the CZ-sandwich block at fixed 2 shared parameters yields +0.04 combined score over single-block variants (which plateau at ~0.68), underscoring that gate-depth per parameter is more critical than raw parameter count.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized local rotations  
  • The 194-parameter Ising-style ansatz scores only 0.61 despite perfect training accuracy; its low parameter efficiency (0.01) leads to overfitting and minimal generalization gain.  
- CRZ-only entanglers without block repetition  
  • The Shared-Parameter CRZ Chain Ansatz (Gen 39) converges fastest (~60 steps) but caps at 0.68 score, showing that substituting CZ→CRZ alone fails to surpass the CZ-chain benchmark without additional block depth.  
- Excessive under-parameterization  
  • Single-block designs collapsing to one “ry_shared”/“rz_shared” pair (Generation 41) lose expressivity entirely, scoring 0.00, demonstrating a lower bound on how much sharing is allowable.  
- Entangler/topology mis-specification  
  • Ring-CZ patterns or redundant RZ layering (Gen 43) and inconsistent schema keys (“wire” vs “wires” in Gen 42) produce validation failures or 0.00 scores, highlighting the need for both correct entanglement topology and strict adherence to the ANSATZ_SPEC schema.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit, repeated dictionary blocks enforce parameter tying  
  • Manually duplicating the ANSATZ_SPEC block (rather than programmatic loops) ensures each gate entry reuses the same param names (“ry_shared”, “rz_shared”) and avoids accidental key mismatches.  
- Linear nearest-neighbor CZ chain  
  • Listing CZ gates in ascending wire order ([0,1], [1,2], …, [6,7]) provides the most effective entangling topology for this data, outperforming ring or CRZ ladders in final score.  
- Parameter naming consistency is critical  
  • Uniform use of “param”: “ry_shared” for all RY gates and “rz_shared” for all RZ gates guarantees maximal parameter reuse; any deviation (as in Gen 42) breaks the schema and fails validation.  
- Block replication over parameter expansion  
  • Copy-pasting the same gate sequence twice in ANSATZ_SPEC doubles circuit depth without increasing parameter count, directly translating into improved expressivity and a higher combined score.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gate-to-parameter ratio predicts score  
  • Seed: 372 gates / 14 params ≈ 26.6 → PE 0.07 → score 0.72  
  • 20-param variants: ~192–372 gates / 20 params → PE 0.05 → score 0.68  
  • 194-param Ising: 194 gates / 194 params → PE 0.01 → score 0.61  
- Convergence vs. entangler type  
  • CZ-chain designs converge in ~90–120 steps for top score 0.72, whereas CRZ-chain designs converge faster (~60 steps) but settle at a lower score (0.68), indicating a trade-off between speed and ultimate expressivity.  
- Depth vs. convergence speed
