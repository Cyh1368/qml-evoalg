# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Heavy parameter sharing on single-qubit rotations combined with a fixed CZ chain yields the top combined score (0.68).  
  • Shared-RY with CZ Chain Ansatz (Gen 2, score 0.68, 20 params, 60 steps) and the current best (Gen 5, score 0.68, 20 params, 60 steps) both use one shared RY angle and shared pre/post RZ layers.  
- Pre- and post-entanglement RZ layers sandwiching a linear CZ entangler chain consistently deliver zero generalization gap and perfect accuracies (1.00/1.00/1.00).  
- A simple nearest-neighbor CZ connectivity is sufficient for expressivity; none of the top-scoring programs needed more complex entanglers (e.g., XX in Gen 3) to reach perfect accuracy.  

## Ineffective Approaches
- Fully parameterized per-wire rotations (146 params) in the 8-Qubit RY–RZ–CZ Linear Ansatz (Gen 0) achieved perfect accuracy but low parameter efficiency (0.01) and only a 0.61 combined score.  
- Introducing individually parameterized XX gates in Linear XX Entangled Rotation Ansatz (Gen 3) reduced depth/gate count (29/138) but still underperformed (score 0.63, efficiency 0.02), indicating gate-count savings alone don’t compensate for higher param count.  
- Alternating RY–RZ–CZ blocks with moderate sharing (38 params) in the 8-Qubit Alternating RY-RZ-CZ Ansatz (Gen 4) needed 90 steps to converge and scored 0.64—showing that partial sharing yields diminishing returns compared to full sharing.  

## Implementation Insights
- Defining multiple gates with the same `"param"` key in ANSATZ_SPEC (e.g. eight RY gates all using `"ry_shared"`) is an effective way to collapse 8 parameters into 1 shared trainable weight.  
- The canonical block structure—[shared-RY layer] → [shared-pre-RZ layer] → [CZ chain] → [shared-post-RZ layer]—is robust: it appears in both Gen 2 and Gen 5 and consistently converges in ~60 steps.  
- Retaining a fixed depth (35) and gate count (186) across shared-parameter variants isolates the benefit of parameter sharing, confirming that param reduction (not depth reduction) drives efficiency gains here.  

## Performance Analysis
- Shrinking parameters from 146 to 20 (Gen 0→Gen 2/5) raised the combined score from 0.61 to 0.68 and lifted parameter efficiency from 0.01 to 0.05, with no loss in accuracy or increase in generalization gap.  
- The best shared-parameter designs converge in 60 steps; unconstrained per-wire designs may converge faster (30 steps in Gen 0) but at the cost of worse overall score and poor parameter efficiency.  
- Gate-count/depth reductions without parameter sharing (Gen 3: 138 gates/29 depth) only achieved 0.63—highlighting that parameter economy via sharing is more impactful than modest circuit slimming.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Tie the pre- and post-entanglement RZ layers to a single shared parameter (“rz_shared”) instead of two (“rz_pre” and “rz_post”). This reduces the total trainable parameters from 3 to 2 (one for RY, one for RZ) and tests whether even tighter sharing preserves perfect accuracy and convergence in ~60 steps while boosting parameter efficiency.  

2. Replace each static CZ in the linear chain with a parametric CRZ gate that all share a single “phi_ent” parameter. This injects tunable entangling strength across the same nearest-neighbor topology without materially increasing parameter count, letting the optimizer fine-tune entanglement amplitude for potential faster convergence or lower loss.  

3. Stack two repeats of the canonical block—[shared-RY]→[shared-RZ]→[CZ chain]→[shared-RZ]—while sharing all parameters across both blocks. This doubles circuit depth to ~70 but holds n_params at 3, allowing you to probe whether increased expressivity via extra layers improves robustness or accelerates convergence beyond 60 steps.  

4. Swap the single shared-axis rotation from RY to RX (i.e. use “rx_shared” on all wires) while keeping the pre/post shared-RZ sandwich and CZ chain intact. This isolates axis choice as a variable, testing if an RX-based variant can match or exceed the 0.68 combined score and perfect generalization of the RY version.  

5. Augment the entangling topology by adding a CZ gate between qubits 7 and 0 to form a closed ring. Retain the existing shared-parameter layers; this slight increase in connectivity may reduce the required number of steps to convergence or improve loss metrics by providing an additional entangling pathway.
