# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating CZ‐chain direction across repeated shared‐parameter blocks:  
  • Generation 66 (“Alternating CZ-Chain Variational Circuit”) uses four RY→RZ→CZ-chain→RZ blocks with the 3rd block reversed ([7→6,…,1→0]) to reach a 0.72 combined score, perfect 1.00/1.00/1.00 accuracies, zero gap, 14 params, 744 gates, depth 185, converging in 30 steps.  
- Extreme parameter sharing of single‐qubit rotations:  
  • Gens 67 (“alternating_rx_ry_rz_czchain”), 73 (“reverse_cz_sweep_before_xx”), and 75 (“close_cz_chain_with_ring”) each collapse all RY and RZ gates into two shared parameters, achieving 0.68 combined score with 1.00 train/val/test, negligible loss (~0.02–0.03), zero generalization gap, 20 params.  
- High gates-per-parameter ratio as a lever for expressivity:  
  • Best performers maintain ≥27 gates/param (Gen 66 ≈53 g/p → 0.72; Gens 67/73/75 ≈37 g/p → 0.68), whereas designs below ~20 g/p cap out at ≤0.68.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-aggressive global sharing with insufficient entangler depth:  
  • Gens 77 (“reverse_forward_cz_zigzag”) and 79 (“bidirectional_cz_zigzag”) use 14 shared parameters over a single bidirectional or ring CZ layer (228 gates, depth 102) but stall at combined scores 0.39 and 0.38, with ~0.5 accuracies.  
- Under-parameterized topologies lacking block repetition:  
  • Single CZ ring or bidirectional chain without multiple alternating blocks fails to capture data structure despite low param count (feedback: “limits expressivity, stalled loss”).  
- Over-parameterization with low g/p:  
  • Per-qubit or per-block unique rotations (e.g., Gen 65’s 26 params/372 gates ≈14.3 g/p) hit perfect accuracies but score only 0.66, showing extra params hurt g/p and plateau performance.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Static ANSATZ_SPEC plus shared keys decouples structure from optimizer:  
  • All top circuits define ANSATZ_SPEC once (EVOLVE-BLOCK) and feed it to a common `_run` backend, enabling rapid swapping of entangling patterns.  
- Block templating with identical param names for all qubits and repeats:  
  • Repeating a single RY→RZ→CZ-chain→RZ template four times while referencing “ry_shared” and “rz_shared” yields only 14–20 unique parameters over hundreds of gates.  
- Alternation via reversed wire lists:  
  • Implementing the zigzag third block by reversing the CZ wire pairs (`[7,6],[6,5],…`) injects non-trivial entanglement without new parameters (used in Gen 66/67).  
- Loop or programmatic wire indexing standardizes entanglers and reduces copy-paste errors, critical when replicating deep chains.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Gates-per-parameter supersedes depth in driving score:  
  • Gen 66’s 744 gates/14 params (≈53 g/p) yields 0.72, whereas Gens 67/73/75 with ~37 g/p plateau at 0.68 despite similar depths (66–125).  
- Convergence speed remains fast when parameter-efficient:  
  • Both the deepest circuit (Gen 66, depth 185) and shallower versions (depth 102–125) converge in ~30–60 steps, showing that few parameters keep optimization efficient.  
- Parameter efficiency (~0.07) aligns with perfect generalization:  
  • All designs with 14–20 shared parameters across ≥558 gates achieve zero generalization gap and perfect test accuracy, confirming minimal distinct parameters drive robustness.
