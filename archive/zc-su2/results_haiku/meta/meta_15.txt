# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- CZ‐Sandwich with Global Sharing: Embedding a linear CZ ladder between minimal shared‐rotation layers yields best scores  
  • Shared-Parameter CZ Ladder Ansatz (Gen 17; seed program) uses one RX and two RZ shared layers sandwiching a 7-gate CZ chain → combined score 0.72, perfect 1.00/1.00/1.00 accuracy, zero gap with only 14 params.  
  • Builds on Gen 2/11’s pattern (20 params, score 0.68) by collapsing two RZ layers into one shared RZ, boosting score by 0.04.  
- Maximal Parameter Sharing Drives Efficiency: Collapsing per-qubit angles into a single global weight improves expressivity per parameter  
  • Gen 17 (14 params, param_efficiency 0.07) outperforms Gen 6 (Paired-Rotation CZ-Chain, 74 params, score 0.62) and Gen 9 (Shared XX Ansatz, 26 params, score 0.66).  
- Fixed Depth & Gate-Count Isolate Parameter Economy: All top CZ-sandwich designs hold depth = 35, gate_count = 186, confirming that reducing parameters—not circuit size—drives score gains.  
- Trade-off: Fewer params → Slower Convergence but Higher Final Score  
  • Gen 2/11 (20 params) converges in ~60 steps; Gen 17 (14 params) in ~90 steps, yet yields the highest combined score.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Under-Parameterized Rotations Collapse Expressivity  
  • Shared-parameter RX/RZ with Linear CZ Chain (Gen 16) used only 2 globals (rx_shared, rz_shared) → failed all tests, score 0.0.  
- Mixing Complex Entanglers without Sufficient Sharing  
  • Shared-Parameter 8-Qubit XX Ansatz (Gen 9) with 26 shared params + nearest-neighbor XX gates only reached 0.66, trailing CZ-sandwich by 0.06.  
- Parametric Two-Qubit Gates Alone Don’t Compensate for Rotation Diversity  
  • 8-Qubit Shared-Rotation CRZ Ansatz (Gen 18) with 4 shared rotation params + parametric CRZ entanglers scored 0.66, indicating sandwiching rotations around CZ yields stronger expressivity.  
- Partial Pairwise Sharing Suboptimal  
  • Paired-Rotation CZ-Chain Ansatz (Gen 6) with pairwise RY/RZ sharing (74 params) scored 0.62—showing full global sharing outperforms local pairing.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Global “param” Key Reuse:  
  • `{"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"rx_shared"}` for i=0…7 collapses eight RXs to one trainable weight; similarly for RZ.  
- Sandwich Block Template:  
  1. Shared RX layer  
  2. Shared RZ “pre” layer  
  3. CZ chain (`[0,1],[1,2],…[6,7]`)  
  4. Shared RZ “post” layer  
  This 4-step block at depth 35/gate_count 186 consistently achieves perfect generalization.  
- Fixed Circuit Budget: Holding depth and gate count constant across experiments isolates parameter count as the primary performance lever.  
- Convergence vs. Parameter Count: More aggressive sharing increases gradient steps needed but attains superior optima.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Parameter Count vs. Combined Score Trend:  
  • 146 params (Gen 0) → score 0.61  
  • 62 params (Gen 7) → 0.63  
  • 38 params (Gen 1/4) → ~0.64  
  • 20 params (Gen 2/11) → 0.68  
  • 14 params (Gen 17) → 0.72  
- Convergence Steps Grow as Params Shrink:  
  • 146 params in ~30 steps → 20 params in ~60 steps → 14 params in ~90 steps.  
- CZ‐Sandwich vs. XX/CRZ Entanglers: CZ-based sandwich designs outperform both nearest-neighbor XX (Gen 9) and CRZ entangler schemes (Gen 18) by ~0.06 combined score.  
- Zero Generalization Gap Across All Shared CZ Designs: Confirms that minimal shared rotations plus CZ ladder fully capture the task without overfitting.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the single “rz_shared” into two distinct parameters—“rz_pre” for the layer before the CZ chain and “rz_post” for the layer after it—while keeping “rx_shared” unchanged. This raises n_params to 3 but gives the optimizer independent control over pre- and post-entanglement phases, which could speed convergence and eke out additional score gains on top of the proven CZ-sandwich template.  

2. Double-stack the existing CZ-sandwich block end-to-end, reusing the same “rx_shared” and “rz_shared” keys across both repeats. This deepens the circuit to depth ≈70 and gate_count ≈372 without adding parameters, probing whether deeper expressivity (two sandwich blocks) can accelerate convergence or boost the final combined score beyond 0.72.  

3. Replace each CZ in the linear chain with a CRZ gate that all share a single new parameter “phi_shared.” This introduces just one extra weight (n_params → 3) but lets the optimizer tune entanglement strength directly, building on the ladder connectivity that has consistently outperformed other topologies.  

4. Close the CZ ladder into a ring by appending a CZ (or a CRZ with the same “phi_shared” if you’ve added it) between qubits [7,0] immediately after the linear chain. This minor connectivity tweak (depth → 36, gate_count → 187) preserves global RX/RZ sharing and may reduce gradient variance or convergence steps by enabling wrap-around entanglement.  

5. Swap out the shared-RX layer for a shared-RY layer—i.e. use `{"gate":"RY","wire":i,"param":"ry_shared"}` on all qubits—while leaving the CZ chain and “rz_shared” sandwich untouched. Keeping n_params=2 and depth=35, this axis variation tests whether Y-axis pre-rotations can match or exceed the expressivity-per-parameter ratio of the current RX/RZ pair.
