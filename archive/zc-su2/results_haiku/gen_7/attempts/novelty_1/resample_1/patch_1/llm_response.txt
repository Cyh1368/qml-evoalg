<DESCRIPTION>
The current implementation uses a linear nearest-neighbor entanglement topology with uniform parameter sharing across all qubits. This approach, while parameter-efficient, may be suboptimal for non-local correlations in the data.

I propose a fundamentally different algorithmic approach:
1. **Non-linear entanglement patterns**: Instead of linear CZ chains, use a mix of short-range and long-range entanglement with strategic qubit pairings (forming a more connected topology).
2. **Heterogeneous parameter sharing**: Rather than full global sharing, use selective sharing that groups qubits by their entanglement roles - some parameters are shared within local clusters while others remain independent for key qubits.
3. **Rotation axis diversity**: Mix RX and RY rotations (not just RY) to explore different manifolds of the Bloch sphere, reducing the effective expressivity bottleneck.
4. **Two-qubit parametrized gates**: Replace fixed CZ gates with parametrized XX/YY/ZZ Ising gates on critical entanglement points, allowing the circuit to adaptively learn the optimal coupling strength.

This approach maintains 20 parameters while increasing circuit expressivity through:
- Better connectivity (qubit 0 connects to 4, creating long-range links)
- Ising gates for adaptive entanglement strength
- Rotation axis variation to break symmetry
- Clustered parameter sharing that preserves local structure

Expected improvements:
- Faster convergence (fewer steps needed due to better expressivity per parameter)
- Potentially lower generalization gap through reduced overfitting to linear patterns
- Better parameter efficiency utilization through targeted expressivity
