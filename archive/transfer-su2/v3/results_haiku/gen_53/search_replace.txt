The recommendation to insert a mid-circuit parity-shared rotation layer is well-motivated:
1. **Parameter Economy**: Using only 2 distinct RY and 2 distinct RZ parameters for 8 qubits (instead of individual per-qubit) reduces the parameter count, which is a dominant scoring factor.
2. **Expressivity-per-parameter**: Placing this between entanglers and phase layers breaks the circuit into tightly-coupled blocks, improving expressivity without blowing up parameter count.
3. **Generalization**: Fewer parameters and tighter structure reduce train-test gap and overfitting.
4. **Hard Sample Separation**: The mid-layer can focus mid-computation on class-relevant correlations, improving worst-group margin.

I will:
- Replace the overly complex current ansatz with a simpler structure: rotation → NN entanglement → mid parity rotation → phase → cross coupling.
- Insert the mid parity layer with 2 parameters (ry_mid_even/odd, rz_mid_even/odd becomes 4 total, but shared).
- Actually simplify to just 2 distinct mid parameters by using even/odd parity.
- Remove the redundant CRZ cross layer and mid-range entanglement that inflated param count without benefit.
- Keep only the most effective layers: NN XX, YY cross, ZZ diagonal, and the new mid-rotation.

This should reduce N_PARAMS from 11 to ~8-9 while improving generalization and worst-group margin.
