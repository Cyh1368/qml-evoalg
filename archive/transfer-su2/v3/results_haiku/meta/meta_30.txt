# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity-based rotation sharing + mixed, multi-scale entanglers  
  • Gen 25 (Parameter-Shared 8-Qubit Ansatz Classifier) and Gen 26 (Parity-based 8-Qubit Quantum Classifier) both achieved the top combined score of 0.56 (train 1.00, val 0.93, test 0.87, gap 0.13) by tying RY/RZ angles across even/odd qubits and interleaving nearest-neighbor XX, cross-layer YY, diagonal ZZ, and ring-loop YY gates.  
- Ring-loop YY enhancement  
  • The explicit ring-loop YY block in Gen 25/26 boosted subgroup margins (worst-group margin 0.33) compared to designs lacking it (e.g., Gen 29’s 0.21 gap, margin ≈ 0.25), indicating non-local cycles improve class separability.  
- Decoupled two-parameter readout calibration  
  • All top performers (Gen 25/26 and earlier Gen 9) appended trainable gain/bias on averaged two-qubit correlators, stabilizing validation margins (Gen 25 validation_margin 0.54) and preventing readout-scale overfitting.  
- Moderate depth with entangler diversity  
  • Although Gen 25 uses depth 27/gate_count 90, its rich entangler mix offsets potential over-depth issues seen in earlier Gen 19–24. Diverse connectivity at each layer outperforms merely increasing depth with homogeneous gates.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-aggressive parameter tying → under-expressivity  
  • Gen 27 (simplified_selective_entanglement) with only nearest-neighbor XX + ring YY (8 total params) scored 0.34 (test 0.53, gap 0.28), showing that dropping cross-layer and diagonal couplings collapses expressivity.  
- Simplified entanglement without cross-layer links  
  • Gen 28 (simplify_ring_entanglement) retained ring-loop YY but removed diagonal ZZ and cross-layer XX/YY pairings, scoring 0.35 (test 0.49, gap 0.20). The absence of multi-scale bridges limited generalization.  
- Parameter splitting without parity structure  
  • Gen 29 (split_zz_diag_parameters) introduced separate ZZ parameters but kept only a single shared rotation layer, resulting in combined 0.42 (test 0.79, gap 0.21). Fine-grained parametrization fails if not anchored by structured rotation sharing and diverse entanglers.  
- Depth increase alone fails  
  • Earlier generations (Gens 15, 18) and even Gen 25’s shallow analogs (depth 18) confirm that simply adding layers without entangler heterogeneity does not close the generalization gap.

## Implementation Insights

- ANSATZ_SPEC + `validate_ansatz_spec` for robust specification  
  • Gen 25’s concise ANSATZ_SPEC listing 24 gate dicts, validated off-block, yields correct param ordering (8 distinct circuit keys), eases block reuse, and prevents specification bugs.  
- Key-to-position mapping for tied parameters  
  • The `key_to_position` lookup in `apply_ansatz_block` centralizes shared-parameter logic across 3 repeats, reducing code duplication and ensuring parity ties are enforced uniformly.  
- Separate circuit vs. readout parameters  
  • Slicing `params[:N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS]` vs. `params[-2:]` keeps circuit feature extraction distinct from gain/bias calibration, mirroring Gen 9 and Gen 25 best practices that improved margin stability.  
- Wide uniform initialization + Adam + early stopping  
  • All top runs (Gen 9, Gen 25/26) use uniform init in [–π,π], Adam lr 0.03, and early stopping (patience 75) on validation loss, avoiding barren-plateau stalls and over-training.

## Performance Analysis

- Best-of-run leap to 0.56 combined score  
  • Gen 25/26’s 0.56 surpasses the next best with mixed XX+YY+ZZ but no ring loops (e.g., Gen 19 at 0.46), confirming ring-loop entanglement’s additive effect (~+0.10).  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with structured tying  
  • Gen 25’s parameter_efficiency 0.09 (10 params) outperforms Gen 29’s 0.06 (13 params) and Gen 27’s 0.05 (8 params), showing that 8–12 well-chosen keys hit a sweet spot.  
- Entangler diversity narrows generalization gap  
  • Mixed XX/YY/ZZ + parity rotations in Gen 25 reduce gap to 0.13 vs. 0.21–0.28 in architectures missing one of these elements.  
- Depth trade-offs dependent on connectivity  
  • While previous shallow designs (depth 15–18) topped at ~0.54, Gen 25’s deeper 27-layer block leverages richer multi-scale links to edge to 0.56, illustrating that increased depth only helps when paired with varied entanglement patterns.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the “diag” ZZ parameter into two distinct keys—e.g. `"zz_04"` for the [0,4] coupling and `"zz_26"` for the [2,6] coupling—in your ANSATZ_SPEC. This preserves the parity-tied rotation layers and rich XX/YY/ZZ mix while giving the optimizer finer control over each long-range diagonal link, a minimal change (+1 param) that can boost test accuracy and subgroup margins without sacrificing parameter efficiency.  
2. Augment the ring-loop entanglement by adding a second “odd” cycle under a new key `"yy_loop_odd"` on wires [(1,3),(3,5),(5,7),(7,1)]. Mirroring the existing even-qubit YY loop, this yields balanced non-local cycles across both parity sublattices, further enhancing class separability as seen when ring-loops were introduced in Gen 25/26.  
3. Break the 3× repetition symmetry by defining three block variants A, B, and C that cyclically rotate only the two-qubit wiring (cross-layer YY and both ZZ couplings) by +1 qubit mod 8 in each variant. Then apply [A,B,C] instead of identical repeats; this introduces entangler-pattern diversity across layers—an evidence-backed regularizer that retains depth and param count.  
4. Introduce deterministic entangler “dropout” in `apply_ansatz_block`: use `skip = block_index % n_entanglers` to omit exactly one XX/YY/ZZ gate per repeat (cycling through the list of gates). This lightweight per-repeat variation regularizes the circuit structure in the spirit of Gen 9’s shallow-varied blocks and can sharpen worst-group margins without adding parameters.  
5. Replace the uniform mean over correlator expectations in `predict_batch` with a normalized weighted sum: allocate one trainable weight per `READOUT_PAIRS` entry, pass them through a softmax to enforce ∑w=1, then compute ∑ₖ wₖ·zₖ before applying gain and bias. This extends the proven decoupled readout calibration pattern by letting the model emphasize its most predictive correlators, tightening margins with only ~M extra parameters.
