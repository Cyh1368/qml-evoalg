# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity‐based rotation sharing with mixed entanglers  
  • Gen 9 (8-Qubit Parity-Sharing Ansatz Classifier, score 0.54) ties RY/RZ by even/odd parity (4 rotation keys) and layers nearest-neighbor XX, staggered YY, and long-range ZZ per block. This yielded perfect train (1.00), extremely high validation (0.96) and test accuracy (0.90), and a small gap (0.10) with only 12 distinct parameters and depth 15.  
- Shallow, repeated shared-block structure  
  • Applying the same 5-layer block 3 times (rather than deeper or distinct blocks) balanced expressivity and regularization. Gen 9 and Gen 7 (score 0.49) both used 3 repeats, achieving high train accuracy (1.00) and strong test performance (0.85–0.90).  
- Trainable gain/bias readout calibration  
  • All top performers (Gen 1, Gen 7, Gen 9) separated correlator measurement from readout calibration via two parameters (gain, bias), stabilizing margins (Gen 9 validation_margin 0.58, worst-group 0.31) without adding circuit complexity.  
- Wide uniform initialization + early stopping  
  • Full–π uniform init on circuit angles prevented identity dead zones. Coupled with Adam + early stopping, this initialization underpinned stable convergence across the best programs (e.g., Gen 9 converged by step 780).

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over‐tying parameters → under‐expressivity  
  • Gen 6 (Shared-parameter Ising ansatz classifier, score 0.42) tied global rotations and entanglers too aggressively (only 8 distinct params) and suffered low test acc (0.66) and a large gap (0.22).  
- Hierarchical parameter sharing without sufficient rotation variety  
  • Gen 8 (Hierarchical Parametric Evolved Ansatz, score 0.36) used just 9 parameters across layered entanglers but achieved only 0.58 test accuracy and low parameter efficiency (0.06), indicating expressivity bottlenecks.  
- Increasing depth without breaking symmetry fails to generalize  
  • Attempts like Gen 6 (depth 34) and Gen 8 (depth 21) stacked more gates but plateaued at or below 0.60 test accuracy, whereas shallower designs in Gen 7/9 outperformed them.  
- Sole reliance on linear CZ chains or global rotations  
  • Previous programs using only CZ chains (e.g., Gen 0) or purely global RY/RZ rotations (akin to “aggressive_param_sharing_ising_couplings”) underfit key state features and achieved test acc ≲ 0.60.

## Implementation Insights

- Leveraging `validate_ansatz_spec` + `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`  
  • The mapping of param names to indices in Gen 9’s block simplifies tying shared parameters across repeats and reduces boilerplate in `apply_ansatz_block`.  
- Direct use of `qml.IsingXX/YY/ZZ` gates  
  • Gen 9 employs 4 XX, 3 YY, and 2 ZZ per block, cutting gate count (~75 total) compared to decomposed variants (∼96–117 gates) while preserving connectivity.  
- Parity‐key abstraction for rotations  
  • Using “ry_even,” “ry_odd,” “rz_even,” “rz_odd” preserves minimal qubit heterogeneity. In Gen 9, this 4-key scheme maintained expressivity with only 8 rotation operations per block.  
- Modular separation of ansatz and readout  
  • All high-performing programs (Gen 1, Gen 7, Gen 9) kept the gain/bias readout outside the ansatz loop, allowing the circuit to focus solely on feature extraction and the readout layer to handle calibration.

## Performance Analysis

- Peak combined score with ∼12–14 parameters and shallow depth  
  • Gen 9’s score 0.54 (n_params 14, distinct 12, depth 15) outstrips Gen 7’s 0.49 (13 params, depth 15) and Gen 1’s test 0.85/val 0.89 (9 params, depth 18).  
- Generalization gap shrinks with mixed, sparse entanglers  
  • Transition from purely nearest-neighbor XX (Gen 1 gap 0.15) to adding staggered YY and diagonal ZZ (Gen 9 gap 0.10) tightened the gap by ~0.05.  
- Validation and worst-group margins track with entangling diversity  
  • Gen 9’s mixed entangler block delivered validation_margin 0.58 and worst_group_margin 0.31, a clear improvement over Gen 7 (validation_margin 0.49) and Gen 6 (0.30).  
- Parameter efficiency optimal around 12 distinct keys  
  • Efficiency (test_acc/ n_params) peaked near 0.06 in Gen 9; fewer keys (Gen 6, 8) under-expressed, while more keys (Gen 3, Gen 7) offered diminishing returns on gap reduction.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce a sparse CRZ layer immediately after the YY entanglement in each block by adding two CRZ gates—e.g. between qubits (1, 3) and (5, 7)—both sharing a new parameter key “crz_cross”. This builds on Gen 2’s success mixing phase‐type entanglers at minimal depth (+2 gates) and should inject long-range phase correlations without blowing the parameter budget.  

2. Break the strict 3-repeat symmetry by defining two block variants A and B that swap which diagonal pairs receive the ZZ coupling (e.g. A uses (0, 3)/(4, 7), B uses (1, 4)/(5, 0)), then apply them in an A–B–A sequence. This alternating pattern preserves ~12–14 total parameters but introduces heterogeneity across repeats, echoing the improved generalization seen in Gen 3 when symmetry was relaxed.  

3. Refine rotation sharing from parity to quartet groups: replace “ry_even/ry_odd” and “rz_even/rz_odd” with four distinct RY keys for qubit pairs [0,1], [2,3], [4,5], [6,7], and likewise four RZ keys. This controlled increase from 4 to 8 rotation parameters per layer injects extra heterogeneity exactly where Gen 9 showed expressivity gains, likely lifting test accuracy and worst-group margins without exceeding ~16 params per block.  

4. Reduce the four per-pair XX parameters to two by grouping edges: one key “xx_even” for (0-1, 4-5) and another “xx_odd” for (2-3, 6-7). This ties symmetric couplings—akin to Gen 6’s sharing but on a per-group basis to avoid under-expressivity—and frees up parameter budget that you can re-invest in new gate types (e.g. the CRZ or quartet rotations) while retaining nearest-neighbor entangling power.  

5. Append a sparse “star” IsingXX layer at the end of each block: connect qubit 0 to qubits 2, 4, 6 with three IsingXX gates under a single new key “xx_star”. This non-local XX entanglement complements the existing diagonal ZZ links, extends Gen 9’s mixed-entangler diversity, and should capture global correlations with only +1 parameter and +3 gates.
