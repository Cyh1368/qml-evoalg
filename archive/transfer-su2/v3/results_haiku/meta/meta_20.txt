# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity-key rotation sharing with mixed entanglers  
  • Generation 9 (score 0.54, test 0.90) ties RY/RZ by even/odd parity (4 rotation keys) and layers nearest-neighbor XX, staggered YY, and diagonal ZZ per 5-gate block, delivering a small generalization gap (0.10) and high margins (validation_margin 0.58).  
- Shallow, repeated 5-layer ansatz blocks  
  • Repeating a 5-gate block three times (depth 15, gate_count 75) in Gen 9 outperforms deeper variants (e.g., Gen 15/18 depth 24, scores 0.40/0.41), striking the best expressivity–regularization balance.  
- Decoupled two-parameter readout calibration  
  • A separate trainable gain/bias stage on averaged two-qubit correlators stabilizes classification margins without adding circuit complexity (used by Gen 1, 7, 9).  
- Wide uniform initialization + Adam + early stopping  
  • Initializing all angles uniformly in [–π,π], then training with Adam and restoring best-weights early stopping yields stable convergence (~780 steps for Gen 9) and avoids dead-zone artifacts.  

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-tying parameters leading to under-expressivity  
  • Gen 16 (10 params, score 0.33, test 0.48) and Gen 17 (8 params, score 0.40, test 0.72) share too aggressively, fitting training but failing to capture test complexity—margins collapse (~0.00).  
- Increasing depth without entangler diversity  
  • Gen 15 and Gen 18 insert extra layers (depth 24) but only reach combined 0.40–0.41, with generalization gaps ~0.23–0.24, showing depth growth alone cannot substitute for varied connectivity.  
- Sole reliance on global or homogeneous entanglers  
  • Gen 20’s heavy Ising-layer sharing (13 distinct params, score 0.30, gap 0.44) overfits despite compactness, indicating that uniform entanglement patterns under-sample critical state correlations.  

## Implementation Insights

- ANSATZ_SPEC abstraction with `validate_ansatz_spec` simplifies tied-parameter management  
  • Defining gates/params in a single list and extracting `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` enables concise, repeatable block definitions, as in the current best (Gen 9) and in all subsequent candidates.  
- Direct use of Pennylane’s IsingXX/YY/ZZ gates reduces decomposition overhead  
  • Gen 9’s blocks (4 XX, 3 YY, 2 ZZ) compile to 75 gates vs. 81–87 in deeper designs, contributing to lower circuit depth and faster training iterations.  
- Parity-key RY/RZ rotation sharing balances expressive power and parameter economy  
  • Four shared rotation keys drive all single-qubit layers in Gen 9, achieving high test accuracy (0.90) with only 12 distinct circuit parameters.  
- Clear circuit vs. readout parameter separation  
  • Storing circuit angles (`params[:N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS]`) separately from gain/bias (`params[-2:]`) keeps the feature-extraction vs. calibration stages modular, improving margin stability.  

## Performance Analysis

- Depth-versus-score trend: depth 15 > depth 18 > depth 21/24  
  • Gen 9 (depth 15, score 0.54) outperforms Gen 16 (depth 18, score 0.33), Gen 17/15 (depth 21/24, score 0.40), and Gen 18 (depth 24, score 0.41).  
- Entangler diversity narrows the generalization gap  
  • Mixed XX+YY+ZZ in Gen 9 yields gap 0.10 vs. XX-only or homogeneous patterns in Gen 16/20 with gaps 0.21–0.44.  
- Parameter efficiency sweet spot ~12 distinct keys (efficiency ~0.06)  
  • Gen 9’s 12 keys (14 total params) hit efficiency 0.06. Circuits under 9 keys (Gen 17) or over 15 keys (Gen 18) see no further gains and often overfit.  
- Validation and worst-group margins correlate with entangler richness  
  • Gen 9’s validation_margin 0.58 and worst_group_margin 0.31 exceed those of less diverse layouts (e.g., Gen 18 margins ~0.48), linking connectivity patterns to robust group-wise performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Separate the shared “zz_diag” parameter into two distinct keys—`zz_03` for wires [0,3] and `zz_47` for wires [4,7]. This adds only one extra distinct key (bringing the block to 13) and tests whether finer control over each diagonal coupling can boost test‐accuracy and margins without materially widening the generalization gap.  

2. After the existing ZZ layer in ANSATZ_SPEC, insert an IsingYY “loop” layer connecting (0,2), (2,4), (4,6), and (6,0) under a single new key `yy_loop`. This four‐gate cycle introduces non-local entanglement reminiscent of a small MPS ring and leverages entangler diversity—shown to narrow group-wise margins—at minimal depth cost.  

3. Replace the uniform average in `predict_batch` with a learnable weighted mean: add one trainable weight per readout pair (e.g. `w_pair_0,…,w_pair_{M–1}`) to `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK`, then normalize these weights to sum to one before taking the mean. This lightweight extension (≈8 extra parameters) lets the model emphasize its most predictive correlators, improving calibration efficiency and worst-group margins without touching the quantum circuit.  

4. Define three ANSATZ_SPEC block variants (A, B, C) each permuting only the diagonal ZZ and staggered YY pairs—for example:  
   • A: ZZ(0,3)/ZZ(4,7), YY(1,2)/(3,4)/(5,6)  
   • B: ZZ(1,4)/ZZ(5,0), YY(2,3)/(4,5)/(6,7)  
   • C: ZZ(2,5)/ZZ(6,1), YY(3,4)/(5,6)/(7,0)  
   Apply them in A→B→C order, tying all rotation and entangler keys across variants. Breaking the strict 3× repetition symmetry has empirically reduced overfitting and boosted generalization.  

5. Append a sparse CRZ layer after the YY stages with two long-range CRZ gates on non‐overlapping pairs—e.g. (1,5) and (3,7)—sharing a new key `crz_star`. Drawing on Gen 2’s success with non‐local phase entanglers, this +2 gates/+1 parameter injection enriches long-range phase correlations without exceeding the 12–14-key sweet spot.
