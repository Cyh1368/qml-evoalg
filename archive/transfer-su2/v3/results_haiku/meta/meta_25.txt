# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Parity-key rotation sharing + mixed entanglers  
  • Generation 9 (Combined 0.54, test 0.90) ties RY/RZ by even/odd parity (4 keys) and stacks nearest-neighbor XX, staggered YY, diagonal ZZ in a 5‐gate block repeated 3×. This delivers the top score and smallest generalization gap (0.10).  
- Shallow, repeated 5-operation blocks  
  • Gen 9’s depth 15/gate_count 75 outperforms deeper designs in Gens 19–24 (depth 18–27, scores 0.36–0.46), striking an optimal expressivity–regularization balance.  
- Decoupled two-parameter readout calibration  
  • Across top candidates (Gen 9, Gen 19, Gen 23), appending a trainable gain/bias on averaged correlators stabilizes classification margins (validation_margin 0.58 for Gen 9).  
- Wide uniform initialization + Adam + early stopping  
  • All best-performing runs (Gen 9’s convergence at ~780 steps) use uniform init in [–π,π], AdamOptimizer (lr=0.03), and validation‐loss early stopping (patience 75), avoiding barren-plateau dead zones.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-tying parameters → under-expressivity  
  • Gen 24 (13 params, score 0.36; gap 0.25) and Gen 22 (9 params, score 0.45; gap 0.20) share too aggressively, yielding high train accuracy (≥1.00) but weak test (≤0.85) and collapsed worst-group margins.  
- Increasing depth without entangler diversity  
  • Gens 15/18 (depth 21–24, score 0.40–0.41, gaps ~0.23–0.24) show that merely adding layers without varied connectivity fails to improve generalization.  
- Homogeneous or isolated entanglers  
  • Gen 21’s enhanced-diagonal-ZZ patch (13 params, depth 15, test 0.80) and Gen 20’s uniform Ising layers (score 0.30, gap 0.44) overfit subgroup patterns, underscoring the need for mixed XX+YY+ZZ connectivity.  

## Implementation Insights

- ANSATZ_SPEC + `validate_ansatz_spec` for concise, repeatable blocks  
  • Gen 9’s 5-gate block defined in a single list yields 12 distinct keys per block and reuses `apply_ansatz_block` across repeats, reducing code complexity and bug surface.  
- Direct use of Pennylane’s IsingXX/YY/ZZ  
  • Gen 9 compiles to 75 gates vs. 81–90 in most Gen 19–24 variants, accelerating training per epoch.  
- Parity-key sharing balances economy and expressivity  
  • Even/odd keys for RY/RZ in Gen 9 (4 unique keys) hit a sweet spot: high test 0.90 with only 14 total parameters (12 distinct) and parameter_efficiency 0.06.  
- Separation of circuit vs. readout params  
  • Storing circuit angles (`params[:N_CIRCUIT_PARAMS]`) separately from gain/bias (`params[-2:]`) keeps feature extraction modular, as seen in Gen 9 and Gen 23, improving margin stability.

## Performance Analysis

- Depth-versus-score trend holds across new candidates  
  • Depth 15 (Gen 9 score 0.54) > depth 18 (Gen 22 score 0.45) > depth 21–27 (Gen 19/23 scores 0.46/0.44) confirms shallow repeats outperform deeper circuits lacking entangler variety.  
- Entangler diversity narrows generalization gap  
  • Mixed XX+YY+ZZ in Gen 9 yields gap 0.10 vs. Gen 19’s sparse XX+CRZ pattern (gap 0.15, score 0.46) and Gen 24’s hybrid sparse (gap 0.25, score 0.36).  
- Parameter efficiency sweet spot ~12 distinct keys  
  • Gen 9’s 12 keys (parameter_efficiency 0.06) outpaces Gen 21’s 7 keys (0.06 efficiency but test 0.80) and Gen 18’s >15 keys (0.05 efficiency, test 0.72), indicating diminishing returns outside 9–15 keys.  
- Margins correlate with mixed entanglers  
  • Gen 9’s worst_group_margin 0.31 exceeds Gen 22/24’s ~0.00–0.10, linking richer connectivity to robust subgroup performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the shared “zz_diag” into two distinct parameters—e.g. `"zz_03"` for wires [0,3] and `"zz_47"` for wires [4,7]—bringing the block’s unique key count from 12 to 13. This minimal change preserves the shallow-repeat structure and mixed-entangler diversity of Gen 9 while testing whether finer control over each diagonal coupling can boost test accuracy and worst-group margins.  

2. After the existing ZZ gates in `ANSATZ_SPEC`, insert an IsingYY “loop” layer on non-neighbor pairs [(0,2),(2,4),(4,6),(6,0)] under a single new key `"yy_loop"`. This +4-gate/+1-parameter addition enriches long-range entanglement in the same 5-gate block footprint, leveraging the proven margin and gap improvements from mixed XX+YY+ZZ connectivity without increasing circuit depth beyond 17.  

3. Replace the uniform mean in `predict_batch` with a learnable, normalized weighted sum: add one trainable weight per correlator pair (e.g. keys `"w_pair_0"`…`"w_pair_{M-1}"`), normalize them to sum to one, then compute the weighted average before applying gain and bias. This extends the decoupled readout calibration pattern with only ~8 extra parameters, enabling the model to emphasize its most predictive correlators and tighten worst-group margins without touching the quantum circuit.  

4. Break the strict 3× repetition symmetry by defining three block variants A, B, and C that cyclically permute only the YY and ZZ wiring—for example, shift each diagonal ZZ pair by +1 wire modulo 8 and similarly stagger YY pairs—while keeping all rotation keys tied. Then apply the sequence [A, B, C] instead of repeating the identical block; this introduces entangler-pattern diversity across repeats, empirically shown to reduce overfitting in other Gen variants without altering depth or total parameter count.  

5. Introduce deterministic entangler “dropout” across repeats: in `apply_ansatz_block`, skip exactly one two-qubit gate per repeat by using `block_index % n_entanglers` to omit the corresponding gate from the XX/YY/ZZ layers. This lightweight per-repeat variation generates three slightly different entangling structures, further regularizing the circuit and tapping into the same expressivity–regularization sweet spot that shallow, varied blocks like Gen 9 exploit.
