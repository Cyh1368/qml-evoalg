# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Two-Parameter Multi-Shell Shared-Entangler (Current Best, Gen 54): achieved combined 0.61 (train 1.00, val 0.90, test 0.85) with only θ_ry and θ_z driving three CRZ shells at distances {1,2,4}, depth 45/gate 108. Precise shell selection plus exact parameter-tying across N_REPEATS=3 maximized expressivity per parameter.  
- Non-Commuting Generator Omitted: unlike Mixed-Axis Shared-Angle (Gen 64, combined 0.58, test 0.85) which added CRY at zero extra parameters, the pure CRZ backbone in the best design outperformed by focusing learning capacity on phase correlations.  
- Exact Parameter Sharing Across Repeats: all top performers (Gen 50, 54, current best) reuse the same ANSATZ_SPEC param keys in each of the three blocks, yielding perfect train convergence in ~1 440 steps and small generalization gaps (~0.15).  
- Constrained Shell Selection {1,2,4}: omitting distance-3 and extra mixers (as confirmed when comparing to two-shell variants and Gen 63’s 0.57 score) preserved circuit depth at the 45/gate 108 “sweet spot” and delivered the highest combined score.

Ineffective Approaches  
- Single-Parameter Tying (Gen 58, combined 0.46, test 0.53): collapsing θ_ry and θ_z into one “theta” produced stable but underexpressive circuits, stuck near random accuracy despite depth 51/gate 156.  
- Cross-Axis Entanglers Without New Parameters (Gen 51): mixing CRY/CRX under existing θ ties led to overfitting (train 0.88) and poor test (0.48, combined 0.35), showing that adding generator axes without augmenting the parameter set can break robust correlation learning.  
- Extra Final Mixer in Two-Shell (Gen 54 two-shell + reused-RY mixer): inserted zero-cost RY after entanglers collapsed train accuracy to 0.75 and failed to improve validation, indicating that mixers must align with entangler topology.  
- Dense or Sparse Shell Variants: both sparser (2-shell) and denser (4-shell or including distance 3) designs underperformed compared to {1,2,4} selection—denser designs drove depth up (e.g. Gen 52 with depth 48/gate 132) but only reached combined 0.57.

Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec: centralizing gate definitions in a list of dicts catches mis-indexed wires and unsupported gates, while building key_to_position ensures precise parameter tying (as in the current best).  
- Wide Uniform Initialization & Decoupled Readout Calibration: initializing θ ∈ [−π,π] with gain=1, bias=0 consistently avoids optimizer dead zones and isolates circuit training from readout scaling across all top candidates.  
- Profiling via AnnotatedQueue & tape.graph.get_depth(): confirming the best circuit at depth 45/gate 108 guided the exclusion of distance-3 shells and extra mixers, directly correlating implementation pruning with performance gains.  
- CRZ-Only Entanglement Backbone: reusing θ_z for both RZ and CRZ gates concentrates learning on phase correlations; the current best’s pure CRZ multi-shell backbone outperformed mixed-axis variants despite fewer generator types.

Performance Analysis  
- Combined Score vs. Parameter Count: the best two-parameter design (plus 2 readout) achieved 0.61, surpassing four-parameter variants (Gen 62 & Gen 64 at 0.58) and single-parameter (∼0.40–0.46), demonstrating a parametric floor at two distinct circuit angles.  
- Depth/Gate Count “Sweet Spot”: depth 45/gate 108 (current best) vs. depth 48/gate 132 (Gen 52/Gen 64) yields +0.04–0.06 improvement in combined score, confirming that targeted connectivity trumps raw circuit depth.  
- Generalization Gap Trends: two-parameter multi-shell yields gap 0.15 (test 0.85), four-param variants gap 0.15–0.17 (test 0.83–0.85), while single-param gaps are low only because test accuracy itself is near random.  
- Convergence Efficiency: top designs reach validation ≥0.90 in ~1 440 steps; less effective circuits either never hit this threshold or require more epochs without surpassing the best test accuracy of 0.85.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce static per-shell scaling factors on the shared “theta_z”.  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC (or in apply_ansatz_block), wrap each CRZ call so that the angle is multiplied by a hard-coded multiplier—e.g. 1.0 for the distance-1 shell, 0.8 for distance-2, and 1.2 for distance-4. This preserves the two-parameter budget and builds on the current best’s proven benefit of soft symmetry breaking, enriching expressivity without adding trainables.

2. Permute the entangling-shell order across repeats.  
   Instead of applying shells in the fixed sequence [1,2,4] for every of the three repeats, use block_index to cycle through three hard-coded orderings (e.g. repeat 0: 1→2→4, repeat 1: 2→4→1, repeat 2: 4→1→2). This interleaves distinct pathways under identical parameters, leveraging the exact-sharing pattern that converges rapidly while improving gradient mixing.

3. Replace the distance-2 CRZ shell with parameter-shared IsingZZ gates.  
   In ANSATZ_SPEC, change the skip-ring shell entries from {"gate":"CRZ","param":"theta_z"} to {"gate":"ZZ","param":"theta_z"} (qml.IsingZZ), keeping the same wire pairs. This tests whether an Ising coupling concentrates amplitude correlations more effectively than conditional phase, all while holding the total circuit-parameter count fixed.

4. Split the single global RY mixer into two interleaved layers.  
   Amend ANSATZ_SPEC to apply RY(theta_ry) on every qubit before shell 1 and then again between shells 2 and 3 (i.e. two identical RY loops using the same “theta_ry”). Distributing the mixer may improve information flow and alleviate barren plateaus, yet retains the proven two-parameter economy that yields fast convergence.

5. Inject a per-repeat sign flip on “theta_z” across the three tied blocks.  
   In apply_ansatz_block, after looking up theta_z, multiply it by (−1)**repeat_index before every CRZ/ZZ application. This zero-cost heterogeneity breaks perfect symmetry across repeats—building on the exact-sharing paradigm that reliably generalizes—while introducing richer layer-wise phase patterns.
