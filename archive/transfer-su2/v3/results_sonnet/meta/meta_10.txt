# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Mixed local and global entanglers with translation-invariant rotations (Gen 3 “Translation-Invariant 8-Qubit Quantum Classifier”): combining even/odd shared RY, an 8-qubit ZZ ring, antipodal CRZ, and a global RZ layer repeated three times produced the jump from a 0.38 combined score (Gen 8/9) to 0.52, with train/val/test = 1.00/0.88/0.84.  
- Parameter tying across repeats and wires: reusing the same 5 distinct circuit parameters over N_REPEATS = 3 (total n_params = 7 including gain and bias) hit the sweet spot—generalization gap 0.16 (Gen 3) vs 0.18 (Gen 8) or 0.32 (Gen 10).  
- Moderate depth (~33) and balanced gate count (84): matching Gen 3’s depth of 33 outperformed deeper symmetric circuits (Gen 9 depth 54, combined 0.38) and shallower chains (Gen 0 depth 20, combined 0.29).  
- Calibrated two-qubit correlator readout with trainable gain/bias: consistent across top performers, but in Gen 3 it yielded the highest validation margin (0.54) and worst-group margin (0.34).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-simplified entangler patterns or too few parameters: Gen 8 (“8-Qubit Shared-Parameter Classifier”) and Gen 9 (“Symmetric Ring Ansatz”) tied only 3–4 ansatz parameters, which limited expressivity and capped combined scores at 0.38 (test ≈ 0.51).  
- Excessive symmetry without long-range coupling: Gen 10 used only translation-invariant RY, global ZZ/CRZ plus shared RZ (n_params = 5), leading to underfitting (generalization gap 0.32, combined 0.35).  
- Implementation errors blocking execution: Gen 7 failed entirely (0.0 score) due to a missing `import itertools`, underscoring the importance of early spec validation.

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC plus validate_ansatz_spec: Gen 3’s explicit gate dictionaries enabled automatic consistency checks and early failure on spec errors, preventing wasted compute on invalid candidates.  
- apply_ansatz_block abstraction with key-to-index mapping: concise loop-based gate application allowed mixing RX/RY/RZ, IsingZZ, CRZ, etc., in a uniform pattern—critical for the Gen 3 design’s flexibility.  
- Shared-parameter blocks across repeats: tying parameters not only reduced the optimization dimension (5 vs. 8+) but also stabilized training, as seen by zero std in val/test accuracies for Gen 3.  
- AnnotatedQueue-based metadata extraction: using `qml.tape.QuantumScript.from_queue` to report depth and gate count (33, 84 in Gen 3) made it easy to correlate circuit complexity with performance.

## Performance Analysis  
- Distinct parameter count vs. combined score: circuits with ~5 unique ansatz parameters (Gen 3) reached 0.52, whereas those with ≤3 (Gen 8/9) plateaued at ~0.38 and those with ≥8 (Gen 0) overfit with a combined ~0.29.  
- Test accuracy uplift from long-range entanglers: adding antipodal CRZ to a ring-only design lifted Gen 3 test_acc to 0.84 vs. Gen 9’s 0.51 despite identical parameter count (7).  
- Generalization gap minimized at mid-range expressivity: Gen 3’s gap of 0.16 beat both under-expressive (Gen 8 gap 0.18) and overly simple translation designs (Gen 10 gap 0.32), confirming the optimal parameter-to-depth ratio.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with performance: Gen 3 achieved 0.12 test_accuracy/param versus 0.065–0.10 in less effective designs, indicating that each added circuit parameter contributed more to predictive power in the mixed entangler, translation-invariant architecture.
