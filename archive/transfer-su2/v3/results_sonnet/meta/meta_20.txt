# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Multi-scale shared entanglers with a single trainable angle  
  • The current best program (combined 0.60; train 1.00/val 0.88/test 0.85, gap 0.15) reuses one “θ_ent” across three distance shells (1, 2, 4) and attains peak test accuracy with only three distinct circuit parameters.  
  • The Distance-Based Fully-Connected Classifier (Gen 12, combined 0.52; test 0.84, gap 0.16) first demonstrated that grouping ZZ/CRZ couplings by distance lifts held-out accuracy from ~0.50 to ~0.85 with only five distinct parameters and zero seed-to-seed variance.  

- Extreme parameter tying across repeats and wires  
  • Tying the same two rotation angles (θ_ry, θ_rz) and one entangler angle over N_REPEATS = 3 (total N_params = 5) yields perfect training (1.00) and highly stable validation/test (0.88/0.85, std 0.00). This pushed the combined score from 0.52 (Gen 12) to 0.60 in the best program.

- Minimal single-qubit rotation family  
  • Using only global RY and RZ gates (no per-qubit parameters) suffices for full Bloch-sphere coverage when interleaved with multi-scale entanglers. Both the best program (depth 45/108 gates) and Gen 12 (depth 54/132 gates) achieve high expressivity with just 2 shared rotation parameters.

- Calibrated two-qubit correlator readout  
  • Averaging fixed dataset-defined Hermitian readouts followed by a trainable gain and bias stabilizes margins (validation_margin ≈ 0.57) and prevents trivial gain inflation. This pattern is common to Gen 6 (0.60 combined) and the current best (0.60).


## Ineffective Approaches

- Translation invariance with limited couplings  
  • The Translation-Invariant Ansatz (Gen 15, combined 0.39; test 0.67, gap 0.27) enforces shared RY on even/odd qubits and ring/antipodal entanglers but omits skip-distance links. Parameter tying reduced overfitting but severely curtailed expressivity.

- Extreme over-sharing without multi-scale links  
  • The Global Shared Rotations + full ZZ entangler (Gen 17, combined 0.41; test 0.50) ties two single-qubit angles and one global IsingZZ, yet underfits (train 0.56) due to lack of coupling diversity.  

- Single-shell shared-entangler underfit  
  • Both the Shared-Entangler Classifier (Gen 18, combined 0.10 param_eff; train/val/test 0.50) and the Multi-Axis Shared-Entangler (Gen 19, combined 0.34; test 0.48, gap 0.34) reuse one entangler across only nearest-neighbor or ring‐plus-diversified gates. Their minimal parameter count failed to capture dataset structure, yielding chance-level accuracy.

- Over-capacity without long-range specificity  
  • The Parity-Symmetric Classifier (Gen 11, combined 0.51; test 0.86) added even/odd rotations (7 params) but missed long-range dilution from multi-scale sharing, capping further gains.  


## Implementation Insights

- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + automated validation  
  • All high-performing candidates use a declarative `ANSATZ_SPEC` list of gate dicts coupled with `validate_ansatz_spec`, catching mis-indexed wires or unsupported gates early and saving computation.  

- Unified `apply_ansatz_block` abstraction  
  • Mapping parameter names to indices via `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` and a single loop over `ANSATZ_SPEC` enables rapid insertion/removal of shells. The best program’s ease of adding distance-2 and distance-4 CRZ links directly translated into its superior performance.

- Consistent depth/gate counting via AnnotatedQueue  
  • Extracting circuit depth = 45 and gate_count = 108 via `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` correlated complexity with performance sweet spots (≈ 45 layers). Both Gen 19 (45 depth) and the best program hit this target, but only the multi-scale design realized its potential.

- Wide uniform initialization in [–π, π]  
  • Initializing all angles across the full period (init_scale = π) avoids dead-zone artifacts. This practice produced zero validation/test std across seeds in Gen 12 and the current best, improving reproducibility over near-zero starts.


## Performance Analysis

- Parameter count vs. combined score  
  • Circuits with ~3 distinct ansatz parameters (current best) achieve combined 0.60, outpacing designs with 5–7 parameters: Gen 12 (7 params, combined 0.52) and Gen 11 (7 params, 0.51). A lean parameter set focused on multi-scale entanglement yields higher per-parameter utility.

- Gate depth sweet spot around 45  
  • Shallower circuits (< 45, Gen 19 depth 45 but single-shell) underfit; deeper ones (> 50, Gen 12 depth 54, Gen 17 depth 54) fail to improve beyond combined ≈ 0.52. The best program balances expressivity and trainability at depth 45.

- Long-range couplings boost test accuracy by ~0.15  
  • Adding antipodal (distance 4) links (Gen 12 vs. ring-only Gen 9) lifted test accuracy from ~0.70 to ~0.84. The current best consolidated this gain into a single θ_ent across three shells.

- Parameter efficiency correlates with generalization  
  • The best model’s parameter_efficiency = 0.17 (test 0.85/5 params) and gap 0.15 surpass Gen 12’s efficiency (~0.12) and gap 0.16, underscoring that multi-scale sharing yields both higher accuracy and tighter train/test alignment.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce mixed‐axis entanglement by appending a CRX gate on each existing shell pair, tied to the same “theta_ent” as the CRZ links.  This keeps the 3-param multi-scale sharing but injects X–Z correlations alongside Z–Z, potentially enriching expressivity without adding parameters, leveraging the success of single-θ_ent sharing across shells.  

2. Relax the extreme repeat‐tying by giving each block its own entangler angle: replace the single “theta_ent” with three tied “theta_ent_0”, “theta_ent_1”, “theta_ent_2”, one per repeat, still shared across distances 1,2,4 in that block.  This adds only two extra parameters but tests whether per-repeat phase shifts improve sequential correlation building, building on the best program’s block-repetition motif.  

3. Add a concluding global RY layer: after the third antipodal CRZ shell insert an RY on every qubit with a new shared parameter “theta_ry_out”.  This single extra rotation stage can perform a final mixer-style adjustment on all Bloch vectors before measurement, akin to QAOA’s mixer, potentially sharpening class separation in the calibrated readout.  

4. Refine the two-qubit readout calibration by grouping the measured pairs into shells (distance 1, 2, 4) and assigning each group its own gain and bias (six new readout params instead of two).  This evidence-driven tweak maintains the calibrated-Hermitian pattern but grants per-scale margin tuning, which may reduce worst-group margin drops while preserving overall gate depth.  

5. Introduce asymmetric mid-range flexibility by splitting the distance-2 shell into two interleaved subsets—e.g. even-indexed and odd-indexed skip links—each tied to its own “theta_ent_mid_even” and “theta_ent_mid_odd”.  This slight parameter increase tests whether directional mid-range unsharing captures dataset anisotropies better than the fully shared skip shell, grounded in the observed underfitting when shells lack targeted flexibility.
