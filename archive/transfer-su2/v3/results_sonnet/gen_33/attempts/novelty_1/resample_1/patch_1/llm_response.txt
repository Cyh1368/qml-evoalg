Since the task explicitly states that fewer distinct trainable parameter names always scores higher, and the recommendation calls for probing whether directional (parity-based) unsharing of the distance-2 skip shell captures anisotropies better than a fully shared skip layer, I rebuild the ansatz around the proven low-parameter global-RY / global-RZ backbone (the 0.61 seed) but replace the single shared entangling parameter for the distance-2 skip-ring with two parity-tied parameters ("theta_ent_mid_even" for skip links starting at even wires, "theta_ent_mid_odd" for those starting at odd wires), while keeping the distance-1 ring and distance-4 antipodal shells tied to the cheap "theta_z" parameter as in the strongest prior design. This keeps total distinct parameters low (4: theta_ry, theta_z, theta_ent_mid_even, theta_ent_mid_odd) while testing whether the small additional degree of freedom in the mid-range shell recovers/improves worst-group margin without reintroducing the parameter bloat that hurt the current candidate.
