<DESCRIPTION>
The current ansatz uses 24 independent parameters (RY+RZ on every qubit plus a final RZ layer), which the metrics show is badly overfitting: train accuracy 0.88 vs validation 0.63 and test 0.56, a 0.31 generalization gap, and the score explicitly penalizes parameter count "with no point of diminishing returns." With a very small training set, this over-parameterized circuit cannot generalize.

I replace the ansatz with a much more parameter-economical, translationally-symmetric design inspired by symmetry-informed variational circuits: split the 8 qubits into even/odd sublattices (a common trick for compressing parameter count while preserving useful expressivity for pairwise correlators), use a single shared entangling parameter around a ring (translation-invariant nearest-neighbor coupling, matching the fixed two-qubit-pair readout structure), and a single shared final-phase parameter. This drops the circuit parameter count from 24 to 4 (a 6x reduction), which should:
1. Drastically improve generalization (fewer degrees of freedom to overfit the small training set).
2. Preserve enough expressivity because the ring of ZZ couplings still creates full connectivity across repeated blocks (3 repeats of the ring is enough to correlate any pair of qubits given the small system size).
3. Keep the circuit shallow-ish and hardware-natural (nearest-neighbor gates only), aiding convergence speed.
4. Directly help the "worst group margin" metric since symmetric parameter tying acts as a regularizer, reducing variance across different validation difficulty groups.

This aligns with the eval's explicit preference: fewer distinct parameter names always scores higher, and robust worst-case separation with a small training set benefits from low-capacity generalizing structures rather than a per-qubit fully independent parameterization.
