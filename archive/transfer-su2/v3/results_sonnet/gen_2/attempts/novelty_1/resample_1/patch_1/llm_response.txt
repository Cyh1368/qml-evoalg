parity_shared_entangling_ansatz

<DESCRIPTION>
The seed spec uses 24 independent parameters (one RY, one pre-RZ, one post-RZ per qubit), which is far too many for a tiny training set and leads to a large train/validation/test gap (0.88/0.63/0.56) and near-zero worst-group margin. Since fewer distinct parameter names strictly improve the score, and generalization on held-out hard samples is what matters, I replace the per-qubit independent rotation layers with a parity-shared parameterization: qubits are split into "even" and "odd" roles, and every RY/RZ layer uses only two distinct angles (one for even-indexed qubits, one for odd-indexed qubits) instead of eight. I also replace the fixed CZ ring with a parametrized CRZ ring that shares a single entangling angle across all edges (plus a closing edge to make it a full ring), so the circuit can learn how much correlation to inject between neighboring qubits — this is a much more targeted lever for shaping the pairwise correlators that the fixed readout measures, at the cost of only one more parameter.

This drops the parameter count from 24 distinct names down to 5 (ry_even, ry_odd, rz_even, rz_odd, ent_theta), a >4x reduction, while retaining enough structural asymmetry (even vs odd qubit roles, plus a trainable entangling ring) to shape the correlators used by the readout. Fewer parameters directly reduces overfitting risk on the small training set, should shrink the train/test gap, and (per the stated scoring rule) improves the score on parameter economy grounds alone, while the tunable entangling ring gives the optimizer a direct handle on the two-qubit correlations that determine the readout — plausibly helping the worst-case validation-group margin as well.
</DESCRIPTION>
