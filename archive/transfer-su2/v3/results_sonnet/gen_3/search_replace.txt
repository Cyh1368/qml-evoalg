low_param_shared_ansatz

<DESCRIPTION>
The current 24-parameter ansatz massively overfits (train 0.88 vs. val 0.63 vs. test 0.56, worst-group margin only 0.03). The instructions state that fewer distinct trainable parameters *always* score higher and that robust separation on the hardest validation group dominates the score. I redesign the ansatz to drastically cut the parameter count via deliberate sharing while keeping enough structural diversity to remain expressive across 3 repeated applications:

- Two single-qubit RY parameters shared across alternating (even/odd) qubits, giving each half-lattice an independent local rotation while still tying 4 qubits per parameter (encourages permutation-symmetric, generalizable features rather than per-qubit overfitting).
- A ring of ZZ Ising couplings with ONE shared parameter, giving nearest-neighbor entangling structure that is repeated identically at every position (translation-invariant ansatz component, a strong inductive bias that tends to generalize better with small training sets).
- A long-range CRZ layer connecting each qubit to its "antipodal" partner (i, i+4) with a single shared parameter, injecting entanglement across the whole register beyond nearest neighbors, important since the readout observable acts on qubit pairs that may be far apart.
- A final shared RZ layer (one parameter) on all qubits, giving a global phase-shaping stage before the next repetition/measurement.

Total distinct circuit parameters: 5 (down from 24), plus the 2 fixed readout params (gain, bias) = 7 total trainable parameters. This should substantially reduce the generalization gap and increase parameter efficiency, while the ring + long-range entangling structure repeated 3 times (per N_REPEATS) still builds meaningful entangling depth for the two-qubit readout to exploit.

</DESCRIPTION>
