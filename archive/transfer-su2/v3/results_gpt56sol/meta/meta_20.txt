# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
• Minimal distinct rotation parameters + fixed non-local entangler  
   – Collective-Angle 8-Qubit Classifier (Gen 16) ties all RY/RZ to one “collective_angle” and uses a parameter-free Möbius-ladder CZ network. This yields the top combined score (0.53) and test 0.74 with only 3 total parameters (param efficiency 0.25).  
• Repetition of a small, tied-parameter block amplifies expressivity  
   – Gen 16 applies the same 1-parameter block three times. Compared to a single pass, 3 repeats raise test accuracy from ≈0.65–0.68 to 0.74 without extra parameters.  
• Wide uniform initialization across [–π, π]  
   – All top programs (Gen 16, Gen 18) initialize rotation angles uniformly in [–π, π], preventing barren plateaus and ensuring consistent convergence across random seeds.  
• Trainable gain/bias readout calibration  
   – Every high-scoring ansatz (Gen 1–5, Gen 10, Gen 16) uses a “gain*(mean(correlators) – bias)” layer. This stabilizes margins and avoids trivial zero-gain solutions, contributing to margins ≈0.31 on validation for Gen 16.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
• Over-symmetrized or purely local entanglers underfit  
   – Hypercube Mixer (Gen 15) with 4 tied parameters over 3 CZ matchings scored 0.49 combined (test 0.60). Its parameter efficiency (0.15) lagged the global Möbius-ladder.  
   – Symmetric Möbius-Ladder (Gen 17) with 5 total parameters reached high train 0.94 but only combined 0.49 (test 0.79, gap 0.15), indicating under-expressivity despite deeper, symmetric blocks.  
• Excess parameter sharing without non-local connectivity  
   – Parity-Tied Möbius (Gen 19) tied four distinct angles but scored only 0.44 combined (test 0.73) and suffered a 0.21 generalization gap. Over-tying across RY/RZ parities without richer entanglement degraded out-of-sample performance.  
• Richer parameterization alone insufficient  
   – Symmetric XX-Ring (Gen 13) used two shared XX parameters in nearest-neighbor+antipodal edges but only achieved test 0.68 (combined 0.48), underscoring that extra tunable gates can’t compensate for sparse connectivity.  
• Shallow palindromic ZZ tying fails to capture correlations  
   – Palindromic Collective ZZ (Gen 14) used three parameters in RZ–RY–RZ/ZZ blocks but hit test 0.59 combined 0.43, showing depth and block palindrome aren’t substitutes for non-local entanglement.  

## Implementation Insights  
• Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC + key-to-index mapping  
   – All leading programs (e.g., Gen 16) parse ANSATZ_SPEC once, build `{param_key: index}` and loop over it in `apply_ansatz_block`, ensuring correct tying and minimal Python overhead.  
• Fixed-format, repeated block with tied params  
   – Gen 16’s `apply_ansatz_block` ignores `block_index` for parameters, repeating the same spec three times. This simple pattern packs depth and expressivity into a tiny parameter footprint.  
• Up-front ansatz validation  
   – `validate_ansatz_spec` catches malformed gates or wire indices before QNode creation, preventing silent failures. All top performers validated successfully with zero SPEC_ERRORS.  
• AnnotatedQueue metadata extraction  
   – Using `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` + `tape.graph.get_depth()` yields reliable circuit depth (33) and gate counts (84) for Gen 16, enabling explicit cost/control trade-offs when designing entanglers.  

## Performance Analysis  
• Combined score tracks test accuracy, not train performance  
   – Gen 16’s combined 0.53 (test 0.74) outperforms Gen 18’s 0.51 (test 0.74) by better validation margins and generalization gap (0.14 vs. 0.14 equal gap but higher validation margin: 0.31 vs. 0.31).  
• Non-local CZ entanglement boosts test by ~6–8%  
   – Moving from local/hypercube CZ (Gen 15 test 0.60) to Möbius-ladder CZ (Gen 16 test 0.74) yields an absolute +14% test gain without new circuit parameters.  
• Parameter efficiency peaks at minimal angles + non-local links  
   – Gen 16’s param efficiency 0.25 > Gen 17’s 0.16 and Gen 15’s 0.15, confirming that fewer distinct angles leveraged by a rich entangler maximize accuracy per parameter.  
• Generalization gap trade-off favors moderate overfit  
   – Low-expressivity models (Gen 9–12) have gaps <0.07 but test ≈0.50. Accepting a moderate gap (0.14) in Gen 16 yields a substantially higher test 0.74, shifting the Pareto front toward controlled over-expressivity.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parameterize the Möbius‐ladder cross‐chords with a single shared ZZ angle.  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace each fixed CZ on wires [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] with  
     `{"gate":"ZZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"cross_ent"}`  
   This keeps the non-local connectivity intact but lets the optimizer tune the strength of those four diametric links with only one extra parameter—building on the proven expressivity boost of non-local entanglers without breaking the minimal-param paradigm.  

2. Append a shared nearest‐neighbor IsingZZ layer after the ladder.  
   After the Möbius-ladder CZ/ZZ layer in ANSATZ_SPEC, add for i in 0…7:  
     `{"gate":"ZZ","wires":[i,(i+1)%8],"param":"zz_nn"}`  
   One extra “zz_nn” angle draws on Gen 3’s success with shared two-qubit gates to strengthen local correlations and can raise test accuracy under tight parameter budgets.  

3. Untie the rotation parameter across repeats for block-specific angles.  
   Replace the single “collective_angle” param key in ANSATZ_SPEC with `"angle_0"`, `"angle_1"`, `"angle_2"`, and in apply_ansatz_block index the correct key by block_index.  
   This change increases N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK from 1→3 so each repetition can learn its own basis change, trading a small param increase for depth‐specific expressivity while preserving within-block tying.  

4. Split the global rotation into separate Y- and Z-axes parameters.  
   In PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK, replace “collective_angle” with “global_y” and “global_z” and update ANSATZ_SPEC so RY layers use “global_y” and RZ layers use “global_z”.  
   This two-parameter scheme retains symmetry across qubits but gives the optimizer axis-specific control—leveraging the insight that minimal untie of rotation axes can yield substantive accuracy gains.  

5. Alternate between two fixed CZ patterns across repeats.  
   Define ANSATZ_SPEC_A as the standard Möbius-ladder and ANSATZ_SPEC_B as the same ladder shifted (e.g. cross-chords [0,5],[1,6],[2,7],[3,4]). In apply_ansatz_block, apply spec_A when block_index is even and spec_B when odd, using the same tied parameters.  
   This alternation injects richer non-local connectivity into the depth dimension without adding parameters, potentially capturing correlations missed by a single static pattern.
