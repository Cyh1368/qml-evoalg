# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

• Minimal distinct rotation parameters combined with a rich, parameter‐free entangler  
  – The Global-Rotation Möbius-Ladder ansatz (current best program) uses only two circuit angles (“global_y”, “global_z”) tied across all 8 qubits, plus a fixed Möbius-ladder CZ network. This achieves test accuracy 0.74 (combined score 0.51) with just 4 total parameters, yielding the highest parameter efficiency (0.18).  
  – In contrast, the Symmetric XX-Ring (Gen 13) tied two parameters in an IsingXX ring+antipodal network and reached only test 0.68 (combined 0.48) despite similar depth and gate count.  

• Non-local entanglement patterns outperform purely local or sparse connections  
  – Adding cross-chord CZ links (0–4, 1–5, 2–6, 3–7) in the Möbius-ladder raises test accuracy from 0.68 (local XX ring) to 0.74 without extra trainable parameters.  
  – Earlier bests (Gen 3 Repeated RY/XX, combined 0.51) also hint that non-nearest-neighbor entanglers boost expressivity under tight parameter budgets.  

• Full‐period random initialization to avoid dead zones  
  – All top‐performing circuits (including the Möbius-Ladder) initialize rotation angles uniformly in [−π, π], preventing the optimizer from getting stuck near the identity and ensuring stable convergence across seeds.  

• Consistent readout calibration via trainable gain and bias  
  – Every high‐scoring ansatz (Gen 1–5, Gen 10) uses the same `gain*(mean(correlators) – bias)` pattern, stabilizing margins and avoiding collapse to zero‐gain trivial solutions.  

## Ineffective Approaches

• Over-tying to a single angle underfits completely  
  – Shared-Parameter Floquet (Gen 9) with one shared rotation angle around a simple CZ ring reached only test 0.51 (combined 0.43), matching random performance.  

• No entanglement yields chance-level accuracy  
  – Parity-Based rotation‐only ansatz (Gen 11) and Shared-Basis euler blocks (Gen 12) both lack two-qubit gates, achieving test accuracies ~0.50 (combined 0.38–0.41).  

• Parameterized two-qubit gates with strict locality still lags  
  – Symmetric XX-Ring (Gen 13) ties two IsingXX parameters in a nearest-neighbour + antipodal pattern; though richer than no entanglement, it caps test at 0.68 and underfits complex correlations compared to the non-local CZ network.  

• Shallow palindromic ZZ with aggressive tying underperforms  
  – Palindromic Collective ZZ (Gen 14) uses three unique parameters in a palindromic RZ–RY–RZ/ZZ block but only reaches test 0.59 (combined 0.43), indicating limited expressivity despite deeper blocks.  

## Implementation Insights

• Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC parsing with a single param‐to‐index map  
  – All successful programs (notably the current best) build `key_to_position` once from `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` and then loop over `ANSATZ_SPEC`, ensuring correct tying and minimal overhead.  

• Up-front ansatz validation decouples errors from training  
  – The `validate_ansatz_spec` function catches malformed gates or wire indices before QNode creation, preventing silent training failures as seen in early prototypes.  

• Explicit metadata extraction guides entangler design  
  – Using `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` plus `tape.graph.get_depth()` and gate counts (e.g., depth 33, gates 84 for the Möbius-Ladder) allows controlled trade-offs between connectivity and circuit cost.  

• Fixed‐structure, repeated‐block layout with tied params  
  – The best program’s `apply_ansatz_block` repeats the same two‐parameter block three times, leveraging repetition for expressivity while preserving a minimal parameter footprint.  

## Performance Analysis

• Test accuracy correlates more with entanglement richness than with depth or parameter count  
  – Global-Rotation Möbius-Ladder (2 distinct params + fixed non-local CZ) test 0.74 > Symmetric XX-Ring (2 distinct params + local XX) test 0.68 > Palindromic ZZ (3 distinct params) test 0.59.  

• Parameter efficiency peaks when minimal angles meet non‐local connectivity  
  – Möbius-Ladder’s parameter efficiency 0.18 tops Symmetric XX-Ring’s 0.17 and Repeated RY/XX’s 0.14.  

• Small generalization gaps (<0.07) occur only at low test accuracy  
  – Under-expressive but heavily tied models (Gen 9–12) have gaps ~0.06–0.07 but test ≈0.50–0.56. Accepting a moderate gap (0.14) in the Möbius-Ladder yields a much higher test 0.74.  

• Combined scores track test accuracy rather than train performance  
  – The current best’s combined 0.51 aligns with its top test accuracy (0.74), whereas several tied‐parameter ansätze plateau at combined ≈0.43–0.48 despite low overfitting.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Parameterize the Möbius-ladder cross chords with a shared IsingZZ angle (“cross_ent”).  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace each fixed CZ on wires [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] with  
     `{"gate":"ZZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"cross_ent"}`  
   This adds only one extra parameter but lets the optimizer tune the strength of nonlocal entanglement—a proven expressivity booster without breaking the minimal-param pattern.  

2. Inject a shared nearest-neighbor ZZ entangler at the end of each block.  
   After the Möbius-ladder CZ layer and before the final RZs, append for i in range(N_QUBITS):  
     `{"gate":"ZZ","wires":[i,(i+1)%N_QUBITS],"param":"zz_nn"}`  
   Gen 3’s shared XX across NN pairs showed that one extra two-qubit trainable angle can significantly raise test accuracy under tight budgets.  

3. Introduce semi-collective rotations by splitting global_y and global_z into even/odd groups.  
   In PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK, replace “global_y”/“global_z” with “y_even”, “y_odd”, “z_even”, “z_odd” and apply RY/RZ accordingly on even vs odd wires.  
   This structured untie doubles your rotation parameters from 2→4, trading a small parameter increase for targeted expressivity while preserving strong symmetry.  

4. Add a readout-focused CRZ layer on the dataset’s correlator pairs.  
   After all N_REPEATS blocks, loop over READOUT_PAIRS and insert  
     `qml.CRZ(params[key_to_position["readout_crz"]], wires=[a,b])`  
   One extra “readout_crz” parameter concentrates additional entangling power right where the fixed Hermitian measurement occurs, building on earlier margin improvements.  

5. Sweep the block‐repetition count around three to optimize depth.  
   Make N_REPEATS a tunable hyperparameter and generate variants with N_REPEATS∈{2,3,4} (depth≈22,33,44) while keeping the Möbius-ladder+rotations spec fixed.  
   Although connectivity matters more than depth, exploring slightly shallower/deeper circuits may uncover a sweet spot that further boosts test accuracy.
