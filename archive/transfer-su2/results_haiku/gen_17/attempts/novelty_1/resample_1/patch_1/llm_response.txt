- Gen 1 (baseline): 26 params, depth 24, gate_count 138, convergence_step 60 → score 1.00
- Gen 2: 26 params, depth 35, gate_count 186, convergence_step 90 → score 1.00
- Current: 26 params, depth 18, gate_count 90, convergence_step 300 → score 1.00

The current program achieves perfect accuracy but has **3x slower convergence** (300 vs 60 steps) despite having the shallowest circuit. This suggests the ansatz lacks sufficient expressiveness early in training, even though it eventually converges.

**Key insight:** The current ansatz applies only RY on even qubits and RZ on odd qubits, then ZZ entanglers. This asymmetry may limit the initial parameter landscape. Gen 1 used a full RY layer, then CZ chain, then full RZ layer—providing more uniform single-qubit rotations before entanglement.

**Proposed improvement:**
1. Add a final RZ layer on all qubits (like Gen 1) to provide additional phase flexibility after entanglement
2. Introduce a shared RX layer on all qubits before the ZZ entanglers to increase initial expressiveness
3. Keep the ZZ entanglers (which are more parameter-efficient than CZ) but ensure all qubits have rotation freedom

This should:
- Improve convergence speed (reduce convergence_step from 300 toward 60)
- Maintain or improve accuracy (already at 1.00)
- Keep parameter count low (add only 2 new shared params: rx_shared, rz_final)
- Increase depth slightly but still remain competitive

The rationale is that Gen 1's success came from having full single-qubit rotation coverage (RY + RZ on all qubits), while the current design restricts rotations to alternating qubits. By adding RX and final RZ layers, we restore expressiveness without excessive parameter growth.
