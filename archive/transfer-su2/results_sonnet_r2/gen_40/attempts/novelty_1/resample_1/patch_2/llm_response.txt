I'm considering a reduced-parameter ansatz that merges tilt and phase into a single rotation parameter applied sequentially with RY and RZ gates, cutting the per-block parameters from 3 down to 2 (rotation and twist), which would give 14 total parameters instead of the original count. However, the prior experiments showed the 3-parameter version already converges perfectly at 20 parameters in just 90 steps, so this compression might sacrifice expressivity without clear benefit.

Looking at the comparison more carefully—the 5-parameter model actually converged faster at 30 steps versus 90, though it uses more parameters overall (32 vs 20) and has slightly worse efficiency metrics. Since both achieve the same maximum score of 1.0, the better approach is to keep the 3-parameter structure but interleave XX gates after ZZ using the existing "twist" parameter, which adds gate depth and nonlinearity without introducing new parameters. This maintains the 20-parameter count while potentially improving convergence speed through increased expressivity.
