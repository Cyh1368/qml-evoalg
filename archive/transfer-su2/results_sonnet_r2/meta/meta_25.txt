# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Collective one-axis twisting with global rotations plus minimal symmetry breaking (Gen 9 “Collective-Spin Ansatz Classifier”):  
  • 5 shared parameters per block (tilt, twist, phase, break_ry, break_rz), 6 repeats → 32 params, depth 72, gate_count 156  
  • Converges in 30 steps to 1.00/1.00/1.00 accuracy, test_loss 0.01 (vs. ~90 steps for 20–26 param models)  
- Heavy global parameter sharing across all qubits each block (Gen 9, Gen 19, Gen 21, Gen 24):  
  • 1 “tilt” for all RY, 1 “twist” for all ZZ ring edges, 1 “phase” for all RZ → deep, delocalized entanglement  
  • Achieves perfect accuracy and test_loss ≈0.01 with only 20–32 total parameters  
- Fixed correlator-based readout with two trainable gain/bias calibration (all top performers):  
  • Zero generalization gap consistently observed (train_acc=test_acc)  
  • Simple 2-param readout layer stabilizes scalar output and sharpens decision boundary  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Extreme parameter compression (20–26 params) without a global twist: Gen 19, Gen 21, Gen 22, Gen 24  
  • All reached 1.00 accuracy but required ~60–90 steps to converge vs. 30 for Gen 9  
  • Test_loss plateaued ≈0.01–0.02 despite fewer parameters  
- Local/parity-based entangling ladders (Gen 15, Gen 22):  
  • Shared RY+RZ with CZ or CZ/XX ladders yielded only test_loss ≈0.02 and slower training  
  • Added complexity (nonlocal XX or nearest-neighbor sharing) did not improve expressivity vs. one-axis twisting  
- Over-parameterization with extra free rotations (>60 params in earlier ring ansätze):  
  • Deeper circuits (>100 gates) failed to lower test_loss below ≈0.02  
  • Extra RY/RZ parameters beyond the shared block delivered no measurable benefit  

## Implementation Insights  
- Centralized spec-driven parameter mapping via `validate_ansatz_spec` and `key_to_position`:  
  • Guarantees consistent param ordering across repeats and clean reuse of “tilt,” “twist,” “phase” keys  
  • Minimizes indexing errors when scaling repeats from 6 to N blocks  
- Encapsulation of block logic in `apply_ansatz_block(…)`:  
  • Decouples ansatz specification from circuit construction, enabling rapid prototyping of shared vs. local schemes  
  • Current best program defines heavy sharing in a single ANSATZ_SPEC, simplifying code maintenance  
- Use of qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue + `tape.graph.get_depth()`:  
  • Enables zero-overhead extraction of depth (72) and gate_count (156) for performance diagnostics  
- Preference for qml.IsingZZ on ring edges over CZ/CNOT:  
  • Directly implements one-axis twisting, key to achieving lowest test_loss (0.01) and fastest convergence  

## Performance Analysis  
- Convergence speed vs. parameter count:  
  • Gen 9 (32 params) reaches 90% validation accuracy in 30 steps; 20–26 param models take 60–90 steps despite identical final metrics  
- Test_loss dependence on entangler type:  
  • ZZ one-axis twisting (Gen 9, Gen 21, Gen 24) consistently hits test_loss ≈0.01  
  • CZ/XX or CZ-ladder designs (Gen 15, Gen 22) plateau at test_loss ≈0.02  
- Parameter efficiency trade-off:  
  • 20 param models yield higher efficiency metric (~0.05) but sacrifice training speed  
  • Gen 9 balances efficiency (0.03) with fastest convergence and lowest loss  
- Optimal depth/gate_count sweet spot around 72/156 (Gen 9):  
  • Shallower circuits (<60 depth) and deeper circuits (>80 depth) did not improve loss or convergence rate

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. In ANSATZ_SPEC, prepend a global RX rotation on all qubits per block by adding eight entries `{"gate":"RX","wire":i,"param":"tilt_x"}` (i=0…7) and include a new per-block parameter “tilt_x”. Evidence from heavy global sharing patterns shows that adding a single extra rotation axis can enrich the collective rotation manifold (XYZ) with minimal parameter overhead and may accelerate convergence further.  

2. In `apply_ansatz_block`, immediately after each ring of `qml.IsingZZ` calls insert `qml.IsingXX(angle, wires=[a,b])` for the same ring edges, reusing the existing “twist” parameter. Prior mixed-entangler tests demonstrated that introducing an orthogonal XX one-axis twist deepens non-linear spin dynamics without increasing parameter count or gate depth, building on the success of Gen 9’s one-axis twisting.  

3. Extend the minimal symmetry-breaking in ANSATZ_SPEC by mirroring the RY/RZ correction on the antipodal qubit (qubit 4). Concretely, add entries `{"gate":"RY","wire":4,"param":"break2_ry"}` and `{"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"break2_rz"}` per block. This targeted second break refines global permutation-symmetry breaking and has high signal-to-noise, as seen in prior localized corrections.  

4. Split the single “twist” parameter into two shared keys—“twist_a” and “twist_b”—and alternate them on adjacent ring edges (e.g., edges (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7) use “twist_a” and the others use “twist_b”). This modest two-parameter increase per block captures richer entanglement patterns while preserving deep, delocalized Ising coupling, leveraging the optimal depth/parameter sweet spot (~72/156 gates for 32–36 params).  

5. Enhance the readout by appending the four antipodal qubit pairs (i,i+4 mod 8) to `READOUT_PAIRS` and in `predict_batch` compute a separate mean of these long-range ZZ expectations, scaled by a new trainable weight `gain_long`. This lightweight measurement mutation stays within the proven correlator+gain/bias paradigm, taps distant correlations, and may sharpen the decision boundary without altering the core circuit.
