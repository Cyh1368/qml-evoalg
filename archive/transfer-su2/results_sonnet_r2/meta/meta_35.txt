# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- One-axis twisting with global rotations plus minimal symmetry breaking (Current Best “Collective-Spin-Inspired” ansatz): 5 shared params per block (tilt, twist, phase, break_ry, break_rz) over 6 repeats → 32 total params. Achieved perfect 1.00/1.00/1.00 accuracies, zero gap, test_loss≈0.01, and convergence_step_mean=30. (Current Best Program)  
- Moderate-sized shared-parameter blocks with built-in symmetry breaking also converge fastest. Generation 32 (“8-Qubit Shared-Parameter Entangling Classifier”) used 4 params/block (parity RY, ring ZZ/XX, global RZ) for 32 total params and reached perfect scores with convergence_step≈30.  
- Pure ring-entangler ansätze with 3 shared params/block (RY, ZZ, RZ) plus 2 readout params (26 total) deliver full expressivity and generalization (Gen 30, 31, 34), achieving test_accuracy=1.00, test_loss≈0.01, but converge more slowly (60–90 steps).  
- Readout calibration (2 trainable gain/bias) on fixed Z–Z correlators is essential: every top program (Gen 30–34, Current Best) used it to eliminate the generalization gap entirely.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-compression (<3 params/block) examples (Gen 33 “Minimal Tilt-Twist Ansatz,” 14 total params) converged only after ~90 steps and plateaued at test_loss≈0.02 despite shallow depth (23) and low gate_count (90), indicating under-parameterization.  
- Omitting symmetry-breaking terms in highly symmetric ansätze slowed learning and raised loss floors: purely shared ring designs (Gen 31, Gen 34) needed 60–90 steps and hovered at validation_loss≈0.02 vs. ≈0.01 with breaking.  
- Excessively independent local rotations (>60 independent params in earlier large ansätze) added depth/gate cost without lowering test_loss below ≈0.02 or speeding convergence.  
- Removing early stopping or readout calibration reintroduced nonzero generalization gaps and required substantially more epochs to perfect training accuracy.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Encoding the ansatz in ANSATZ_SPEC with `validate_ansatz_spec` and a `key_to_position` map ensures consistent parameter indexing and rapid block redesigns. The Current Best’s specification of 5 keys/block succinctly captures global vs. breaking operations.  
- The `apply_ansatz_block` abstraction cleanly decouples gate structure from circuit logic, facilitating experiments across 3-param vs. 5-param vs. 4-param blocks with minimal code changes.  
- Leveraging `qml.IsingZZ` on ring edges realizes dense one-axis twisting: Current Best applies 8 ZZ gates per block (48 total), driving rich entanglement at low param cost.  
- Querying depth and gate_count via `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` + `tape.graph.get_depth()` provides immediate resource feedback, guiding block design towards the depth≈72/gate_count≈156 sweet spot observed in the Current Best.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Convergence speed scales with circuit param count and symmetry breaking:  
   • ~32 params w/ breaking → convergence_step≈30 (Current Best, Gen 32)  
   • 26 params w/o breaking → 60–90 steps (Gen 30, 31, 34)  
   • 14 params → ~90 steps (Gen 33)  
- Test_loss clusters: ≈0.01 for global ZZ-twisting designs with symmetry breaking (Current Best, Gen 32) vs. ≈0.02 for purely shared or minimal-param models (Gen 30, 31, 33, 34).  
- Depth/gate trade-off: deeper circuits (depth≈72/gate_count≈156) yield fastest training and lowest loss; shallower circuits (depth≈23/gate_count≈90) reduce resource use at the cost of slower convergence and higher loss.  
- Parameter efficiency vs. speed: lean 14-param Gen 33 has highest efficiency (~0.07) but slowest convergence; 26–32 param models balance efficiency (~0.03–0.04) with rapid convergence and lowest test_loss.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend the global‐tilt layer to two axes. In ANSATZ_SPEC, prepend eight RX gates keyed by a new per‐block parameter “tilt_x” alongside the existing RY “tilt”. Prior tests showed that adding one extra shared rotation axis dramatically enlarges the collective‐spin manifold and can shave off convergence steps with only one extra param per block.  

2. Introduce a mixed ZZ/XX one‐axis‐twist. In apply_ansatz_block, immediately after the eight qml.IsingZZ(“twist”,…) calls insert eight qml.IsingXX(angle, wires=…) operations reusing the same “twist” key (or a new “twist_xx” key if you want two separate params). Mixed‐entangler layers have yielded richer nonlinearity without doubling block depth in previous Gen-9 variants.  

3. Break block‐repetition symmetry by alternating twist parameters. Replace the single “twist” key in ANSATZ_SPEC with two keys, “twist_even” and “twist_odd”, and in apply_ansatz_block select which to use based on block_index % 2. Splitting the one‐axis‐twist into alternating streams captures more diverse entanglement patterns while only adding one extra param per block.  

4. Augment readout with single‐qubit magnetization. After computing the mean two‐qubit correlator z in predict_batch, also call circuit_pair_expectations for PauliZ on each qubit, average to a scalar z_single, and introduce trainable gain_single and bias_single to your final prediction. Empirically, combining single‐ and two‐qubit observables tightens the decision boundary without touching the core ansatz.  

5. Add a second symmetry‐breaking site on the antipode. In ANSATZ_SPEC, append per‐block entries  
   {"gate":"RY","wire":4,"param":"break2_ry"} and {"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"break2_rz"}.  
   Dual‐site symmetry breaking has been shown to eliminate residual invariances and can push the test_loss below the ~0.01 plateau observed with only one break site.
