# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Collective one-axis twisting plus global rotations with minimal symmetry breaking  
  • Gen 9’s “Collective-Spin Ansatz Classifier” repeats a 5-parameter block (global RY tilt, ring-edge ZZ twist, RZ phase, and two symmetry-breaking rotations) six times (32 total params). It achieved perfect accuracy (1.00), the lowest test_loss (0.01), and ultra-fast convergence (30 steps), outpacing all lighter or heavier designs.  
- Heavy parameter sharing across the entire 8-qubit register  
  • Using a single “tilt,” “twist,” and “phase” parameter per block via qml.IsingZZ on every ring edge yields deep, delocalized entanglement with minimal angles—key to Gen 9’s expressivity and stability.  
- Simple correlator-based readout with two trainable calibration parameters  
  • All top performers (Gen 9, 16, 17, 18, 20, 23) measure fixed two-qubit Z–Z correlators and apply a gain/bias layer, delivering zero generalization gap with only two extra parameters.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Extreme parameter compression slows convergence  
  • Gen 16, 17, 20, and 23 (20–26 total params) all converged in ~60–90 steps to 1.00 accuracy/test_loss ≈0.01, versus Gen 9’s 30-step convergence, indicating diminishing returns on ultra-compact parametrizations.  
- Parity- or local-shared rotation schemes add complexity without gains  
  • Gen 17’s merging of pre-RZ into RY and nearest-neighbor ZZ sharing matched 1.00 accuracy but did not improve loss or speed compared to the simpler collective-sharing in Gen 9.  
- Over-parameterization without expressivity boost  
  • Earlier ring ansätze with 60+ circuit params reached only test_loss ≈0.02 despite deeper circuits, showing that adding free RY/RZ parameters beyond the shared block offers no measurable benefit.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Centralized parameter indexing via validate_ansatz_spec and ordered_unique_keys  
  • Ensures consistent key ordering and catches spec errors early—crucial for correct parameter reuse across repeats in Gen 9.  
- apply_ansatz_block’s key_to_position mapping  
  • Encapsulates shared-parameter logic in one place, allowing a single “twist” angle to control all eight IsingZZ interactions and a single “tilt” for all RY gates.  
- qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue for zero-overhead metadata  
  • Enables rapid extraction of circuit depth (72) and gate_count (156) without simulation, facilitating trade-off assessments as done for Gen 9.  
- Use of qml.IsingZZ for global entangling  
  • Realizes the one-axis-twisting model directly on ring edges, key to achieving Gen 9’s superior test_loss (0.01) and rapid convergence.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Convergence speed vs. parameter count  
  • Gen 9 (32 params) achieves 90% validation accuracy in 30 steps; comparable 20–26-param models (Gen 16/17/20/23) require 60–90 steps despite identical final accuracy/loss.  
- Test loss plateau at ≈0.01 with moderate parametrizations  
  • Both Gen 9 (32 params) and 24–26-param ring/ZZ ansätze reach test_loss ≈0.01, but only Gen 9 does so with minimal training iterations.  
- Parameter efficiency trade-offs  
  • Gen 23’s 22 params yields a higher raw efficiency metric (0.05) but slower training; Gen 9 balances efficiency (0.03) with the fastest convergence and lowest loss.  
- Depth and gate_count balance matters  
  • Gen 9’s depth 72 and 156 gates produce the best loss/convergence trade-off. Shallower (depth 55, 192 gates in Gen 18) or deeper circuits did not yield lower test_loss or faster training.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Augment each block with a global RX tilt. In ANSATZ_SPEC, prepend eight RX gates sharing a new per-block parameter “tilt_x” (one angle applied to all qubits). This adds only one extra parameter per block but enriches the collective rotation manifold in XYZ, leveraging the same heavy sharing that makes the RY–ZZ–RZ sequence so expressive and stable.  

2. Introduce a dual-axis one-axis twist using existing parameters. In apply_ansatz_block, immediately after the ZZ entanglers insert qml.IsingXX(angle, wires) for each ring edge, reusing the same “twist” key. This orthogonal XX twist deepens non-linear spin dynamics without increasing parameter count or significantly raising gate count, building on prior successful mixed-entangler experiments.  

3. Add a second symmetry-breaking term at the antipodal qubit. Extend ANSATZ_SPEC in each block with {"gate":"RY","wire":4,"param":"break2_ry"} and {"gate":"RZ","wire":4,"param":"break2_rz"}. By mirroring the localized correction at qubit 0 on qubit 4, you refine global permutation symmetry breaking and may reduce convergence steps, at only two extra angles per block.  

4. Split the ZZ twist into two edge-groups. Replace the single “twist” parameter in ANSATZ_SPEC with two shared keys “twist_a” and “twist_b”, assigning them alternately to adjacent ring edges (e.g., twist_a on (0–1,2–3,4–5,6–7), twist_b on the other four). This modest increase in per-block parameters lets the circuit capture richer entanglement patterns while preserving deep, delocalized Ising coupling.  

5. Capture long-range correlations in readout. Expand READOUT_PAIRS to include the four antipodal pairs (i, i+4 mod 8), then in predict_batch introduce a third calibration weight “gain_long” that scales the mean of these global ZZ expectations separately from nearest-neighbor correlators. This lightweight measurement mutation taps distant correlations and stays within the proven simple correlator+gain/bias paradigm that achieved zero generalization gap.
