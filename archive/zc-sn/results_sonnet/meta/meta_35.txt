# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Symmetric single-qubit rotation tying  
  • Gen37 (“8-Qubit Symmetric CRZ-RY/RX Ansatz”) ties RX parameters across qubit pairs (i, 7–i), halving the RX layer’s trainable count (from 8→4) and reducing total params to 98, yet still achieves a top combined score of 0.87 (train/val/test = 0.91/0.89/0.87, gap 0.04).  
- Three-tier entanglement hierarchy (ring, skip-2, diameter)  
  • Combining nearest-neighbor alternating CRZ rings, a shared-param skip-2 layer, and a shared-param diameter layer (Gen37) delivers both strong expressivity and a minimal generalization gap (0.04), outperforming skip-only designs (Gen36 gap 0.10).  
- Alternating shared-parameter ring entangler  
  • The even/odd CRZ alternation used in Gen31, Gen33, Gen34, and retained in Gen37 consistently boosts expressivity over uniform sharing—Gen37 and Gen34 both hit 0.87 combined versus 0.81 in uniform-one-param seeds.  
- Exclusive use of parameterized CRZ gates  
  • All top variants (Gen31, Gen33, Gen34, Gen37) rely solely on CRZ for two-qubit links, smoothing optimization and yielding higher scores than mixed CZ/CRZ ansätze (Gen22/23 at 0.76).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized entanglement without tying  
  • Gen34 and Gen36 each use 164 parameters (depth 111/142) but see only marginal score gains (0.87/0.84) and poor parameter efficiency (0.01), indicating diminishing returns past ~120 parameters.  
- Mid-circuit RZ alone  
  • Gen36 added a mid-circuit RZ layer to the seed but, lacking diameter entanglement or parameter tying, saw a drop to 0.84 combined and a widened gap (0.10), showing that extra single-qubit layers must be paired with structured entanglers.  
- Deep circuits without global links  
  • Gen31’s 126-depth ring-only ansatz achieves 0.86 but lacks long-range diameter gates, capping its combined score below the 0.87 frontier despite faster convergence (~480 steps).  
- Uniform parameter sharing on long-range links  
  • Seed-style single-param skip entanglers alone limit expressivity: although they regularize, they require support from alternating rings and diameter gates to reach top accuracy.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Clear parameter naming supports modular tuning  
  • Consistent labels (“crz_ring_even”, “crz_ring_odd”, “crz_skip”, “crz_diameter”) clarify optimizer roles and aid code maintenance, as seen in all top-scoring programs.  
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC blocks  
  • Encapsulating the ansatz in one evolve block isolates circuit design from data-handling code, ensuring reproducibility across gen31–37 variants.  
- Parameter tying for regularization  
  • Symmetric RX tying in Gen37 acts like L2 regularization: it cuts free parameters by 40% versus Gen34/36 and preserves performance, demonstrating that thoughtful sharing can substitute for added depth.  
- Hierarchical entanglement layering

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce alternating shared-parameter CRZ on your skip-2 layer. Replace the single “crz_skip” with two trainable parameters, “crz_skip_even” and “crz_skip_odd,” and assign them alternately to edges [0,2], [2,4], [4,6], [6,0] (even) and [1,3], [3,5], [5,7], [7,1] (odd). This echoes the proven alternating‐ring pattern (Gen31/33/37) and should boost expressivity on long‐range links while keeping the parameter count at 99→100.  

2. Split the diameter entangler into two shared parameters. Instead of one “crz_diameter,” use “crz_diam_even” on antipodal pairs [0,4] and [2,6] and “crz_diam_odd” on [1,5] and [3,7]. This two‐parameter hierarchy extends the ring/skip alternation to global links, offering richer global mixing with only +1 parameter.  

3. Experiment with mixed-axis entanglement on skip-2 links. Keep “crz_skip_even” on your even skip edges but swap the odd skip layer to a new “cry_skip_odd” parameterized CRY gate. By combining CRZ and CRY in a shared‐parameter fashion, you diversify interaction axes—an idea that succeeded in Gen25’s ring—and might further smooth the loss landscape without extra depth.  

4. Insert a lightweight mid-circuit RZ rotation layer with symmetric tying. Between your RX and skip/diameter blocks, add per-qubit RZ gates “rz_mid_i” on wire i, but tie them symmetrically so rz_mid_0=rz_mid_7, rz_mid_1=rz_mid_6, etc. This adds only 4 new parameters and ∼8 gates, enriching local phase control in a regularized fashion analogous to the RX symmetry in Gen37.  

5. Stack a second tied “sandwich” block reusing all existing parameters. After the diameter layer, append a copy of (RY→CRZ ring_even/odd→RX symmetric→CRZ skip→CRZ diameter) without introducing new parameter names. This doubles circuit depth (~284) but holds the parameter count at 98, testing whether deeper, fully tied hierarchies can push beyond the current 0.87 frontier.
