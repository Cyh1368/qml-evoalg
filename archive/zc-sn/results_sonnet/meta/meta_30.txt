# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating shared‐parameter ring entanglement  
  • Seed program (“RY→CRZ ring even/odd→RX→CRZ skip”, Combined 0.87, 116 params, depth 136) used distinct “crz_ring_even”/“crz_ring_odd” on nearest-neighbour rings, driving fast convergence (180 steps) and high expressivity per gate.  
  • Gen29/Gen30 (Combined 0.85, 164 params, depth 142/132) also applied alternating CRZ rings and saw marked gains over uniform sharing.  
- Four-layer rotation–entanglement “sandwich”  
  • RY → CRZ ring → RX → CRZ skip layout in seed and Gen29/30 balanced local mixing with long-range links at moderate depth (~136), outperforming deeper or extra-layered circuits.  
- Single shared-parameter skip-2 CRZ blocks  
  • The “crz_skip” block across all skip-2 pairs in seed and Gen29 enforced regularization, cutting free parameters (116 vs. 164) and achieving a tighter generalization gap (0.06 vs. 0.09).  
- Exclusive use of parameterized CRZ over fixed CZ/CNOT  
  • Seed and top variants relied solely on CRZ entanglers, yielding smoother optimization and higher combined scores than mixed CZ/CRZ designs (Gen22/23 at 0.76).  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mixed CRZ + CZ chain entanglement  
  • Gen22/Gen23 (Combined 0.76, depth 132) combined skip-2 CRZ with a linear CZ chain, resulting in high depth, poor parameter efficiency (0.01), and limited validation gains.  
- Over-parameterized entanglement without structured sharing  
  • Gen29/Gen30 increased to 164 params and depth up to 142, plateauing at 0.85 with low efficiency and, in Gen30, slower convergence (~540 steps).  
- Uniform or non-alternating parameter sharing  
  • Simple one-parameter ring entanglers (e.g. Gen24 in earlier tests) stalled at ~0.81, lacking the expressivity boost from alternating strengths.  
- Misconfigured ansatz specifications  
  • Gen32 failed all tests (score 0.0) due to unsupported gate/wire fields, underscoring strict validation requirements.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Descriptive parameter naming  
  • Naming “crz_ring_even”/“crz_ring_odd” clarifies optimizer updates on alternating edges, as in the seed and Gen25.  
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC isolation  
  • Encapsulating all gate definitions in a dedicated evolve block ensures reproducibility and avoids accidental code coupling.  
- Consistent RY→CRZ→RX→CRZ ordering  
  • Maintaining this sequence (seed, Gen29/30) correlates with top performance; deviations or added fixed-gate layers coincide with score drops.  
- Favor parameterized two-qubit gates  
  • Exclusive use of CRZ entanglers provides trainable interaction strengths, smoothing the loss landscape more effectively than fixed CZ/CNOT mixes.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Score evolution  
  • Gen22/23 (0.76) → Gen29/30 (0.85) → seed (0.87), tracking the move from mixed to fully parameterized, alternating-share entanglers.  
- Generalization gap reduction  
  • From 0.11 (Gen22/23) → 0.09 (Gen29) → 0.06 (seed), reflecting stronger regularization via structured sharing.  
- Optimal depth window  
  • Peak performance centers around depth ~136; Gen29’s 142 layers offered minimal further gains.  
- Convergence speed  
  • Seed program converged in 180 steps, markedly faster than Gen30 (~540 steps), tying convergence efficiency to balanced parameter count and depth.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce alternating shared‐parameter CRZ on your skip‐2 layer. Replace the single `crz_skip` with two parameters—`crz_skip_even` and `crz_skip_odd`—and assign them alternately to edges [0,2] (`even`), [1,3] (`odd`), [2,4] (`even`), etc. This directly extends the proven ring‐alternation pattern to long‐range links, boosting expressivity while preserving the tight regularization that yields a low generalization gap.  

2. Append a sparse diameter entanglement block. After the existing skip‐2 layer, add a single‐parameter CRZ on wires [0,4], [1,5], [2,6], [3,7] (e.g. `"param":"crz_diameter"`). By injecting minimal, global connectivity in one trainable degree of freedom, you mirror the skip‐2 regularization at a longer range—likely enhancing test‐set mixing without bloating parameter count or depth.  

3. Insert a lightweight mid‐circuit RZ rotation layer. Between the RX layer and your skip entanglement, add per‐qubit gates `{"gate":"RZ","wire":i,"param":"rz_mid_i"}` for i=0…7. Prior “lightweight mid‐layer” experiments showed such non‐commuting rotations enrich the ansatz manifold and can reduce convergence steps by injecting extra local phase flexibility at negligible depth cost (~+8 gates).  

4. Stack a second shared‐parameter “sandwich.” Duplicate the RY→CRZ ring→RX→CRZ skip block in your EVOLVE‐BLOCK, reusing all original parameter names (`ry_0…ry_7`, `crz_ring_even/odd`, `rx_0…rx_7`, `crz_skip`). This doubles circuit depth to ~272 while holding parameters at 116, testing whether deeper, fully tied structures can further raise the expressivity‐vs‐generalization trade‐off unlocked by the seed design.  

5. Explore mixed‐axis alternating entanglement in skip links. In your skip‐2 layer, keep `crz_skip_even` on even edges but replace odd‐edge CRZ with CRY using a new parameter `cry_skip_odd`. Alternating two different controlled rotations leverages the same parameter‐sharing advantage as Gen25’s ring but adds axis diversity, potentially smoothing the loss landscape more than a homogeneous CRZ‐only block.
