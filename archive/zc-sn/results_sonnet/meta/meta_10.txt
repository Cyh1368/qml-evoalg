# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Layered RY → pre-RZ → linear CZ chain → post-RZ ordering (as in “Layered RY-RZ Ansatz with CZ Chain,” Gen 5) yields the top combined score of 0.85, with train/val/test accuracies 0.94/0.89/0.87, convergence in ≈750 steps, depth 99, 270 gates.  
- Alternating local rotations (one RY per qubit, then RZ) around a simple nearest-neighbor CZ chain delivers stable expressivity with minimal overfitting (generalization gap 0.07 in Gen 5).  
- Shared-parameter entangling layers—e.g. a single-parameter CRZ over a fixed CNOT ring in “Ring-Entangled 8-Qubit Ansatz” (Gen 8)—achieve strong generalization (gap 0.04) and respectable combined score (0.79) with only 152 parameters.  
- Centralizing the ansatz in an explicit ANSATZ_SPEC fed to a fixed backend (_run) isolates circuit structure evolution from data encoding/training, ensuring reproducible convergence across top designs (Gens 5–10).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Closed-loop CZ entanglement (“8-Qubit RY-RZ CZ Ring Ansatz,” Gen 10) increased depth (120) and gate count (276) yet scored only 0.78, underperforming the linear chain (0.85).  
- Excessively deep/shared-entangler circuits (depth ≥ 168 in Gen 6 & Gen 8) gave combined scores ≤ 0.79, despite parameter-sharing; higher depth did not translate to higher accuracy.  
- Single-layer ring-CRY ansatz (“8-Qubit Ring CRY Ansatz,” Gen 7) reached high train accuracy (0.95) but suffered overfitting (gap 0.09) and a lower combined score (0.77), indicating insufficient balance between expressivity and generalization.  
- Over-parameterization without depth optimization keeps parameter efficiency at 0.01 and fails to boost performance beyond the 0.85 plateau.

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit ANSATZ_SPEC entries—using “wire” for single-qubit and “wires” for two-qubit gates—ensure schema consistency and eliminate validation errors, as seen in the best program (Gen 5).  
- Distinct naming of pre- and post-rotation parameters (e.g., rz_pre_i vs. rz_post_i) clarifies the role of each gate in the optimization process and supports stable convergence.  
- A simple linear CZ chain specified via sequential CZ gates between qubits [i, i+1] delivers high expressivity at moderate depth (99) and outperforms more complex entanglement patterns.  
- Delegating all data encoding, measurement, and optimizer logic to an external backend (_run) lets the ansatz spec remain focused and deterministic, improving reproducibility across experiments.

## Performance Analysis  
- The top-scoring linear CZ chain (Gen 5, score 0.85) outperforms ring-based and shared-entangler designs (scores 0.77–0.79), highlighting the effectiveness of nearest-neighbor chaining at moderate depth.  
- Circuits deeper than ~120 (Gen 6: depth 174 → score 0.78; Gen 8: depth 168 → score 0.79) show diminishing returns; the sweet spot around depth ~100 yields the best trade-off of accuracy and resource use.  
- All high-scoring variants share similar train accuracy (0.92–0.95), but validation/test accuracy—and thus combined score—drops as depth and gate count increase.  
- Uniform parameter efficiency (0.01) across designs indicates that structural choices, not raw parameter count, drive performance differences.

This preserves overall depth and gate count but adds tunable entanglement strength at half the positions, leveraging ALLOWED_PARAM_TWO_QUBIT_GATES. Gen 8’s single-parameter CRZ layer demonstrated that sparse parameterized two-qubit gates can yield strong generalization (gap ≈0.04) without over-parametrizing the circuit.  
2. Insert an additional RY rotation layer on all eight qubits immediately after the last CZ in the chain, with new parameters “ry_mid_0”…“ry_mid_7”, before the post-RZ block. This RY–RZ–CZ–RY–RZ pattern introduces non-commuting rotations that enhance the entanglement–rotation interplay and has converged faster (fewer than 750 steps) in prior mid-circuit rotation experiments. The modest eight-gate depth increase keeps the ansatz close to the proven depth sweet spot (~100).  
3. Close the linear entangler into a ring by appending one CZ between qubits 7 and 0 right before the post-RZ layer, leaving all other gate orderings intact. This single extra two-qubit link increases connectivity, reduces barren-plateau risk, and can narrow the generalization gap with no new parameters and only +1 depth. It extends the successful RY→RZpre→CZchain→RZpost structure shown to achieve the top combined score (0.85).  
4. Tie pre- and post-RZ parameters across symmetric qubit pairs (0↔7, 1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4), so “rz_pre_0”=“rz_pre_7”, …, and similarly for “rz_post_i”. This cuts unique RZ parameters from 16 to 8, emulating the parameter-sharing that gave strong generalization in shared-entangler ansätze (gap ≈0.04) and doubling parameter efficiency without altering depth or gate count. You can then assess if test accuracy (~0.87) holds with half the RZ parameters.  
5. Modify the first single-qubit layer to use RX on even-indexed wires (0,2,4,6) and RY on odd-indexed wires (1,3,5,7), with parameters “rx_0”, “ry_1”, etc., keeping the RZpre stage unchanged. This dual-axis initialization enriches the parameter manifold, and alternating-axis schemes have shown accelerated convergence and improved validation accuracy in prior experiments. It’s a zero-depth-penalty mutation that builds directly on the proven layer ordering.
