# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating shared‐parameter ring entanglement  
 • In the “Alternating ring-and-skip entangling ansatz” (Gen 25, combined 0.87) using two distinct CRZ parameters for even/odd NN links (“crz_ring_even”/“crz_ring_odd”) boosted the combined score from 0.85 (seed) to 0.87, improved validation accuracy (0.90) and kept the generalization gap low (0.06).  
- Sparse long-range entanglement as regularizer  
 • A single shared CRZ on skip-2 pairs (Gen 25) delivered strong long-range mixing without overfitting, converged in 180 steps, and matched the seed’s stability while raising accuracy.  
- Layered RY→CRZ ring→RX→CRZ skip “sandwich”  
 • Maintaining the four-layer sandwich at moderate depth (136) outperformed deeper or more parameter-heavy circuits (Gen 26 depth 142 → 0.86 combined; Gen 27 depth 120 → 0.75).  
- Shared-parameter entangling blocks for regularization  
 • Both Gen 25 (Alternating ring…) and Gen 28 (Layered Ansatz with Parameter Tying, 0.86 combined) show that tying entangler parameters effectively controls overfitting and stabilizes training.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mirrored pre/post-RZ rotations without alternating patterns  
 • “Ring + Shared-CRZ Ansatz” (Gen 24) used symmetric RZ layers and a single CRZ for opposite pairs but scored only 0.81; the uniform sharing lacked the expressivity boost of alternating parameters.  
- Complex mixed CZ/CNOT rings  
 • The “Circular Entangling Rotation Ansatz” (Gen 27) interleaved CZ and CNOT in a 120-layer ring, but high gate count and depth led to a drop to 0.75 combined, indicating overcomplex entanglers hamper learning.  
- Over-parameterization in entanglers  
 • “Eight-Qubit Layered Variational Ansatz” (Gen 26) with 200 parameters and 324 gates achieved 0.86—no better than the 116-parameter Gen 25—showing raw parameter count without structured sharing yields diminishing returns.  
- RX layer tying without targeted entanglement patterns  
 • Gen 28 tied RX rotations symmetrically across qubit pairs (92 params) but plateaued at 0.86, underperforming the alternating-ring approach, suggesting entanglement sharing is more impactful than rotation tying alone.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Clear, descriptive parameter naming  
 • Using “crz_ring_even”/“crz_ring_odd” in Gen 25 maps optimizer updates directly to alternating edges, improving gradient clarity and convergence consistency (zero parameter‐wise std).  
- Modular EVOLVE-BLOCK separation  
 • Isolating the ANSATZ_SPEC in a self-contained block (all top programs) ensures reproducible gate placements and prevents accidental cross-contamination with data encoding or training code.  
- Consistent rotation–entanglement ordering  
 • All highest‐scoring variants (seed, Gen 25, Gen 28) follow RY→entangler→RX→entangler; deviating from this order (e.g., inserting extra CZ/CNOT layers) correlates with performance drops.  
- Prefer parameterized two-qubit gates (CRZ) over unparameterized CZ/CNOT  
 • CRZ entanglers in Gen 25 and the seed grant trainable strength to each interaction, yielding higher expressivity per gate and smoother optimization compared to fixed-angle two-qubit gates.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Top combined scores align with targeted parameter sharing  
 • Gen 25 (0.87) > seed (0.85) > all other variants (0.75–0.86), demonstrating that the alternating shared-parameter ring plus skip-2 entanglement is the current optimal pattern.  
- Depth vs. score trade-off  
 • Increasing depth from ~100 to 136 layers (Gen 25) yielded a +0.02 gain in combined score, but further depth to 142 (Gen 26) saw no additional improvement, marking ~136 as a practical upper bound in this family.  
- Parameter count less predictive than sharing structure  
 • Despite counts ranging 92–200, combined scores cluster (0.86–0.87) when sharing is well structured; unshared or overly mirrored parameters (Gen 24, Gen 27) fall to 0.81 or below.  
- Generalization gap remains small with shared entanglers  
 • Both Gen 25 and Gen 28 report gaps of 0.06–0.08, whereas complex or non‐shared designs see gap stability but lower absolute accuracies, indicating shared entanglers help maintain generalization at high performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Extend alternating sharing to the skip‐2 layer. Instead of a single `crz_skip`, define two parameters—`crz_skip_even` and `crz_skip_odd`—and assign them alternately to your skip‐2 connections ([0,2] uses `crz_skip_even`, [1,3] uses `crz_skip_odd`, etc.). This mutation directly applies the proven benefit of alternating shared strengths from the ring entangler to your long‐range links, boosting expressivity while retaining regularization.  

2. Add a sparse skip‐3 entanglement block. After your existing skip‐2 layer, append a new CRZ layer on wires [0,3],[1,4],[2,5],[3,6],[4,7],[5,0],[6,1],[7,2] all sharing a single parameter `crz_skip3`. This low‐parameter, long‐range mixer builds on the success of sparse CRZ regularization, further enhancing global connectivity without overfitting.  

3. Insert a mid‐circuit RZ rotation layer before skip entanglement. Between your RX layer and the skip block, add per‐qubit RZ gates—`{"gate":"RZ","wire":i,"param":"rz_mid_i"}` for i=0…7. Evidence from earlier “lightweight mid‐layer” proposals shows that extra non‐commuting rotations between entanglers enrich the ansatz manifold and can accelerate convergence with minimal depth increase (~+8).  

4. Explore a parameter‐shared double “sandwich.” Duplicate the entire RY→CRZ ring→RX→CRZ skip block and append it to the EVOLVE‐BLOCK, reusing the same parameter names (`ry_0…ry_7`,`crz_ring_even/odd`,`rx_0…rx_7`,`crz_skip`). This doubles circuit depth while holding parameter count constant (~116), testing whether a deeper, fully tied stack can surpass the current generalization bound.  

5. Introduce mixed‐axis entanglement in your ring layer. Keep `crz_ring_even` on even edges but replace odd‐edge CRZ with CRY gates using a new parameter `cry_ring_odd`. By alternating two different trainable two‐qubit rotations (CRZ/CRY), you tap into richer entanglement axes—extending the alternating‐parameter principle that underpins Gen 25’s success.
