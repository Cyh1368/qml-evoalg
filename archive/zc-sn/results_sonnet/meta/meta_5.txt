# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Layered single-qubit rotations framing a linear CZ entanglement chain consistently deliver high accuracy (0.85 combined score). First realized in “Linear CZ-Entangled RY-RZ Ansatz” (Gen 1) and replicated in Generations 2–4 and the current best (“8-Qubit RY-RZ Chain Ansatz”), all achieving 0.94/0.89/0.87 train/val/test.
- Symmetric per-qubit RY → pre-RZ → CZ chain → post-RZ ordering provides sufficient expressivity to converge stably in ~750 steps with a small generalization gap (0.07).
- Strict consistency in ansatz specification keys (“wire” for single-qubit, “wires” for two-qubit) eliminates validation errors and enables seamless backend execution, as seen in the corrected Gen 1+ implementations.
- Delegating encoding, measurement, and optimization to a fixed backend while evolving only the ANSATZ_SPEC reduces implementation complexity and focuses improvements on circuit structure.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Inconsistent JSON keys (“wire” vs. “wires”) in the initial “8-Qubit Chain Entangling Ansatz” (Gen 0) caused validation failures and a 0.0 score, despite a shallow entangling pattern.
- A fixed, single-layer CZ chain without interleaved rotations lacked the expressivity to train, as evidenced by Gen 0’s failure.
- Over-parameterization via redundant rotation layers leads to extremely low parameter efficiency (0.01) across all high-scoring designs, indicating diminishing returns from extra parameters.
- Excessive circuit depth (99) and gate count (270) in the successful patterns suggest resource inefficiency, even though they do not directly hurt accuracy.

## Implementation Insights
- Centralizing the ansatz definition in ANSATZ_SPEC and invoking `_run(ANSATZ_SPEC, …)` in `run_experiment` ensures that only the circuit structure evolves, while training and metrics remain stable.
- Explicitly enumerating one RY and one RZ (pre + post) per qubit, then a nearest-neighbor CZ chain, yields 146 parameters; this pattern is directly tied to the 0.85 combined score in the best program.
- Consistent use of “wire” for single-qubit gates and “wires” for two-qubit gates prevents schema mismatches, as corrected after Gen 0.
- The current best program’s simplicity—no loops or conditionals around the spec, no dynamic gate choices—highlights that clarity and determinism in ansatz definitions aid reproducibility and stable convergence.

## Performance Analysis
- Transitioning from the failed Gen 0 (0.0 score) to Gen 1 with the layered RY–RZ–CZ pattern yielded an immediate jump to 0.85, maintained through Gen 4 and the current best.
- Across all successful runs: train_accuracy_mean=0.94, validation_accuracy_mean=0.89, test_accuracy_mean=0.87, generalization_gap=0.07, convergence≈750 steps—indicating a robust plateau in performance with the chosen ansatz.
- Depth (99) and gate count (270) are constant among high-scoring variants, suggesting that further accuracy gains will require structural innovations rather than scaling up circuit size.
- Parameter efficiency remains at 0.01 in all top designs, underscoring a strong correlation between over-parameterization and resource waste without additional accuracy benefit.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Replace a subset of fixed CZ gates with parameterized two-qubit gates (e.g., “CRZ” or “CRY”) in the linear chain. For example, swap every other CZ for a CRZ with its own trainable parameter (using the “wires” key), so you maintain depth and gate count but add tunable entanglement strength—leveraging the backend’s support for ALLOWED_PARAM_TWO_QUBIT_GATES to boost expressivity without over-parameterizing single-qubit layers.  
2. Close the linear chain into a ring by adding one CZ between qubits 7 and 0 at the end of the entangling block. This one extra two-qubit connection preserves the same single-qubit layering (RY→RZ pre→CZ chain→RZ post) but increases connectivity, potentially reducing the optimization barrier and shrinking the generalization gap while keeping the structure’s symmetry.  
3. Introduce parameter sharing for RZ rotations: tie “rz_pre_i” and “rz_post_i” parameters across symmetric qubit pairs (e.g., qubit 0 with 7, 1 with 6, etc.). By reducing the total number of unique parameters from 16 to 8, you test whether the same high accuracy (≈0.87 test) can be achieved with fewer parameters, improving parameter efficiency without changing the overall ansatz depth.  
4. Add a mid-circuit single-qubit RY layer immediately after the CZ chain and before the final RZ post-layer. This RY–RZ–CZ–RY–RZ pattern preserves the proven symmetric ordering but gives the circuit an extra non-commuting rotation group, which may enhance entanglement–rotation interplay and converge faster or yield a small accuracy boost beyond the 0.85 combined score.  
5. Experiment with alternating single-qubit rotation axes: replace the first RY layer on every even-indexed qubit with RX rotations (keeping odd qubits as RY). This mixed-axis initialization tests whether a richer initial parameter manifold (using both RX and RY) can accelerate convergence or improve validation accuracy, while still adhering to the successful layer-by-layer design and key consistency (“wire”).
