# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Alternating-strength ring entanglement with shared parameters  
  • Programs that interleave two shared CRZ parameters—`crz_ring_even` and `crz_ring_odd`—around the ring consistently reach a combined score of 0.89.  
    – Current Best (Gen44‐style) and Gen47 (“Parameter-Shared Layered Quantum Ansatz”) both employ this alternating ring and hit 0.89.  
    – In contrast, uniform-strength ring layers in Gen49 only yield 0.87.

- Sparse skip-2 CRZ layers with single shared parameter  
  • Injecting one CRZ “skip-2” layer per block (param `crz_skip`) closes the generalization gap to ~0.06–0.07 and maintains high test accuracy (0.89–0.92).  
    – The current best program converges in 150 steps with gap 0.06; Gen51 mirrors these benefits (gap 0.07, converge 150).  
    – Designs lacking this sparse long-range link (e.g. Gen49 without skip) see gap widen to 0.08.

- Parameter-shared block duplication  
  • Defining a compact 4-layer block and setting `ANSATZ_SPEC = _BLOCK + _BLOCK` (depth ≃ 220) boosts combined score by ~0.03 over a single block (Gen43→Gen44), with no parameter count increase.  
    – Current best program and Gen47 both use this trick to double depth, converge in 150–540 steps, and achieve top scores.  
    – Unguided depth increases without sharing (Gen48, Gen50) converge in 720+ and plateau at ≤ 0.87.

- Orthogonal single-qubit rotation sandwich (RY→CRZ→RX→CRZ(skip))  
  • Placing RY rotations before the first entangler and RX afterward maximizes expressivity.  
    – Current best: train 0.97/test 0.91; Gen47: train 0.95/test 0.92.  
    – Variants that mix in extra RZ layers (Gen49) or CZ gates (Gen43) underperform (combined 0.86–0.87).

## Ineffective Approaches

- Over-parameterized entanglement without hierarchical sharing  
  • Gen48 uses 140 parameters spread over deep 232-layer circuits but achieves only 0.85 combined and a large gap (0.11).  
  • Gen50 stacks three blocks for depth 304/660 gates with just shared nearest-neighbor CRZ, yet also stalls at 0.87.

- Extra mid-circuit RZ or mixed two-qubit gates  
  • Gen49’s mirrored RX–mid-circuit RZ layers and sparse diametric entanglers bump depth to 244 but only reach 0.87 combined.  
  • Introducing CZ alongside CRZ (Gen43) yields 0.86 and widens the gap to 0.07, diluting benefits of parameter sharing.

- Unguided depth increases without alternating or skip connections  
  • Gen48 and Gen50 deepen circuits without alternating-strength rings or skip-2 layers and see no score gains, slower convergence (> 400 steps), and overfitting.

## Implementation Insights

- Modular block definition and reuse  
  • All top performers define a single `_BLOCK` and then write `ANSATZ_SPEC = _BLOCK + _BLOCK` (or more copies) to decouple depth from unique parameters.  
    – The current best program’s 220-layer, 468-gate circuit reuses 19 parameter names twice to keep `n_params = 116`.

- Explicit, role-based parameter naming  
  • Parameters named `ry_i`, `rx_i`, `crz_ring_even`, `crz_ring_odd`, and `crz_skip` clarify each gate’s function and simplify sharing across blocks.  
    – This convention appears in Gen44 (current best), Gen47, and Gen51, all hitting 0.89 combined.

- Minimal wrapper overhead  
  • A thin `run_experiment(**kwargs)` that directly forwards `ANSATZ_SPEC` to `_run` ensures that performance differences trace purely to ansatz structure.

- Critical gate sequencing  
  • Consistently ordering RY→CRZ(even/odd)→RX→CRZ(skip) across top programs underpins their superior train/test splits and rapid convergence.

## Performance Analysis

- Combined score comparisons  
  • Best (current program & Gen47 & Gen51): 0.89  
  • Runner-ups with altered entanglers or extra layers (Gen49, Gen50): 0.87  
  • Over-parameterized or mixed-gate designs (Gen48, Gen43): 0.85–0.86

- Generalization gap trends  
  • Alternating ring + skip-2 pattern: gap 0.06–0.07 (current) and 0.03 (Gen47)  
  • Deep but unshared: gap 0.11 (Gen48) or 0.08 (Gen49)

- Convergence speed  
  • Hierarchical sharing + block duplication: ~150 steps (current, Gen51, Gen50)  
  • Extra mirroring without skip: 210 steps (Gen49)  
  • Over-parameterized deep: 540–720 steps (Gen47, Gen48)

- Parameter efficiency plateau  
  • All top‐scoring circuits use ~0.01 parameters per gate at ~116–128 parameters. Increasing beyond this yields diminishing returns, confirming a structured-sharing sweet spot.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. In Layer 4, split `crz_skip` into two shared parameters—`crz_skip_even` on skip‐2 edges [0,2],[2,4],[4,6],[6,0] and `crz_skip_odd` on [1,3],[3,5],[5,7],[7,1]—mirroring the ring’s alternating‐strength sharing. This preserves the 8-long skip links while borrowing the proven alternating pattern (current best & Gen47) to tighten long-range connectivity and potentially reduce the generalization gap.  

2. Within each block, append a diameter entanglement sub‐layer right after the skip connections: add CRZ gates on antipodal pairs [0,4]&[2,6] with parameter `crz_diam_even` and on [1,5]&[3,7] with `crz_diam_odd`. By introducing just two new shared parameters, you extend the alternating‐strength motif to global links—an approach that Gen35/Gen38 showed can boost mixing without blowing up parameter count.  

3. Enhance two-qubit axis diversity in the ring entangler: in Layer 2, replace every odd‐edge CRZ (wires [1,2],[3,4],…) with a CRY gate using a single shared `cry_ring_odd`, while keeping `crz_ring_even` on even edges. This has precedent in Gen25/Gen38 for enriching expressivity via mixed interaction axes, and it costs no extra depth or gate count.  

4. Probe deeper hierarchical sharing by tripling the block stack—set `ANSATZ_SPEC = _BLOCK + _BLOCK + _BLOCK`—while reusing all 116 parameter names. Top‐score circuits plateau at ~0.89 with two blocks, but this tests whether further depth under the proven RY→CRZ(even/odd)→RX→CRZ(skip) structure can edge the combined score higher without sacrificing the ~150‐step convergence.  

5. Insert a lightweight, mirror-paired mid-block RZ phase layer between Layer 3 (RX) and Layer 4 (skip): define four shared parameters `rz_mid_0` (for qubits 0&7), `rz_mid_1` (1&6), `rz_mid_2` (2&5), and `rz_mid_3` (3&4), then do `_BLOCK = […] + _MID_RZ + […]` within each block. Prior ablations show that structured phase control sharpens local expressivity and can accelerate convergence with only +4 parameters.
