<DESCRIPTION>
The previous generation split the single shared skip-2 parameter into two shared parameters (crz_skip_even/odd), which increased train accuracy but widened the train-validation gap (0.08) compared to the single-parameter version (0.06 gap, higher score 0.87). This suggests the added expressivity from splitting is overfitting rather than helping generalization, and doing it homogeneously with CRZ only explores rotations around a single axis (Z).

To recover generalization while keeping the useful long-range connectivity, I alternate the rotation axis on the skip-2 layer: keep CRZ on the even-indexed skip edges (param `crz_skip_even`) but switch the odd-indexed skip edges to CRY (new param `cry_skip_odd`). This introduces axis diversity (mixing Z and Y controlled rotations) in the same sparse, parameter-shared long-range entangling layer. Axis diversity increases the Hilbert-space coverage of the entangling layer without adding new independent parameters (still just 2 params for 8 gates), which should smooth the optimization landscape and potentially reduce the generalization gap while preserving or improving validation/test accuracy, consistent with the reasoning that mixing non-commuting rotation generators (Y and Z) creates richer entanglement structure than a single-axis-only approach.
</DESCRIPTION>
