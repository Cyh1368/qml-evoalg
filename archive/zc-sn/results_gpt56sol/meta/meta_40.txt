# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Dual-phase RZ “sandwich” around a single nearest-neighbor CZ chain with full post-entanglement parameterization, parity-shared pre-phase, and per-qubit RY frontend (Seed program): depth 99, 270 gates → combined 0.87 (train 0.94/val 0.89/test 0.89), gap 0.05.  
- Independent RY rotations on all 8 qubits as an input encoding layer appears in all top performers (Seed, Gen 39, Gen 41) and correlates with high train accuracy (≥ 0.93).  
- Parity-shared pre-entanglement RZ using two parameters (“rz_pre_even”/“rz_pre_odd”) cuts parameter count to 110 while preserving validation/test accuracy (Seed vs. full 8-param pre-phase in earlier designs).  
- Linear nearest-neighbor CZ connectivity (7-gate open chain) balances expressivity and hardware efficiency, outperforming ring or chorded layouts in combined score and gate count.

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Long-range “chord” entanglers (Gen 38 “parity_shared_chords”): depth 129, 390 gates → combined 0.77, test 0.85. Dense CZ+chords increased overhead without boosting score.  
- Dual CZ matchings (disjoint + full-chain) in Gen 39 (“dual_parity_phase_sandwich”): although stable training (gap 0.02) and combined 0.87, test 0.85 lags seed’s 0.89, and depth (132→132) plus 408 gates incur noise.  
- Multi-block entanglement (Gen 41 “merge_rz_bridge”): depth 160, 468 gates → combined 0.87 but slow convergence (~1,170 steps) and no test-accuracy gain.  
- Over-parameterized second CZ sandwich (Gen 33/42): both deliver only combined 0.77 at 408 gates, showing that repeating CZ chains without reducing depth harms performance.

## Implementation Insights  
- Block-structured ANSATZ_SPEC (RY → RZ pre → CZ chain → RZ post) in the Seed program yields the lowest depth (99) and gate count (270), facilitating compiler optimizations and clearer debugging.  
- Descriptive parameter naming (“ry_0…7”, “rz_pre_even/odd”, “rz_post_0…7”) clarifies parity sharing vs. full parameterization and reduces binding errors, as seen in the Seed vs. Gen 39’s more opaque naming.  
- Strict gate-set discipline (only RY, RZ, CZ) aligns with hardware-native operations and contributes to the gate-count “sweet spot” (~270) for optimal fidelity.  
- Replacing a second CZ layer with a full per-qubit post-phase (Seed’s 8 distinct rz_post parameters) enhances test accuracy over parity-shared post-phases (Gen 39) while adding only 6 extra gates.

## Performance Analysis  
- The Seed program (depth 99, 270 gates) matches the top combined 0.87 of heavier designs but outperforms them on test accuracy (0.89 vs. ≤ 0.87) and converges faster (~450 steps).  
- Designs above ~390 gates (Gen 38 at 390, Gen 39/42 at 408, Gen 41 at 468) plateau at combined ≤ 0.87, confirming diminishing returns beyond the ~270-gate sweet spot.  
- Parity-shared RZ sandwiches with only two pre-phase parameters (Seed and Gen 39) both hit 0.87, but full post-phase in the Seed yields a higher test (0.89 vs. 0.85).  
- Narrowing the generalization gap (Seed gap 0.05 vs. Gen 41 gap 0.07) while maintaining a low gate count drives better hardware feasibility and model robustness.
