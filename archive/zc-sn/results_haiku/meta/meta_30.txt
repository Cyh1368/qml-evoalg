# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Pairwise-shared RY rotations (“ry_a”/“ry_b”) across qubit indices, as in the Paired-Parameter Layered CZ/CRZ Ansatz (Gen 24) and the current Seed program, consistently yielded a combined score of 0.91 and stable convergence (~330 steps).  
- Two-stage RZ layers bracketing the entanglement block (“rz_pre_a/b” before CZs, “rz_post_a/b” after CRZs) produced a low generalization gap (0.02) at depth 154 and gate count 312 (Gen 24 and Seed).  
- Alternating two-parameter reverse CRZ chain—using “crz_rev_a” on odd reverse links and “crz_rev_b” on even links—enhanced expressivity without increasing parameter count, lifting performance to 0.91 combined versus 0.90 for single-parameter chains.  
- Maintaining a strictly linear CZ entanglement chain (seven CZs) rather than introducing skip-links preserved circuit simplicity and reliability, matching the best-in-class performance of the Seed program.

## Ineffective Approaches
- Expanding to a three-stage mid-circuit RZ structure (Phase-Controlled Ansatz, Gen 26) increased depth to 160 and parameters to 56, but only reached a combined score of 0.90 and test accuracy of 0.88, indicating diminishing returns from extra phase layers.  
- Over-parameterized or untethered reverse-chain designs (e.g., dual-param reverse CRZ without strict sharing in Gen 23) converged very quickly (~120 steps) yet suffered a larger gap (0.04) and lower test accuracy.  
- Introducing non-linear or skip-link CZ augmentations (Gen 30) broke validation entirely—zero combined score—demonstrating that deviation from a simple linear chain can violate implicit ansatz constraints.  
- Simply increasing gate count or depth without targeted parameter-sharing (Shared-Parameter Layered Entanglement, Gen 28, and Hybrid CZ-CRZ, Gen 29) yielded only 0.89 combined, with slower convergence (~300 steps) and no accuracy gains.

## Implementation Insights
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC as a clear JSON-style list with explicit parameter labels (“ry_a”, “ry_b”, “rz_pre_a”, “rz_post_b”, “crz_rev_a”, “crz_rev_b”) enforces consistent sharing and maps directly to high parameter efficiency (0.02).  
- A modular block structure—initial RY → pre-RZ → CZ chain → reverse CRZ → post-RZ—mirrors the Seed program’s layout, easing both evolution and debugging of ansatz changes.  
- Alternating parameter assignments across wires can be automated (e.g., via list comprehensions cycling through param names), reducing boilerplate and human error.  
- Tying every pair of qubits to the same rotation parameter (8 wires → 2 shared params per layer) halves unique parameters without loss of expressivity, as evidenced by the Seed’s 50-parameter design matching or exceeding larger circuits.

## Performance Analysis
- The top-performing designs (Gen 24 and Seed) achieved 0.91 combined at exactly 50 parameters, depth 154, and 312 gates. Designs deviating above or below these specs underperformed (Gen 26: 0.90 at 56 params/160 depth; Gen 28/29: 0.89 at 50 params/154 depth but with less optimal CRZ sharing).  
- Parameter efficiency (n_params / gate_count ≈ 0.02) is necessary but must be paired with targeted patterns (two-stage RZ, alternating CRZ) to realize top scores.  
- A generalization gap of ~0.02 (Seed) strikes a better test-accuracy balance (0.90) than a zero gap with lower test accuracy (Gen 26’s 0.00 gap, 0.88 test).  
- Convergence around 300–330 steps (Seed/Gen 24) correlates with best generalization; ultra-fast (< 200 steps) or very slow (> 350) training often coincides with larger gaps or lower final accuracy.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a mid-circuit phase layer: between the CZ chain and the reverse‐CRZ block, add RZ gates with two new shared parameters “rz_mid_a” on qubits [0,2,4,6] and “rz_mid_b” on [1,3,5,7]. This extends the proven two‐stage RZ sandwich to three stages at minimal cost (just 8 extra gates, +2 params), giving the circuit additional phase flexibility without appreciably increasing depth or gates.

2. Lightly parameterize the forward entanglement pass: replace every second CZ in the linear chain—specifically links [1,2], [3,4], and [5,6]—with CRZ gates sharing a single new parameter “crz_fwd.” This mirrors the seed’s successful reverse‐CRZ sharing up-front, adding only one extra parameter to probe whether modest tunability in the forward chain further boosts expressivity per parameter.

3. Append a global mixing layer after the final post-RZ block: apply a shared RX rotation “rx_global” on all qubits, then a shared RY rotation “ry_global.” These two parameters introduce a lightweight, high‐leverage mixer just before measurement—leveraging the strength of shared single-qubit rotations to better explore the Hilbert space without significantly altering depth or gate count.

4. Prepend an auxiliary initial phase block: before the existing RY layer, insert RZ rotations using two new shared parameters “rz_init_a” on [0,2,4,6] and “rz_init_b” on [1,3,5,7]. This small 8-gate addition enriches the circuit’s early‐stage phase landscape, potentially smoothing optimization dynamics and improving convergence consistency, while preserving the seed’s highly efficient pairwise-sharing scheme.

5. Explore richer reverse-CRZ asymmetry by cycling three shared parameters: instead of just “crz_rev_a”/“crz_rev_b,” introduce “crz_rev_c” and assign the seven reverse links in repeating order (a, b, c, a, b, c, a). This +1-parameter tweak tests whether a slight increase in reverse‐pass variety—beyond two distinct phases—can further tighten the generalization gap without sacrificing the low‐parameter regime that underpins the seed’s success.
