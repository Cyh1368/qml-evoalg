# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Alternating shared RY rotations across qubit pairs (parameters “ry_a”/“ry_b”)  
  • Seed (Combined 0.91) vs. Gen 20/23 (0.90) shows that this two-parameter alternation consistently boosts expressivity.  
- Two-stage RZ layers bracketing entanglement (“rz_pre_a/b” before CZ, “rz_post_a/b” after CRZ)  
  • Seed converges in 180 steps with a 0.01 gap, outperforming Gen 22/25 (depth 172, 270–300 steps, gap 0.02).  
- Bidirectional CZ + single shared-CRZ reverse chain  
  • Seed’s one “crz_rev” on seven reverse links yields efficiency 0.02 and score 0.91, vs. Gen 23’s two-parameter reverse CRZ (gap 0.04).  
- Compact ansatz (44 params, depth 154, 312 gates)  
  • Matches or exceeds larger designs (Gen 22/25: 62–68 params, 420+ gates) while improving convergence speed and generalization.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-parameterized entanglement or mixing layers  
  • Gen 22 (62 params) and Gen 25 (68 params) both stall at 0.90 combined, with deeper circuits (≥172) and slower convergence.  
- Skip-link or non-linear entanglement patterns  
  • Gen 27’s skip-link CZ drops to 0.89, despite zero gap, showing linear chains plus reverse CRZ are more robust.  
- Untied two-qubit rotations or dual-parameter reverse chains  
  • Gen 23 (56 params, 123 depth) converges quickly (~120 steps) but suffers a larger gap (0.04) and lower test accuracy (0.92 → 0.91).  
- Excessive depth/gate count without parameter tying  
  • Designs >166 depth/336 gates fail to surpass the 0.90 threshold despite more parameters.  

## Implementation Insights  
- JSON-style ANSATZ_SPEC with consistent labels (“ry_a”/“ry_b”, “rz_pre_*/rz_post_*”, “crz_rev”)  
  • Enforces sharing, simplifies maintenance, and directly maps to high param efficiency and stable convergence in Seed.  
- Modular block structure in the spec  
  • Clear segments—initial RY, pre-RZ, CZ chain, reverse CRZ, post-RZ—correlate with reproducible training dynamics and mirror the Seed’s best-in-class performance.  
- Single shared-CRZ parameter for reverse entanglement  
  • Maximizes entangling expressivity per parameter (efficiency 0.02) without overfitting, a key factor in Seed’s 0.91 score.  
- Minimal wrapper (`run_experiment(ANSATZ_SPEC, **kwargs)`)  
  • Keeps ansatz definition isolated from training logic, ensuring consistent benchmarking across experiments.  

## Performance Analysis  
- Seed vs. 0.90-scoring designs  
  • Seed (0.91 combined, gap 0.01, 180 steps) outperforms Gen 20/22/23/25 (0.90 combined, gaps 0.02–0.04, 120–300 steps).  
- Depth/gate count trade-off  
  • Optimal region around 154 depth/312 gates; deeper circuits (>166/336) do not improve scores.  
- Convergence dynamics  
  • Balanced designs (Seed: 180 steps) yield better generalization than ultra-fast (Gen 23: 120 steps, gap 0.04) or slow (>270 steps) convergers.  
- Parameter efficiency threshold  
  • Maintaining efficiency ≥ 0.02 (44–50 params) is necessary but not sufficient; combining with two-stage RZ and single shared CRZ yields the top score.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a “mid-RZ” stage between the CZ chain and reverse CRZ: add two new shared parameters “rz_mid_a” on qubits [0,2,4,6] and “rz_mid_b” on [1,3,5,7]. This three-stage RZ sandwich (pre-, mid-, post-entanglement) builds directly on the Seed’s two-stage RZ success, adding just 2 parameters to enhance phase flexibility without appreciably altering depth or gate count.  
2. Lightly parameterize the forward entanglement block by replacing every second CZ with a shared-parameter CRZ “crz_fwd” (e.g. CRZ on [1,2],[3,4],[5,6], keep CZ on the other links). Mirroring the efficiency of the single shared “crz_rev,” this adds only one extra parameter to probe whether minimal tunable rotations up-front can boost expressivity per parameter.  
3. Append a two-gate global mixing layer after the final “rz_post” block: apply RX on all qubits with parameter “rx_global,” then RY with “ry_global.” This 2-parameter shared mixer capitalizes on the proven power of shared rotations to maximize Hilbert-space exploration immediately before measurement, at minimal cost to depth or gate count.  
4. Split the reverse CRZ chain into two shared parameters—“crz_rev_a” governing odd links ([7→6],[5→4],[3→2],[1→0]) and “crz_rev_b” on even links ([6→5],[4→3],[2→1]). By adding just one extra parameter, this slight asymmetry tests whether richer reverse-pass variety can tighten the generalization gap while preserving fast convergence.  
5. Prepend a lightweight initial mixing block: after the existing “RY” layer add alternating shared rotations “ry_c” on qubits [0,2,4,6] and “ry_d” on [1,3,5,7], followed by a single shared “rz_extra” on all qubits. These 3 extra parameters boost the Seed’s effective RY/RZ alternation at the circuit start, potentially improving early-stage expressivity and smoothing out optimization dynamics.
