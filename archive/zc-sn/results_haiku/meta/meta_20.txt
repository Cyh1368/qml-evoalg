# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Pairwise sharing of single-qubit rotations  
  • Across the top programs (Gen 11 “Seed”/current best, Gen 17 “Minimal-Parameter Shared-Entanglement Ansatz”), alternating qubits share RY (“ry_a”/“ry_b”) and RZ (“rz_pre_*”/“rz_post_*”) parameters.  
  • This pattern yields high parameter efficiency (0.02), rapid convergence (∼180 steps), combined scores ≥ 0.88 and minimal generalization gaps (0.00–0.01).

- Bidirectional entanglement chains with shared CRZ reverse pass  
  • Gen 11 and Gen 17 both use a zero-cost forward CZ chain (7 gates) followed by a single shared-parameter CRZ in the reverse direction.  
  • Gen 11 achieves a combined 0.91 score and 0.01 gap with only one “crz_rev” parameter, outperforming designs with untied two-qubit rotations.

- Two-stage RZ layers around entanglement  
  • Distinct “rz_pre_a/b” before and “rz_post_a/b” after entangling blocks (Gen 11) improve expressivity per parameter and stabilize training.  
  • This separation correlates with the fastest convergence (180 steps) among all evaluated ansätze.

- Parameter efficiency threshold ≥ 0.02  
  • All designs hitting ≥ 0.02 efficiency (44–50 params) score ≥ 0.88 and generalize tightly (gap ≤ 0.02).  
  • Falling below this threshold (e.g., Gen 13 with 38 params but different sharing) yields slower convergence and lower scores.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Dense or untied two-qubit parameterization  
  • Ring-Cross Ansatz (Gen 16) and other CRY-heavy patterns violate validation rules and score 0.0; earlier Gen 8 dense CRY scheme scored 0.76 with a 0.12 gap.  
  • Over-parameterized two-qubit gates inflate depth without boosting accuracy, leading to overfitting or outright invalid circuits.

- Over-sharing in a single operational block  
  • Gen 18 “dual_grouping_lightweight_ansatz” collapses rotations and entanglement into one block, omits required encoding/measurement steps, and scores 0.0 due to validation failures.

- Excessive depth/gate count without efficient tying  
  • Gen 12’s 111-depth, 420-gate design with 86 untied parameters only reaches 0.87 combined and 0.06 gap, converging in 540 steps—diminishing returns from brute-force layering.

## Implementation Insights

- JSON-style ANSATZ_SPEC with uniform parameter labels  
  • Consistent naming (“ry_a”/“ry_b”, “rz_pre_a”/“rz_pre_b”, “rz_post_a”/“rz_post_b”) across wires succinctly enforces sharing and simplifies code maintenance (Gen 11, Gen 17, Gen 19).

- Clear block structure in ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • Separating: (1) shared RY, (2) pre-RZ, (3) CZ chain, (4) shared CRZ reverse pass, (5) post-RZ—enhances readability, encourages modular tuning, and correlates with top performance (Gen 11).

- Single shared CRZ parameter for reverse entanglement  
  • One “crz_rev” reused across seven reverse-chain gates injects nontrivial correlations at minimal parameter cost, boosting expressivity without sacrificing efficiency.

- Minimal wrapper around ANSATZ_SPEC  
  • The simple `run_experiment(**kwargs)` forwarder keeps training/measurement fixed and isolates the ansatz definition, reducing integration complexity and ensuring consistent benchmarking.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Insert a mid-circuit RZ layer between the forward CZ chain and the reverse CRZ pass, using two new shared parameters “rz_mid_a” on qubits [0,2,4,6] and “rz_mid_b” on [1,3,5,7]. This creates a three-stage RZ structure (pre-, mid-, post-entanglement) that builds on the Gen 11 two-stage success and adds only 2 parameters to boost phase flexibility without sacrificing efficiency.  

2. Hybridize the forward entanglement block by replacing every other CZ with a shared-parameter CRZ gate “crz_fwd” (e.g. use CZ on [0,1], CRZ on [1,2], CZ on [2,3], …, CRZ on [5,6]). This lightly parameterizes the forward pass to probe whether minimal tunable two-qubit rotations up front can further enhance expressivity per parameter, mirroring the efficiency of the reverse shared CRZ.  

3. Augment the zero-cost CZ chain with extra skip-link CZs between qubit pairs (0–2, 2–4, 4–6, 6–0) in the forward block. These additional entangling edges leverage the original linear topology but broaden long-range connectivity at zero parameter cost, potentially tightening nonlocal correlations as suggested by star/fan-out motifs.  

4. Append a two-gate global mixing layer after the final RZ block: apply RX on all qubits with a single parameter “rx_global”, then RY with “ry_global”. This 2-parameter shared mixing mirrors the proven power of shared RY/RZ layers, maximizing Hilbert-space exploration just before measurement while keeping the total parameter count minimal.  

5. Split the reverse CRZ chain into two shared parameters—“crz_rev_a” on the odd-indexed links ([7→6], [5→4], [3→2], [1→0]) and “crz_rev_b” on the even links ([6→5], [4→3], [2→1]). By doubling entangling-angle variety with only one extra parameter, this builds on the original single “crz_rev” economy to test whether slight asymmetry can further boost expressivity without compromising convergence speed.
