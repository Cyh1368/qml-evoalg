# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Three-stage phase layering with mid-circuit reuse: the current best program (Seed/Gen 50) uses shared pre-entanglement RZ parameters twice (pre- and mid-circuit) plus a distinct post-entanglement RZ layer. This yielded the top combined score of 0.91 (train 0.92/val 0.94/test 0.89, gap 0.02) and convergence in 180 steps—beating Gen 64’s 0.90 (no mid-circuit reuse) and Gen 73’s 0.87.  
- Alternating reverse-chain CRZ parameters (`crz_rev_a`/`crz_rev_b`): implemented in the best program and in Gen 62, this pattern consistently cuts convergence to ~180 steps. By contrast, deep or non-alternating designs (Gen 73, Gen 67, Gen 66) require 420–720 steps.  
- Balanced parameter budget (~50 unique parameters) with pairwise sharing: the best program’s 50 parameters (versus 38 in Gen 66/69 or 86 in Gen 67) maximize expressivity without over- or under-parameterizing, achieving the highest parameter efficiency (0.02) and combined score (0.91).  
- Linear forward CZ chain plus alternating reverse CRZ: this structured entanglement—used by the best program—outperforms ring or non-linear topologies (Gen 73’s ring wrap achieved only 0.87) and supports a compact depth (160) and gate count (360).

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Over-deep, high-gate circuits with aggressive layer sharing (Gen 66 “double_layer_shared_params” and Gen 69 “dual_layer_param_shared”): depth 256, 540 gates, 38 parameters → combined 0.89, ~720 optimization steps. Extra layers add optimization cost without score gain.  
- Over-parameterization without mid-circuit reuse (Gen 67 “restore_linear_crz_chain”): 86 parameters, depth 154, 312 gates → combined 0.86 and generalization gap 0.03. Simply adding parameters fails without structured phase layering.  
- Ring-topology wrap (Gen 73 “ring_topology_wrap_cz_crz”): depth 138, 372 gates, 50 parameters → combined 0.87, gap 0.07, ~420 steps. Deviating from the linear+alternating-CRZ backbone harmed generalization and convergence.  
- Two-layer full parameter reuse (Gen 66/69): halved parameter count (38) but doubled depth/gates → slower training and plateaued at 0.89 combined, showing that extreme sharing across layers can undercut expressivity.

## Implementation Insights  
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC list: defining each gate as `{"gate":…, "wires"/"wire":…, "param":…}` makes it trivial to insert or reorder RZ layers, CZ chains, or CRZ alternations.  
- Suffix-based pairing for parameter names (`_a`, `_b`): used for `ry_a`/`ry_b`, `rz_pre_a`/`rz_pre_b`, `crz_rev_a`/`crz_rev_b` to enforce consistent sharing patterns and enable compiler optimizations.  
- Zero-cost mid-circuit phase injection: reusing `rz_pre_a`/`rz_pre_b` in the middle of the circuit (between CRZ reverse chain and final RZ) adds expressivity without new parameters—key differentiator of the best program.  
- Manual alternation in reverse entanglement: explicitly listing CRZ gates with `crz_rev_a` on even-indexed links and `crz_rev_b` on odd links delivers stable gradients and rapid convergence.

## Performance Analysis  
- Peak combined score (0.91) aligns with depth ~160 and gate count ~360 in the best program; shallower (<140, Gen 73) or deeper (>250, Gen 66/69) circuits plateau at 0.87–0.90.  
- Convergence steps strongly correlate with reverse-chain alternation plus mid-circuit reuse: designs lacking one or both elements need ≥420 steps (Gen 73: 420; Gen 67: 540; Gen 66: 720), while the best program converges in 180 steps.  
- Parameter efficiency (combined score / n_params) peaks at ~0.02 in the best program and Gen 64 (50 params), versus 0.01–0.015 in over- or under-parameterized variants (Gen 67, Gen 66/69).  
- Generalization gap minimizes (0.02) only when three-stage RZ layering and alternating CRZ are combined; other patterns yield gaps of 0.01 (over-shared, 38 params) or up to 0.07 (missing mid-circuit reuse or ring entanglement).
