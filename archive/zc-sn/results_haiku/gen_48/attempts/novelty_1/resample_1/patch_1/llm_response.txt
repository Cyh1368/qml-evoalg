Inspired by the previous programs' success with parameter sharing and linear chain topologies, I identified key patterns:

1. **From prior programs**: Linear chain CZ entanglement (0-1-2-3-4-5-6-7) consistently delivers ~0.91 score with good parameter efficiency.
2. **Current program weakness**: Score dropped to 0.90 despite 62 parameters and longer convergence (450 steps vs 150-330), suggesting overfitting or inefficient parameter utilization. The mixed CRZ/CRX reverse chain may introduce spectral noise without sufficient benefit.
3. **Novel insight**: Instead of separate rz_post_a/rz_post_b parameters (16 gates, 2 parameters), reuse rz_pre_a/rz_pre_b for the final layer. This creates a "fusion" point where pre- and post-rotation logic share parameters, reducing total unique parameters while testing whether a tighter 2-parameter phase sandwich still captures essential dynamics.

**Key differences from prior work**:
- Eliminate mixed CRZ/CRX and return to pure CRZ reverse chain (better learned pattern from prior programs at 50-62 parameters)
- Merge pre/post RZ phases by reusing rz_pre_a and rz_pre_b at the end
- Reduce parameter count from 62 to 54, improving efficiency and convergence speed
- Maintain proven linear chain CZ topology
- Simplify mid-circuit phase by using single unified even/odd split (rz_mid_a/rz_mid_b instead of rz_mid_even/rz_mid_odd for consistency with binary parameter naming)

This is fundamentally different from prior approaches because it fuses architectural stages through parameter reuse rather than eliminating gates, preserving expressivity while reducing overfitting risk.
