# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Symmetry-aware parameter sharing by board region (corners, edges, center)  
  • Current best (“crz_shared_symmetry_mixer” style) uses 3 keys for RY and 3 for RZ per block, 54 params → combined 0.48, gap 0.02, depth 46, gates 237.  
  • Generation 13 (shared_crx_with_fixed_cz) also groups rotations by region (54 params) and achieves combined 0.46.  

- Fixed CZ entangling layers in place of parametrized two-qubit rotations  
  • Current best replaces trainable CRZ/CRX with eight fixed CZs/block → 25% fewer gates (237 vs. 315) and depth drop from 76 to 46, boosting combined from 0.42 (Gen 12) to 0.48.  

- Lightweight class-specific output rotations  
  • Adding small “ry_*_out” adjustments per symmetry class (9 gates/block) is present in the best program (combined 0.48) and in Gen 17 (shared_rz_final_sharpening) to improve readout separation.  

- Three feature-map uploads × two ansatz repeats  
  • All top performers (current best, Gen 10) use N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2. This layout balances expressivity with trainability and underpins low generalization gaps (~0.02).

## Ineffective Approaches

- Parametrized two-qubit rotations (CRX/CRY/CRZ)  
  • Gen 14 (shared_crx_cry_symmetry_compact) includes CRX/CRY entanglers, yielding depth 76, gates 315, combined 0.44, test 0.35 and a 0.12 gap—no expressivity gain but higher vanishing-gradient risk.  

- Excessive circuit depth and gate count (≥70 depth, ≥290 gates)  
  • Gen 12 and Gen 14 depth ≥76 stalled at combined ≤0.44; Gen 15 (none) depth ~52 but only 48 params still fell to combined 0.38, showing that depth alone cannot compensate for poor entanglement/parameter balance.  

- Over-sharing without entanglement diversity  
  • Gen 15 (48 params, minimal CZ ring+hub) achieved minimal overfitting but low accuracy (combined 0.38); too few unique parameters limited pattern discrimination.  

## Implementation Insights

- Decoupled ANSATZ_SPEC plus validate_ansatz_spec for rapid ansatz iteration  
  • All top designs swap only the ANSATZ_SPEC block; validate_ansatz_spec catches wiring and parameter mistakes before training.  

- O(1) parameter lookup via ordered_unique_keys and key-to-index mapping  
  • The best program builds key_to_position once per block, avoiding Python‐level searches inside apply_ansatz_block and reducing overhead during backprop.  

- Fixed-entangler CZ layers shrink execution graph  
  • Replacing trainable two-qubit gates with plain qml.CZ reduces tape operations from 315→237, accelerates autograd, and lowers gradient saturation as seen in the current best.  

- AnnotatedQueue for accurate circuit_metadata  
  • Using qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue to record operations/depth ensures precise measurement of circuit complexity (depth 46, gates 237) that directly correlates with performance.

## Performance Analysis

- Best at ~54 total parameters, moderate depth (~46), low gate count (237)  
  • Current best: combined 0.48, train 0.32, val 0.44, test 0.33, gap 0.02, eff 0.009.  
  • Models with 66–72 params or depth >70 (Gen 12, 14, 16, 17) saw combined ≤0.46 and gaps ≥0.10.  

- Generalization gap minimal when depth ≤53 and parameter budget 50–60  
  • Current best and Gen 10 both hold gap ~0.02. Increasing depth or trainable two-qubit gates inflates gaps to 0.12–0.17.  

- Parameter efficiency peaks with compact, symmetry-shared designs  
  • Combined 0.48 at 54 params → efficiency ~0.009, outperforming Gen 13 (54 params, eff 0.01) and Gen 16 (72 params, eff 0.01) despite similar param counts.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Cycle through three disjoint CZ‐only entangling motifs across the uploads. Instead of the same eight CZ edges in each block, define three fixed CZ patterns (e.g. drop one ring edge and add one hub edge per upload) so each feature-map re-upload sees a different sparse connectivity. This preserves the low two-qubit gate count (≈8 CZs/block) while enriching mixing geometry—an approach known to boost expressivity without adding parameters.  

2. Append a lightweight shared RZ_out adjustment immediately after the existing RY_out layer. Mirror the “ry_*_out” pattern by adding rz_corner_out, rz_edge_out, and rz_center_out (nine additional single-qubit gates, three params) to the end of each block. Empirical results from Gen 17 show that small class-shared phase shifts sharpen decision boundaries in the measurement basis at minimal cost.  

3. Refine the corner symmetry class into two diagonal subclasses to capture distinct “three-in-a-row” features. Replace ry_corner/rz_corner with ry_corner_a/rz_corner_a for qubits (0,4) and ry_corner_b/rz_corner_b for qubits (2,6), while keeping edge and center parameters shared. This only adds four parameters (bringing the block to ∼58 params) yet leverages existing symmetry sharing to better discriminate diagonal win patterns.  

4. Introduce three learnable scalar weights in the class_expectations mapping to re-weight qubit groups per output. Define alpha_edge, alpha_corner, alpha_center and compute  
   cross = α_edge·mean(z_edges),  
   circle = α_corner·mean(z_corners),  
   draw = α_center·z_center.  
This adds three parameters at the readout stage and lives entirely in the autograd graph, giving the network flexibility to emphasize or attenuate region-specific signals without altering circuit depth.  

5. Augment each ansatz block with two fixed diagonal CZs linking the center qubit to opposite corners. For example, add CZ([0,8]) and CZ([4,8]) (or their rotated equivalents) alongside the existing ring+hub CZs. This minimal connectivity boost (2 CZs/block, +2 operations) targets under-represented diagonal mixing paths essential for “three-in-a-row” detection, while keeping total gate count comfortably under ~260.
