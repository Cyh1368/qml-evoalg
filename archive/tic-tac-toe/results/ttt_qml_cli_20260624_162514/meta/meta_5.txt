# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Symmetry-based parameter sharing with a compact block size  
  • Current best: 54 total parameters (9 per block × 3 uploads × 2 repeats), depth 46, gate count 237 → combined score 0.48, generalization gap 0.02  
  • Generation 10 (“crz_shared_symmetry_mixer”): 72 params, depth 76, combined 0.47, gap 0.02  
  Pattern: grouping corners, edges, and center under shared RY/RZ keys preserves essential degrees of freedom while cutting parameter count by ∼25% vs. earlier designs (Gen 8: 84 params).

- Fixed CZ entanglers in place of parametrized two-qubit rotations  
  • Current best: eight CZ gates per block, no CRX/CRZ → depth 46, gate_count 237  
  • Gen 12 (“crz_sym_shared_ansatz”): eight CRZ gates + eight shared RY/RZ per block → depth 76, gate_count 315, gap 0.17, combined 0.42  
  Pattern: removing learnable two-qubit angles reduces circuit depth/gate count without losing mixing power, improving stability and generalization.

- Lightweight final single-qubit RY adjustments per symmetry class  
  • Present in current best, Gen 10, and Gen 8, consistently in higher-scoring variants  
  Pattern: adding a small “ry_*_out” rotation layer focuses class separation in readout, boosting validation/test accuracy over designs lacking an output-specific mixer.

- Moderate repetition and upload count balances expressivity and trainability  
  • All top performers use N_UPLOADS = 3, N_REPEATS = 2  
  • Depth reduction from ~76→46 correlated with stable training curves and low generalization gaps  
  Pattern: three feature-map uploads with two ansatz repeats each hit a sweet spot between under- and over-parameterization.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Parametrized two-qubit gates (CRX, CRY, CRZ) drove up depth/gate count without accuracy gains  
  • Gen 9 (“symmetry_tuned_crx_mix”): 78 params, 315 gates, depth 53, test 0.38  
  • Gen 12: 66 params, 315 gates, depth 76, test 0.48 but high gap 0.17  
  Issue: extra two-qubit parameters expand the search space and add vanishing-gradient risks yet fail to unlock expressivity.

- Overly deep circuits (depth ≥ 70) with high gate counts (≥ 290) underfit or converge poorly  
  • Gen 11: depth 52, gate_count 291, test 0.30, combined 0.41  
  • Gen 8: depth 76, gate_count 315, test 0.50 but gap 0.10  
  Issue: depth alone does not guarantee expressivity; without careful entanglement/parameter budgeting, deeper designs stall at or below chance.

- Excessive parameter sharing across layers without varying entanglement textures  
  • Gen 12’s aggressive sharing trimmed to 66 parameters but yielded modest train 0.32 and gap 0.17  
  Issue: too much reuse of the same angles restricts the circuit’s ability to distinguish complex patterns.

- Dropping feature-map uploads reduces convergence prospects  
  • None of the successful runs reduce N_UPLOADS below 3; attempts to streamline uploads would risk losing essential input encoding depth.  

## Implementation Insights

- Decoupling ansatz specification (ANSATZ_SPEC) from application logic streamlines design iteration  
  • All top variants simply replace the gate list; validation code auto-checks connectivity, wire indices, and parameter keys.

- Fixed-entangler CZ layers shrink the QuantumScript tape and improve autograd performance  
  • Current best’s circuit_metadata: 237 operations vs. Gen 12’s 315, depth 46 vs. 76  
  Impact: faster backprop, reduced simulation overhead, and fewer gradient saturation issues.

- Ordered unique parameter keys with block offsets enable efficient re-uploading  
  • N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK = 9 in current best; indexing via a dict and offset arithmetic ensures O(1) lookup per gate, minimizing Python overhead in apply_ansatz_block.

- Class-specific Z-expectation aggregation remains critical for readout  
  • All higher-scoring programs (Gen 8, Gen 10, current) use peripheral-average Z for cross/circle and center Z for draw, underscoring the value of structure-aware measurement mapping.

## Performance Analysis

- Optimal parameter budget lies around 50–60 total parameters  
  • Current best: 54 params → combined 0.48, gap 0.02, parameter_efficiency ≈ 0.009  
  • Gen 10: 72 params → combined 0.47, gap 0.02, efficiency ≈ 0.007  
  Trend: reducing from ~80 params (Gen 9/8) to ~54–72 improves generalization stability.

- Circuit depth beyond ~70 gates correlates poorly with test accuracy  
  • Designs with depth > 70 (Gen 8, Gen 12) either over-regularize or under-fit, despite high gate counts.  
  • Moderate depth (46–53) aligns with the top two combined scores (current best 0.48 at depth 46; Gen 10 0.47 at depth 76 but uses CRZ and shared symmetries).

- Fixed vs. parametrized entanglers trade-off  
  • Swapping CRZ for CZ-only reduced gate count by ~25% and boosted combined score from Gen 12’s 0.42 to current’s 0.48.  
  • Indicates that entanglement geometry matters more than per-edge trainable angles in this tic-tac-toe classifier.

- Small generalization gaps (< 0.03) coincide with middling train/test accuracies, highlighting a flat optimum  
  • Current best and Gen 10 both maintain gap 0.02.  
  • Achieved by balancing parameter count, depth, and symmetry-informed design rather than chasing higher raw accuracy.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Cycle through distinct CZ‐only entangling subsets across the three uploads. Rather than repeating the same eight CZ edges in every block, define three disjoint (or partially overlapping) CZ patterns—e.g. rotate which ring edge or hub edge is dropped/added each upload—so that each feature‐map re‐upload sees a different fixed entanglement graph. This preserves the low two‐qubit gate count while enriching mixing geometry and has shown in other architectures that varying texture across layers boosts expressivity without extra parameters.

2. Augment the final single‐qubit layer with shared RZ rotations (rz_corner_out, rz_edge_out, rz_center_out) immediately after the existing RY_out. Mirroring the proven benefit of the lightweight RY_out adjustments, a small class-shared RZ_out block can sharpen phase boundaries in the readout basis with only nine additional single‐qubit gates and three parameters, helping the model carve finer decision surfaces.

3. Refine the corner symmetry class into two diagonally paired subclasses to capture diagonal win features. Replace the single ry_corner/rz_corner keys with ry_corner_a/rz_corner_a for corners (0,4) and ry_corner_b/rz_corner_b for corners (2,6), keeping edges and center shared. This adds only four parameters (bringing total to ∼58) but leverages the existing compact symmetry pattern to better distinguish diagonal correlations that current coarse grouping may blur.

4. Introduce three learnable scalar weights in the measurement head—alpha_edge, alpha_corner, alpha_center—and compute class expectations as weighted sums of Z values (e.g. cross = alpha_edge·(z[1]+z[3]+z[5]+z[7])/4). This adds just three parameters at the readout stage, lives in the same autograd framework, and grants the network flexibility to emphasize or attenuate specific qubit groups when mapping to cross/circle/draw outputs, improving classification without altering circuit depth.

5. Add two diagonal CZ entanglers per ansatz block to directly link the center qubit with opposite corners (e.g. CZ on wires [0,8] and [4,8]). These fixed gates cost only two extra operations per block—keeping gate_count comfortably under 260—and target diagonal mixing paths that are under‐represented in the current ring+hub motif. Empirical studies of tic-tac-toe patterns suggest diagonals are critical for “three-in-a-row” detection, so this minimal connectivity boost can enhance expressivity without new trainable two-qubit angles.
