# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Periphery-vs-Center Parameter Sharing  
  • Circuits that split single-qubit rotations into “periphery” (corners + edges) vs. “center” groups consistently top the leaderboard.  
  • Gen20/22 (Combined 0.51 at 54 params, depth 40), Gen31 (Combined 0.51, depth 52), and the current best Gen32 (Combined 0.53, depth 52) all employ this 2-group RY layout.  
- Small Shared Z-Phase Block  
  • Introducing one shared RZ on periphery qubits (plus a separate center RZ) breaks parity and boosts expressivity.  
  • First seen in Gen29 (Combined 0.44 → Gen31 0.51) and retained in Gen32 (Combined 0.53).  
- Sparse, Shared Parametric Entanglers  
  • Replacing six static CZs with eight tunable gates—4×CRX on ring edges and 4×CRZ on spokes—but sharing only two parameters (ent_rx, ent_rz) yields the best score (Gen32 Combined 0.53).  
  • Outperforms both fully static CZ (Gen20/22 0.51) and heavy CRX/CRZ schemes (Gen30 0.41).  
- Final Compact RY “Output-Sharpening” Layer  
  • A last-layer RY on periphery vs. center qubits consistently adds ~3–4 test-accuracy points.  
  • Present in Gen13, Gen20/22, Gen31, and essential to reach 0.53 combined in Gen32.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Grouped CRX/CRZ Across All Grid Edges (Gen30)  
  • Despite respecting symmetry, grouping two-qubit rotations into too many buckets led to severe underfitting (Combined 0.41, gap 0.25) and gradient dilution.  
- Over-Splitting Symmetry Regions (Gen33)  
  • Aggressive region sharing with only 66 params gave test 0.43 and zero gap—model is too small to fit the task.  
- Full Parametric Two-Qubit Rotations Without Sharing (Gen23)  
  • 60 params of unshared CRX/CRZ achieved Combined 0.50 but train ≈ 0.38 and high loss—extra parameters in the graph do not translate to usable expressivity.  
- Excessive Depth Without Smarter Entanglement (Gen27)  
  • Stepping from depth 40→70 at 54 params dropped Combined from 0.51→0.47. More layers alone hurt trainability.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Early ANSATZ_SPEC Validation  
  • Calling `validate_ansatz_spec` up front (Gen20/22/31/32) catches miswiring before any training overhead.  
- O(1) Parameter Lookup in QNode  
  • Gen32 builds `key_to_position` once per block in `apply_ansatz_block`, avoiding repeated Python searches and slim­ming the autodiff graph.  
- Precise Circuit Metadata via AnnotatedQueue  
  • Gen32’s use of `qml.queuing.AnnotatedQueue` + `QuantumScript.from_queue` yields exact depth (52) and gate count (291), tightly correlating complexity to performance.  
- Static Built-In Gates for Autograd Efficiency  
  • Favoring direct `qml.CRX`/`qml.CZ` calls over higher-level wrappers minimizes graph size; Gen32 uses only built-in gates inside the QNode.  

## Performance Analysis  
- 54 Parameters Is the Sweet Spot  
  • All top circuits cluster at 54 learnable parameters: Gen20/22/31 (Combined 0.51), Gen32 (0.53). Fewer or more params underperform.  
- Depth vs. Expressivity Trade-Off  
  • Shallow circuits (depth 40–52) with targeted entanglement outperform deeper ones (depth 70+) even if gate counts rise modestly (237→291).  
- Gate Count Secondary to Depth  
  • Gen32 accepts +54 gates over Gen20 but stays within depth 52; achieving +0.02–0.04 combined gains demonstrates that depth reduction trumps minimal gate count.  
- Stable Generalization Gap (~0.05) Aligns with Top Scores  
  • Circuits scoring ≥0.51 combined (Gen20/22/31/32) maintain a low gap (~0.02–0.05). Circuits with large gaps (≥0.10 in Gen30/23) correlate with poor test performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Implement per-upload rotated entanglement subsets. Rather than repeating the exact same four-ring + hub CRX/CRZ pattern in each of the three uploads, define three non-overlapping “rotations” of the ring edges (e.g. in upload 0 omit edge (0,1) and replace with (2,7), in upload 1 omit (2,3) and replace with (4,1), etc.) while keeping the total entangler count and depth fixed. This preserves the proven sparse-entangler motif (Gen32) but enriches the mixing pathways, which prior rotated-CZ seeds lifted Combined score by ~0.02 without extra parameters.  

2. Append a shared output RZ sharpening layer after the existing RY_out. In each ansatz block add two new parameters—“rz_out_periphery” on qubits 0–7 and “rz_out_center” on qubit 8—immediately following the final periphery/center RY. Small end-of-circuit Z-phases have consistently added ~3–4 test-accuracy points (Gen29 → Gen31, Gen32), yet cost only two extra parameters and negligible depth.  

3. Refine periphery RX sharing into corner vs edge buckets. Replace the single shared “ent_rx” on all eight periphery RXs with two parameters, “rx_corner” for qubits 0,2,4,6 and “rx_edge” for 1,3,5,7, while retaining “ry_center” for qubit 8. This bespoke split captures distinct corner/edge geometry without exceeding the 54-parameter sweet spot, building on the success of periphery-vs-center grouping in top circuits.  

4. Introduce three learnable readout weights in class_expectations. Replace the fixed averages with α_edge·mean(z_edges), α_corner·mean(z_corners), and α_center·z_center, adding only three scalars post-measurement. Similar lightweight readout reweighting schemes have sharpened class separation in related QML tasks with virtually no circuit overhead.  

5. Insert a mid-block shared Z-phase layer before RX mixing. In each ANSATZ_SPEC block, immediately after the four CRX/CRZ entanglers and before the RX mixing, apply RZ gates on periphery qubits (param “rz_block_periphery”) and on the center qubit (param “rz_block_center”). Early parity breaking via mid-circuit RZs first lifted Combined from 0.44 to 0.51 (Gen29 → Gen31) and complements the existing small-Z-phase and final RY structure without increasing depth.
