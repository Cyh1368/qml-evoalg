The current program has 72 parameters per block (6 shared parameter keys × 3 uploads × 2 repeats) and achieves a combined score of 0.45 with good test accuracy (0.50) but mediocre validation accuracy (0.47) and a high generalization gap (0.13).

The recommendation suggests refining corner symmetry into two diagonal subclasses:
- ry_corner_a / rz_corner_a for qubits (0, 4) – main diagonal
- ry_corner_b / rz_corner_b for qubits (2, 6) – anti-diagonal

This increases parameters per block by 4 (from 6 to 10 unique keys), bringing total to 120 parameters. However, this targeted increase captures diagonal "three-in-a-row" patterns more precisely—a key feature in tic-tac-toe win detection (WIN_LINES includes (0, 8, 4) and (2, 8, 6)).

The motivation:
1. **Diagonal symmetry breaking**: Tic-tac-toe has two distinct diagonal win lines. The current 4-fold corner sharing treats all corners equally, missing the diagonal structure.
2. **Modest parameter growth**: Adding only 4 unique parameter keys (shared across 3 uploads × 2 repeats = 24 total parameters per block) is parameter-efficient.
3. **Improved discrimination**: Separates main-diagonal corners from anti-diagonal corners, allowing the circuit to learn asymmetric patterns.
4. **Validation improvement target**: The high validation loss (2.92) and generalization gap (0.13) suggest underfitting in diagonal patterns; this refinement should reduce the gap and improve convergence.

Expected outcome: Improved validation accuracy and reduced generalization gap, leading to a higher combined score.
