# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- CRX “ring” + CRY “hub” entanglement yields the largest lift.  
  • Current best program: Combined 0.60 (train 0.57, val 0.65, test 0.50; 66 params, depth 70) uses a single shared CRX around the outer eight qubits and a single shared CRY from center to edges.  
  • Compared to Gen 39 (“add_cry_ring_after_crx_ring,” Combined 0.49; depth 118) and Gen 35 (“add_crz_hub_edge_layer,” Combined 0.41; depth 156), this specific ring+hub pairing improves combined score by ≥ 0.11.  
- Three feature re-uploads × two ansatz repeats at moderate depth (~70) maximizes expressivity per gate.  
  • The best design converges to 0.65 validation accuracy in 100 epochs at depth 70 vs. deeper (118–156) circuits that stalled around 0.51–0.53.  
- Symmetry-aware sharing over corners/edges/center compresses to 66 parameters with balanced regularization.  
  • Shared pre/post RZ and RY rotations plus shared CRX/CRY parameters yield a small generalization gap (0.07) while enabling higher train accuracy (0.57).  
- RX-based feature map with ±2π/3 rotations stabilizes gradient flow.  
  • The smooth rise to 0.65 validation accuracy in the best model contrasts with jittery training in Gen 36 (Combined 0.48) and Gen 37 (Combined 0.46).

## Ineffective Approaches
- Swapping CRY hub for CRZ or CZ-based hubs underfits severely.  
  • Gen 35 (“add_crz_hub_edge_layer”): Combined 0.41, train 0.23—aggressive sharing + CRZ hub lacked expressivity.  
- Excessive sharing without diverse two-qubit gates stalls train accuracy.  
  • Gen 37 (“none” patch): Combined 0.46, train 0.33; Gen 38 (“enhanced_hub_spoke_ansatz”): Combined 0.43, train 0.32—high depth (118, 285 gates) but low expressivity.  
- Adding depth alone fails without new entanglement motifs.  
  • Gen 39 (“add_cry_ring_after_crx_ring”): increased to depth 118, gate 309 but only Combined 0.49.  
- Reducing repeats or uploads compounds underfitting.  
  • Gen 35’s limited repeats and aggressive sharing yielded minimal train progress despite 156-layer depth.

## Implementation Insights
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC + apply_ansatz_block empowers rapid ansatz mutations.  
  • The best program’s five-layer spec (pre-RZ, RY, CRX ring, CRY hub, post-RZ) consistently maps to depth 70 and 261 gates.  
- validate_ansatz_spec catches topological errors early.  
  • All high-scoring candidates passed adjacency checks against GRID_EDGE_SET, ensuring intended spatial entanglement.  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript.from_queue enables on-the-fly depth/gate tracking.  
  • Comparing best (depth 70, 261 gates) vs. other candidates (depth 118+), this tooling guided the pruning of unnecessary layers.  
- Structured feature_map implementation stabilizes training across boards.  
  • Applying RX(FEATURE_SCALE × cell) uniformly supports consistent gradient behavior leading to smooth validation gains.

## Performance Analysis
- Best combined score jump of ~0.11–0.12 over next best designs.  
  • Current best 0.60 vs. Gen 39 0.49 and Gen 36 0.48.  
- Depth ≈ 70 outperforms deeper (> 117) circuits.  
  • Optimal depth/gate budget (70/261) beats 118/309 and 156/357 designs, confirming targeted entanglement trumps raw depth.  
- Parameter efficiency correlates with expressivity.  
  • Best: 0.50 test acc / 66 params ≈ 0.0076 vs. ≤ 0.005 in under-expressive models.  
- Balanced generalization gap enables higher test accuracy.  
  • Gap 0.07 in best model vs. tiny gaps (< 0.03) but low accuracy (< 0.48) in heavily shared under-expressive designs.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Append a CRY “ring” layer immediately after the existing CRX ring in each ansatz block.   
   • In your ANSATZ_SPEC, after the eight `{"gate":"CRX",…,"param":"crx_ring"}` entries, insert eight matching `{"gate":"CRY","wires":[i,j],"param":"cry_ring"}` over the same perimeter edges.  
   • Evidence: dual CRX+CRY around the ring enriches two‐qubit correlations with minimal depth overhead and mirrors the high‐lift CRX+CRY hub motif.  

2. Split the single `cry_hub_edge` parameter into two orientation‐specific hub parameters.   
   • Replace each hub gate’s `"param":"cry_hub_edge"` with `"param":"cry_hub_horiz"` for wires [8,1] & [8,5] and `"param":"cry_hub_vert"` for wires [8,3] & [8,7].  
   • Evidence: anisotropic sharing preserves symmetry‐aware compression while capturing distinct vertical vs. horizontal center‐to‐edge entanglement, likely raising expressivity without extra gates.  

3. Introduce diagonal CRX couplings between opposite edge qubits.   
   • After the perimeter entanglement layer, add two `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[1,5],"param":"crx_diag"}` and `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[3,7],"param":"crx_diag"}` entries in each block.  
   • Evidence: targeted cross‐board connections capture fork‐threat patterns with just two extra gates, extending the core principle that selective two‐qubit diversity drives performance.  

4. Grant slight per‐upload flexibility by decomposing shared entanglement angles into a base plus upload‐specific deltas.   
   • Define parameters `"crx_ring_base"` and `"crx_ring_delta_upload0/1/2"`, and in `apply_ansatz_block` set angle = base + delta for each upload index; repeat similarly for your hub parameters.  
   • Evidence: maintains global parameter sharing for strong regularization while allowing each of the three feature uploads to fine‐tune entanglement, boosting expressivity as seen in delta‐parameter schemes.  

5. Insert a lightweight feature re‐upload between the two repeats in each upload.   
   • In your circuit loop, after the first `apply_ansatz_block` call of an upload, invoke `feature_map(board)` (or apply RX rotations only on edges) before the second repeat.  
   • Evidence: mid‐upload data refresh reinforces gradient flow and has accelerated convergence in multi‐upload QML circuits, without changing total upload count or depth.
