# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Parameterized CRX “ring” + CRY “hub” entanglement  
  • Current best program (Combined 0.60; train 0.57, val 0.65, test 0.50, gap 0.07) uses a CRX ring on the perimeter and CRY hub from center to edges.  
  • Achieves a ~0.15–0.16 lift in validation accuracy over the best CRX–CRZ (Combined 0.49) and CZ–CRZ (Combined 0.48) models, without increasing depth (70 gates vs. 70).  
- Three reuploads × two repeats layout  
  • Maintains sufficient circuit depth (70) and expressivity with only 66 parameters.  
  • Balances trainability and capacity—enables convergence to 0.65 validation accuracy in 100 epochs/30 steps.  
- Symmetry-aware parameter sharing across corners/edges/center  
  • Reduces parameter count to 66 while preserving rotational invariances.  
  • Combined with mixed two-qubit gates, this sharing yields a small generalization gap (0.07) versus ~0.12 in aggressive-sharing-only designs.  
- RX-based feature map with equidistant rotations  
  • Uniform scaling of board‐value inputs supports stable gradients, as evidenced by consistent convergence in the best model.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Pure CZ/CRZ entanglement patterns  
  • Cross-Hybrid QML (Gen 25, Combined 0.48) and CZ+CRZ-only ansätze (Gen 20/24) stalled at ~33–38% test accuracy.  
  • Lacked parametrized mixing angles, under-expressed spatial correlations in board geometry.  
- CRX–CRZ mixed entanglement  
  • Hybrid CRX-CRZ Tic-Tac-Toe Ansatz (Gen 29, Combined 0.49) improved slightly but still underfits compared to CRX+CRY designs.  
  • Zero generalization gap indicates capacity ceiling, not overfitting.  
- Deep circuits without entanglement diversity  
  • Hierarchical Symmetry-Preserving (Gen 28, Combined 0.48, depth 119) and deeper_crx_ring (Gen 29 depth 118) saw no score gains despite +40 gates.  
  • Extra depth alone fails to capture required nonlocal correlations.  
- Excessive symmetry sharing with uniform two-qubit gates  
  • Symmetry-Aware Hierarchical QML (Gen 26, Combined 0.44) underfits (train 0.32, val 0.49) despite only 84 parameters and depth 70.  
  • Indicates that parameter sharing must be paired with diverse entangling operations.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Explicit class-expectation mapping aligns qubit geometry to labels  
  • Averaging edge-qubit Zs for “cross,” corner-qubit Zs for “circle,” and center qubit for “draw” consistently decouples class outputs.  
- Rigorous ansatz-spec validation  
  • The `validate_ansatz_spec` routine catches non-adjacent wires and invalid gate keys early, ensuring correct entanglement topologies.  
- Modular block indexing via N_UPLOADS × N_REPEATS  
  • Current best: 3 uploads × 2 repeats × 11 unique parameter keys = 66 total parameters.  
  • This modular layout allowed direct comparison of parameter–accuracy trade-offs across candidates.  
- Use of Pennylane’s AnnotatedQueue for metadata  
  • Automatic depth and gate_count extraction informs complexity vs. performance analysis (e.g., 70 vs. 119 depth comparisons).  

## Performance Analysis  
- Entanglement choice dominates accuracy gains  
  • CRX+CRY (Combined 0.60) ≫ CRX+CRZ (0.49) ≈ CZ+CRZ (0.48) ≫ pure sharing (0.44).  
- Depth increases without gate diversity yield no score improvement  
  • Models with depth ~118–119 achieved ≤0.49 combined, identical to shallower CRX–CRZ designs.  
- Generalization gap correlates with underfitting vs. expressivity  
  • Under-expressive models (pure CZ/CRZ or uniform sharing) showed tiny gaps (0.00–0.05) but low accuracy (~0.35–0.48).  
  • Best model’s gap of 0.07 balances expressivity and regularization.  
- Parameter efficiency remains low but improves with accuracy  
  • Best model: test 0.50 / 66 params ≈ 0.0076, compared to ≤ 0.005 in lower-accuracy CRX–CRZ and CZ–CRZ variants.

   In your EVOLVE-BLOCK, immediately after the eight CRX entries, add eight `{"gate": "CRZ", "wires": [i, j], "param": "crz_ring"}` over the same perimeter edges. Evidence: mixed CRX+CRY gave a +0.15 lift, and a small CRZ phase layer should enrich two-qubit correlations without increasing depth.

   Replace each `{"gate":"CRX","wires":[u,v],"param":"crx_ring"}` with `"crx_ring_h"` if (u,v) is a horizontal edge and `"crx_ring_v"` if vertical. Evidence: directional parameterization can capture anisotropic board correlations and only doubles ring parameters (from 1 to 2), still keeping total params ≲68.

   In `apply_ansatz_block`, detect `repeat_index == 1` and apply `qml.CRY(angle, wires=[u,v])` for the eight perimeter edges, with a new `"cry_ring"` key in ANSATZ_SPEC. Evidence: the best program’s mixed CRX ring + CRY hub minimized its generalization gap; extending CRY mixing into the ring is likely to boost expressivity and regularization.

   Modify `feature_map` so that upload 0 uses RX, upload 1 uses RZ, and upload 2 uses RY (all scaled by FEATURE_SCALE). Evidence: the RX-only encoding gave stable gradients; orthogonal encodings on successive uploads can expose complementary board features and accelerate convergence.
