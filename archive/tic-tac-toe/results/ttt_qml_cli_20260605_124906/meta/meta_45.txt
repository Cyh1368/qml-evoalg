# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- Five-layer entanglement motif (pre-entanglement RZ → RY → CRX_ring → CRY_hub → post-entanglement RZ) repeated over three uploads and two repeats yields peak expressivity.  
  • Seed “ShinkaEvolve” ansatz: combined 0.60 (depth 70, 261 gates, 66 params).  
- Circuit depth ≈ 70 gates and gate count ≈ 261 is a “sweet spot.”  
  • Seed (depth 70) outperforms any variant; shallower or deeper designs (e.g., Generation 58 at depth 138) underfit.  
- Four-fold rotational symmetry sharing into three groups (corners, edges, center), applied both pre- and post-entanglement, balances expressivity and generalization.  
  • Seed uses distinct rz_pre_corner/edge/center and rz_post_corner/edge/center, for 66 parameters and a generalization gap ~ 0.07.  
- Single shared parameter for all perimeter CRX gates and one for all hub CRY gates concentrates entangling power without blowing up the parameter count.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Mixing two-qubit gate types or swapping roles degrades performance.  
  • Gen 62 (“none” patch, mixed CRX/CRY/CRZ hub): 0.45 combined.  
  • Gen 57 (“hybrid_crx_crz_hub_ansatz” in prior evaluation): 0.44 combined.  
- Over-aggressive parameter sharing or tying reduces expressivity and induces underfitting.  
  • Gen 61 (tie_pre_post_rz_params → 60 total parameters): 0.41 combined, train 0.33.  
  • Gen 60 (extreme collapse to 30 params): underfit at 0.46 combined despite tiny generalization gap.  
- Replacing parametrized entanglers with CZ/CRZ or non-parametric gates cripples expressivity.  
  • Gen 58 (outer CZ + CZ hub + CRX ring): 0.39 combined.  
- Increasing circuit depth beyond ~70 or parameter count far from ~66 yields poorer results.  
  • Gen 58 at depth 138: combined 0.39; Gen 59 at depth 84: combined 0.42.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Declarative ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec ensures correct gate ordering and early structural checks, simplifying ansatz modifications.  
- key_to_position mapping in apply_ansatz_block translates shared-parameter keys to indices in a hot loop with minimal Python overhead.  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript.from_queue accurately captures circuit depth and gate count before training, guiding design towards the optimal ~70-gate circuit.  
- Group-shared RZ/RY rotations around a compact CRX_ring+CRY_hub block concentrate expressive power in few parameters, as exemplified by the seed’s superior combined score of 0.60.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The seed program achieves combined 0.60, test accuracy 0.50, validation accuracy 0.65, generalization gap 0.07; the next-best variant (Generation 63 “Ring-Hub QML Ansatz”) peaks at combined 0.48, test 0.48, gap 0.12.  
- Depth/gate_count: only circuits at depth ≈ 70 and gate_count ≈ 261 reach combined > 0.50; deviations on either side drop combined by ≥ 0.14.  
- Parameter budget: ~66 parameters is optimal. Moving to ~48 (Gen 63) or ~30 (Gen 61) or ~108 (Gen 58) params reduces combined by 0.12–0.21.  
- Strong generalization gaps (~0.05–0.08) co-occur with high combined scores; gaps ≤ 0.03 indicate underfitting (Gen 64, Gen 65).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the edge symmetry group into horizontal and vertical subgroups. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the single “rz_pre_edge”/“rz_post_edge” and “ry_edge” keys with separate “…_edge_h” (wires 1, 5) and “…_edge_v” (wires 3, 7) parameters. This adds only 4 extra parameters but lets the circuit distinguish horizontal vs. vertical edge correlations—leveraging the same four-fold rotational symmetry motif that gave the seed its strong expressivity.  
2. Double up the perimeter CRX ring within each ansatz block using the existing crx_ring parameter. Immediately after your eight CRX gates around the ring, append a second pass of CRX on the same wire pairs in reverse order. This “ring-reinforcement” deepens entanglement without increasing the parameter count and stays close to the ~70-gate budget that underpins the seed’s peak performance.  
3. Insert a second RY rotation layer between the CRY-hub entanglement and the post-entanglement RZ. Define three new shared keys “ry2_corner”, “ry2_edge”, and “ry2_center” and apply them to the same 9 wires. This 3-parameter, 9-gate addition captures nonlinear effects introduced by the hub entanglers, boosting expressivity in a tightly controlled way.  
4. Replace the lone post-entanglement RZ on the center qubit with a pair of diagonal CRY couplings per block. Remove the “rz_post_center” gate and instead add two CRY gates from qubit 8 to opposite corners (e.g. 0 & 4 on even blocks, 2 & 6 on odd blocks) sharing a new “cry_diag” parameter. This swap preserves overall depth while enriching diagonal connectivity that is known to be critical for corner–center interactions.  
5. Alternate between two ansatz-block variants across uploads to diversify entanglement timing. Define a second ANSATZ_SPEC variant where you swap the temporal order of the CRX_ring and CRY_hub layers, then, in apply_ansatz_block, choose spec A or B based on upload_index % 2. This implements controlled pattern diversity across the three re-uploads—building on past success with block-ordering swaps—without changing depth or total parameters.
