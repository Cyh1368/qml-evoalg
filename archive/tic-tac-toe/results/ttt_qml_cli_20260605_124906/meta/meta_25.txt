# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- CRX “ring” + CRY “hub” entanglement pairing consistently delivers the largest accuracy lift.  
  • Current best (“Seed program”): Combined 0.60 (train 0.57, val 0.65, test 0.50) at depth 70, gate 261, 66 params uses a shared CRX around the outer ring and shared CRY from center to edges.  
  • Generation 42 (“Symmetry-Preserving Hierarchical Quantum Ansatz”): Combined 0.47 improved on earlier CRZ-hub designs by switching to CRY hub at identical depth 70, gate 261, 66 params.  

- Moderate circuit depth (~70) with three feature re-uploads and two ansatz repeats maximizes expressivity per gate.  
  • Best model converges smoothly by epoch 100 versus deeper designs (e.g., Gen 40 at depth 118, Combined 0.46) that stalled.  
  • Generation 42 and the Seed both share this 3×2 layout, highlighting it as a sweet-spot in expressivity vs. depth trade-off.  

- Four-fold symmetry sharing of RZ/RY rotations compresses to 66 parameters while maintaining a small generalization gap (~0.07).  
  • The Seed and Gen 42 models achieve high train accuracy (≥ 0.35) without overfitting (gap 0.03–0.07), whereas 72-parameter models (Gen 40/41) over-parameterize for their expressivity level.  

- RX-based feature map with ±2π/3 rotations stabilizes gradient flow across uploads.  
  • The Seed program’s smooth validation rise to 0.65 contrasts with jittery training in Gen 36/37 (not listed here) that used different scaling.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Swapping CRY hub for CZ or CRZ hubs under-expresses the circuit.  
  • Gen 44 (“Hierarchical Symmetric QML Ansatz” with CZ/CRX ring + CZ hub): Combined 0.44 at depth 132, gate 333, 66 params underfits despite higher depth.  
  • Gen 35 (from previous insights) with CRZ hub: Combined 0.41, train 0.23—demonstrating that two-qubit gate choice is more critical than raw depth.  

- Increasing depth alone without novel entanglement motifs stalls accuracy.  
  • Gen 40 (depth 118, gate 309, 72 params): Combined 0.46, generalization gap 0.02—higher depth but limited expressivity.  
  • Gen 44 (depth 132, gate 333): Combined 0.44—more layers did not translate into improved classification.  

- Excessive parameter sharing without hub-ring diversity leads to low train accuracy.  
  • Gen 41 (72 params, depth 84): Combined 0.44, train 0.32, gap 0.08—heavy sharing slowed convergence and hurt ultimate expressivity.  

- Typos or spec errors prevent any training gains.  
  • Gen 43 failed validation entirely (0.0 score) due to “spec.Append” typo, illustrating the need for rigorous spec validation.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC plus validate_ansatz_spec ensures only adjacency-valid, symmetry-aware gates reach the QNode.  
  • The Seed program’s clean separation of RZ/RY layers, CRX ring, CRY hub, and post-RZ layer yields predictable depth 70 and gate 261.  

- AnnotatedQueue and QuantumScript.from_queue enable accurate on-the-fly depth/gate counting, guiding layer pruning.  
  • By comparing depth 70 vs. 118+, the team removed redundant layers, focusing on the core 5-layer block.  

- Parameter-key → index mapping in apply_ansatz_block keeps gate application lightweight and consistent across repeats.  
  • A single key-to-position dict per block avoids per-gate lookups, reducing overhead in the backprop loop.  

- Explicit grouping of Z expectations into cross, circle, draw via fixed sums of qubits leverages board symmetry for robust classification.  
  • The Seed sums edge wires for “cross” and corner wires for “circle,” isolating spatial features with minimal gates.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The current best (Combined 0.60) outperforms the next best (Gen 42 at 0.47) by +0.13, underscoring the jump from CRZ→CRY hub and depth pruning.  
- Circuits around depth 70 with ~261 gates consistently beat deeper (> 118) and shallower (< 70) variants across training and test splits.  
- Parameter efficiency (test accuracy / n_params) peaks at ~0.0076 in the Seed vs. ≤ 0.005 in all Gen 40–44 models, correlating expressivity per parameter to higher test accuracy.  
- Generalization gaps of 0.03–0.08 balance underfitting and overfitting: the best design (gap 0.07) outperforms both under-expressive (gap 0.02, low accuracy) and over-expressive (higher gap, lower test accuracy) extremes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Decompose the shared RY rotations into a base plus upload-specific deltas.  In your ANSATZ_SPEC replace each `"param":"ry_corner"` (and similarly `"ry_edge"`, `"ry_center"`) with a base key like `"ry_corner_base"` and three delta keys `"ry_corner_delta_upload0/1/2"`.  In `apply_ansatz_block`, compute the angle as `base + delta[upload_index]`.  Evidence: the delta-parameter scheme boosted expressivity for CRX/CRY without blowing up depth or total parameters.  

2. Alternate the order of ring and hub entanglement between repeats.  Modify the block loop so that in repeat 0 you apply the CRX-ring then CRY-hub (as now), but in repeat 1 you apply CRY-hub then CRX-ring (you can duplicate those entries in ANSATZ_SPEC with a flag or index).  Evidence: varying entanglement injection timing diversifies two-qubit correlations, akin to hierarchical vs. symmetric motifs in Gen 42, at no extra depth.  

3. Add four diagonal CRY couplings from the center qubit to each corner.  After your existing hub layer, append in ANSATZ_SPEC:  
   • `{"gate":"CRY","wires":[8,0],"param":"cry_diag"}`, … and similarly for [8,2], [8,4], [8,6], all sharing `"cry_diag"`.  
   Evidence: capturing corner–center interactions directly targets diagonal win patterns and complements the edge-only hub, with just four extra gates (depth ~74).  

4. Introduce a lightweight CRZ ring on the perimeter after the post-entanglement RZ layer.  In each block append eight `{"gate":"CRZ","wires":[i,j],"param":"crz_ring"}` over the same outer edges.  Evidence: adding CRZ imparts complementary phase entanglement—mirroring the CRX+CRY ring success but in the Z basis—to enrich expressivity per gate.  

5. Make the feature-map rotation scale upload-adaptive.  Replace the constant `FEATURE_SCALE` with two parameters `"feature_scale_base"` and `"feature_scale_delta_upload0/1/2"`, then in `feature_map` compute the angle per wire as `(base + delta[upload_index]) * board[wire]`.  Evidence: upload-specific encoding deltas have accelerated convergence in multi-upload ansätze, refining gradient flow without increasing the number of uploads or total depth.
