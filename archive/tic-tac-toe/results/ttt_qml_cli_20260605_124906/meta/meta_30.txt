# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns  
- CRX-ring + CRY-hub entanglement pairing yields the largest accuracy gains  
  • Seed program (“ShinkaEvolve tic-tac-toe QML ansatz”): combined 0.60 (train 0.57, val 0.65, test 0.50) at depth 70, 261 gates, 66 params.  
  • Gen 42 (“Symmetry-Preserving Hierarchical Quantum Ansatz”): combined 0.47 at identical depth/gate/param budget.  
- Moderate circuit depth (~70) with 3 feature-map uploads × 2 ansatz repeats maximizes expressivity per gate  
  • Seed converges smoothly by epoch 100, whereas deeper designs (Gen 45/48 at depth 117–118) plateau below 0.50 combined.  
- Four-fold symmetry-based sharing of RZ/RY rotations (66 parameters) balances expressivity and overfitting  
  • Seed’s generalization gap (0.07) sits in the sweet-spot between under-expressive (<0.02 gap, low accuracy) and over-expressive (>0.08 gap) extremes.  
- RX-based feature map with ±2π/3 scaling stabilizes gradient flow across uploads  
  • Seed’s smooth validation rise contrasts with jittery training in variants that altered feature-map scaling.  

## Ineffective Approaches  
- Replacing the CRY hub with CZ or CRZ reduces expressivity  
  • Gen 45 (CZ/CRX ring + CZ hub): combined 0.41, test 0.42, generalization gap 0.10 at depth 117, 60 params.  
  • Gen 48 (CRZ-ring + CRZ hub): combined 0.48, test 0.33 at depth 118, 72 params.  
- Over-parameterizing per-upload deltas for RY rotations leads to underfitting  
  • Gen 46 (base+delta RY per upload): combined 0.39, test 0.27 with 120 params, 423 gates, despite increased parameter count.  
- Adding “adaptive” or intermediate gates without pruning depth hurts performance  
  • Gen 50 (enhanced hierarchical with adaptive RX/RY/CRY): combined 0.44, test 0.32 at depth 71 but 90 params and 285 gates; no convergence gain.  
- Specification errors or invalid gate wiring prevent any training gains  
  • Gen 49 failed validation (0.0 score) due to invalid gate definitions.  

## Implementation Insights  
- Modular ANSATZ_SPEC + validate_ansatz_spec enforces gate validity and adjacency before QNode construction  
  • Seed’s clean 5-layer block (pre-RZ, RY, CRX ring, CRY hub, post-RZ) yields consistent depth 70 and 261 gates.  
- Single key-to-index mapping per block reduces overhead in apply_ansatz_block  
  • Seed’s one dict lookup per gate avoids repeated parameter-key searches in backprop.  
- AnnotatedQueue + QuantumScript.from_queue provide on-the-fly depth and gate counts for pruning  
  • Seed measures depth/gate counts early, guiding the 5-layer block design over deeper alternatives.  
- Explicit expectation grouping leverages board symmetry for robust class outputs  
  • Seed maps edges → cross, corners → circle, center → draw with minimal extra operations.  

## Performance Analysis  
- The seed ansatz (combined 0.60) outperforms all Gen 45–50 variants by ≥0.12  
  • Highest competitor: Gen 48 at combined 0.48 (–0.12), depth 118 vs. seed’s 70.  
- Depth ≈70/gate_count ≈261 is the consistent sweet-spot  
  • Circuits deeper than ~88 gates (Gen 46/48) underperform; circuits trimmed to ~70 gates excel.  
- Parameter efficiency peaks with ~66 params (seed: 0.50 test acc / 66 params ≈ 0.0076)  
  • All larger-param models (≥72 params) show ≤0.005 efficiency and lower test accuracy.  
- Balanced generalization gap (~0.05–0.08) correlates with highest test accuracy  
  • Seed gap 0.07 vs. under-expressive gap 0.02 (low accuracy) or over-expressive gap >0.10 (instability).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Alternate the entanglement ordering between repeats.
