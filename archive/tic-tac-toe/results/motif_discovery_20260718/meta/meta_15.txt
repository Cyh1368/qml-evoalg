# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Pre- and post-RZ symmetry sharing  
  • Generation 21 (“symmetry_shared_prepost_rz_with_shared_zzz_and_crz”) added both pre- and post-RZ layers on corners, edges, and center, raising the combined score from 0.63 (Gen 14) to 0.65 without a large parameter increase (60→66).  
- Shared CRZ center-edge couplings plus CZ lattice backbone  
  • All top performers (Gen 14, 17, 20, and especially Gen 21) retained a CZ grid and shared CRZ on the four center-edge pairs, yielding stable entanglement and boosting test accuracy up to 0.68 in Gen 21.  
- Win-line ZZZ motifs in a reuploaded ansatz  
  • Embedding eight MultiRZ rotations on the tic-tac-toe winning lines across 3 feature uploads × 2 repeats consistently outperformed flat two-qubit‐only designs. Gen 21 reached 0.65 combined vs. 0.62 in the heavy‐motif Gen 16 variant.  
- Batch parameter broadcasting with validation-loss early stopping  
  • Executing the entire mini-batch in one broadcasted QNode call (∼7× speedup) and using L2‐loss early stopping stabilized training to zero variance (std 0.00) and a small generalization gap (0.04 in Gen 21).

## Ineffective Approaches

- Unconstrained motif over-parameterization  
  • Gen 16’s 96-parameter, 101-depth circuit (full ZZZ + CCRZ on lines) stalled at a combined score of 0.62—adding gates without symmetry sharing under-utilizes capacity.  
- Excessively deep circuits  
  • Gen 20 increased depth to 142 (72 parameters) but only matched a 0.63 combined score. Depth beyond ~118 layers incurs overhead without accuracy gains.  
- Single-layer RZ/RY without post-rotation  
  • Gen 14 employed one RZ and RY block before motifs and reached only 0.63. Omitting a post-rotation layer limits the circuit’s ability to mix motif outputs.  
- Dropping the CZ backbone or misaligned three-qubit definitions  
  • Earlier seeds that removed the CZ lattice or mis‐keyed ZZZ/CCRZ gates (e.g., Gen 15) either underfit (combined ≲0.60) or failed to train, underscoring the need for both local entanglement and strict gate‐spec validation.

## Implementation Insights

- Centralized parameter indexing  
  • Defining a unique, ordered PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK and a key→position map simplifies slicing a 66-dimensional parameter vector per block, as in Gen 21’s `apply_ansatz_block`.  
- qml.MultiRZ and qml.ctrl abstraction  
  • Using `qml.MultiRZ(angle, wires=[…])` for ZZZ and `qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[…])` for CCRZ integrates three-qubit gates seamlessly into backprop, preserving gradient fidelity in Gen 21 and predecessors.  
- Single-call batch inference  
  • Broadcasting `circuit_z_expectations` over the batch dimension achieves identical outputs and gradients to per-sample calls but at ∼7× speedup—critical for training depth-118 circuits within the epoch budget.  
- Validation-loss early stopping with weight restore  
  • Tracking L2 loss with patience=75 and `min_delta=1e-4`, then restoring the best parameters, produced zero-variance accuracy across splits in Gen 21 and capped overfitting (gap 0.04).

## Performance Analysis

- Score trend vs. parameter count  
  • Gen 16 (96 params) → combined 0.62  
  • Gen 14/17/20 (60–72 params) → combined 0.63  
  • Gen 21 (66 params) → combined 0.65  
  A moderate parameter budget (~66) with symmetrized rotations and motifs yields the best efficiency.  
- Depth sweet spot at ~118 layers  
  • Designs at depth 118 (Gen 14 and Gen 21) outperformed both shallower (101 layers, Gen 16) and deeper (142 layers, Gen 20) circuits.  
- Generalization gap correlates with symmetry complexity  
  • Models with both pre/post layering and shared CRZ (Gen 21) achieved a 0.04 gap, tighter than the ~0.08–0.09 gaps of simpler motif-only or single-layer models.  
- Parameter efficiency peaks when accuracy is high  
  • Gen 21 achieved test 0.68/66 params ≈ 0.01 efficiency—better than both bloated (Gen 16: 0.006) and overly compact (early shared-only: 0.01 at lower accuracy) designs.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the ZZZ “zzz_line” parameter into three orientation‐specific keys (“zzz_horiz”, “zzz_vert”, “zzz_diag”) in your ANSATZ_SPEC. Map the three horizontal win lines to “zzz_horiz”, the three vertical lines to “zzz_vert”, and the two diagonals to “zzz_diag”. This preserves symmetry sharing within each orientation while granting the circuit the flexibility to weight horizontal, vertical, and diagonal correlations differently—building directly on Gen 21’s winning-line motif success without increasing the overall parameter count beyond eight keys.  

2. Split the shared CRZ center-edge coupling into two orientation groups: define “crz_edge_horiz” for the (1,8) and (5,8) edges and “crz_edge_vert” for the (3,8) and (7,8) edges. This fine-grained sharing tests whether horizontal vs. vertical center-edge entanglement contributes unevenly to classification, extending the proven CRZ backbone motif in a more expressive yet still efficient fashion.  

3. Loosen the repeat symmetry on local RZ rotations by giving each repeat its own pre- and post-RZ keys (e.g. “rz_pre_corner_r0”/“rz_pre_corner_r1”, “rz_post_edge_r0”/“rz_post_edge_r1”, etc.). This per-repeat parameterization allows the two repeats within each upload to specialize their local phase adjustments independently, probing whether modest liftings of symmetry boost representational power while staying within the depth-118, ~66-parameter sweet spot.  

4. Augment the CZ backbone and CRZ layer with four CRX entanglers on the center-corner pairs ([0,8],[2,8],[4,8],[6,8]) all sharing a new parameter key “crx_center_corner”. By introducing lightweight X-basis two-qubit correlations analogous to the successful Z-basis CRZ links, you diversify entanglement patterns without materially raising gate count or depth.  

5. Alternate three-qubit motif bases across repeats by inserting XXX-type interactions on even repeats: wrap each ZZZ MultiRZ motif in Hadamard gates on its three wires and assign these a new shared key “xxx_line”. For odd repeats use the original ZZZ, for even repeats use the Hadamard-ZZZ-Hadamard blocks. This mixes Z- and X-basis triple interactions in the same eight winning-line positions, testing whether axis-diverse entanglement further boosts accuracy at the same parameter efficiency.
