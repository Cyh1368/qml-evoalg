# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

• CCRZ‐only winning‐line motifs on eight tic-tac-toe lines  
  – The current best “seed” program (combined 0.72, test 0.76, gap 0.01) and Gen 44 both allocate only doubly-controlled RZ gates (CCRZ) to the eight winning lines. This exact alignment yields the top combined score and minimal generalization gap.  

• Symmetry-grouped single-qubit rotations compressed to 13 params per block (78 total)  
  – All high-performing ansätze share RY, RZ, RX angles across corners, edges, and the center. The seed program’s 4-layer block with 3 uploads × 2 repeats (depth ≈106, gate_count ≈309) hits an expressivity sweet spot without over-parameterizing.  

• Four-layer structured ansatz block × (3 uploads × 2 repeats)  
  – Layer sequence:  
      1. Shared RY on corners/edges/center  
      2. CCRZ motifs on win lines  
      3. Grid CRZ entanglement  
      4. Shared RZ+RX  
  Repetition of this block delivers stable high accuracy (train 0.76/val 0.79/test 0.76) with zero seed variance.  

• Single-call batch QNode + validation‐loss early stopping with restore-best-weights  
  – The seed program leverages Pennylane broadcasting (~7× speedup) and halts training when validation L2-loss fails to improve by ≥1e-4 for 75 epochs. This protocol yields deterministic convergence (std 0.00) and a tiny gap of 0.01.  

## Ineffective Approaches

• Mixing unconditioned ZZZ triples with CCRZ dilutes inductive bias  
  – Gen 53 (symmetry_line_motif_crossover) and Gen 55 (symmetry_motif_hybrid_ansatz) introduce ZZZ alongside CCRZ on winning lines, but only reach combined 0.68 and 0.63 respectively, with gaps 0.05–0.04. Feedback cites over-parameterization and low parameter efficiency.  

• Excessive circuit depth/gate count degrades efficiency and score  
  – Gen 50 (interleaved_crx_entanglement) with depth 161/gates 435 scores only 0.64. Gen 51 (structured_line_correlations_with_crx) and Gen 53 have depths 118–135 and fail to exceed 0.68. Circuits deeper than ~120 add gates without boosting test accuracy.  

• Parameter counts beyond 78 saturate or harm performance  
  – Gen 51’s 84 parameters yield combined 0.64 (gap 0.06), while Gen 52 (102 params) only reaches 0.70. Expanding beyond the 78-param budget does not improve expressivity or generalization.  

• Adding generic entanglers (CRX) without strict motif alignment fails generalization  
  – Gen 50 interleaves CRX with CRZ but scores 0.64 with a modest gap, showing that unaligned two-qubit controls weaken the problem-specific inductive bias of CCRZ motifs.  

## Implementation Insights

• Native CCRZ via qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[…]) enforces problem-specific logic  
  – The seed program’s use of `qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=wires[:2])` for CCRZ on winning lines directly encodes “two in a row → rotate third,” boosting both accuracy and gap minimization.  

• Explicit ANSATZ_SPEC + PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK mapping ensures slicable flat parameter vectors  
  – Defining the gate sequence and deriving a key-to-index map (via `validate_ansatz_spec`) allows consistent parameter reuse across blocks, simplifying the 78-param layout and enabling symmetry sharing.  

• Batch broadcasting of feature maps and expectations for speed without numerical loss  
  – `predict_batch` calls the QNode once over the batch dimension, replicating per-sample outputs exactly (max abs diff 0.0) but reducing runtime by ~7× on default.qubit/backprop.  

• Validation-loss early stopping with restore-best-weights yields deterministic convergence  
  – Configuring patience = 75 and min_delta = 1e-4 stops training at the true valley of validation L2-loss and restores those weights, producing the minimal generalization gap (0.01) and zero std across seeds.  

## Performance Analysis

• Combined score peaks at 0.72 for the CCRZ-only, 78-param ansatz (seed program)  
  – No other variant in the evaluated set exceeds combined 0.68. The seed’s train/val/test accuracies (0.76/0.79/0.76) and gap 0.01 contrast with mixed-motif or over-deep designs that plateau at ≤0.68 with gaps 0.04–0.06.  

• Depth ≈106/gate_count ≈309 is the expressivity–complexity sweet spot  
  – Seed and Gen 44 both operate near this depth and achieve top scores. Circuits deeper than ~118 or shallower than ~100 underperform, confirming a narrow optimal band.  

• Generalization gap strongly correlates with motif purity and circuit size  
  – CCRZ-only designs yield gap 0.01, mixed ZZZ+CCRZ designs gap 0.04–0.05, and over-deep/over-parameterized setups gap 0.06 or higher.  

• Parameter efficiency (test_accuracy/n_params) saturates at ≈0.01 for 78-param CCRZ-only ansätze  
  – Increasing parameters fails to raise test accuracy, thus lowering efficiency. The seed’s 0.76/78≈0.01 outperforms all broader or deeper variants.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Experiment with a 2-upload × 3-repeat layout (N_UPLOADS=2, N_REPEATS=3) instead of the current 3×2.  This preserves the same total of six ansatz blocks (and roughly the same depth/gate count) but shifts more trainable‐ansatz depth deeper into the circuit.  By trading earlier re-uploads for additional within-block expressivity, you can test whether concentrating CCRZ-driven layers later improves downstream generalization without increasing parameter count.  

2. Tie each symmetry-grouped RX and RZ pair to a single “rot” parameter per group.  In ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the separate `rz_corner`+`rx_corner` (and similarly for edges and center) with one `rot_corner` key and apply qml.RZ(angle) → qml.RX(angle) sequentially.  This reduces N_PARAMS_PER_BLOCK by four, testing whether a more compact 9-param block retains the seed’s 0.76 test accuracy while boosting parameter efficiency.  

3. Split the grid-CRZ layer into two semantically distinct parameters—`crz_perimeter` for the eight ring edges and `crz_spoke` for the four center spokes—rather than one `crz_grid` and one `crz_to_center`.  Update ANSATZ_SPEC so that the four exterior edges use `crz_perimeter`, the alternate four use `crz_perimeter`, and all spokes use `crz_spoke`.  This retains the same 12 two-qubit gates and total depth but gives the optimizer finer control over perimeter versus spoke correlations, leveraging the proven benefit of motif-aligned parameterization.  

4. Introduce a mixed three-qubit motif in Layer 2 by alternating CCRZ and ZZZ across repeats: keep all eight CCRZ gates in repeat 0 (under `ccrz_line`/`ccrz_center`) and, in repeat 1, swap them for qml.MultiRZ (ZZZ) on the same winning-line triplets under new keys `zzz_line` and `zzz_center`.  This leaves depth, gate count, and parameter count unchanged but probes whether complementing pure CCRZ with collective triple-Z rotations can capture additional line correlations.  

5. Add a final symmetry-grouped RY “readout bias” layer immediately before measurement within the last ansatz block.  In ANSATZ_SPEC, append nine RY gates on corners, edges, and center using new keys `final_ry_corner`, `final_ry_edge`, and `final_ry_center`.  Because RY commutes with our downstream Z-measurements, this incurs zero extra circuit depth and gives the model an additional 3 parameters to fine-tune its output mapping, potentially nudging test accuracy upward.
