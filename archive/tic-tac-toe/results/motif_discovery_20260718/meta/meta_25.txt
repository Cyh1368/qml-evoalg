# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

• CCRZ-Based Winning-Line Motifs  
  – Generation 13 (“winning_line_ccrz_and_local_mixing”) replaced multi-RZ ZZZ gates with doubly-controlled RZ (CCRZ) on the eight winning lines, boosting its combined score to 0.72 (train/val/test = 0.76/0.79/0.76, gap 0.01) versus 0.66–0.68 in ZZZ-heavy variants (e.g., Gen 18 at 0.67, Gen 21 at 0.65).  
  – The current best program’s explicit two-in-a-row conditioning (qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[…])) directly encodes the game rule and yields both higher accuracy and tighter generalization.

• Four-Layer Symmetry-Grouped Ansatz  
  – A repeating block of: (1) grouped RY rotations, (2) CCRZ line motifs, (3) grid-backbone CRZ entanglement, and (4) grouped RZ/RX rotations—as used by Gen 13 and replicated in the seed program—strikes an optimal balance of expressivity and parameter compactness.  
  – This template outperformed flatter two- or three-layer designs (e.g., Gen 16’s mixed ZZZ/CCRZ block at combined 0.62) and over-deep motif variants.

• Pruned Depth (~106)  
  – Reducing redundant three-qubit layers lowered circuit depth from ~118–166 (e.g., Gen 23 at depth 166, combined 0.67; Gen 25 at depth 130, combined 0.71) down to 106, improving convergence speed without sacrificing accuracy.

• Batch Broadcasting + Validation-Loss Early Stopping  
  – All top-performers (Gen 13, Gen 25) used a single-call, batch-broadcasted QNode (∼7× speedup over per-sample loops) with Adam and validation-loss early stopping (patience 75, Δ 1e-4), yielding zero standard deviation across repeats.

## Ineffective Approaches

• ZZZ-Only Triple Interactions  
  – Generations 18 and 19, which substituted CCRZ with ZZZ on winning lines, stalled at combined scores of 0.67 and 0.64 despite similar parameter counts (~84), indicating that uncontrolled triple-Z rotations under-encode line correlations.

• Over-Parameterization of Triples  
  – Gen 26’s expansion to 126 parameters (depth 123) drove test accuracy down to 0.53 (gap 0.10), showing that simply adding more three-qubit gates without structure degrades performance.

• Excessive Depth Without Symmetry Sharing  
  – Circuits exceeding ~130 layers (e.g., Gen 19 depth 130, combined 0.64; Gen 20 depth 142, combined 0.63) incurred higher gate counts and overfitting without accuracy gains compared to the depth-106 design.

• Omission of Controlled-Phase Structure  
  – Variants that dropped CCRZ in favor of only ZZZ or single-layer motif insertions (e.g., Gen 16’s flat multi-ZZZ/CCRZ block) peaked at combined ~0.62, reinforcing the need for explicit two-in-a-row control.

## Implementation Insights

• Centralized ANSATZ_SPEC + KEY-TO-POSITION Mapping  
  – Defining the entire block structure in `ANSATZ_SPEC` and deriving `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` enables clean slicing of the 78-dim parameter vector per block, avoiding indexing errors when experimenting with new gates.

• Use of qml.ctrl for CCRZ  
  – Implementing CCRZ via `qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[…])` retains backpropagation fidelity and cleanly captures two-in-a-row logic, directly aligning ansatz structure with tic-tac-toe winning patterns.

• Symmetry-Grouped Rotations Reduce Parameter Count  
  – Grouping corner, edge, and center RY/RZ/RX keys shrank per-block unique parameters from 84 to 13, enforcing geometric priors that compress the search space while preserving expressivity.

• Single-Call Batch QNode  
  – The pattern  
    `circuit_z_expectations(boards, params)`  
  broadcast over the batch dimension delivered identical gradients to per-sample loops but ran ∼7× faster, making depth-106 Gen 13 training feasible within typical epoch budgets.

## Performance Analysis

• Gen 13 vs. Other Candidates  
  – Gen 13’s combined 0.72 at depth 106 (78 params) outperforms Gen 25’s 0.71 at depth 130 (84 params) and far surpasses ZZZ-heavy or over-deep circuits (0.63–0.68).

• Depth Correlation with Score  
  – Circuits ≤110 depth (Gen 13) consistently outscored those at ~118 (Gen 21, 0.65) and ≥130 (Gen 19, 0.64), suggesting a “sweet spot” around 100–110 layers for this task.

• Parameter Budget vs. Efficiency  
  – Modest parameter increases—from 66 in some ZZZ variants to 78 in CCRZ-based designs—pay dividends in accuracy, yielding the best net parameter efficiency (~0.01) by aligning gates with domain motifs.

• Generalization Gap Trends  
  – Controlled three-qubit interactions and symmetry sharing (Gen 13) drove the gap down to 0.01, compared with 0.04–0.07 in ZZZ-heavy or under-shared models, confirming stable generalization stems from rule-aligned gate structures.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the “ccrz_line” key into orientation‐specific parameters (“ccrz_horiz”, “ccrz_vert”, “ccrz_diag”).  
   In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the single “ccrz_line” parameter on the eight winning‐line CCRZ gates with three keys: one for the three horizontal lines, one for the three vertical lines, and one for the two diagonals. This tests whether the model can differentially weight row vs. column vs. diagonal correlations without increasing depth or gate count, building directly on Gen 13’s winning‐line motif success.

2. Split “ccrz_center” into two distinct keys (“ccrz_edge_center” and “ccrz_diag_center”).  
   Reassign the four center‐involving CCRZ interactions so that the two edge–edge–center triples share “ccrz_edge_center” and the two corner–center–corner triples share “ccrz_diag_center.” This lightweight refinement (adding only one extra parameter) probes whether center‐anchored triple interactions benefit from geometry‐specific phase angles, leveraging the tight generalization observed in the current best program.

3. Add a mirrored CRX entanglement layer immediately after the existing CRZ grid.  
   In Layer 3 of ANSATZ_SPEC, insert CRX gates on the same grid and center–edge pairs under a new shared key “crx_grid.” By diversifying entanglement into the X basis with a single extra parameter and no added circuit depth (stacking with CRZ wires), you can capture complementary two‐qubit correlations while preserving the depth‐106 “sweet spot.”

4. Parameterize local RZ and RX rotations per upload instead of globally.  
   Rename keys like “rz_corner”/“rx_corner” to “rz_corner_u0”/“rx_corner_u0”, “rz_corner_u1”/“rx_corner_u1”, and “rz_corner_u2”/“rx_corner_u2” (and similarly for edge and center). 5. Alternate the three‐qubit motif basis by interleaving CCRZ and CCRX across repeats.
