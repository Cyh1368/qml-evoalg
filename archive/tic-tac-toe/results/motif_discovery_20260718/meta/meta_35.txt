  – Layer 2: CCRZ line motifs  
  – Layer 3: grid-based CRZ entanglement (ring plus center spokes)  
  – Layer 4: grouped RZ+RX rotations by corner/edge/center  
  – Both top performers (Seed and Gen 42) repeated this block across three data uploads and two repeats, striking an optimal expressivity-to-parameter balance (78 parameters).

• Sweet-spot circuit depth around 100–112 layers  
  – The best circuits (Seed at depth 106, Gen 42 at depth 112) reached combined 0.72, whereas deeper variants dropped to 0.67–0.62 (e.g., Gen 31 at depth 130 scored 0.67; Gen 33 at 142 scored 0.62).

• Batch broadcasting + validation-loss early stopping

  – Validation-L2-loss early stopping with restore-best-weights produced zero run-to-run variance (validation_accuracy_std = 0.00).

• ZZZ-heavy or mixed triple-gate motifs  
  – Gen 31 (“Triple Gate Motifs” with ZZZ+CCRZ) and Gen 41 (“3-Qubit Motif QML Ansatz”) achieved only 0.67 combined, with larger generalization gaps (0.05).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Subdivide the “ccrz_line” parameter into three orientation-specific keys (“ccrz_horiz”, “ccrz_vert”, “ccrz_diag”) in ANSATZ_SPEC, assigning the three horizontal winning-line CCRZ gates to “ccrz_horiz”, the three vertical to “ccrz_vert”, and the two diagonals to “ccrz_diag”. This preserves depth and gate count while letting the model learn whether row, column, or diagonal two-in-a-row correlations deserve different phase strengths—a fine-grained extension of the CCRZ-only motif that drove the Seed and Gen 42 successes.  

2. Introduce per-repeat symmetry relaxation by duplicating each grouped single-qubit rotation key into “ry_corner_r0”, “ry_edge_r0”, “ry_center_r0” for repeat 0 and “ry_corner_r1”, “ry_edge_r1”, “ry_center_r1” for repeat 1 (and similarly for RZ/RX). This adds only 18 extra parameters yet allows each repeat to adapt its local rotation symmetry independently, probing whether mild symmetry breaking across repeats can close the last generalization gap without upsetting the four-layer block’s balance.  

3. Enrich the grid-entanglement layer by interleaving a shared CRX gate after each CRZ in Layer 3: for every “CRZ” on GRID_EDGES and GRID_TO_CENTER, follow immediately with `qml.CRX(crx_grid, wires=[…])` under one new “crx_grid” parameter. Because CRX and CRZ commute on distinct control-target pairs, this augments X-basis entanglement alongside Z-basis correlations at zero depth cost, potentially capturing complementary inter-cell dependencies that the pure CRZ backbone might miss.  

4. Alternate the three-qubit motif basis across repeats: in repeat 0 keep the existing CCRZ line motifs under “ccrz_line”/“ccrz_center”, and in repeat 1 replace each with a controlled-RX version (`qml.ctrl(qml.RX, controls=[i,j])(θ, wires=k)`) under new params “ccrx_line” and “ccrx_center”. This orthogonal basis alternation enriches the line-correlation subspace without changing block count or circuit depth, building directly on the successful controlled-phase structure while exploring whether X-rotated two-in-a-row semantics improve classification.  

5. Make the feature-map rotation scales learnable and group-specific by replacing the fixed FEATURE_SCALE constant with three trainable parameters (“scale_corner”, “scale_edge”, “scale_center”) and applying `qml.RX(scale_group * board[..., wire])` for each wire’s group in feature_map. This adds only three parameters to the global vector, lets the model adapt the input embedding to emphasize corner/edge/center features, and extends the proven symmetry-grouping principle to the data encoding stage.
