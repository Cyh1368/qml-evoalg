# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- Structured three-qubit motifs on tic-tac-toe winning lines (ZZZ) combined with a few targeted doubly-controlled RZ rotations (CCRZ) yielded the top combined score of 0.59 (train 0.65/val 0.64/test 0.58, gap 0.07). This is the Configuration-5 “Structured Triple-Gate Tic-Tac-Toe QNN” and matches the current best seed run.
- Pre- and post-entanglement RZ layers with symmetry-based parameter sharing (corners, edges, center) stabilized training across uploads and repeats without bloating parameter count (150 total) and contributed to zero stddev in accuracies.
- Three data re-uploads with two ansatz repeats each provided consistent feature re-injection that differentiated high-scoring architectures (Generation 5/current best) from single-upload designs, enabling deeper interaction patterns without overfitting.
- Batch broadcasting of the QNode over the sample dimension (7× speedup on default.qubit/backprop) was critical for reaching convergence thresholds in reasonable time, ensuring stable loss trajectories in the best program.

## Ineffective Approaches

- Purely motif-based ZZZ layers without selective CCRZ controls (Generation 6 “Motif-Discovery Tic-Tac-Toe QML Ansatz”) plateaued at combined score 0.58 (train 0.62/val 0.58/test 0.58, gap 0.04) despite only 54 parameters, indicating ZZZ alone lacks sufficient expressivity.
- Adding excessive depth and gates (Generation 12 “Motif-Targeted” with 118 depth/333 gates) degraded test accuracy to 0.49 (combined 0.55), showing that unpruned motif layers can harm generalization.
- Simply increasing parameter count (Generation 0 with 162 params) did not improve beyond a 0.58 combined score, highlighting that more parameters without targeted three-qubit structure is wasteful.
- Over-entangling via full grid-edge CZs plus all possible triple-gate permutations (without domain guidance) led to vanishing parameter efficiency (≈0.00) and no test-score gains in several variants.

## Implementation Insights

- Leveraging qml.MultiRZ for ZZZ interactions and qml.ctrl(qml.RZ) for CCRZ integrates seamlessly with backprop, enabling efficient gradient flow through three-qubit gates (as in the best program’s ANSATZ_SPEC).
- Grouping RZ parameters by qubit symmetry (corners, edges, center) reduced the per-block key count from a potential 9+9 to 3+9+3, striking a balance between expressivity and overparameterization.
- The generic `apply_ansatz_block` pattern with a central `PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK` lookup and block-index offsets simplified extending the gate vocabulary (single, two-qubit, and three-qubit) without altering the training loop.
- Broadcasting the input batch into a single QNode call (`circuit_z_expectations`) and then reshaping outputs allowed ~7× faster throughput, making routines like early-stopping and frequent evaluation practicable on CPU simulators.

## Performance Analysis

- Across seven evaluated programs, the maximum test accuracy clustered tightly at ~0.58–0.59; only the structured triple-gate variant reached 0.58 with no stddev, underlining its stability.
- Generalization gaps ranged from 0.03 (Generation 7) to 0.07 (current best and some others); the best balance of test accuracy and gap was still the structured ZZZ+CCRZ design despite its slightly higher gap.
- Parameter efficiency was effectively zero for all high-param designs (78–162 params), with only the 54-parameter motif-only model reaching 0.01—but at the cost of worse overall accuracy.
- Depth beyond ~100 layers (Operations >300) correlated with performance drops; the current best’s depth of 106 and gate count of 321 sat at a “sweet spot” before over-entanglement degraded generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Introduce targeted two-qubit CRZ entanglers on the high-leverage edge-center pairs. In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the fixed CZ gates on wires [1, 8] and [5, 8] with parametrized CRZ operations (qml.CRZ) using two new keys (e.g. “crz_edge_pair1” shared between [1, 8] & [5, 8], and “crz_edge_pair2” shared between [3, 8] & [7, 8]). This adds mid-level conditional rotations exactly where three-qubit CCRZs proved powerful, without a big depth penalty.  

2. Test replacing low-impact ZZZ motifs with CCRZ controls on non-central winning lines. Swap out the “zzz_bottom” ([6, 5, 4]) and “zzz_right” ([2, 3, 4]) MultiRZ blocks for doubly-controlled RZs (qml.ctrl(qml.RZ)) parameterized by “ccrz_bottom” and “ccrz_right.” This probes whether selective three-qubit controls can outperform uniform ZZZ on less critical lines.  

3. Apply symmetry-based parameter sharing to all CCRZ gates. Merge your existing “ccrz_corner_center_main” and “ccrz_corner_center_anti” into a single key (e.g. “ccrz_diag_shared”), and map it to both diagonal triples ([0, 4, 8] and [2, 6, 8]). Reducing CCRZ parameter count in this way echoes the success of the corner/edge/center RZ grouping and should improve parameter efficiency.  

4. Explore deeper local repeats by increasing N_REPEATS from 2 to 3. Keep N_UPLOADS=3 and the current ANSATZ_SPEC, but set N_REPEATS=3 so each upload has three ansatz blocks. Monitor the resulting circuit depth (aiming to stay near the ~100–120 sweet spot) to see if the extra repeat yields gains without triggering over-entanglement.  

5. Augment your triple-gate vocabulary with X-basis entanglers on winning lines. For each WIN_LINES triple, insert a new block in ANSATZ_SPEC that wraps a ZZZ MultiRZ with Hadamard gates on its wires (i.e. H on each qubit, MultiRZ(angle_xxx), H on each qubit), using new shared keys like “xxx_top”, “xxx_diag”, etc. This introduces complementary XXX-type interactions and tests whether axis-diverse three-qubit rotations can further boost generalization.
