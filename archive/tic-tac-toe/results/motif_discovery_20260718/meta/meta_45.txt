# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

• CCRZ‐only winning‐line motifs aligned to the eight tic-tac-toe lines  
  – The current best (“Seed program for ShinkaEvolve…”) and Gen 44 both restrict three-qubit gates to doubly-controlled RZs (CCRZ) placed exactly on the eight winning lines. Each achieves a combined score of 0.72, test accuracy ≈ 0.76–0.79, and a minimal generalization gap of 0.01.  

• Symmetry‐shared single-qubit rotations compressed into 13 params/block (78 total)  
  – All top-scoring ansätze (seed & Gen 44) share RY, RZ, and RX angles across corners, edges, and center, hitting an expressivity sweet spot. This yields stable accuracies (zero std) and parameter efficiency ≈ 0.01.  

• Four-layer structured ansatz block × (3 uploads × 2 repeats)  
  – Layer 1: shared RY; Layer 2: CCRZ motifs; Layer 3: grid CRZ; Layer 4: shared RZ+RX. Repeating this block gives depth ≈ 106–112 and gate count ≈ 279–309, maximizing performance without over-deepening.  

• Single-call batch QNode + validation-loss early stopping  
  – Both the seed program and Gen 44 execute full mini-batches via Pennylane broadcasting (~7× speedup) and employ L2-loss early stopping with restore-best-weights. This eliminates run-to-run variance (std = 0.00) and ensures minimal gap.  

## Ineffective Approaches

• Mixing unconditioned ZZZ triples with CCRZ degrades robustness  
  – Gen 43 (mixed ZZZ+CCRZ, 78 params) scores 0.69 combined with gap 0.04; Gen 52 (ZZZ+CCRZ+CCRZ, 102 params) only reaches 0.70 combined with gap 0.05. Introducing ZZZ dilutes the problem-specific inductive bias of CCRZ.  

• Pure ZZZ three-qubit motifs underperform CCRZ-only designs  
  – Gen 46 (72 params, ZZZ-only motifs) achieves just 0.66 combined, test accuracy 0.69, and gap 0.05; Gen 49 (72 params, triple-Z) hits test 0.77 but higher gap (0.04) and no improvement in combined score.  

• Over-parameterization and excess depth hurt efficiency  
  – Gen 52 with 102 params/depth 135 scores only 0.70 combined and parameter efficiency remains at 0.01. Circuits deeper than ~120 drastically increase gate count without raising accuracy.  

• Hybrid entanglers without problem alignment fail generalization  
  – Gen 47’s CZ+CRX hybrid grid＋winning-line ZZZ yields the worst combined score (0.63) and a large gap (0.10), showing that adding generic entanglers without strict motif placement undermines inductive bias.  

## Implementation Insights

• Explicit ANSATZ_SPEC + PARAMETER_KEYS_PER_BLOCK mapping  
  – Defining the gate sequence in a single list and deriving key-to-index mappings ensures consistent slicing of the flat parameter vector, as seen in the seed program’s `validate_ansatz_spec` and `apply_ansatz_block`.  

• Native CCRZ via qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[…])  
  – Both the seed and Gen 44 use `qml.ctrl(qml.RZ, controls=[i,j])` to implement doubly‐controlled rotations directly, enforcing two-in‐a-row logic and boosting both expressivity and generalization.  

• Batch broadcasting of feature maps and expectations  
  – `predict_batch` leverages Pennylane’s broadcasting to compute B circuits in one QNode call. The seed program reports mathematically identical outputs but ~7× faster training on default.qubit/backprop.  

• Validation-loss early stopping with restore-best-weights  
  – Training halts when validation L2-loss fails to improve by at least 1e-4 for 75 epochs, then restores the best parameters. This protocol yields the minimal gap (0.01) and stable test accuracies (std = 0.00).  

## Performance Analysis

• Combined score peaks at 0.72 for CCRZ-only, 78-param ansätze  
  – Seed and Gen 44 both achieve combined 0.72, test accuracy ≈ 0.76–0.79, gap = 0.01, depth ≈ 106–112, gate_count ≈ 279–309. No other configuration surpasses this.  

• Introducing ZZZ triples yields marginal train gains but hurts gap  
  – Gen 43 and Gen 49 push train accuracy to ~0.82, but their gaps remain ~0.04, pulling combined scores down to 0.69–0.70.  

• Parameter count beyond 78 saturates performance  
  – Increasing to 84 params (e.g., via extra motifs) or 102 params (Gen 52) fails to boost test accuracy or efficiency, confirming 78 is the optimal budget for this task.  

• Deeper circuits (>120 depth) do not improve test accuracy  
  – Gen 52 (depth 135) and Gen 43 (depth 118) underperform relative to depth 106. This underscores the expressivity–complexity trade-off and the existence of a “sweet spot” at ~106 depth.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the CCRZ “line” and “center” parameters into orientation-specific keys.  In your ANSATZ_SPEC, replace the single `ccrz_line` and `ccrz_center` params with three separate keys—`ccrz_horiz`, `ccrz_vert`, and `ccrz_diag`—and assign the horizontal, vertical, and diagonal winning-line CCRZ gates to these respectively.  This follows the CCRZ-only motif success while giving the optimizer fine-grained control over row, column, and diagonal phase correlations without altering depth or gate count.  

2. Subdivide the grid CRZ entanglers by edge orientation.  Instead of one `crz_grid` parameter for all ring edges, define `crz_horiz` for the four horizontal edges and `crz_vert` for the four vertical edges (keeping `crz_to_center` for spokes).  This maintains the same number of gates and depth, adds only one extra parameter, and tests whether the model benefits from learning distinct horizontal versus vertical inter-cell couplings.  

3. Enhance Layer 3 entanglement with commuting CRX gates at zero extra depth.  After each CRZ in the grid and spoke layers, insert a `CRX` on the same wire pair under a new shared key—e.g. `crx_grid`.  Because CRX and CRZ commute on different pairs, this costs no additional circuit depth but introduces complementary X-basis correlations that may capture inter-cell dependencies beyond pure phase entanglement.  

4. Diversify three-qubit motifs by alternating CCRZ and controlled-RX across repeats.  In repeat 0 of each block keep the existing CCRZ winning-line gates, but in repeat 1 swap them for `qml.ctrl(qml.RX, controls=[...])` under new params `ccrx_horiz`, `ccrx_vert`, and `ccrx_diag`.  This preserves the two-in-a-row logic and overall gate count while broadening the subspace of line correlations the circuit can express.  

5. Make the feature-map scale learnable and group-specific.  Replace the fixed `FEATURE_SCALE` with three trainable parameters—`scale_corner`, `scale_edge`, and `scale_center`—and apply `qml.RX(scale_group * board[..., wire])` in `feature_map`.  This extends the proven symmetry-sharing principle to the input encoding, allowing the model to adapt the strength of corner, edge, and center embeddings for better initialization of the downstream CCRZ-driven ansatz.
