# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- Introducing trainable CRY “line” entanglers between adjacent grid cells (Generation 9 “trainable_cry_line_features”) boosted combined score from 0.39 → 0.53 by explicitly encoding two‐in‐a‐row correlations.  
- Sharing RY decoders across the D₄ orbits (corners, edges, center) reduced parameters to 72 while preserving expressivity, achieving 0.43/0.56/0.52 train/validation/test accuracies and a combined 0.53 score (Generations 9 & 10).  
- Broadcasting center information via CRY gates to edge midpoints enabled low-depth routes for diagonal and center‐row/column evidence, contributing to stable performance with only 285 gates (depth≈102).  
- Maintaining a 3-upload × 2-repeat re-uploading layout balanced depth vs. expressivity: all top performers used N_UPLOADS=3, N_REPEATS=2 to attain the best scores without overfitting.

## Ineffective Approaches
- The 6-block orbit-based ansatz in Generation 7 (90 params, depth 138, 411 gates) achieved only 0.39 combined score—underfitting persisted despite higher gate counts.  
- Swapping CRY entanglers for CNOT/CRX mixers (Generation 11 “cnot_crx_line_mixer”) yielded near-random accuracies (~33%/40%/35%) and negligible parameter efficiency, indicating that unstructured mixers failed to capture game symmetries.  
- Overly shallow, symmetry-shared circuits without explicit two-cell “line” features (Generation 12 “cry_lines_with_diag_paths”) plateaued at ~0.44 combined score, showing limited expressivity when higher-order features were omitted.

## Implementation Insights
- Defining ANSATZ_SPEC as a list of gate‐dicts with shared `param` keys, then validating and auto-indexing via `validate_ansatz_spec`, streamlines spec mutations and ensures consistency across uploads and repeats.  
- Grouping per-qubit Z expectations into three class outputs—edges for “cross,” corners for “circle,” center for “draw”—avoids extra classical layers and directly leverages circuit measurements for multi-class logits.  
- Building a `key_to_position` map once per block in `apply_ansatz_block` eliminates repeated string lookups, simplifying gate instantiation and minimizing runtime overhead.  
- Relying on Pennylane’s backprop autograd and a modest Adam step size (0.03) provided stable convergence without elaborate learning‐rate schedules.

## Performance Analysis
- Combined score steadily rose: 0.39 (Gen 7) → 0.45 (Gen 11) → 0.53 (Gen 9/10), underscoring the impact of structured CRY line features and D₄ orbit sharing.  
- All 72-parameter designs converged to a generalization gap of 0.02–0.08, confirming that underfitting—not overfitting—is the primary bottleneck.  
- Parameter efficiency held at ~0.01 test-accuracy per parameter across top circuits, suggesting diminishing returns from further parameter cuts without richer feature maps.  
- Reducing depth from 138 to as low as 84 gates (Generation 9) did not degrade the 0.53 score, indicating potential for further circuit compression while maintaining classification accuracy.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. Split the shared “cry_pair_to_edge” parameter into two orbit-specific keys—one for horizontal adjacencies and one for vertical adjacencies.   In practice, replace  
    {"gate":"CRY","wires":[0,1],"param":"cry_pair_to_edge"}  
   …  
   with two params “cry_pair_horiz” on the four horizontal pairs and “cry_pair_vert” on the four vertical pairs.   This modest parameter increase (<+1 param per orbit) builds on the success of line entanglers by letting the circuit weight row vs. column correlations differently, addressing the underfitting gap.  

2. Likewise, split the “cry_pair_to_center” aggregator into separate row and column parameters (“cry_center_to_row” vs. “cry_center_to_col”).   Assign the two CRY gates on the middle row (wires [7,8] and [3,8]) to “cry_center_to_row” and the two on the middle column ([1,8] and [5,8]) to “cry_center_to_col.”   This orbits-hared refinement preserves symmetry sharing while letting the circuit tune row vs. column aggregation strengths independently.  

3. Augment the ansatz with a new set of CRY entanglers between each edge‐corner adjacent pair (e.g. wires [1,0], [1,2], [3,2], [3,4], [5,4], [5,6], [7,6], [7,0]) using a shared “cry_edge_to_corner” parameter.   These gates explicitly encode diagonal two‐cell correlations in the same adjacency graph, leveraging the proven power of trainable CRY line features to capture off-axis winning patterns.  

4. Introduce a parallel phase-based entangling layer by adding CRZ gates mirroring each existing CRY block (pair-to-edge, pair-to-center, center-to-edge), with orbit-shared parameters (e.g. “crz_pair_to_edge”, “crz_pair_to_center”, “crz_center_to_edge”).   The dual-axis (Y and Z) nonlinearities have boosted expressivity in other QML settings and can be slotted directly into the ANSATZ_SPEC without altering the upload/repeat layout.  

5. Experiment with deepening local mixing by increasing N_REPEATS from 2 to 3 (keeping N_UPLOADS=3), or by alternating the order of the three two-qubit blocks across repeats.   Prior top circuits balanced depth and expressivity at 84–102 gates, and raising repeats modestly may close the underfitting gap while still fitting within a ~130-gate budget.
